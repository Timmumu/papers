Reif, Jr., Department of Civil and Environmental Engineering, New Jersey Institute of Technology, Newark, NJ 07102, USA ARTICLE INFO Keywords: Structural health monitoring Signal processing Building information modeling Digital twins ABSTRACT Structural health monitoring (SHM) of civil infrastructure is critically important due to its direct influence on public safety and economic activities. Exiting Building Information Modeling (BIM)-based SHM systems often use offline data processing techniques to analyze and visualize structural health data. Despite some of them adopting Internet of Things (IoT) to enable real-time sensor data collection, sensor data quality still remains uncertain due to a lack of sensor signal preprocessing in those existing systems. Additionally, the IoT-based SHM systems often disregard structural analysis domain knowledge, which is important for accurate and precise SHM. Therefore, there is still a need to improve existing systems and practices by enabling more efficient and reliable data collection and processing as well as providing more representative SHM data visualization in BIM. As such, this paper proposes an intelligent BIM-enabled digital twin (DT) framework that integrates wireless IoT sensing and communication, digital signal processing (DSP), and structural analysis domain knowledge. The proposed system (1) leverages IoT sensing and wireless communication to enable autonomous and real-time SHM sensor data collection and transmission, (2) applies and compares multiple DSP techniques to preprocess the sensor data/ signals, and (3) innovatively embraces structural analysis expertise into structural behavior visualization in BIM by performing sensor data interpolation for enabling the visualization of structural behaviors at different loca - tions of a structural element/component. The proposed BIM-enabled DT framework was demonstrated and tested for monitoring and visualizing the structural deformations of critical structural components using a prototyped structural frame subject to bending forces. The developed framework could be used and extended for any structural elements (such as beams, columns, trusses, slabs, arches, bracings, walls, footings, foundations, and girders, among others) and could be applied to any kind of structure. Experimental results showed that the proposed framework could effectively monitor and intuitively visualize the structural deformations under different load configurations with a high DT updating frequency of 5 Hz. The innovation of this study is reflected by integrating structural analysis expertise with IoT-enabling sensing data analytics in order to improve the representativeness of real-time structural behavior visualization in BIM and to advance the DT-based SHM systems in a faster and more adaptive direction. Ultimately, this paper contributes to the body of knowledge by developing a generic and easily extendable BIM-enabled DT framework for SHM with high sensor data quality and improved visualization to advance the existing practices of BIM-based SHM for civil infrastructure asset management. 1. Introduction Civil infrastructure encompasses a wide range of physical structures and facilities (such as buildings and bridges) that play a crucial role in the development and functioning of societies, such as providing the foundation for economic growth, public safety, and overall well-being ( Hu et al., 2024 ). Thus, the health condition of civil infrastructure is critically important to society due to its direct impact on public safety, economic activities, quality of life, resilience to natural disasters, and even public trust ( Osei-Kyei et al., 2021 ). As a result, a collapsed bridge will lead to disruptions in transportation, injuries and potential loss of life, delays in the delivery of merchandises and emergency response, chal - lenges due to disruption to transportation network, cascading damages to other surrounding infrastructure such as pavements, and erosion of public confidence ( FHWA, 2023 ). Therefore, it is significantly necessary to dynamically monitor the conditions of the civil infrastructure or its critical components by providing real-time data on the structural integrity, performance, and safety. Capitalizing on the advancement of the Internet of Things (IoT) technology, plenty of IoT-based structural health monitoring (SHM) systems have been developed for a variety of applications in the civil infrastructure sector ( Tong et al., 2019; Peng et al., 2023; Hu and Assaad, 2023 ). More specifically, IoT-based SHM uses an interconnected network of sensors (e.g., strain gauges) and other devices such as 4G modem embedded in the infrastructure. It collects data on many pa - rameters such as strain and vibration frequency and then transmits the data to a centralized platform using different communication protocols (e.g., Bluetooth, LoRaWAN, and Zigbee) ( Gao et al., 2018; Assaad et al., 2023 ). Afterwards, the data is processed, analyzed, and made accessible to infrastructure asset owners or other stakeholders for decision-making purposes. Empowered by more advanced communication technology and stronger computational capability of machines, digital twin-enabled IoT-based SHM systems have been developed recently for real-time data transmission and processing. A digital twin (DT) is a virtual replica of a physical object that mimics its real-world counterpart by incorporating data from various sources such as sensors, simulations, and historical information ( Botín-Sanabria et al., 2022 ). Building information modeling (BIM), on the other hand, provides many added benefits and advantages. In fact, BIM has been widely used to supplement IoT-based SHM by (1) creating a digital model of a structure with incorporated geometric information, materials, and components, (2) providing a user-friendly visualization environment to display the structural behavior and performance, and (3) offering high customizability to multiple items such as digital model ’ s property, level of detail, and view interface ( O ’ Shea et al., 2020; Gragnaniello et al., 2024 ). Therefore, it is beneficial to integrate IoT-based SHM with BIM because the research on BIM-IoT integration is still considered to be in the initial phases; hence, requiring substantial efforts to gain a deeper understanding of the current situation ( Tang et al., 2019 ). In addition, most of the existing studies on IoT-based SHM barely addressed the sensor data quality issue that could be caused by noises or missing values before transmitting the data for subsequent analysis ( Sakr and Sadhu, 2023 ). Lack of adequate SHM data pre-processing unavoidably reduces the reliability of IoT-based SHM systems due to uncertain sensor data quality. While some studies have attempted to integrate BIM and IoT for SHM ( Scianna et al., 2022 ), they commonly have a lack of demonstrating the interoperability of their BIM models and do not justify the rationale for guiding sensor installation on the structure or structural components either. Therefore, there is still a need to enhance the existing practices of BIM-enabled and IoT-based SHM by handling the sensor data quality issue, enabling BIM model interoperability, as well as explaining the logic followed for guiding sensor installation. To address this need, the goal of this paper is to develop an intelligent BIM-enabled digital twin framework that embraces IoT sensors, wireless communication, digital signal processing, and structural analysis domain knowledge for autonomously sensing and visualizing the structural deformation of the critical components of a structure in a real-time manner. More specifically, an intelligent BIM-enabled digital twin framework represents a sophisticated integration of BIM, digital twin technology, and intelligent systems ( Hu and Assaad 2024b ). BIM provides a digital representation of physical and functional characteristics (e.g., geometry, and material properties) of an infrastructure and/or its critical compo - nents. This digital representation will be used as the base to develop a digital twin model of the physical structure. A digital twin model is a virtual representation of the physical infrastructure, which works as a dynamic digital counterpart that mirrors the behaviors, status, and performance of its real-world counterpart in real-time. The real-time correspondence between the virtual and physical counterparts are enabled by intelligent systems with high-speed communication capa - bility. An intelligent system refers to technologies such as artificial in - telligence, machine learning, and data analytics allowing machines to perform tasks that typically require human intelligence. Such a system can collect and analyze vast amounts of data from IoT sensing networks or other sources to identify patterns and perform predictions with minimal human intervention. Therefore, BIM-enabled digital twins for SHM hold great significance because of its capability of allowing (1) comprehensive data integration to enable a holistic understanding of the structure ’ s behaviors, performance, and condition ( Sharma et al., 2022 ), (2) early detection of structural issues for supporting timely in - terventions to prevent structural failures or accidents, and (3) imple - mentation of preventative maintenance strategies to extend the lifespan of the structure, minimize downtime, and reduce maintenance costs ( Errandonea et al., 2020 ). The contributions of this study are summarized as follows: • Proposing a BIM-enabled digital twin framework with much higher updating frequency to advance the development of real-time SHM systems toward faster and more adaptive direction • Incorporating digital twins with BIM interoperability to allow seamless real-time SHM data exchange for supporting structural analysis, smart sensing, and structural behavior visualization • Innovatively integrating the rich structural analysis domain knowl - edge with sensing data analytics to unleash the potential of existing BIM software for more intuitive structural behavior representation and visualization 2. Literature review The existing body of knowledge on SHM is huge and includes various studies with multiple applications and methods. Since it is not feasible or possible to review all existing studies in the broader SHM field, this section focuses on relevant studies that have considered the use of BIM for SHM-related applications and approaches. This particular focus helps in offering better insights on the existing relevant literature and hence better identifying and highlighting the knowledge gaps that this paper specifically aims to address. 2.1. BIM-based SHM: General work mechanism and applications BIM functions as a comprehensive and digital representation of the physical and operational attributes of a structure. BIM emerges as an indispensable tool to optimize and document the performance of a structure across its lifespan. Based on its strong capability of information modeling and visualization, BIM enables a move toward intelligent management-related practices of structures with the use of smart sensing ( Kensek, 2014 ). Using facility management (FM) as an example, previous research endeavors have sought to merge BIM with a variety of sensing technologies and devices across multiple domains within building automation ( Abideen et al., 2022; Altohami et al., 2021 ). These studies focus on energy management in residential buildings, and employing sensors for measuring temperature, humidity, and energy consumption ( Abdelalim et al., 2017; Chang et al., 2018; Costa et al., 2013; Ozturk et al., 2012; Xie et al., 2020 ). Meanwhile, some other studies particularly concentrated on structural monitoring and assess - ment, either within the framework of operations and maintenance (O & M) or under the umbrella of SHM ( Panah & Kioumarsi, 2021 ). Optimal method for laser distance signal preprocessing Fig. 9 shows a sample of raw distance signal collected by the TOF 10120 laser distance sensor, in support of developing an optimal signal denoising method for laser distance signal preprocessing. As shown in Fig. 9 , the raw distance signal is comprised of distance measurements collected during three sub-periods: 0 ~ 90 s, 91 ~ 315 s, and 316 ~ 420 s. In particular, the signals from 0 ~ 90 s and 316 ~ 420 s contain static distance measurements, which means that the distance information is collected when both the physical structure and laser distance sensor remain stationary. However, the signals from these two periods fluctuate up and down and show great variance of the distance measurements, ranging from 270 mm up to 290 mm. Compared to the actual fixed distance of 278 mm, the absolute distance measurement error is about 8 ~ 10 mm, causing a low distance measurement precision if a millimeter-level deflection is desired. Regarding the signal during 91 ~ 315 s, it shows an obvious bell-shape pattern when lateral load is applied and released periodically, demonstrating the effectiveness of the sensor for distance measurement. Meanwhile, the signal segments for the static distance measurements have SNR of 67.19 and 71.53, which are much greater than that of the dynamic distance signal (i.e., 29.58). This further indicates that the quality of the static distance signal seg - ments is higher than that of the dynamic distance signal segment. The entire signal has low smoothness with an overall low SNR of 29.56 due to the noises caused by ambient light, object reflectivity, and interfer - ence from other electronics. This further shows and validates that an optimal signal processing method is required to clean the laser distance signal by removing the noises so that the signal becomes smooth to better represent the actual distance measurements. Fig. 10 ~ 12 show the raw distance signal as well as the preprocessed distance signals using both FFT and WT algorithms with different parameters. As shown in Fig. 10 , one significant observation is that the entire preprocessed signal tends to be smoother when the power spectral density threshold (PSDT) increases from 60 to 100 V 2 /Hz but has minor changes in smoothness with a higher PSDT than 100 V 2 /Hz. In addition, regardless of the PSDT, FFT performs well to smooth the signal (i.e., dynamic distance measurement) with lower SNR. For the high-SNR distance signal segments that represent static distance measurements, FFT could remove some noises as the preprocessed signal segments are smoother compared to the original signal segments. Therefore, the FFT denoising method performs the best for distance signal denoising with a PSDT of 100 V 2 /Hz. However, the preprocessed signal segments for the high-SNR signal segments are still subject to instability because the distance measurements show a wavy rather than a flat pattern in the preprocessed signals. This observation further reveals that both the PSDT and SNR would affect the performance of FFT on laser distance signal denoising. FFT can particularly well denoise the signals with low SNR yet suffer from lower performance on handing the noises in the high SNR signals. Figs. 11 and 12 display the raw distance signal and the preprocessed distance signals by WT using ‘db1 ′ kernel and ‘sym4 ′ kernel with other different parameters, respectively. A series of trial experiments show that wavelet coefficient threshold (WCT) of 25 works the best for both the ‘db1 ′ kernel and ‘sym4 ′ kernel. Therefore, the WCT is fixed as 25 when investigating the impact of another parameter (i.e., signal decomposition level) on the performance of the WT with any of these two kernels. Fig. 11 shows two major observations. First, regardless of the signal decomposition level (DL), a WT with ‘db1 ′ kernel generates a pre - processed signal with ladder-like patterns. The insight is that ‘db1 ′ (i.e., Daubechies 1) is a simple wavelet that resembles a step function, which has a compact support and is particularly effective at representing and detecting abrupt changes or step-like features in signals ( Daubechies and Sweldens, 1998 ). Due to this unique characteristic, for all preprocessed signals, WT with ‘db1 ′ kernel well differentiates the signals from static distance measurements to dynamic distance measurements, especially when the DL is greater than 3. Second, a higher DL enables the pre - processed data by ‘db1 ′ kernel to be smoother with less fluctuations. For example, when the DL is at 3, the preprocessed signals for both static and dynamic distance measurements still contain plenty of step-ups and step-downs. Once the DL reaches 6, the preprocessed signal tends to be stable, which indicates that continuously increasing the signal DL may not be able to improve the performance of WT with ‘db1 ′ kernel. Therefore, the optimal parameter for WT with ‘db1 ′ kernel includes a WCT of 25 and a DL of 6. Similarly, Fig. 12 shows the raw distance signal and the preprocessed distance signals by WT with ‘sym4 ′ kernel at different signal decompo - sition levels. In Fig. 12 , overall, the WT with ‘sym4 ′ kernel well handles both high- SNR and low-SNR signals, especially at a higher signal DL. For instance, a ‘sym4 ′ kernel with higher DL results in flatter and smoother signals for the high-SNR signals without compromising the denoising performance for low-SNR signals as the preprocessed low-SNR signals have minor changes. The results demonstrate that the proposed DK-DTM framework significantly enhances the accuracy and efficiency of O&M processes for aging bridges, addressing key gaps in existing digital twin methodologies. Accord- ing to the 2023 Statistical Bulletin of Transportation Industry Development, there were 1,079,300 highway bridges nationwide by the end of 2023. The rapid increase in the number of bridges, combined with higher vehicle density and load demands, has created significant challenges in ensuring their safe and efficient operation and maintenance (O&M) [1]. Aging bridges face structural issues such as cracking, water seepage, and damage to expansion joints, requiring advanced solutions for long-term monitoring and maintenance. In the United States alone, approximately USD 20.5 billion is needed annually to address the Appl. Sci. 2025, 15, 231 https://doi.org/10.3390/app15010231 Appl. Traditional visual inspections of bridges are labor-intensive and rely heavily on sub- jective judgments, often leading to inconsistent and error-prone results. This highlights the need for automated solutions that can improve both the accuracy and the efficiency of bridge health monitoring. Recent developments in computer vision techniques and image processing algorithms show potential in bridge inspection and structural health monitoring (SHM) of bridges [3]. Despite their widespread applications, computer vision techniques and image processing algorithms still face challenges, including noise interference, limited sensitivity, the complexity of setting appropriate alarm thresholds, and sensor reliability. Especially when dealing with complex bridge structures, these factors may result in missed detections, false positives, or system malfunctions, thereby affecting the overall effective- ness and reliability of the bridge health monitoring system [ 4]. It is therefore necessary to establish a smart O&M system that can monitor structural dynamics and capture spa- tiotemporal details for analyzing structure deterioration patterns and assessing structure failure risks. A trustworthy BDT enables seamless integration between physical and virtual envi- ronments by providing an accurate digital representation of physical structures, utilizing real-time data, and facilitating closed-loop interactions between the virtual and physical bridges [5]. The integration of virtual and physical systems overcomes limitations in time, space, cost, and safety, enabling comprehensive real-time assessments and predictive main- tenance for bridge structures. Therefore, DT technology can be applied in the field of bridge SHM, significantly improving its operational efficiency. In fact, DT technology has been extensively applied in various fields, including manufacturing, aerospace, transportation, healthcare, and energy. Structural Health Monitoring Over the past few decades, driven by advancements in sensing technology, data processing, and artificial intelligence (AI), bridge structural health monitoring (SHM) technology has experienced significant advancements. In the past, SHM mainly relied on manual inspections and non-destructive testing (NDT) techniques, such as acoustic emission, X-ray diffraction, and interferometry [7]. However, these methods have certain limitations, particularly in addressing complex bridge structures, as well as in real-time monitoring and accuracy [8]. Recent developments have underscored the potential of big data and AI for transform- ing SHM, particularly in terms of interpreting vast amounts of data to facilitate effective condition assessment [9]. The advent of digital technologies has facilitated a transition to more sophisticated, automated SHM systems. Long-span bridges worldwide, such as the Tsing Ma Bridge in Hong Kong and the Akashi Kaikyo Bridge in Japan, have been equipped with long-term monitoring systems, enabling continuous data collection and damage detection [10]. These systems utilize networks of sensors, including strain gauges, fiber optics, and wireless sensors, to monitor key parameters such as vibration, strain, and temperature [11]. The integration of robotics, including ground, wall-climbing, and flying robots, has further enhanced these monitoring capabilities by enabling access to hard-to-reach areas and increasing the efficiency of data collection [ 12]. The Internet of Things (IoT) has also contributed to the transition from traditional wire-based systems to wireless, real-time SHM, enhancing data accessibility and system efficiency [ 13]. For instance, Serlenga et al. (2021) demonstrated the importance of integrating diverse sensing techniques, such as seismic and electromagnetic methods, to monitor the Gravina Bridge in Matera, Italy [14]. Their study showcased how multi-source data fusion enables a holistic analysis of structural behavior, improving the reliability of SHM systems while addressing challenges associated with merging heterogeneous data types. While significant progress has been made, several challenges persist in SHM, particularly regarding data interpreta- tion, model accuracy, and system reliability. Traditional data-driven methods, although widely used, often struggle to accurately capture the complexity of bridge behavior under varying environmental and operational conditions [15]. Heterogeneous data from diverse sensor types further complicate SHM processes, requiring advanced data fusion techniques to improve model accuracy and system reliability [ 9]. In this context, the evaluation of safety formats for non-linear finite element analysis offers valuable insights. For instance, Tur, Viktar V . et al. (2020) critically analyzed widely used methods such as the partial factor method, global resistance methods, and probabilistic approaches, highlighting significant uncertainties and statistically vague formulations in current structural codes [16]. These findings emphasize the need for advanced methodologies to address variability in struc- tural responses and improve the reliability of safety evaluations, particularly for aging bridge infrastructure. Numerical modeling approaches, including non-linear analysis meth- ods, further contribute to the field by addressing uncertainties in structural performance assessment. As noted by De Matteis et al. (2023), non-linear analysis frameworks enable accurate simulations of bridge behavior, providing robust methodologies for assessing safety under complex conditions [17]. The integration of big data and AI technologies into SHM offers significant potential to address these gaps. Machine learning algorithms, particularly those used for vibration- and image-based SHM, have exhibited considerable potential in damage detection, pattern recognition, and predictive analysis [18]. However, many of these systems are still in the developmental phase, and challenges remain, such as managing heterogeneous data from different sensor types and improving the reliability of real-time predictions [19]. Digital Twin T echnique The concept of the DT, first proposed by Michael Grieves, was initially applied in product lifecycle management (PLM) [20]. NASA pioneered the application of DT tech- nology in the aerospace field in 2010. As technologies such as the Internet of Things (IoT), artificial intelligence (AI), and big data advance, DT technology has gradually expanded into more industries [21]. DT technology has become widespread across sectors such as manufacturing, health- care, aerospace, and construction infrastructure [22]. In manufacturing, the adoption of DTs has been key in facilitating cyber-physical integration, optimizing production efficiency, and enabling predictive maintenance through real-time data analysis [23]. Leveraging data- driven approaches, DTs have transformed smart manufacturing, allowing for enhanced decision-making and streamlined operations across the product lifecycle [24]. In healthcare, it supports personalized treatment and public safety management [20]. In construction and infrastructure, especially in bridge SHM, DT technology facilitates real-time bridge moni- toring and predictive maintenance [25]. Digital twins rely on technologies such as IoT, big data analytics, cloud computing, and AI [26]. Modern DTM not only encompasses physical and virtual entities but also integrates data, services, and networks, enhancing interaction between the physical and virtual [27]. (2020) demon- strate the potential of DT-based SHM systems for large infrastructure, highlighting the importance of real-time data integration in bridge management [29]. In bridge monitoring, technologies like machine vision, edge computing, and big data fusion enhance real-time monitoring and data processing efficiency for bridges. Machine vision and multi-source data fusion technologies enable effective monitoring of bridge traffic loads and structural health, facilitating timely detection of potential issues and preventive maintenance [30]. It enables multi-dimensional monitoring of bridge structures, allowing timely, data-driven maintenance decisions [31]. Despite significant advances in many fields, DT technology still faces numerous challenges in practical applications. Constructing and maintaining DTM requires extensive data support, with high costs for data collection, processing, and storage, particularly in the monitoring of large infrastructure such as bridges [28]. Moreover, as scenarios grow more complex, creating a multi-dimensional, multi-scenario DTM presents substantial technical and resource challenges [27]. For infrastructure such as bridges, achieving multi-source data fusion and processing, along with building a high-precision, automated DTM, remains a central focus of current research. 3. Sensor Deployment and Data Acquisition In BDT modeling, an effective sensor network is essential for acquiring high-quality data that accurately reflect the condition of the bridge [ 43]. Each type of sensor plays a distinct role in capturing specific aspects of the structure’s behavior, and their placement must be carefully planned to ensure comprehensive monitoring. Accelerometers are used to capture the dynamic responses of the bridge, particularly vibrations induced by traffic loads, wind, and environmental forces [ 44]. These sensors are typically installed on the main girders, deck, and critical load-bearing elements. Their placement is designed to monitor vibrations across various sections of the structure, pro- viding a comprehensive view of the bridge’s dynamic behavior. The data collected by accelerometers are essential for identifying modal parameters, such as natural frequencies and mode shapes, which help assess the structural integrity and detect abnormal vibrations that may indicate damage or degradation. Strain gauges are installed at key structural locations, such as piers, abutments, and support beams, where significant stress is expected. These sensors continuously monitor the strain in the bridge components, allowing engineers to measure how the structure responds to loads over time. The data collected reflect the bridge’s deformation under varying loads and provide insights into localized stress concentrations, which are critical for identifying potential fatigue and material failure. Strain data are fundamental for long-term health assessments, helping to detect early signs of structural weakening. Temperature sensors are distributed across the bridge to monitor environmental and material temperatures, particularly in areas prone to significant thermal expansion and contraction [45]. These sensors are often placed on the deck, beams, and supports where temperature-induced stress may occur. The data collected are essential for adjusting other sensor measurements, such as strain and displacement, as temperature variations directly affect these readings. Accurate temperature data are crucial for distinguishing between temperature-driven structural changes and true material deformations, ensuring the reliability of time series analysis. Global Navigation Satellite System (GNSS) sensors are placed on the bridge’s key components, such as the deck and towers, to measure displacements and monitor large- scale deformations [ 46]. These sensors are particularly useful for tracking movements due to settling, shifting foundations, or long-term environmental effects. The GNSS data enable the monitoring of slow, cumulative deformations that can impact the stability of the bridge over time. By providing precise measurements of large-scale shifts, GNSS sensors are essential for evaluating overall structural stability. The data collected from these sensors are time-stamped and synchronized to form comprehensive time series datasets. These datasets undergo further preprocessing to denoise and standardize the data for analysis. Introduction In the advancement of railway construction in mountainous regions, tunnels constitute more than 80% of the total infrastructure, with many being deeply buried and facing disaster risks such as high geostress, high geothermal conditions, and sudden water inrush (Li et al., 2021a). Typically, engineers depend on geophysical and sensor data to predict the geological conditions ahead of the tunnel face and to formulate the corresponding construction plans. Owing to the concealed and spatiotemporally heterogeneous nature of the tunnel environment, this setting transforms into a complex and open spatial environment (Wang et al. , 2021 ). In actual construction, engineers often depend on the interpretation of advanced geological prediction data to forecast the geological conditions, attributable to the limitations of detection meth- ods (Cui et al. , 2015; Huang et al. , 2022; Xin-rong et al. , 2015). Additionally, during construction, risks associated with high-temperature water inrush in these geothermally active environ- ments are more complex than traditional hazards such as high rock temperature or sole water inrush (Wang et al., 2024a). This type of thermal damage constitutes a systemic and comprehensive risk, arising from the interplay of stress, seepage, and temperature fields, which poses significant challenges in risk prevention and control during construction (Liu et al., 2023c). Existing simulation methods that focus on single disaster types are inadequate in accounting for the coupled effects between multiple disaster risks, thereby hindering ac- curate risk assessment. In disaster simulations, certain key parameters cannot be measured accurately, leading to a significant discrepancy between the calculated results and the actual situation. Consequently, addressing the second core challenge in this study involves constructing a thermo-hydro-mechanical (THM) coupling water inrush simulation model featuring accurate key parameters. To address these challenges, the solution strategy must be intimately aligned with practical engineering considerations, necessitating a focus not only on precision but also on efficiency—the hallmark of digital twin (DT). Through the integration of multi-source real-time data, mechanistic models, and expert knowledge, DT provides an accurate representation of the physical world and facilitates risk assessment, prediction, and decision-making (El Saddik et al., 2021). Recently, bolstered by the integration of the Internet of Things, big data analysis, and advanced simulation algorithms, DT has seen a progressively wider application in tunnel engineering (Hu et al., 2021). Indeed, DT offers several advantages in tunnel engineering, including: (1) DT enables the integration of multi-source observational data and facilitates the real-time updating of geological models (Wu et al., 2022). This rapid updating mechanism enhances the timeliness and accuracy of the model, enabling it to more accurately reflect the actual underground situation. (2) DT employs advanced machine learning methods to delineate the characteristics and trends of incomplete data in the tunnel and to unveil its underlying principles (Latif et al., 2023; Erharter et al., 2022). (3) Through the integration of models from various disciplines (Tao et al., 2022), DT is capable of accurately simulating the disaster process due to the coupling effect of multiple factors in tunnels. Although DT has found applications in domains like tunnel rock design (Xiaojun et al., 2023), operations and maintenance (Yu et al., 2021), and lighting environment optimization (Shen et al., 2022), its deployment in tunnel geological modeling and disaster simulation remains limited. This limitation emerges due to the specialization of these methods for specific scenarios, resulting in a lack of universality. Therefore, in addressing the aforementioned core challenges, this paper proposes a geological modeling and water inrush simulation analysis method tailored for DT applications. Specifically, this study advocates for the adoption of voxel models as the foundational framework for DT. Voxel models are considered ideal owing to their ability to store multiple attributes of the geological environment and to enable efficient dynamic updates (Wu et al., 2022). The contributions of this study are as follows: (1) A voxel model was utilized to integrate multi-source geophysical data, effectively characterizing the multi-dimensional attributes of the tunnel environment. The Gaussian Process (GP) method was employed to establish the nonlinear relationship between these attributes and adverse geological conditions. This approach addresses the limitation of traditional single data sources, which fail to fully capture the com- plexity of the surrounding rock, thereby enhancing the accuracy and reliability of the DT model of the tunnel geological environment. (2) We propose a parameter calibration method based on the Gaus- sian Process-Differential Evolution (GP-DE) back-analysis to improve the reliability of the water inrush simulation by accurately calibrating the tunnel environmental parameters. This method addresses the high parameter uncertainty and insufficient simulation precision in tradi- tional models when dealing with complex underground environments. (3) We applied the proposed method to a section of a tunnel with a burial depth of 230 meters in southwest China, which is prone to ultra-high-temperature water inrush. The experimental results prove the effectiveness and superiority of our method. DT modeling of geological environments The crux of tunnel geological DT modeling resides in constructing high-precision 3D geological models. Existing methods are broadly categorized into three types: geological statistical methods, implicit modeling methods, and geophysical exploration interpretation meth- ods: (1) Geological statistical methods: For modeling rock mass frac- tures, after collecting and statistically analyzing fracture orientation data, stochastic Discrete Fracture Network (DFN) geological models can be generated using methods such as Monte Carlo simulations. These models aim to statistically replicate the actual distribution of rock fractures (Gottron and Henk, 2021; Li et al., 2021b; Fan et al., 2023b). Additionally, borehole data surrounding the tunnel can be used in conjunction with Pluri-Gaussian Simulation (PGSIM) and Sequential Gaussian Simulation (SGSIM) to generate 3D spatial variability and uncertainty models of geological-geotechnical parameters (Gangrade et al., 2022a,b). Although geological statistical methods demonstrate significant potential, their effectiveness remains highly dependent on the integrity and representativeness of the underlying datasets (Ogbu et al., 2024). (2) Implicit modeling methods: These methods create three- dimensional geological models by solving implicit functions to re- construct surfaces that represent the isosurfaces of geological bodies’ geometric shapes (Zhong et al., 2021). Typically, functions such as Radial Basis Functions (RBF) and Hermite functions are employed to formulate interface functions, using geological points, boreholes, and other data as constraints to determine optimal geological interfaces. Boolean operations are then used to segment different geological bod- ies. This approach offers several advantages, including a high degree of automation, smooth model surfaces, and the elimination of topological errors (Sun et al., 2024). However, implicit modeling faces challenges when applied to tunnel geological modeling, particularly in moun- tainous tunnel construction. The harsh environmental conditions and technical limitations often result in the collection of limited borehole or cross-sectional data, leading to incomplete and sparse datasets (Xiong et al., 2018). This issue is especially pronounced with small datasets, where the spatial resolution of the generated geological model may not meet the high-precision requirements essential for DT applications. (3) Geophysical exploration interpretation methods: These meth- ods construct geological models by observing and interpreting various changes in geophysical fields (Rezaie et al., 2017; Olierook et al., 2021; Panzera et al., 2022). In the tunneling field, techniques such as TSP and Tunnel Reflection Tomography are commonly used to determine the spatial distribution of faults, fractured zones, and underground cavities (Fan et al., 2021; Pan et al., 2020). Additionally, methods like TEM and IP are frequently employed to detect water content and delineate the spatial distribution of water-rich zones (Nie et al., 2024; Tunnelling and Underground Space Technology incorporating Trenchless Technology Research 154 (2024) 106110 2 Y. He et al. Khan et al., 2020). Each of these methods requires specialized data processing software. However, these geophysical techniques operate independently, with each method capable of interpreting and analyzing only a specific type of data. This lack of integrated interpretation leads to limited accuracy in the inversion process (Li et al., 2014). In response to these challenges, and considering the increasing application and notable successes of machine learning in underground engineering in recent years (Huang et al., 2021), this paper presents a multi-source data fusion-based machine learning approach for tunnel geological environment modeling. By fully integrating multi-source data and uncovering hidden features, this method significantly en- hances the accuracy and reliability of the modeling process. 2.2. Simulation of high-temperature water inrush Presently, theories such as peridynamics (Gao et al., 2021), finite element method (Wang et al., 2023), discrete element method (Zhou et al., 2020), and smoothed particle hydrodynamics (Fan et al., 2023a) are utilized to simulate and analyze the spatiotemporal dynamics of wa- ter inrush disasters. To address the coupling effects of high geotempera- ture, osmotic pressure, and geostress, many researchers have developed various THM coupling mechanism models, including saturated porous medium THM coupling models (Zhao et al., 2020), THM coupling models that consider rock damage (Shi et al., 2023), and thermal- nonlinear hydraulic-mechanical models (Liu et al., 2018). However, the focus of most research on rock stability analysis or on the influential factors frequently does not directly guide tunnel construction (Peng et al., 2022), primarily attributed to the poor construction environment and technical limitations that result in incomplete data collection, hindering accurate determination of the boundary conditions and key parameters. Nevertheless, with the aid of the back-analysis technique, it remains possible to calibrate the numerical simulation parameters utilizing some monitoring data in the tunnel. Back-analysis involves inferring unknown environmental parameters through iterative opti- mization utilizing displacement, stress, and water inrush data recorded dur ... to document vault settlement, integrating two displacement monitoring sections into the model. More- over, a monitoring surface is strategically placed at the tunnel face to collect data on water inflow and the average surface temperature at the tunnel face. 5.3.2. Back-analysis The range of values for environmental parameters within the tunnel is detailed in Table 1 . The model applies elastoplastic theory and the Mohr–Coulomb failure criteria to define mechanical parameters. Elastic modulus and cohesion, identified as irreversible variables in Liu’s research ( Li et al. , 2023), are thus earmarked for back-analysis. Porosity, permeability, and hydraulic gradient, vital for influencing groundwater flow and the stability of subterranean structures, are deemed critical hydraulic parameters and selected for back-analysis. In the context of thermal analysis, the geothermal gradient and thermal conductivity, pivotal for simulating geothermal flow, are chosen as pa- rameters for back-analysis. The determination of these parameter value ranges is based on laboratory analyses and extensive references from the Engineering Geology Handbook ( The Compilation Committee of Engineering Geology Handbook, 2007) and ‘‘Code for Geological Inves- tigation of Water Resources and Hydropower Engineering’’ ( GB50487, 2008), ensuring a thorough and comprehensive approach to accurately modeling the tunnel environmental conditions. Due to the large number of variables, UED is employed. A 30-level uniform experimental design table is selected, designing 30 combi- nations of 17 parameters, and the parameters of each scheme are incorporated into the mechanical-hydraulic-thermal multi-field coupled model for finite element calculations. This yields values for the settle- ment of vault, water inflow, and tunnel face surface temperature. Simulation based on optimal parameters Optimal parameters were applied for simulation, as depicted in Fig. 14. The analysis of the temperature field revealed a gradual increase in temperature from the surface to deeper underground lev- els. However, temperatures near the tunnel walls remained lower, illustrating the thermal conductivity characteristics of the subsurface environment and the cooling effects induced by tunneling activities. Regarding the seepage field, the findings indicate that water pressure peaks within water-rich areas, subsequently dispersing towards the tunnel’s palm surface and adjacent areas of lower pressure. This behav- ior aligns with established theories of water movement within porous media. Integrating these multifaceted simulation outcomes into a voxel- based DT framework significantly broadens the model’s applicability and depth. Moreover, this integration provides extensive data support, crucial for the design and execution phases of tunnel construction projects. 5.3.4. Reliability evaluation To verify the accuracy of the proposed method, predictions re- garding the vault settlement, surrounding rock temperature, and water inflow at positions 6 m and 15 m ahead of the excavation face were made. These predictions were then compared with on-site monitoring data, as detailed in Table 3. The forecasts for vault settlement and internal rock temperature were highly accurate, exhibiting a MAPE of 5.4% and 2.9%, respectively. However, the accuracy of water inflow predictions at the tunnel face was comparatively lower, at 16.1%. Field observations indicate that the tunnel water inflow at the face is signif- icantly influenced by the location and quantity of deepened boreholes. Consequently, variability in predicted water inflow is anticipated, given the non-uniform distribution of surrounding rock fractures. The results suggest that the excavation analysis, based on the DT model, closely aligns with the subsequent construction excavation pro- cess. The DT model of the tunnel’s geological environment, constructed in this study, accurately depicts the actual distribution of water-rich and fractured zones in the tunnel, showcasing high accuracy and reli- ability. 5.4. Discussion High-temperature water inrush poses significant risks to complex mountainous tunnel construction. Accurate geological modeling and reliable simulation analysis are crucial for disaster prediction and the optimization of construction design. This paper distinguishes itself from previous studies on water inrush disaster simulation in several key aspects: (1) This study improves the efficiency of tunnel engineering. By leveraging a digital twin framework, we integrated multi-source hetero- geneous data, multidisciplinary mechanistic models, and high- performance algorithms to achieve automated and precise geological modeling as well as reliable simulation of water inrush hazards. In practical applications, the entire process of data acquisition, process- ing, analysis, and visualization is completed in approximately 2 h. In contrast, according to feedback from on-site engineers, traditional rock mass environment prediction typically takes around 2 days, in- volving manual sketching of the tunnel face, processing of advance geological prediction data, followed by interpretation by specialized engineers, and finally sending back a comprehensive assessment re- port to guide construction (Shi et al., 2017; Liu and Gan, 2023; Liu et al., 2009; Huang et al., 2022). This approach not only significantly improves efficiency but also ensures accuracy. Compared to the uncer- tainties that may arise from manual interpretation, the results obtained through high-performance machine learning algorithms and coupled simulations in this study are far more reliable. (2) An innovative approach is introduced by grading two key factors that influence water inrush: rock mass integrity and water inflow inten- sity. These classifications are then used to develop a high-resolution digital twin geological model, significantly enhancing the reliability of simulations. A key aspect of this research is the integration of high-resolution advanced geological forecasting data, which has en- abled the construction of a geological model with a resolution of 1 meter—sufficient to support high-precision water inrush simulations. In contrast, previous studies typically relied on geological models de- rived from interpolated sparse borehole data, whose resolution is far inferior to that of models obtained directly from geophysical data at the tunnel face (Liu et al., 2022; Xie et al., 2023; Wang et al., 2024b; Gao et al., 2019). The inaccuracies in these lower-resolution geological models and their boundary conditions have often compromised the reliability of simulation. (3) This paper presents a novel GP-DE back-analysis parameter calibration method developed within a THM coupled model. By uti- lizing temperature, water inflow, and displacement monitoring data from within the tunnel, this method directly or indirectly calibrates the key simulation parameters, thereby further enhancing the reliability Tunnelling and Underground Space Technology incorporating Trenchless Technology Research 154 (2024) 106110 12 Y. He et al. of the simulations. In contrast, previous studies have typically relied on displacement observations alone for back-analysis to calibrate rock mass parameters (Zhang et al., 2020; Deng et al., 2022; Li et al., 2023), an approach that is evidently insufficient for the complexities of a THM-coupled tunnel environment. Additionally, many studies have depended on empirical values for numerical simulation parameters. Due to the inherent differences between geological environments, these empirical values often lack broad applicability, leading to relatively lower reliability in the simulation results. At the same time, the method proposed in this paper also has the following limitations: (1) The 3D back-analysis accuracy for the water-rich broken zone heavily depends on the comprehensiveness of the sample database. Challenges in data collection and a lack of diversity in the dataset limit the model’s ability to cover all complex geological conditions, affecting its applicability and precision. (2) The employed mathematical model has been simplified to suit the tunnel environment, which may not sufficiently depict high-temp- erature inrush water scenarios in more complex geological settings. 6. Conclusions This study introduces a novel method for simulating high-temp- erature water inrush incidents based on a DT model of the tunnel’s geological environment, aimed at tackling water inrush and high tem- peratures in complex tunnel construction. The approach integrates voxel models with advanced geological prediction data, utilizing high- performance machine learning algorithms to construct a DT model of rock mass integrity and water inrush intensity. By combining machine learning algorithms with the finite element method and employing GP-DE for multi-source tunnel data joint back-analysis, the study accu- rately estimates optimal tunnel environmental parameters, facilitating reliable simulation analysis of high-temperature water inrush. Overall, this methodology provides a novel approach to addressing complex challenges in tunnel engineering and establishes a solid foundation for future research. Future developments will build on this study, further enhancing digital twin models and simulation methods to more effectively mitigate various disaster risks in tunnel engineering. Introduction The mode of production in the construction industry is characterized by intensive labor input, a large amount of investment, and multi-participation [1]. The construction mode relying on a large number of employees, and the worker’s site construction leads to frequent accidents. The modular construction method of the prefabricated building effectively reduces the pressure of site construction, but there are still serious safety prob- lems. Among them, the most common accidents are in the hoisting stage, such as “falling” and “suffering from mechanical or component collision” [ 2]. Therefore, the safety risk management in the hoisting of prefabricated buildings cannot be overemphasized. The current research on construction safety management and control mainly focuses on the construction of a safety risk management model and safety risk analysis methods. The safety risk management model refers to the systematic modeling of safety risk manage- ment to simulate [3] and optimize [4] the safety problems on the construction site. That is, to simulate the details of the construction and set the safety risk attributes, to analyze the hypothetical construction scenarios, and to most effectively reduce the occurrence of accidents in the construction stage. Ding et al. [5] proposed a web-based safety risk early warning system for subway construction, which adopted a multi-source information fusion model for safety risk management and safety risk early warning. Gunduz et al. [6] set up a dynamic risk management and control model to optimize construction safety management by considering the joint problems among construction stakeholders given the changes in Sensors 2021, 21, 3583. https://doi.org/10.3390/s21113583 https://www.mdpi.com/journal/sensors Sensors 2021, 21, 3583 2 of 20 construction conditions. Lin et al. [ 1] proposed an integrated framework for structural safety closed-loop management based on multi-source data integration to realize visual- ization of the construction process, generation of the time-varying structural model, and dynamic simulation, monitoring, and evaluation of structural safety. Although, scholars have long found that security risk management has difﬁculty following the dynamics of the construction site and lacks real-time means of information interaction. It is still difﬁcult to establish an effective information integration and sharing mechanism in the current research. Besides, today’s widely used Building Information Modeling (BIM) can be independently used as a method of security risk management [7]. Some scholars [8,9] use semantic networks and the Internet of Things (IoT) to optimize the integration of the BIM and physical data, but the interaction between BIM model and physical data is still limited to the existing system. It would be better to build a collaborative working framework that avoids silos of information. Digital Twin (DT) can integrate multi-source heterogeneous information to solve the problems of data isolation, fragmentation, and stagnation in the security risk management model [10]. DT and BIM can be combined to improve the efﬁciency of safety management. The safety risk analysis method refers to the use of reasonable methods to analyze the safety state of the current construction condition under the assumption that the trend of risk change will remain relatively stable in the short term, so as to infer and predict the occurrence of future accidents [11]. At present, the Fuzzy Analytic Hierarchy Process (F-AHP), Support Vector Machine (SVM), Bayesian Network (BN), and other quantitative methods are mostly used. For example, Gabriel et al. [12] studied the inﬂuencing factors of construction risk on site, calculated the index weight and comprehensive risk with an analytic hierarchy process, and evaluated the construction risk on site. Dai et al. [ 13] proposed a new neural network metamodel for reliability analysis based on wavelet SVM to evaluate the reliability of structures. Zhou et al. [ 14] put forward the deﬂection risk analysis model of an underground diaphragm wall based on a Bayesian network to realize the safety risk control in the whole process of subway construction. However, there are some limitations in the methods commonly used at present. For example, F- AHP is complicated to calculate the evaluation of multi-factor and multi-level. SVM is not suitable for large sample data processing. BN needs a lot of data for training and is prone to poor classiﬁcation accuracy. Moreover, the coupling relationship and interaction among safety risk factors are not considered in the current risk safety analysis and research, so it is difﬁcult to solve the complex coupling problems in the safety management of hoisting construction. Literature Review This paper presents the design of a DT-based framework for prefabricated building hoisting safety risk management, as well as the construction method of the digital twin security risk coupling model in the framework. This section summarizes the research related to this method in the current literature. 2.1. Multisource Data Fusion Framework Based on DT The concept of Digital Twin (DT) was ﬁrst proposed by Professor Grieves [15] and applied to the military industry and aerospace ﬁelds. As a key enabling technology to solve the problem of physical integration of intelligent manufacturing information and implement the concept and goal of intelligent manufacturing [ 11], DT has been widely concerned and studied by the academic community, and gradually expand the application ﬁeld. Tao et al. [16,17] summarized the general digital twin framework and put forward the concept of the DT ﬁve-dimensional model by studying the application requirements of complex mechanical and electrical equipment, stereoscopic warehouse, medical treatment, manufacturing workshop, smart city, and other ﬁelds. DT also meets the requirements of information and physical data fusion in the ﬁeld of construction engineering. At present, the research on the application of digital twin theory in the ﬁeld of architectural engineering has laid a certain foundation. For example, Lu et al. [18] proposed a system framework of digital twin for architecture and urban design, and based on this framework, developed the digital twin model for operation and maintenance management of the West Cambridge site of the University of Cambridge, UK. Liu et al. [ 19,20] proposed a DT-driven ﬁre safety dynamic evacuation method in the aspect of risk management to realize the three- dimensional model of building safety evacuation, and real-time dynamic planning of an evacuation route according to ﬁre development situation. In the aspect of risk assessment, the safety assessment method of prestressed steel structure in service was studied based on DT technology, and the safety assessment framework of prestressed steel structure based on DT technology was put forward. Dongmin et al. [ 21] put forward a framework that integrates DT with blockchain to realize information sharing in construction. To better apply the DT framework in the construction ﬁeld, it is necessary to ﬁnd an appropriate information exchange and display platform, so many scholars use BIM for development [22–24]. BIM can provide a three-dimensional geometric model of the physical space, which can better simulate the construction site. Meanwhile, BIM can establish the interactive integration mechanism of data information and visualize the perception data and processing data [ 25]. To improve the efﬁciency of virtual and real information interaction and better meet the management needs, the realization of BIM visualization in the safety management framework of the hoisting process is usually achieved by extending Industry Foundation Classes (IFC) [ 26]. The IFC deﬁning the corresponding set of safety attribute and key parameters to achieve the interconnection of the BIM model and the perception data of the framework. After analyzing the perceptual information, management decisions and safety warnings can be made. According to this point, BIM functional model and BIM ﬁle database can be established to support the visual display of data information by the BIM model. However, in order to provide more effective and timely support for management decisions, BIM visualization requires real-time perception and interaction of data with new technological means [27]. At present, some scholars [ 28] combine the Internet of Things (IoT) to carry out more convenient information collection, such as combining Radio Frequency Identiﬁcation (RFID), wireless communication technology [29–31], and intelligent devices [32]. However, the combination of IoT and BIM lacks the function of data mining and analysis. A more ideal way is to use the digital twin framework to realize the fusion of virtual space and physical space. This is also the future model of prefabricated building hoisting safety risk management to be realized. Sensors 2021, 21, 3583 4 of 20 2.2. Security Risk Coupling Analysis Coupling was originally derived from the concept of physics. In the context of safety risk management, the coupling is interpreted as the degree of interaction between different risk systems or risk factors in complex engineering activities, as well as their inherent correlation [33]. In recent years, the research on the coupling with risk is mainly in the ﬁelds of geology, aviation, navigation, etc., and gradually extends to the ﬁelds of coal mining and trafﬁc management. For example, Zhang et al. [34,35] used the N-K model to analyze the coupling relationship among risk factors of a gas explosion, and concluded that the probability of gas explosion increases with the increase of the number of risk factors. Xue et al. [36] built a risk coupling model for the risk assessment of high-speed railway projects based on the system dynamics method, which can identify the key coupling effects that increase risks, although their research applied the coupling model for analysis and studied the coupling mechanism of risk accidents. However, the analysis was based on a single risk factor, and the correlation between risks was not analyzed. The association rule algorithm is used to mine the degree of dependence and asso- ciation relationship between an event and other events and explore its association rules. The relevant rules can be used to predict future events or the development trend of the system. Therefore, association relationship mining (ARM) is used in risk management and accident analysis. For example, Abhishek et al. [37] used the ARM algorithm to analyze and process the unknown relationship between data, so as to evaluate and manage security risks. Xu et al. [38] used ARM to mine association rules between risk factors and unsafe behaviors of controllers, so as to establish a risk prediction model for unsafe behaviors of air trafﬁc controllers. Apriori algorithm [39] is the most classic one in association rule mining, which takes Support and Conﬁdence as evaluation criteria to mine frequent itemsets of transactions. However, for massive data sets, the mining process may be time-consuming. Therefore, scholars continue to improve the ARM algorithm. For example, Aqra et al. [40] proposed a method to solve the problem of rescanning a previously mined database and al- low the retrieval of knowledge that meets multiple thresholds without starting the learning process fro ... anner to destroy the coupling network and prevent the evolution of coupling risks. In addition, each coupling result and operation probability will be used as the basis for the next update, and the coupling data of each update will be backed up to the data cloud platform for storage for review and system maintenance. Conclusions and Future Works The current research on construction safety management and control mainly focuses on the construction of a safety risk management model and safety risk analysis methods. In the aspect of the establishment of a security risk management model, the difﬁcult problem of security risk management lies in the difﬁculty of establishing effective information integration and sharing mechanism. Due to the lack of real-time information interaction means, it cannot follow the dynamics of the construction site well. In the research of safety analysis methods, the common methods do not consider the coupling relationship and interaction among safety risk factors. It is difﬁcult to solve the complex coupling problems in hoisting construction safety management. In this paper, a safety risk management framework for prefabricated building hoisting based on DT is proposed. On the basis of this framework, the construction method of the DT coupling model is studied. The model integrates the Internet of Things (IoT) and Building Information Modeling (BIM), as well as the security risk analysis method combining the Apriori algorithm and complex network. Considering the coupling relationship between hoisting risk factors, the hierarchical model of hoisting safety accidents in space-time dimension is established. Apriori algorithm is used to mine association rules among risk factors of hoisting construction. According to the mining results of association rules, the coupling relationship of safety risk factors in hoisting construction is analyzed by using a complex network. The BIM model is used to visually display the data information, so as to guide the site hoisting construction. The methods have been validated and tested in a large-scale prefabricated construction projects, proving the value of the framework, and the effectiveness of the diagnostics methods. The main contributions of this study are as follows: (1) DT can improve the safety management method and mode of construction. (2) A safety risk coupling method for a safety risk coupling method for prefabricated building hoisting based on DT is proposed. The interaction mechanism between physical information and virtual information in the framework is described. The efﬁciency of information integration and sharing is improved. It ﬁlls the vacancy of coupling analysis of security risk factors. Effective safety management and decision-making are realized. This study provides a good method to integrate multi-source heterogeneous informa- tion in the process of hoisting safety risk management of prefabricated buildings. Solve the problems of data isolation, fragmentation, and stagnation in the security risk management model. In addition, how to integrate this method with other prefabricated buildings hoisting safety twin services also needs to be further explored. Introduction Fire protection has become a vital research topic in the field of public safety protection since there have been considerable property damage arising from fire incidents and fire casualties. Building fire protection (BFP) system is capable of responding quickly to a fire emergency, which is of great significance to reduce losses. In conventional fire alarm sys - tems, human reactions are required to inform the fire department to track down the fire location and organise an evacuation after a fire incident is detected [ 1 ]. In most cases, if people are not alerted in time or fail to control the fire at its early stages, the fire will be more serious and cause more losses [ 2 ]. For instance, the delayed evacuation of the Grenfell Tower Fire in London in June 2017 was cited as a great tragedy, which gutted the entire building, killing 80 people and injuring over 70 people [ 3 , 4 ]. In addition, insufficient information regarding the fire incident may hinder the organisation of evacuation and rescue [ 5 ]. Accordingly, there is an urgent need for an intelligent fire protection system that can display information regarding fires and provide rational strategies to deal with fire incidents automatically at an early stage. An intelligent fire protection system refers to a comprehensive building automation system that comprises several basic subsystems (e. g., fire alarm systems, smoke control systems, fire door monitoring systems, sprinkler systems, emergency broadcast systems, as well as evacuation guidance systems). Intelligent fire protection entails acti - vating the fire alarm system, analysing the current fire situation, and then sending commands to other subsystems to respond to fire emer - gencies. In the above processes, timely feedback and integration of in - formation are of high importance. The application of some emerging technologies in this regard has been investigated extensively. For example, building information modelling (BIM) provides a three- dimensional (3D) view of a building and stores and retrieves the infor - mation regarding the building. The vivid 3D environment created using BIM may provide visual guidance for fire rescue operations [ 6 ]. Furthermore, the Internet of Things (IoT) is an integration of technolo - gies that creates connections between all information sensing devices and the internet to support effective sensing, reliable transmission, and centralised management of data collected by IoT end products [ 7 ]. The integration of BIM and IoT systems is capable of presenting the real-time state and the as-built structures of a building. Fang et al. [ 5 ] developed a framework of an IoT-aided system that can integrate real-time moni - toring data of the fire environment and people counting collected by multiple BIM detectors and sensors to dynamically optimize building evacuation. Moreover, the integration of BIM, IoT, virtual reality (VR), and augmented reality (AR) can underpin path finding in fire rescue * Corresponding author at: Department of Civil Engineering, Shanghai Jiao Tong University, Shanghai, China. In brief, the above existing technologies have the potential to increase BFP efficiency. However, since recorded data are collected in different formats from a wide variety of sources, and the design of building structures and IoT networks are complicated, data interopera - bility and system coordination pose critical challenges to intelligent control of the fire protection system during fire emergencies. Digital twins (DTs) are considered as an applicable solution to the above challenges. The emergence of the DT has increased efficiency in numerous industries. DT’s applied research in the architecture, engi - neering, and construction (AEC) domain has aroused wide interest since 2018 [10]. The DT-driven approach is capable of facilitating the man - agement, planning, forecasting, and presentation of buildings, in - frastructures, or city assets [11]. Since DT enables the integration of virtual representation and real-time data, dynamic monitoring and predictive operation can be achieved through facilities management [12,13]. The predictive approach can reduce operational cost and time loss. On that basis, the fire scene information collected from the IoT systems can be integrated with BIM models to provide a digital repre - sentation of the fire protection system. Data analysis and emergency simulation can be achieved using the virtual fire protection system. Thus, DT serves as the real world’s intelligent brain to coordinate various subsystems to cope with fire incidents. The application of DT to the intelligent control of BFP systems comprises two crucial aspects as follows: (1) the digital representation is a high-precision mapping of real-world objects; (2) dynamic updating mechanisms are essential to ensure that the virtual is always in sync with the real. However, previous research has placed a major focus on anomaly detection or prediction based on deep learning methods. Research on the interactive feedback between the virtual and the real is still required, which takes on a great significance to the intelligent control of the fire protection system. Furthermore, as the requirements for building information vary throughout the lifecycle and the data are in different formats (e.g., building models, videos, pictures, documents), a unified data descrip - tion is expected to solve the issue of data exchange and reuse. This study describes the use of DTs and semantic web technologies for the intelligent control of BFP systems. Data are organised as a linked graph based on the Resource Description Framework (RDF) [14], such that a uniform representation syntax is provided for building data in different formats. Accordingly, the query language, SPARQL Protocol and RDF Query Language (SPARQL) [15], can be adopted to retrieve building information efficiently [16]. Moreover, ontology is a common vocabulary and formal description [17] to define how the above data are linked, and how computers understand the above data, such that the semantic interoperability between the data can be addressed. In the AEC domain, since a unified data integration environment is created, the use of semantic web technologies has been studied as a promising strategy for data interoperability [18,19], information exchange [20,21], and complex cross-domain linkage [22]. A series of works on the develop - ment of ontologies (e.g., IoT sensor observations ontology (SSN) [23], brick ontology [24], and building topology ontology (BOT) [25]) have laid a basis for the integration of BIM data and IoT data. In this study, an ontology is discussed for data fusion based on the above ontologies, and a rule model and a process model are built to achieve the interaction mechanism between the virtual and the real. In Section 2, recent literature on the application of BIM and IoT in fire risk management, DT modelling, and semantic web technologies used for data interoperability in the AEC domain is reviewed to identify the research gap. In Section 3, the use of DT and sematic web technologies is described for the intelli - gent control of the fire protection system. Fire risk management: application of BIM and IoT Fire risk management is of great significance to building operations that comprise fire protection design, fire/smoke spread limitation, fire extinguishing, and escape probability [26]. It is a complicated task, and there are several factors for the efficiency of fire risk management, which include the characteristics of the occupants, the building facil - ities, and the features of the fire [27]. An integrated environment capable of managing and analysing the above factors should be estab - lished [28]. The use of BIM has shown its potential as such a solution. From the perspective of modelling, BIM can be considered as a 3D model with considerable building information. The geometric data of BIM are beneficial to the assessment of the reliability of the design of fire protection facilities [28], planning escape routes [29,30], and demon - stration of the real-time fire status [31]. In addition, the informatization features of BIM (e.g., information storage, extraction, and retrieval) can be employed as the database for BIM-based fire safety information management. For instance, Wang et al. [32] applied BIM to support asset management for fire safety (e.g., the maintenance information of fire safety equipment). Wu and Ma [33] proposed a BIM-based fire emer - gency management system that comprises a fire intelligence monitoring module, a fire warning module, a fire response module, as well as a fire treatment module. The proposed system is capable of acquiring fire in - formation, determining fire status, and making decisions for trapped people. However, existing research has mainly focused on the fire pro - tection design and escape probability, whereas a series of closed-loop control logic of fire sensing, fire/smoke spread control, fire extinguish - ing and evacuation security based on BIM has not been established. To achieve the above goals, BIM should be integrated with the fire pro - tection system to achieve fire protection equipment management while enabling the fire protection system to respond promptly and rapidly in fire emergencies. In general, the rapid response of fire protection systems comprises early detection and reasonable reactions. The former provides more time for escaping and extinguishing the fire by using the detectability of the terminals (e.g., the very early smoke detection apparatus [34]). In this study, the reasonable reaction of the fire protection system is primarily analysed, with a stress on fast actions after a fire incident is detected (e. g., activating fire extinguishing terminals, opening smoke systems, and guiding escape). Compared with conventional manual operation, the emergence of IoT, a network of smart devices, enables automated con - trol of sensing terminals by providing an environment where the devices can communicate with and sense each other. Nevertheless, existing research has placed a focus on monitoring [35,36] and sensing data management [2]. Since BIM provides a 3D geometry model and building design information, and IoT enables data connection, communication, and collection from devices, the integration of BIM and IoT has been applied to road compaction quality monitoring and management [37], automatic fault detection and building diagnostics [13], as well as thermal comfort building assessment [38]. However, there has been rare research on fire risk control, especially on data-driven decision-making about the control of fire protection systems. As a result, a methodology integrating BIM and IoT is proposed to develop an intelligent control strategy for BFP systems. The challenges originate from the realisation of the control mechanism and the inter - operability between BIM data and IoT data [39]. Accordingly, a DT framework is built, and semantic web technologies are adopted to solve the above challenges. 2.2. Subsequently, the definition of DT has been enriched in the application of the aerospace L. Jiang et al. Furthermore, model construction and model interaction are involved from the modelling perspective. In the AEC domain, the DT model has been commonly constructed based on BIM. A light weighted BIM is employed to establish the DT-BIM hoisting planning model for prefabricated component hoisting man - agement [45]. The model covers hoisting environment information (e. g., construction site layout), hoisting route information (e.g., hoisting point and installation point), and prefabricated component information (e.g., 3D shapes). Intelligent control of smoke control systems The smoke control system also plays a critical role in preventing damage caused by smoke. In accordance with the Code for Design of Automatic Fire Alarm System in China, the smoke control system is required to start up in the fire compartment where there are two inde - pendently alarming smoke detectors. An earlier start-up time can improve the performance of smoke control [ 71 ]. However, the start-up time of the smoke control system heavily relies on manual activation, including estimating the fire location and confirming the present fire compartment. In some cases, people should find the corresponding smoke exhaust fan and turn on it by hand, which is time-consuming. In this study, a process model ( Fig. 19 ) and a set of rules ( Table. 10 ) are proposed to achieve the intelligent control mechanism of the smoke control system. First, the diagnosis rule, R11, was employed to check whether there were two alarming smoke detectors for the respective fire compartment. The process model was activated when the query result of rule R11 was “ true ” . Subsequently, two strategies were considered in the process model. First, the exhaust fans of the fire compartment where there were two independent alarming smoke detectors started. The process rule, R12, was adopted to find the corresponding outlets and fans. The inner SELECT clauses aim at limiting the variable ?fc as the target fire compartment. Second, the air supply fan of the staircase through which people were escaping became open. For each staircase, the process rule R13 was used to examine the number of existing people. If the query result of R13 is “ true ” , the process rule, R14, is used to obtain the corresponding devices. For both strategies, the opening signals were transmitted to the devices, and the model was updated based on the update rule R16 simultaneously.
