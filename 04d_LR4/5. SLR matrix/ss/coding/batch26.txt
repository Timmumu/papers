This transformation is driven by digital fabrication, which serves as a critical physical in- stantiation of the CPPS framework, providing an essential link betweendigitaldesigndataandphysicaloutput[2].Thecoreof this paradigm is the closed-loop cyberphysical process, where robotic systems, governed by digital instructions, are enhanced by integrated sensors that provide real-time feedback, enabling instantaneous in-process corrections [3,4]. The adoption of spe- cialised, ‘expert’ AI models—convolutional neural networks (CNNs) for perception, long short-term memory networks (LSTMs) for sequence prediction, and reinforcement learning (RL) for control—trained on large, curated, and annotated datasets of defects, has proven to be efective for simple tasks; however,thisapproachisconstrainedbyhardwarerequirements and the cost of data acquisition and model training, creating a formidable barrier to entry [6]. The integration of LLMs enables robots to reason abouttheirphysicalenvironmentthroughvisualinput,acritical step towards generalisable autonomy. Governed by a considerable number of interdependent design, environmental, and production parameters, and extremely sensitive to any variation in these parameters, 3D concrete printing (3DCP) exemplifes a complex CPPS in the modern construction industry. In 3DCP, a robotic system precisely controlsthedepositionofmaterial,translatingdigitalgeometries into physical structures layer by layer [12], and allowing the sustainable production of bespoke, structurally optimised ele- ments with a high degree of geometric freedom [13]. Journal of Infrastructure Preservation and Resilience Integrating multi-source data for life-cycle risk assessment of bridge networks: a system digital twin framework Ziheng Geng1, Chao Zhang2, Yishuo Jiang3, Dora Pugliese1 and Minghui Cheng1,4* Abstract Bridges are critical infrastructure assets that face a variety of stressors throughout their service life, requiring a life- cycle approach to assess their risk profile. Recent advancements in sensing and monitoring technologies provide a powerful data foundation to improve the accuracy of life-cycle risk assessment (LCRA). However, existing works that incorporate data for probabilistic risk assessment typically focus on individual bridges and rely on single-source data, limiting their scope and applicability. To this end, a system digital twin (SDT) framework based on Bayesian network (BN) is proposed to integrate multi-source data for LCRA of bridge networks. Specifically, the SDT can capture correlations and interdependencies across various scales, including within individual components (e.g., multiple failure modes), between components within a system (e.g., bridges along a route), and across interconnected systems (e.g., bridge and hydraulic systems). It integrates data from various sources including bridge inspections, traffic moni- toring facilities, and water watch stations. A coastal bridge network in Miami-Dade County, FL, is used as an illustrative example to demonstrate how the SDT integrates multi-source data for risk assessment. Additionally, several future scenarios are hypothesized to showcase the applicability and flexibility of the proposed framework in supporting risk management for infrastructure systems. Keywords System digital twin, Life-cycle risk assessment, Infrastructure management, System reliability, Bayesian network Introduction Bridges are critical assets within infrastructure systems, playing a pivotal role in ensuring the functionality and connectivity of modern communities and cities. How - ever, these assets are vulnerable, facing a variety of stress- ors throughout their service life. Factors such as natural hazards, aging materials, and excessive loading can accel- erate the deterioration of bridges and undermine their reliability under both normal and extreme condi - tions [1, 2]. This underscores the necessity for a life- cycle approach to risk assessment in bridge networks. Life-cycle risk assessment (LCRA) provides a holistic approach for assessing the evolving risks associated with a structure/system over its lifespan [3, 4]. This approach involves identifying potential hazards, estimating their probability of occurrence, and assessing their potential consequences. With the risk profile, LCRA facilitates informed decision-making in planning inspection, main - tenance, repair actions to ensure long-term sustainabil - ity and resilience [5, 6]. J Infrastruct Preserv Resil (2025) 6:9 Recent breakthroughs in sensing and monitoring technologies have significantly improved the acquisi - tion of multi-source data reflecting bridge conditions, thereby opening new avenues for enhancing LCRA of bridge networks. For example, non-destructive testing (NDE) techniques have been powerful tools for detect - ing material-level flaws in bridges. Common NDE meth - ods, such as ground penetrating radar [8, 9], close-range photogrammetry [10], infrared thermography [8, 11], and terrestrial laser scanning [12] have demonstrated efficacy in detecting defects, rebar corrosion, and crack - ing. Additionally, structural health monitoring (SHM) has emerged as an advanced approach for comprehensive assessment of structural condition. SHM systems utilize diverse sensors to capture physical measurements asso - ciated with loads (e.g., bridge weigh-in-motion for traffic loads [13]), environmental stressors (e.g., acoustic sensor for scour detection [14]), and bridge responses (e.g., opti- cal-fiber sensors for strain [15, 16] and vibration moni - toring [17]). Collectively, these innovations produce an extensive and comprehensive dataset that is expected to be used by LCRA models to improve prediction accuracy and reduce model uncertainty. With the ability to leverage diverse data sources in the real world, digital twin (DT) technology is increas - ingly recognized as a transformative tool for life-cycle management of infrastructure systems. Specifically, DTs are virtual representations of physical assets or systems, continuously synchronized with their physical counter - parts through bidirectional information exchange [18]. The distinguishing features of DTs lie in their ability to (a) integrate observations from physical asset to dynami - cally update the state of digital asset and (b) leverage the updated digital state to inform decision-making for the operation, maintenance, and management of physical assets. However, the development of DTs for LCRA of bridge networks faces two critical challenges. First, bridge networks are complex systems comprising multiple inter- connected sub-systems, such as bridges, transportation networks, and rivers. These sub-systems exhibit correla - tions and interdependencies across various scales, both within individual sub-systems (e.g., multiple structural components of a single bridge and multiple bridges along a route) and across different sub-systems (e.g., bridges subject to river scour). Therefore, a systems approach is required to effectively capture these interrelationships. Second, risk assessment of bridge networks requires the integration of heterogeneous data sources. For example, bridge inspections and river measurements inform the failure probability of bridges, while traffic volume data aids in assessing the consequences of bridge failures. These datasets typically differ in format and update fre - quency, underscoring the need for a data assimilation approach to seamlessly integrate multi-source data into the DT. To address these research challenges, this paper devel - ops a scalable computational framework for LCRA of bridge networks. The framework enables the develop - ment of a system digital twin (SDT) at a large scale (e.g., city- and county-scales), the integration of data from diverse sources and different systems, and the considera - tion of complex statistical correlations among the ran - dom variables and data sources within the same system and across different systems. A case study in Miami-Dade County, FL, is conducted to demonstrate the proposed framework. Specifically, data from diverse sources including bridge inspections, traffic monitoring facilities, and water watch stations are utilized to update the risk profile of the bridge network. To the best knowledge of the authors, this case study is the first DT that reaches the county scale, interlinks three systems for probabil - istic modeling and risk updating, and incorporates the statistical correlations. Additionally, three scenarios are presented to illustrate the flexibility of the proposed framework, showcasing the capacity of SDT to adapt to real-world changes. Literature review Life‑cycle risk assessment of bridge networks Extensive efforts have been dedicated to applying LCRA to spatially distributed bridge networks within trans - portation systems. Risk analysis typically involves two components: (a) evaluating the failure probability of indi- vidual bridges over time, and (b) estimating the direct and indirect consequences of bridge failures. For failure probability analysis, previous studies have developed time-variant reliability analysis frameworks for various failure-inducing factors, including floods [19, 20], colli - sions [21, 22], traffic loads [23, 24], material deterioration [25, 26], earthquakes [27, 28], tsunamis [1, 29], storm surges [30, 31], and climate change [7, 32]. Recognizing that adjacent bridges are exposed to similar environ - mental stressors and traffic patterns, event-based simu - lations [33, 34] and random field modeling [35, 36] have been employed to model the spatial correlation of bridge failures. For failure consequence assessment, estimat - ing indirect consequences, i.e., the cost associated with reduced functionality across the bridge network, remains a challenging task due to the need to simulate the varia - tion in traffic flow resulting from bridge failures. Exist - ing studies have advanced traffic assignment analyses by incorporating user decision-making behaviors [6], emphasizing extreme risk scenarios [4], and enhancing computational efficiency [37, 38]. Additionally, with the rapid advancement of machine learning, surrogate modeling techniques such as Page 3 of 20 Geng et al. J Infrastruct Preserv Resil (2025) 6:9 random forests [ 39], deep neural networks [ 40, 41], and graph neural networks [ 42, 43] have been increas - ingly utilized in the reliability analysis and LCRA of bridge networks to improve computational efficiency. However, a critical limitation of the abovementioned studies lies in their reliance on simulation-based approaches, where certain model parameters are assumed to remain constant over time. Therefore, these parameters fail to capture the dynamic evolution of bridge networks and may not be the best represen - tation of the actual conditions. It may lead to potential discrepancies between model predictions and real- world performance, limiting their applicability in engi - neering practice. Digital twin for bridge asset management Recent studies have explored the implementation of DTs in bridges asset management. For example, DTs have been developed using building information mod - eling (BIM) and augmented with inspection data such as 3D scanning [ 44], digital image correlation (DIC) [45], and weigh-in-motion (WIM) [ 46]. Extending these advancements, modeling enabling technologies, including geometry, measurements, simulations, and other data-driven techniques, have been integrated to develop cyber-physics DTs [ 47– 49]. Additionally, several studies have focused on the dynamic decision- making feedback loops within DTs, employing proba - bilistic graphic models as the computational cores and utilizing Bayesian inference to update the digital state and inform predictive maintenance decision-making [50– 52]. Despite significant advancements, existing DTs for bridge asset management suffer from two limitations. First, existing works predominately focus on developing DTs for individual bridges. However, bridges are inte - gral components of spatially distributed transportation networks, where the failure of a single bridge can trig - ger cascading effects, such as traffic detours and delays. These indirect impacts frequently surpass direct con - sequences by several orders of magnitude [ 53], under - scoring the necessity for modeling bridge networks as complex, interdependent systems. Second, current DTs with probabilistic modeling capabilities primarily rely on single-source data for risk updating. This reliance on siloed data source constrains the capacity of DTs to capture the dynamic evolution of real-world condi - tions, thereby undermining their predictive accuracy. Given the diversity and heterogeneity of real-world data, a comprehensive approach is required to syner - gistically integrate data from multiple sources for risk updating. Limitations and future work Although the proposed framework demonstrates fea - sibility and applicability in assessing life-cycle risk for bridge networks, it suffers from certain limitations. First, a significant challenge is the storage and computational efficiency associated with large-scale BNs. For example, the BN in this study comprises 6,478 nodes and 10,584 edges, occupying 97,484.8 MB memory to store its CPTs. This presents a substantial computational burden. As the size and complexity of the network grow, the expo - nential increase in memory requirements can severely hinder the practical implementations of the proposed framework. It can extract a trunk network from the origi - nal large-scale BN based on available data sources and given performance measures. By reducing the size of the network, it can significantly lower the computational overhead, facilitating efficient Bayesian inference. Build - ing on these advancements, future work should focus on seamlessly integrating custom BN with the proposed framework to develop scalable solutions that can han - dle the computational demands of increasingly large and complex networks. Second, due to the limitations in available resources, the current framework has not yet been validated. Future work should focus on assessing the performance and quality of the SDT to ensure its applicability in practical engineering scenarios. Specifically, the validation can be implemented with the aid of field and laboratory testing. The proposed framework can identify a list of vulner - able bridges within the bridge network. For these iden - tified bridges, field coring can be conducted to extract core samples from their structural components. These samples can then be tested in the laboratory to analyze their properties such as microstructural characteris - tics, apparent electrical resistivity, compressive strength, chloride ingress, and carbonation depths. These labora - tory analyses provide quantitative metrics that reflect the physical condition of bridges. To validate the framework, the same procedures should be applied to bridges classi - fied as low risk by the SDT. By comparing the laboratory results from vulnerable and low-risk bridges, valuable insights into the predictive accuracy and robustness of the framework can be derived. Third, the proposed framework primarily focuses on risk assessment of bridge networks. It integrates the probability of bridge failure with the associated failure consequences to identify the risk within the bridge net - work. However, it does not include the system’s response to address these threats. Future work should extend the framework to incorporate restoration and resilience. Resilience is a performance indicator that evaluates the system’s capacity to withstand, adapt to, and recover from disruptions [54, 78, 79]. It highlights the ability of a system to recover to its previous functionality after a disruption. Restoration models, which define the recov - ery pattern of the system, are essential tools for quanti - fying resilience [80]. Incorporating these elements will enhance the utility of the framework in infrastructure management, better assisting in decision-making regard - ing inspections and maintenance of infrastructure. Summary and conclusions This paper develops a system digital twin (SDT) frame - work based on Bayesian network (BN) to monitor the life-cycle risk of bridge networks. A prototype graph structure of SDTs is first developed at the bridge level to capture the correlations within individual compo - nents (e.g., multiple failure modes of bridges) and to integrate data from various sources. The prototype is then extended to the network level by embedding spatial geometry of cities or counties. The resulting network- level graph structure can capture interdependencies both within a single system (e, g., bridges along a route and river gages monitoring the same river) and across interconnected systems (e.g., bridge, transportation, and hydraulic systems). With the graph structure, Monte Carlo simulation is conducted to generate a dataset to fit the conditional probability tables (CPTs) of the BN. To streamline the Bayesian inference over time, the BN nodes are classified into three types: data nodes, analysis nodes, and performance nodes. This classification facili - tates automated Bayesian inference using multi-source data with different update frequencies. A real-world bridge network in Miami-Dade County, FL, is used as a case study to illustrate the feasibility and applicability of the proposed framework for life-cycle risk assessment. The following conclusions are drawn: The graph structure is constructed using three databases: the bridge database, traffic database, and river database. By embedding the spatial geometry of the county, a graph structure with 6,478 nodes and 10,584 edges is created, integrating 1,034 Page 18 of 20Geng et al. J Infrastruct Preserv Resil (2025) 6:9 bridges, 1,261 STCSs, and 10 river gages within Miami-Dade County. This demonstrates the scal - ability of the proposed SDT framework. The graph structure utilizes a clustered graphi - cal structure to capture the statistically correlated variables, including river flow and stage data from both individual and adjacent river gages. These cor - related river flow and stage nodes are connected to a common source node, demonstrating the capacity of the SDT framework to model the complex cor - relations at the county scale. A future scenario is hypothesized to showcase how the SDT integrates diverse data sources for risk inference. The bridge conditions are assumed to deteriorate over time, whereas traffic volumes and river stage/flow are assumed to increase gradually. The results reveal a general upward trend in net - work risk, punctuated by localized fluctuations. This demonstrates the capability of the proposed framework in updating the risk of bridge network using available multiple-source data. Three representative scenarios are selected: the construction of new bridges, the closure of aging bridges, and data out of range. The proposed frame - work effectively addresses these changes by modify - ing localized CPTs without reconfiguring the entire network. The updated BN retains its integrity and functionality for risk assessment across all scenar - ios. The results show the flexibility of the proposed framework in accommodating the dynamic evolu - tion of urban bridge networks.
