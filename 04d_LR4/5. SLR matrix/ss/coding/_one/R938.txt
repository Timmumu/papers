Structural Control and Health Monitoring, 2026; 2026:8514242 1 of 25 https://doi.org/10.1155/stc/8514242 Integrated Computational Methods for Design, Control, and Optimization of Adaptive Structural Systems Structural Control and Health Monitoring 1 | Introduction The construction industry is transitioning from fragmented, craft-based methodologies to integrated cyberphysical pro- duction systems (CPPS) [1]. This transformation is driven by digital fabrication, which serves as a critical physical in- stantiation of the CPPS framework, providing an essential link betweendigitaldesigndataandphysicaloutput[2].Thecoreof this paradigm is the closed-loop cyberphysical process, where robotic systems, governed by digital instructions, are enhanced by integrated sensors that provide real-time feedback, enabling instantaneous in-process corrections [3,4]. This data exchange enables fabrication to transition from a static operation to an adaptiveproductionprocess[5].CPPSarecharacterisedbyhigh complexity, driven by many interconnected parameters that ultimately afect the quality of the fnal product; traditional automationbasedonpresetrulesfailsundercomplex,nonlinear, andunpredictableconditions.Inthesecases,integratingartifcial intelligence (AI) is a compelling solution. The adoption of spe- cialised, ‘expert’ AI models—convolutional neural networks (CNNs) for perception, long short-term memory networks (LSTMs) for sequence prediction, and reinforcement learning (RL) for control—trained on large, curated, and annotated datasets of defects, has proven to be efective for simple tasks; however,thisapproachisconstrainedbyhardwarerequirements and the cost of data acquisition and model training, creating a formidable barrier to entry [6]. Furthermore, any shift in the operational environment or a requirement for a new capability often mandates a complete retraining pipeline with newly col- lected data: This inherent narrowness limits generalisability across diferent setups and defect types [7]. Atransformativealternativeemergeswithlargelanguagemodels (LLMs) and multimodal LLMs (mLLMs). These foundation models,builtonthetransformerarchitecture,arepretrainedon vast, diverse corpora and exhibit remarkable general-purpose reasoningcapabilities[8,9].Thetransformer’scoremechanism, theattention model, enables it to contextually weigh the rele- vance of all elements in a sequence—whether words in a sen- tence or patches in an image—allowing for a holistic understanding that mirrors the interconnected nature of com- plex systems [10]. TheprofoundreasoningandplanningcapabilitiesofLLMsand mLLMsareincreasinglybeingleveragedtotacklelong-standing challenges in embodied AI. Rather than directly controlling actuators,thesemodelsoftenactashigh-levelreasoningengines. Aprominentapplicationisintaskplanning,whereahigh-level natural language instruction is decomposed by an LLM into a sequence of executable subtasks. Furthermore, LLMs have shown promise in code generation, where a natural-language command is translated directly into executable code for robot control [11]. The integration of LLMs enables robots to reason abouttheirphysicalenvironmentthroughvisualinput,acritical step towards generalisable autonomy. Governed by a considerable number of interdependent design, environmental, and production parameters, and extremely sensitive to any variation in these parameters, 3D concrete printing (3DCP) exemplifes a complex CPPS in the modern construction industry. In 3DCP, a robotic system precisely controlsthedepositionofmaterial,translatingdigitalgeometries into physical structures layer by layer [12], and allowing the sustainable production of bespoke, structurally optimised ele- ments with a high degree of geometric freedom [13]. Beyond 3DCP,roboticfabricationforconstructionspansawidespectrum of applications, including automated bricklaying and, more generally, prefabricated component assembly, as well as sub- tractive machining of building elements, all unifed by the principle of direct digital-to-physical translation [14]. Themainissuewith 3DCPas acomplex systemisdetermining standardised processes to systematically obtain the required mechanicalperformance(grade)of3D-printedconcrete(3DPC) independently of the specifc materials and hardware/software setup. Thematerialis,in fact,anisotropic andcontingentupon precise control of design, production and environmental pa- rameters [15], with diferent failure modes and typologies [16]. Traditional automation, based on preset rules, fails under the complex, nonlinear and unpredictable conditions characteristic of both indoor and outdoor fabrication settings [17]. In this context, the principles of high-level planning and multimodal reasoningdemonstratedbyLLMsinroboticsapplicationsshare a conceptual synergy with advanced automation, as shown re- centlywiththeproposeddecentralised,AI-drivenworkfowsfor standardisingandoptimising3DCP[18–20]:Inbothcontexts,the LLM serves as an intelligent intermediary that translates high- level goals into a structured, executable robotic process. This paper posits that general-purpose LLMs, conditioned not through training or fne-tuning but through advanced prompt engineering, can act as high-level orchestrators for adaptive 3DCP (A3DCP). The proposed framework utilises an mLLM/ LLMpairtoperformfew-shotin-contextreasoningonreal-time sensordata,diagnosingprocesshealthandsuggestingcorrective actions. This approach bypasses the need for specialised AI agents,insteadleveragingthebroadinterdisciplinaryknowledge and reasoning capabilities of foundational models to tackle the 3DCP CPPS (Figure1). Due to health and safety concerns and the inherent risks of robotic fabrication, amplifed by AI-driven automatic adjust- ments, this seminal work was conducted by implementing adigitaltwinoftheA3DCPprocess.Thestructureofthedigital twin,describedindepthinthemethodologysection,comprises materialcharacterisation,optimisation,uncertaintypropagation, statistical analysis and an AI-enabled simulated production process.
