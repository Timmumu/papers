F:Scenario Modeling for Urban Road Emergency Management: A Cognitive Digital Twin –Based Method Gang Yu 1; Jialin Li 2; Jing Xiong 3;M i nH u 4; Ruoceng Zeng 5; and Vijayan Sugumaran 6 Abstract: The cognitive digital twin (CDT) concept provides a promising way to organize and integrate the full life-cycle data of urban road emergency scenarios, as well as facilitate scenario deduction and prediction. The construction of twin models constitutes the prerequisite for the practical implementation and application of CDT. However, the existing research on emergency scenario twin modeling falls short in providing a unified modeling method that comprehensively captures the multidimensional characteristics of scenario elements. This defi- ciency, coupled with inadequate analysis of interelement interactions and constraints posed by semantic inconsistencies and interoperability among twin models, hampers the accurate mapping of physical scenarios and their evolution dynamics. In response to the multielement, uncertainty, and dynamic nature of urban road emergency scenarios, this paper proposes a CDT modeling method for urban road emergency scenarios. First, the definitions of element-level and scenario-level twin models are elucidated in three dimensions —geometry, attribute, and behavior—in conjunction with resilience theory. Second, a modeling method based on SysML and ontology is proposed for twin models at different levels, and a SysML2Ontology transformation tool is developed to enable the systematic construction of emergency scenario cog- nitive digital twin (ESCDT). Furthermore, a CDT framework for urban road emergency management centered on twin models is introduced. Finally, the North-South Elevated Road in Shanghai, China, is used as the engineering application object. The feasibility of our modeling methodology is validated through a case study, which involves the construction of the ESCDT for a collision accident. The model can be further utilized for emergency scenario deduction, ultimately elevating the effectiveness of emergency management. DOI: 10.1061/JCCEE5. Introduction With the rapid growth of the world’s population and the acceleration of urbanization, vehicles and traffic flows are soaring, increasing traffic emergencies such as bad weather, traffic accidents, or road failures. They can lead to disruptions in the operation of the trans- portation system, placing great pressure on the reliable travel and robust operation of urban roads (Stefaniec et al. 2020), directly threat- ening the resilience of urban road systems ( Osman 2021). The resil- ience of urban road systems is generally considered to be the ability to absorb, adapt, and quickly recover after being disturbed (Bešinovi´c 2020). When a traffic accident occurs, resilience is mainly ensured through technical and organizational means, that is, urban road emer- gency management (Y u et al. 2024). Effective urban road emergency management for enhanced resilience necessitates the integration of the unique characteristics of the urban road during traffic accidents, real-time situational awareness, and foresight of potential accidents. (Ford and Wolf 2020 ). To satisfy these requirements, scholars have been applying digital twin (DT) technology to improve the resilience of urban transportation systems ( Miglani and Kumar 2019 ). Braik a n dK o l i o u(2023) proposed a DT framework for emergency manage- ment of transportation infrastructure during natural disasters that fo- cuses on data acquisition, analysis, and reasoning, integrating physics-based and data-driven models to simulate and predict facility performance and assisting in the adjustment of emergency strategies and planning. Feng et al. (2023) analyzed the data sharing problem in the Internet of vehicles transportation system from a DT perspective, developed travel behavior models under uncertain events, and estab- lished a blockchain-enabled DT transportation system to improve traffic network resilience. However, the urban road system is com- plex, consisting of people, vehicles, roads, and environments. The interactions between various elements during traffic accidents are un- certain and dynamic, which challenge the ability to effectively model, understand, and predict system performance ( Alibrandi 2022). Cognitive digital twin (CDT) empowers DT with enhanced cog- nitive capabilities and realizes DT integration with the help of seman- tic technologies such as ontology and knowledge graph (Zheng et al. 2022), which facilitates the integration of life-cycle data, informa- tion, and knowledge of urban road emergency scenarios. When urban road emergencies occur, twin modeling of emer- gency scenarios requires recognizing the complex dynamic coupling relationships between multiple scenario elements and focusing on the chain process of scenario evolution and its mutation characteristics. In response to addressing the issue of completeness in twin modeling, this paper proposes the multilevel and multidimension definition for emergency scenario twin models, informed by resilience theory and accounting for the multifaceted, uncertain, and dynamic nature of urban road emergency scenarios. Subsequently, in view of the char- acteristics of twin models at different levels and dimensions, a sys- tematic method of model construction and integration is proposed, aimed at unraveling the underlying mechanisms governing the inter- actions among elements. Finally, with the twin model as the core, a CDT framework is proposed for urban road emergency management. The remaining part of this paper is organized as follows. First, the research related to DT-based emergency management is reviewed in terms of both models and modeling approaches, and the research gap is identified. The next section introduces and meticulously discusses the CDT model construction and integration method for urban road emergency scenarios, together with an ex- position of the CDT framework tailored for this setting. Then, an emergency scenario twin modeling case under a collision accident is introduced to verify the proposed method. Finally, the results and future directions of the study are discussed and summarized. Related Work DT provides a new way to synchronize the physical and virtual worlds ( Grieves 2014 ). In the field of emergency management, DT leverages high-fidelity models and dynamic simulations up- dated in real time to help decision-makers generate insights into current scenario during and after the emergency occurrence, thus enabling enhanced emergency management and significantly in- creased resilience ( Braik and Koliou 2024b ). However, existing re- search shows that ensuring consistency and interoperability of models is both important and challenging for DT-based emergency management ( Deng et al. 2021 ). To address this challenge, recent studies have combined semantic modeling and system engineering with DTs to improve interoperability and connectivity among twin models ( Rožanec et al. 2022 ). On this basis, the concept of CDT has been introduced ( Adl 2016 ), and recent works have explored the potential of CDT in smart manufacturing systems. Nevertheless, in the field of emergency management, there remains a need for further investigation into the CDT model and associated modeling methodologies, which are the core of the CDT application. Twin Models for Scenario-Based Emergency Management DT can be defined as a multiphysics, multiscale model where digital models are simulated, analyzed, and updated based on data obtained from the physical world (Longman et al. 2023). Therefore, the foun- dation of DT-based emergency management is the twin modeling of emergency scenarios in the physical world. Classical disaster theory identifies hazard elements, affected elements, and environment ele- ments as the constituent elements of emergency scenarios in the physical world ( Blaikie et al. 2014 ).
