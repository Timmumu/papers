F:OPEN Scientific Reports | (2026) 16:27367 1| https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-58063-3 Multi-line LiDAR 3D construction environment modeling and BIM consistency update method for digital twins Chen Liu1, Xin Xu1, Xiaofeng Ding1 & Guangmei He2 Construction digital twins aim to maintain an up-to-date, BIM-consistent as-built state from streaming multi-line LiDAR to enable progress verification, dimensional compliance control, and proactive safety management on construction sites. However, converting sequential LiDAR observations into a temporally stable, element-centric BIM state is difficult in realistic AEC environments. Existing approaches mainly fall into geometry-driven Scan-to-BIM registration frameworks and learning-based point cloud perception models, with some system-level digital-twin frameworks built on BIM-centric reasoning. Purely geometric alignment is brittle under occlusion, clutter, and repetitive structures, and registration uncertainty can propagate to downstream progress and deviation conclusions. Meanwhile, generic perception networks are often trained on static frames, lack temporal stabilization, and do not explicitly optimize BIM-consistent element association or geometry-aware alignment cues. In addition, many digital-twin systems expose limited, loosely coupled state representations that hinder causal, reliable site updates. To address these gaps, we propose LiBiDT (LiDAR–BIM Digital Twin), a causal LiDAR–BIM digital-twin model—where “causal” refers to computational online updating in which only past observations enter the current state—that integrates spatio-temporal mapping, geometric– semantic perception, and scan-to-BIM association for consistent state evolution. LiBiDT consists of Spatio-Temporal Global Registration (STGR) with LiDAR–IMU–GNSS priors, Multi-task Perception (MTP) using a PointNet++ backbone with semantic/instance/normal heads, and Scan-to-BIM Association and State Updating (S2B) via normal-guided point-to-plane refinement, bipartite matching with fused overlap/deviation/semantic evidence, and exponential moving average (EMA) stabilization. The Decision and Risk Analytics (DRA) layer turns the BIM-aligned state into auditable construction- management indicators—weighted completion, deviation summaries, and three forward-evaluable safety/compliance hazard flags. Experiments on three public benchmarks (Hilti SLAM, CV4AEC 2024 Scan-to-BIM, and S3DIS Area-5) cover trajectory, perception, and association evaluation. On CV4AEC 2024 Scan-to-BIM, LiBiDT reaches association F1 = 0.837 and Chamfer Distance (CD) / surface Root Mean Square Error (RMSE) of 3.2/3.5 cm; relative to the perception-replacement baseline (PointNet++ + S2B, F1 = 0.758) the gain is large, while relative to the strongest competing baseline—CoFiNet (Reg) + S2B at F1 = 0.822—the absolute association gain narrows to 0.015 F1 points, with the bigger gains coming from temporal identity consistency (IDF1 0.854 → 0.861) and ID-switch reduction. A held-out tile-stream case study visualises BIM-element state evolution, alignment refinement, EMA-stabilised evidence streams, and hazard-flag firing as the scan progressively covers the floor; a strict temporal validation under active construction will require time-stamped multi-acquisition data and is left to future work. Ablation results confirm that evidence stabilization and multi-cue matching contribute complementary gains in accuracy and temporal consistency, yielding a favorable efficiency–accuracy trade-off. Building Information Modeling (BIM) provides a design-time, element-centric representation, but the as-built scene evolves continuously and often deviates from the planned model. Construction digital twins aim to close this loop by fusing sensing data with BIM to maintain an up-to-date, operationally interpretable state of the site1–3. Among available sensing modalities, multi-line LiDAR (Light Detection and Ranging) offers dense 3D geometry with robust depth measurements, enabling frequent capture of complex construction scenes; however, turning streaming LiDAR into a temporally consistent, BIM-aligned, element-level state remains challenging due to clutter and occlusion, repetitive structures, coordinate-frame drift, heterogeneous supervision for perception, and the need for causal, on-site operation. A first family of approaches is geometry-driven Scan-to-BIM alignment and model-based comparison, typically centered on coarse-to-fine registration and handcrafted geometric cues. Representative Scan-to-BIM frameworks exploit planar primitives or congruent sets to initialize the alignment and then refine it 4. While effective in controlled settings, these methods are brittle under partial observations, non-model objects, and symmetries that induce ambiguous alignments; moreover, uncertainty in geometric matching can propagate to downstream “progress/quality” conclusions when the digital twin is built from imperfect registrations 4. More broadly, systematic reviews note that fully automated, robust Scan-to-BIM remains difficult in realistic Architecture, Engineering, and Construction (AEC) scenarios, especially when the environment is incomplete, cluttered, or only partially scanned5. A second family focuses on learning-based point cloud understanding. Deep networks for 3D segmentation and feature learning have significantly advanced geometric–semantic perception 6–8. Construction-oriented studies further demonstrate that learned scene understanding can support Scan-to-BIM automation and downstream reconstruction9. Nonetheless, these models are commonly trained on static frames and do not, by themselves, enforce temporally stable predictions across a LiDAR stream, nor do they guarantee BIM-consistent element associations and geometry-aware alignment cues needed by Scan-vs-BIM workflows. In addition, instance-level consistency and robust clustering remain non-trivial in cluttered scenes even with discriminative embedding objectives10. A third family concerns digital-twin systems and construction management analytics (progress, quality, and safety) built on BIM-centric reasoning. Prior work highlights the promise of digital twins for cyber-physical integration but also emphasizes interoperability, real-time updating, and reliable data-to-model synchronization as persistent gaps 2,3. On the safety side, BIM-based hazard identification and prevention can encode design- time priors and rules, yet its operational effectiveness hinges on accurate as-built localization of hazards and protections, which is difficult without a robust, continuously updated scan-to-BIM state 11. Consequently, construction-management indicators often remain loosely coupled to the sensing and alignment stack, limiting reliability when scene observations are noisy, intermittent, or partially missing. In summary, existing models provide valuable components, but they do not fully deliver a causal, BIM- consistent digital-twin update that tightly couples spatio-temporal mapping, geometric–semantic perception, element-level association, and construction-management indicators from LiDAR streams. The main problems with these methods are as follows: • Fragmented sensing-to-BIM state updating : Many approaches stop at per-scan registration or per-frame segmentation, without an explicit element-level digital-twin state and a causal update mechanism that is ro- bust to occlusion and intermittent observations2,3. • Brittle scan-to-BIM association under real site conditions: Purely geometric alignment and matching can be ambiguous in symmetric, cluttered, and partially observed scenes, and uncertainty in scan-vs-BIM geom- etry can distort downstream conclusions4. • Limited integrated, geometry-aware state for site management : Many systems do not expose a unified, BIM-aligned geometric–semantic state that can be updated online and used consistently across progress, dimensional-compliance, and deviation monitoring11. To address these limitations, we propose LiBiDT (LiDAR–BIM Digital Twin), a four-stage digital-twin model that performs Spatio-Temporal Global Registration (STGR), Multi-task Perception (MTP), Scan-to-BIM Association and State Updating (S2B), and Decision and Risk Analytics (DRA) in a strict causal cascade. The key idea is to treat globally registered LiDAR as a scene-level backbone, attach geometric–semantic predictions to it, and update BIM element states through robust association and temporally filtered evidence, producing an auditable interface of progress and deviation indicators6,12.
