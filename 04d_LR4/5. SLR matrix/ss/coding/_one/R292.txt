Traditional ﬁre protection systems only detect ﬁres when their characteristics are already visible, and ﬁre recognition delays have become a signiﬁcant problem. This paper presents a digital twin framework for managing building ﬁre safety in real time. Sensor data and a Bert_C model are used to analyze the transition of a ﬁre from smoldering to ﬂaming combustion. The results show that the model achieved 96.1% accuracy and 96.2% F1 score, and the framework signiﬁcantly improves the timeliness and accuracy of ﬁre warnings and visualizes 3D ﬁre scenarios, providing powerful technical support for ﬁreﬁghting and emergency rescue operations. This study conﬁrms the feasibility of real-time ﬁre safety management using 3D environments and digital twins, providing an innovative solution for ﬁre safety management in the built environment. In the event of ﬁre emergencies, the system ensures swift responses by providing real-time, comprehensive decision-making support to administrators, facilitating the effective implementation of emergency mea- sures. 2 Related work 2.1 Digital twin The concept of the digital twin was ﬁrst introduced by Dr. Grieves in 2003 under the term "Mirrored Space Model" [13]. The initial framework of the digital twin was identi- ﬁed as comprising physical products, virtual products, and their interconnections [ 14]. Gabor et al. [ 15] described the digital twin as an advanced simulation created using domain expertise and real-world data, capable of achieving accu- rate simulations across varying temporal and spatial scales. Tao et al. [16] emphasized that digital twin modeling serves as the core of representing physical entities and, driven by data and models, enables monitoring, simulation, and pre- diction. Subsequently, Tao and Zhang [ 17] proposed that a comprehensive digital twin comprises ﬁve key compo- nents: physical entities, virtual entities, interconnections, data, and services, with data being fundamental for gener- ating new knowledge. These elements collectively form the core structure of the digital twin [ 18]. A digital twin is not merely a digital representation of a physical entity; it also facilitates real-time acquisition and analysis of operational, surrounding, and status-related information, thereby support- ing decision-making processes. With advancements in the Internet of Things (IoT), big data, artiﬁcial intelligence, and high-performance computing, the concept of the digital twin has evolved and found applications beyond manufacturing. Its adoption has expanded into diverse ﬁelds, including smart cities, healthcare, aerospace, and transportation. Digital twin technology represents a relatively novel approach in the domain of building ﬁre safety. To address the increasingly stringent requirements for ﬁre safety in complex and dynamic building environments, a digital twin framework integrating IoT, building information modeling (BIM), and advanced machine learning techniques has been developed. This framework enables real-time monitoring and dynamic simulation of critical elements, including the internal environment, ﬁre protection systems, and personnel distribution within a building, by constructing a digital repre- sentation of the physical structure.
