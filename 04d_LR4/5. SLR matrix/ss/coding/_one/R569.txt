Traditional simulation methods, focusing on isolated disaster scenarios, fall short in addressing the multifaceted nature of these risks due to geological ambiguity and data incompleteness. Digital twin technology presents an effective solution to these challenges; however, its core challenge lies in how to utilize digital twin technology for data-model-co-driven simulation analysis of coupled multi-physical fields in situations of incomplete data. This paper introduces a digital twin paradigm for the simulation analysis of water inrush, which significantly enhances efficiency and accuracy through the integration of advanced machine learning and finite element analysis techniques. Specifically, this is achieved by combining a high-precision geological modeling method based on Gaussian Processes (GP) with a parameter calibration method through Gaussian Process-Differential Evolution (GP-DE) back-analysis. Firstly, a voxel structure is utilized to integrate the multi-field attribute features of the tunnel environment. Secondly, through the integration of multi-source advanced geological prediction data, we construct a dynamic digital twin model of the tunnel environment leveraging machine learning techniques. To overcome the issue of low modeling accuracy, the GP is employed, enhancing the exploitation of latent information within multi-source geophysical data. Lastly, we utilize the GP-DE back- analysis method to calibrate the parameters of the tunnel environment, thereby enhancing the precision and reliability of water inrush simulations. The method has been validated through application to a section of an ultra-high-temperature water inrush tunnel in China, featuring a burial depth of 230 meters. The accuracy of the method is corroborated by the monitoring data from the tunnel, supporting dynamic optimization design and safety prevention measures during construction. 1. Introduction In the advancement of railway construction in mountainous regions, tunnels constitute more than 80% of the total infrastructure, with many being deeply buried and facing disaster risks such as high geostress, high geothermal conditions, and sudden water inrush (Li et al., 2021a). Typically, engineers depend on geophysical and sensor data to predict the geological conditions ahead of the tunnel face and to formulate the corresponding construction plans. Owing to the concealed and spatiotemporally heterogeneous nature of the tunnel environment, this setting transforms into a complex and open spatial environment (Wang et al. , 2021 ). In actual construction, engineers often depend on the interpretation of advanced geological prediction data to forecast the geological conditions, attributable to the limitations of detection meth- ods (Cui et al. , 2015; Huang et al. , 2022; Xin-rong et al. , 2015). Additionally, during construction, risks associated with high-temperature water inrush in these geothermally active environ- ments are more complex than traditional hazards such as high rock temperature or sole water inrush (Wang et al., 2024a). This type of thermal damage constitutes a systemic and comprehensive risk, arising from the interplay of stress, seepage, and temperature fields, which poses significant challenges in risk prevention and control during construction (Liu et al., 2023c). Existing simulation methods that focus on single disaster types are inadequate in accounting for the coupled effects between multiple disaster risks, thereby hindering ac- curate risk assessment. In disaster simulations, certain key parameters cannot be measured accurately, leading to a significant discrepancy between the calculated results and the actual situation. Consequently, addressing the second core challenge in this study involves constructing a thermo-hydro-mechanical (THM) coupling water inrush simulation model featuring accurate key parameters. To address these challenges, the solution strategy must be intimately aligned with practical engineering considerations, necessitating a focus not only on precision but also on efficiency—the hallmark of digital twin (DT). Through the integration of multi-source real-time data, mechanistic models, and expert knowledge, DT provides an accurate representation of the physical world and facilitates risk assessment, prediction, and decision-making (El Saddik et al., 2021). Recently, bolstered by the integration of the Internet of Things, big data analysis, and advanced simulation algorithms, DT has seen a progressively wider application in tunnel engineering (Hu et al., 2021). Indeed, DT offers several advantages in tunnel engineering, including: (1) DT enables the integration of multi-source observational data and facilitates the real-time updating of geological models (Wu et al., 2022). This rapid updating mechanism enhances the timeliness and accuracy of the model, enabling it to more accurately reflect the actual underground situation. (2) DT employs advanced machine learning methods to delineate the characteristics and trends of incomplete data in the tunnel and to unveil its underlying principles (Latif et al., 2023; Erharter et al., 2022). (3) Through the integration of models from various disciplines (Tao et al., 2022), DT is capable of accurately simulating the disaster process due to the coupling effect of multiple factors in tunnels. Although DT has found applications in domains like tunnel rock design (Xiaojun et al., 2023), operations and maintenance (Yu et al., 2021), and lighting environment optimization (Shen et al., 2022), its deployment in tunnel geological modeling and disaster simulation remains limited. This limitation emerges due to the specialization of these methods for specific scenarios, resulting in a lack of universality. Therefore, in addressing the aforementioned core challenges, this paper proposes a geological modeling and water inrush simulation analysis method tailored for DT applications. Specifically, this study advocates for the adoption of voxel models as the foundational framework for DT. Voxel models are considered ideal owing to their ability to store multiple attributes of the geological environment and to enable efficient dynamic updates (Wu et al., 2022). The contributions of this study are as follows: (1) A voxel model was utilized to integrate multi-source geophysical data, effectively characterizing the multi-dimensional attributes of the tunnel environment. The Gaussian Process (GP) method was employed to establish the nonlinear relationship between these attributes and adverse geological conditions. This approach addresses the limitation of traditional single data sources, which fail to fully capture the com- plexity of the surrounding rock, thereby enhancing the accuracy and reliability of the DT model of the tunnel geological environment. (2) We propose a parameter calibration method based on the Gaus- sian Process-Differential Evolution (GP-DE) back-analysis to improve the reliability of the water inrush simulation by accurately calibrating the tunnel environmental parameters. This method addresses the high parameter uncertainty and insufficient simulation precision in tradi- tional models when dealing with complex underground environments. (3) We applied the proposed method to a section of a tunnel with a burial depth of 230 meters in southwest China, which is prone to ultra-high-temperature water inrush. The experimental results prove the effectiveness and superiority of our method.
