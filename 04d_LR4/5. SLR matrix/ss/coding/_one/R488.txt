By combining the computational power of PINN for solving structural mechanics problems with BIM’s data management and modeling capabilities in construction engineering, a multi-layer, multi-dimensional intelligent framework for predic- tion and optimization is developed. BIM models provide crucial data, including geometric details, material properties, and construction process information, which are used to create a digital twin of the bridge. Leveraging PINN’s robust fitting ability, physical laws such as stress, strain, and equilibrium equations are incorporated into the neural network to predict the bridge’s performance under various operational conditions. The integration of physical constraints during PINN training enhances both the accuracy and reliability of the predictions. Subsequently, optimization algorithms refine the bridge design based on these predictions, ensuring improvements in performance while meeting key criteria such as safety, cost-effectiveness, and environmental impact. This method offers a scientifically rigorous and highly accurate approach to supporting the design and construction of prefabricated bridges, advancing intelligent construction and sustainable building practices. Experimental results demonstrate that the integrated PINN-BIM model outperforms tradi- tional methods, exhibiting superior accuracy, generalization capability, and offering a novel solution for efficient bridge engineering construction and performance optimization. Traditional structural design methods often rely on empirical formulas, finite element analysis, and other methods. Although they can provide some evaluation of structural per - formance, they still have certain limitations when facing complex mechanical behavior, uncertainty during construc - tion, and the influence of different environmental conditions. Especially when considering the nonlinear behavior of materials, construction sequence, and coupling of multiple physical fields, traditional methods often struggle to achieve comprehensive and accurate performance prediction and optimization [1 ]. In recent years, Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs) have gradually become a powerful tool for solving complex engineering problems as an emerging machine learning technology. PINNs combine physical laws with neural networks to effectively model systems and make efficient predictions and optimizations based on physical equations, especially in situations where data is scarce. The emergence of this technology provides new ideas for the performance analysis of bridge structures [2 ]. On the other hand, Building Information Modeling (BIM), as an integrated digital modeling tool, has been widely used in the construction industry, especially in bridge design and construction management. BIM can provide accurate geometric modeling, construction planning, and material infor - mation, thereby helping designers, engineers, and construction personnel to collaborate and make better decisions. However, BIM itself still has certain limitations in the in-depth analysis of structural performance and multi-physics coupling simulation [3 ].A significant advancement in addressing these limitations is the integration of live data meas- urement systems directly installed on structures, which enables the continuous monitoring of real-time performance data. By incorporating these sensor-based measurements into BIM, it becomes possible to create more trustworthy and dynamic models that reflect the actual behavior of the structures under varying conditions [4 ]. This approach not only enhances the accuracy of BIM models but also strengthens their predictive capabilities. The integration of real-time monitoring data is gaining increasing global recognition as an essential practice for enhancing the reli- ability and functionality of BIM, particularly in the context of structural performance analysis. This practice is gaining traction due to its potential to bridge the gap between theoretical modeling and actual physical performance, thus providing more reliable insights for maintenance, safety, and optimization [5 ].Physics-Informed Neural Networks. Therefore, combining PINN with BIM technology to form a new intelligent design and optimization method can make up for the shortcomings of traditional methods. By combining the precise structural data provided by BIM with the physical constraints of PINN, mechanical behavior, dynamic changes during construction, and external environmental influences can be fully considered in performance prediction and optimization, thereby achieving more accurate and comprehensive prediction and optimization of bridge structural performance. This can not only enhance the safety and reliability of bridge design, but also provide more scientific and systematic decision support for the construction of prefabricated bridges. Therefore, this article aims to explore the theoretical framework and application path of combining PINN and BIM technology, provide new technical solutions for predicting and opti- mizing the structural performance of prefabricated bridges, and provide theoretical support and methodological guidance for practical problems in engineering practice. In this study, the term "performance" refers to a comprehensive set of mechanical and operational characteristics of bridge structures. Specifically, we focus on several key aspects: ✥ Stress State: The distribution and magnitude of stresses within the bridge components under various loading conditions. ✥ Deformations and Displacements: The geometric changes and movements of the bridge structure, including deflections and rotations, under different operational scenarios. ✥ Operability: The ability of the bridge to function effectively under normal usage conditions, including traffic loads and environmental influences. ✥ Extreme Events: The structural behavior and resilience of the bridge during extreme conditions such as high winds, earthquakes, or other natural disasters. By addressing these aspects, our method aims to provide a holistic view of the bridge’s performance, ensuring that the design and optimization processes consider all critical factors that affect the bridge’s safety, durability, and efficiency. It embeds physical equations directly into the training process of neural networks (NNs), enabling the model to not only learn patterns from data but also automatically satisfy physical constraints during the solving pro - cess. This approach allows for a more accurate and complex representation of practical engineering problems. In this context, physical laws refer to the fundamental equations that describe physical phenomena, and deep learning refers to advanced algorithms that simulate the human brain’s neural structure for pattern recognition and problem-solving. In Fig. 1, “PDE” denotes the partial differential equation, “BC” denotes the boundary condition, and “IC” denotes the initial condition. Unlike traditional deep learning methods, PINN does not rely on a large amount of annotated data, but can ensure the physical consistency of the model output by incorporating known physical laws (such as fluid mechanics equations, structural mechanics equations, etc.) into the loss function of the network. This method is particularly suitable for solving problems that are difficult to efficiently solve through traditional numerical methods, especially when dealing with com- plex boundary conditions, nonlinear phenomena, or large-scale data.
