With increasing traffic volume and extreme weather conditions, these bridges face threats to their functional and structural safety during their service life, necessi - tating timely health monitoring and condition assessment. In recent years, digital twin (DT) has achieved sig - nificant progress in bridge monitoring and assessment, effectively addressing the shortcomings of traditional methods. However, current bridge DTs, especially those for cable-supported bridges, still face a series of issues, such as ambiguous concepts and lack of unified standards. To enrich the discussion on this topic, this paper explores the current research trends and frameworks of bridge DT through bibliometric analysis and a comprehensive review of the latest advancements, providing a systematic overview of relevant research on health monitoring and condition assessment of cable-supported bridges based on DT. Firstly, the origin, defi - nition, and characteristics of DT are introduced. Secondly, a systematic literature review is employed to statis - tically analyze the current research status and discuss the construction methods of the DT system. Subsequently, a novel three-layer framework for DT is proposed, which can be applied to health monitoring and condition assessment of cable-supported bridges. Finally, the challenges and future trends of DT in this field are discussed. The challenges consist of seven aspects, which are data management, modeling, security and privacy, service applications, intelligence, integration, and standardization. Future DT development will focus on advancing digitization, integration, and intelligence, while deepening theoretical research and practical applications. The establishment of a reliable operational condition assessment framework is also an important trend. 1. Introduction Cable-supported bridges, as crucial transportation infrastructure, face significant threats to their functional and structural safety over their service life due to material degradation and various other influencing factors, including traffic overloads, vehicle or ship collisions, fatigue cracking, steel corrosion, fires, typhoons, earthquakes, and especially the ever-increasing traffic demands and changing weather conditions [1] . Garg et al. [2] analyzed the failure causes of 2010 bridges from 1977 to 2017 and found that natural disasters accounted for 80.30 %, material degradation for 10.10 %, traffic overloads for 3.28 %, and human-induced disasters for 2.19 %. In reality, there are numerous specific cases of bridge damage. For example, the Humber Bridge in the UK, which experienced excessive wear of its bearings due to extreme loads [3] , the Martinus Nijhoff Cable-Stayed Bridge in the Netherlands, which showed fatigue damage much earlier than expected during its design service life [4] , and the Jiangyin Suspension Bridge in China, which suffered from the failure of expansion joints due to traffic loads [5] . Therefore, long-term and effective health monitoring and condition assessment of cable-supported bridges throughout their service life are crucial. Traditional monitoring and assessment methods for cable-supported bridges have met certain demands but still face chal - lenges or issues [6 – 8] . Currently, digital twin (DT) has become a research hotspot [9 – 11] due to its unique advantages and is widely used for monitoring, pre - dictive maintenance, and analysis of various physical assets. DT can be considered as a virtual model that represents an object throughout its lifecycle, maintaining its state, properties, and operational dynamics. Background 2.1. Cable-supported bridges Cable-supported bridges, primarily consisting of suspension bridges and cable-stayed bridges, represent one of the most significant in - vestments in the national transportation network. The main components include towers, main cable/stay cables, and the deck. Fig. 1 illustrates schematic diagrams of both suspension bridge and cable-stayed bridge. Although cable-supported bridges account for only about 1.5 % of all bridges, they are an effective structural form for large spans and intri - cate environments [13 – 15] . In bridge categories with spans exceeding 200 m, cable-supported bridges account for over 90 % [16] . With a service life exceeding 100 years, cable-supported bridges may undergo material aging, traffic loads [17] , hurricanes or typhoons [14] , earth - quakes [18] , fires [16] , etc., during their lifespan, which can signifi - cantly impact the functionality and structural safety of the bridges. 2.2. Methods of health monitoring and condition assessment Ensuring the safety and uninterrupted operation of cable-supported bridges is a key concern for stakeholders. Health monitoring and con - dition assessment of these bridges are essential processes for addressing the aforementioned issues. There are various traditional methods for health monitoring and condition assessment of cable-supported bridges. The most basic method, and one adopted by most countries, is visual inspection for bridge condition assessment. However, visual inspection involves extensive manual labor and is prone to neglecting defects [6,8] . Bridge testing can inspect the overall structure or major components of a bridge, providing valuable data support for bridge monitoring and assessment. But this method typically requires bridge closures, which can have certain traffic and economic impacts. Structural Health Monitoring (SHM) is described as the application of sensing technology and analysis of structural characteristics to identify structural damage or degradation [19] .
