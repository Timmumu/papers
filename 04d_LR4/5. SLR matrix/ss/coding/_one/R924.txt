Brillouin fiber optic sensors and mobile augmented reality-based digital twins for quantitative safety assessment of underground pipelines Minghao Li, Xin Feng * , Yang Han Faculty of Infrastructure Engineering, Dalian University of Technology, Dalian, China ARTICLE INFO Keywords: Underground pipelines Distributed fiber optic sensors Digital twin Mobile augmented reality (MAR) Joint data – physics driven model Structural safety assessment ABSTRACT The impossibility of visual inspection and the complexity of combined loads hamper the quantitative assessment, lifetime prediction and control of underground pipelines during their lifecycles. A methodology based on mobile augmented reality (MAR) and Brillouin fiber optic sensors (BFOSs) is presented to build a digital twin (DT) for underground pipelines. Field experiments were carried out to demonstrate that the proposed method can quantitatively assess and predict the structural safety of an underground pipeline from the DT. The results demonstrate that the distributed sensor networks can measure important but unpredictable deformations (i.e., longitudinal bending and axial thermal strain), the joint data-physics driven model can estimate the structural stress state more accurately than the common calculation model, and the MAR-based human – asset interaction interface enables more intuitive, efficient, automated operation and maintenance (O & M). In the future, in-line robotic systems and localized damage models should be further adopted for lifecycle O & M. 1. Introduction Underground pipelines are widely used engineering assets, and their safety management is easily neglected because they are not visible and direct structural damage inspection is difficult [ 1 ]. However, with the rapid growth of underground infrastructure assets and the deterioration of existing structures over time, the increasing number of public emer - gencies caused by underground pipelines have brought safety manage - ment to the forefront [ 2 ]. Underground pipelines have a complex spatial distribution and are controlled by different departments. In addition, there are significant differences in the structural states of pipelines caused by specific operating conditions, such as foundation settlement and internal fluid pressure/temperature. Therefore, the digitalization and automation of safety management for underground pipelines is still a challenging issue. As a next-generation technology that can integrate a series of advanced technologies (e.g., sensors, modeling and visualization), emerging digital twin (DT) technology, which is considered to be an efficient intelligence solution for digitalization and automation, uses digital models to dynamically monitor, describe, simulate, predict and control physical counterparts in a timely manner [ 3 – 5 ]. Recently, DT technology has drawn much attention from both the academic and in - dustrial communities [ 6 ]. Gartner predicted that 50% of large industrial corporations would use DT technology by 2021, corresponding to a 10% improvement in effectiveness [ 7 ]. Since the National Aeronautics and Space Administration (NASA) gave the first specific definition in 2012 [ 8 ], the DT concept has gradually been extended from the field of in - dustrial manufacturing to construction engineering and from theoretical research to application practice [ 9 – 13 ]. At the asset scale, Lin et al. [ 14 ] proposed a DT-based collapse fragility assessment method for long-span cable-stayed bridges during strong earthquakes. Mohammadi and Taylor [ 15 ] introduced a smart city DT paradigm that revolutionized human - – infrastructure interactions and urban planning through the Internet of Things (IoT) and virtual reality (VR). Lu et al. [ 10 , 16 ] presented a sys - tem architecture for DT technology that is specifically designed at the building and city levels and developed a DT demonstrator at the West Cambridge site of the University of Cambridge in the UK. Although each of these cases has its own unique definitions and requirements [ 5 ], a successful DT paradigm relies on (1) the mutual interaction between the physical asset and its DT, which is related to high-performance sensor technologies and high-fidelity data communication; (2) DT modeling approaches that should focus on both geometrical appearance and the ability to carry out asset-specific analysis, prediction, optimization and decision-making support; and (3) a human – asset interaction interface presented to the end users, which should improve the ability to under - stand the unique characteristics of an existing physical asset and be * Corresponding author. In pipeline engineering, Siemens proposed the Pipeline 4.0 concept [ 17 ]. Pipeline 4.0 utilizes DT technology to explore innovative ap - proaches to further reduce the operating expenses of pipeline systems with no risk or cost. In the current phase, Pipeline 4.0 mainly focuses on developing DTs for pumping stations rather than pipelines. PetroChina has adopted digital recovery techniques, including aerial photogram - metry, 3D laser scanning, 3D terrain construction, oblique photogram - metry and digital 3D modeling, to build the DT of the China – Myanmar oil and gas pipeline [ 18 ]. Stennikov et al. [ 19 ] presented an object- oriented computer modeling technology to construct the DTs of pipe - line systems for online hydraulic calculations. Lieberman et al. [ 20 ] discussed the potential benefits of using big data analytics and IoT principles to develop functional DTs of underground infrastructures and improve livability and resilience in modern cities. Carvalho [ 21 ] pre - sented a DT framework combining building information modeling (BIM) and asset management (AM) techniques to support the maintenance and operation of water and wastewater utility assets. According to a litera - ture survey, pipeline DT technology is still in its infancy, and there are few reports on using DTs for the structural safety assessment of under - ground pipelines. The application of DTs to underground pipelines faces the following challenges. (1) The sensor network technique underpins the mutual in - teractions between DTs and hidden buried assets. Although nonde - structive test (NDT)-based in-line inspection (ILI) tools, including magnetic flux leakage (MFL), ultrasonic wall measurement (UT) and remote field eddy current (RFEC) tools, are commonly adopted in oil and gas pipelines, they are local inspection techniques rather than real-time sensing techniques. However, these methods cannot measure the stress states along a pipeline. The supervisory control and data acquisition (SCADA) system uses only sparse low-sampling transducers to monitor the internal pressure, flow rate and temperature parameters of pipelines; however, this system is unable to provide information regarding struc - tural deterioration during a lifecycle. The comprehensive sensing of the structural state, including both stress/deformation and deterioration in the pipeline, is recognized as one of the most critical issues for pipeline DTs. (2) Human – asset interactions and visualization are vital for un - derground pipelines since they are not visible in the buried environ - ment. The state of the art is summarized in Table 1 . Augmented reality (AR) is considered to be a friendly human – asset interaction technique due to its fast communication, 3D visualization and beyond-reality interaction for underground pipelines. However, the current AR-based modeling and visualization methods mainly focus on the geometric attribute information (design and construction phases) rather than the dynamic evolution of structural states (operation and maintenance (O & M) phase). During the O & M phase, which covers > 80% of the lifecycle, pipelines suffer from corrosion and load or operating condition changes, resulting in the dynamic evolution and deterioration of the structural state over time. Therefore, the current studies lack a modeling and interaction approach for the dynamic evolution of the pipeline structural state to guide lifecycle safety management. To address these challenges, a framework for DTs is presented in this study for underground pipelines by using mobile augmented reality (MAR) and Brillouin fiber optic sensors (BFOSs). BFOSs, in addition to IoT-based pressure and temperature sensors, constitute distributed sensor networks, which are used to comprehensively sense the structural stress states of underground pipelines. Then, a joint data-physics driven model is presented to achieve dynamic modeling of the evolution and deterioration of specific asset structures. A MAR-based human – asset interaction interface is proposed to visualize geometric attributes, real- time monitoring data and structural safety assessment results. The feasibility of the proposed methodology for automated safety assessment was validated by experimental investigations in a real pipe network. 2.
