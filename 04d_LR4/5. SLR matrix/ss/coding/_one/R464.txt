Introduction Earthwork engineering is an essential component of construction projects and is commonly used in infrastructure development and real estate ventures. It plays a pivotal role in the field of hydraulic engineering (Moselhi and Alshibani, 2009). Earthwork engineering typically involves various types of equipment, such as dump trucks (for transporting materials between loading and filling areas), pavers (for the laying and initial compaction of crushed stones) and compactors (to increase the density of foundation materials and reinforce structural stability). These pieces of equipment ensure the smooth operation of projects (Parente et al., 2015). With the continuous advancement of automated construction technologies, the demand for the efficient allocation and scheduling of earthwork construction equipment at construction sites has become increasingly prominent during the optimization process of Downloaded from http://www.emerald.com/ci/article-pdf/doi/10.1108/CI-05-2025-0221/11424842/ci-05-2025-0221en.pdf by City University of Hong Kong user on 16 June 2026 construction plans ( Liu et al., 2022 ). Efficient allocation and scheduling of earthwork construction equipment enhances operational efficiency and ensures orderly progress, thereby improving overall construction quality . Hence, the rational and efficient allocation and scheduling of earthwork construction equipment has become a hot topic in the field of earthwork construction. Currently , heuristic algorithms are predominantly used in engineering practice for the optimization of equipment allocation and scheduling processes. These algorithms aim to compute the global optimal solution simultaneously to plan the equipment usage of the entire construction process ( Parente et al., 2015 a; Lin et al., 2012 ; Parente et al., 2015 b; Du et al., 2016 ; Miao et al., 2024 ; Shehadeh et al., 2022 ). However , unexpected events, such as adverse weather conditions, equipment failure and traffic congestion, during open-pit earthwork construction may impact the construction progress, resulting in deviations between the actual construction schedule and the initially optimized construction plan ( Hasan et al., 2018 ). Leveraging digital twin (DT) to acquire real-time construction information, detect dynamic deviations and continuously adjust optimal plans through dynamic optimization methods can help ensure controlled project progress and holds significant research value through the timely correction of deviations ( Liu et al., 2022 ). W ith the maturation of the DT technology , real-time construction information can now be sensed and traced ( Jiang et al., 2022 a). In current architecture, engineering and construction (AEC) practice, DT s are predominantly implemented as virtual representations that mirror physical assets, processes and systems ( Ghansah and Lu, 2023 ) and reported applications lar gely cluster around monitoring-oriented scenarios (e.g. progress, safety , asset condition; Radzi et al., 2023 ). However , such implementations largely emphasize monitoring-oriented mirroring of assets, processes and systems ( Ghansah and Lu, 2023 ; Radzi et al., 2023 ; Pour Rahimian et al., 2022 ) with comparatively limited attention to decision support for scheduling and control. Accordingly , harnessing the visibility and traceability affo rded by DT s to tackle the allocation and scheduling of heavy construction equipment on smart sites remains an important and underexplored problem warranting systematic investigation. The rolling-horizon optimization (RHO) strategy ( Sethi and Sor ger , 1991 ) is a dynamic optimization and feedback control method applied across various fields, such as operations management ( Alimian et al., 2022 ), energy management ( Bertazzi and Maggioni, 2018 ) and transportation planning ( Cordeau et al., 2015 ). This strategy operates on a closed-loop optimization model that continuously progresses along a timeline. This strategy optimizes the control objectives within the current time window based on predictive models, outputting the optimal decision for the current time window . This decision is then fed back into the actual construction process, thus advancing the time window to determine the actual construction status of the next time window for further decision optimization ( Sethi and Sor ger , 1991 ). Metaheuristic algorithms are commonly used in optimization processes, where the hyperparameter settings of such algorithms directly influence the optimization performance. Therefore, inspired by the aforementioned studies, this study aimed to establish a closed- loop model for construction processes based on a DT -enabled RHO strategy to address the flexible scheduling problem of multi-objective automated earthwork construction site equipment, ensuring construction progress. In the optimization process, considering the drawbacks of time-consumption and nonadaptive offline parameter tuning commonly associated with algorithmic optimization processes ( Eiben et al., 1999 ), an Improved Aquila Optimizer (AO) based on online parameter tuning was adopted. This algorithm uses an online parameter control strategy that enables an adaptive adjustment of the AO parameters for dynamic optimization problems. Related works 2.1 Applications of digital twin in earthwork construction DT s are dynamic virtual counterparts of physical entities, processes, services and systems ( van der Heijden, 2023 ). A canonical DT architecture consists of physical space, virtual space and an information connection and processing layer that couples them. Its core principle is the bidirectional mapping between physical assets and their virtual models, enabling real-time sensing, prediction and optimization of the underlying system ( Glaessgen and Star gel, 2012 ). W ithin the AEC sector , DT applications are concentrated in design, construction scheduling and operations and maintenance (O&M). In the design phase, DT s integrate conceptual, architectural and structural models on a unified platform to support holistic computation and simulation, thereby enabling accurate prediction of asset performance and informing early design modifications ( Kan and Anumba, 2019 ).
