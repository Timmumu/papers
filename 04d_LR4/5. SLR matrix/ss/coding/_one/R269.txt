Abstract This study addresses the challenges of real-time response and closed-loop decision- making in smart construction. We propose a dual-loop collaborative framework integrating Building Information Modeling (BIM), the Internet of Things (IoT), and LSTM-Attention networks, validated via a 261-sensor deployment in the Taipei Bridge and Tunnel Joint Project. The architecture achieves sub-second synchronization and enables multi-objective optimization using NSGA-II. Experimental results demonstrate statistically significant improvements ( p < 0.001): a 46.2% reduction in system latency, a 41.4% decrease in safety incidents, an 18.5% reduction in direct costs, and an 11.1% decline in CO₂ emissions, while maintaining a 99.1% safety compliance rate. The framework successfully bridges the gap between digital models and physical realization, providing a scalable solution for adaptive and sustainable construction management. Keywords Smart construction, Digital twin, BIM-IoT, LSTM-attention, Multi-objective optimization 1 Introduction Intelligent construction is undergoing a key transformation from “endpoint perception” to “overall collaboration” [ 1]. This transformation is critically important for the con - struction industry, which not only contributes about 40% of the global carbon emissions [2], but also faces severe challenges such as high energy consumption, low efficiency and frequent safety accidents [ 3, 4]. Although the deep integration of building information modeling (BIM), Internet of Things and artificial intelligence is regarded as the core path to improve the transparency of construction and the agility of decision-making [ 5], and promotes the evolution from “one-way monitoring” to “digital twin collaboration” [ 1], practical problems such as sensor noise, model heterogeneity and decision-making delay remain prominent. Taking the Taipei Bridge and Tunnel Joint Project in 2024 as an example, although more than 260 sensors were deployed on site, the average delay of BIM updating and real-time decision-making exceeded 13 s [ 3]. Moreover, the manual recheck during the peak hours of crane operation will increase the delay by 5 to 7 s, which will seriously endanger the “golden window period” of safety intervention [6], highlighting the defects Page 2 of 19 Su Discover Applied Sciences (2026) 8:769 of one-way data flow in fast-paced construction [ 1, 7]. Existing research has several core limitations, including a lack of real-time capability, limited attention to end-to-end delay, packet loss compensation, and prediction algorithms [ 3, 8]. At the same time, there are issues such as insufficient closed-loop integrity, with control strategies mostly rely - ing on manual scheduling or static rules, and a lack of adaptive “prediction-execution- feedback” mechanisms [5, 9]. In addition, cost, schedule and safety objectives are often optimized in isolation, lacking unified indicators and dynamic trade-off analysis, which makes it difficult to achieve multi-objective coordination [ 10, 11]. The disconnection between the digital model and the physical realization seriously restricts the sub-second response ability required by the high-density construction environment. Based on the above findings, a new dual-loop collaborative framework is designed and verified in this study. The architecture realizes real-time synchronization through the data loop of sensor → gateway → MQTT → BIM, and realizes closed-loop control with the help of time series prediction [ 12]. This mechanism not only shortens the round- trip time of information between physical domain and digital domain, but also uses deep attention network to predict the construction progress, equipment health and environ - mental safety in real time, thus realizing multi-objective parallel optimization [ 13, 14]. It is worth noting that the dual-loop architecture we designed is scalable, allowing sensor nodes to be added and enabling additional business rules to be integrated into decision- making processes [15, 16]. This study aims to clarify the semantic interoperability mechanism of dual-loop archi - tecture, and define the field mapping and timestamp synchronization strategy between edge gateway, MQTT proxy and IFC entity. Secondly, the collaborative advantages of the framework in delay convergence, risk identification and economic benefits are veri - fied by experimental data. Finally, the quantitative relationship between interface delay, packet loss rate and security events can be summarized, which provides practical guid - ance for the implementation of large-scale intelligent construction sites [17, 18]. 2 Literature review 2.1 Technological foundations and persistent gaps in enabling components The intelligent transformation of the construction industry is driven by the integration of dual carbon targets and core digital technologies. The integration of BIM and Internet of Things (IoT) is the key to create digital twins, with research progressing from seman - tic mapping [ 19] to practical synchronization protocol [ 5] and spatial integration [ 3]. At the same time, deep learning models (such as LSTM network to enhance attention mechanism) have also made breakthroughs in equipment and process risk prediction [20, 21], and multi-objective optimization algorithms (such as NSGA-II and MOEA) have evolved to include sustainable development goals such as carbon emissions [ 22, 23]. However, there are still significant gaps in these components. BIM-IOT integration usually lacks real-time, and the reporting delay exceeds the critical threshold [ 3], pre- venting the full incorporation of environmental data. Predictive models usually analyze a single risk factor independently, ignoring the coupling between equipment operation and environmental conditions. Optimization algorithms mainly rely on static data, and rarely achieve integrated dynamic optimization of safety, cost, schedule and environ - mental impact within a unified framework. Page 3 of 19 Su Discover Applied Sciences (2026) 8:769 2.2 Systemic architectural challenges: from cloud-edge deployment to closed-loop integration In addition to individual technology, agile computing architecture is needed to realize real-time closed-loop system. Edge computing provides a low-latency basis for respon - sive decision-making [ 15, 16]. However, the current mainstream system architecture usually limits the function of edge devices to data preprocessing. This not only fails to fully tap the potential of localized intelligent processing but also fails to establish a robust and fault-tolerant cloud-edge collaborative mechanism to achieve uninterrupted control. This reveals a key systemic gap: although various component technologies (such as BIM, Internet of Things, and edge computing) are constantly improving, the overall architecture framework that can seamlessly integrate and operate together is still lack - ing. Specifically, the lack of real-time semantic synchronization, the lack of dynamic closed-loop control, and the limitations of extensible cloud-side cooperation mechanism all constitute the bottleneck of the core architecture layer. These shortcomings hinder the fundamental transformation of intelligent building systems from isolated techni - cal applications to collaborative network physical systems that can coherently complete “perception-analysis-decision-execution” . 2.3 Evolution of multi-objective optimization research: from economic scheduling to sustainability synergy Facing the challenge of multi-objective decision-making in construction management, multi-objective optimization algorithm provides an effective solution tool. Many stud - ies, such as Nusen et al. [ 17], combine genetic algorithm with BIM to schedule renova - tion projects, while Alothaimeen et al. use non-dominated sorting genetic algorithm II (NSGA-II) to optimize the cost and sustainability scores of green building projects [ 23]. Besides the classic NSGA-II, advanced optimization algorithms such as multi-objective ant colony optimizer (MOALO) and multi-objective equilibrium optimizer (MOEO) show excellent convergence and coverage in solving complex engineering problems. These provide additional algorithm options for dealing with nonlinear multi-modal opti- mization objectives in buildings [ 11, 24].
