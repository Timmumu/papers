ABSTRACT Earthquakes pose severe threats to urban safety. Traditional prediction methods fail to fully quantify seismic uncertainties and address low-probability, high-impact ‘‘long-tail risks’’. This paper proposes a partially validated probabilistic urban seismic damage prediction framework integrating large- scale scenario ensembles and digital twins. The framework is conceptually designed with four layers: data fusion, digital twin modeling, scenario ensemble simulation, and probabilistic assessment. Due to current data and computational constraints, empirical validation is limited to two core modules—the PI-GAN ground motion generation module and the probabilistic damage cloud module—using the 2021 Y angbi Ms6.4 earthquake (single event, single region). The remaining two modules (infrastructure cascading failure and social behavior modeling) are presented as conceptual designs requiring future validation. The validation results show that the Physics-Informed Generative Adversarial Network (PI-GAN) generates ground motions with ≤5% PGA error and ≤10% response spectrum error, while the probabilistic damage cloud module achieves 77.7% overall classification accuracy (with 79.5% recall, 87.3% precision, and 83.2% F1-score) and 89.3% structure-damage matching degree. The framework enables a shift from empirical judgment to data-driven pre-simulation, providing a partially validated and technically feasible solution for improving the accuracy and comprehensiveness of urban seismic risk assessment, while clearly acknowledging that full- chain validation—particularly for extreme M≥7.0 scenarios and unvalidated modules—remains as future work. DIGITAL TWIN AND PROBABILISTIC SEISMIC RISK ASSESSMENT As a core enabling technology for deep integration of cyber-physical systems, digital twin technology provides an unprecedented platform for refined and dynamic disaster sim- ulation at the urban scale [4]. It has been applied to construct seismic response models of buildings [5], visualize seismic damage simulation results [6], and even evaluate the seismic resilience of urban lifeline systems [7]. Recent advances in probabilistic seismic risk assessment have increasingly incorporated stochastic rupture simulations within PSHA frameworks, aiming to better characterize source and path uncertainties [8], [9]. However, existing DT-based earth- quake risk frameworks often lack integration with large-scale, physics-consistent scenario ensembles and empirical valida- tion against real seismic events. How to combine large-scale seismic scenario ensembles with digital twin technology for probabilistic and forward-looking urban-scale seismic damage prediction remains a challenge requiring in-depth research [10]. C. SCOPE AND VALIDATION STATUS OF THIS STUDY It is important to clarify at the outset that the proposed framework is presented as a conceptual architecture with partial empirical validation. Specifically, the current study validates two of the four framework modules: (1) the PI-GAN-based ground motion generation module, and (2) the probabilistic damage cloud module. The infrastructure cascading failure module and the Agent-Based Modeling (ABM) social behavior module are described in their concep- tual design but have not been empirically tested against real earthquake data. The validation is further limited to a single moderate-magnitude event (2021 Y angbi Ms6.4) and a single village (Huaian Village, 112 buildings). Generalizability to other tectonic settings, urban typologies, or large-magnitude (M≥7.0) scenarios remains unvalidated and is a direction for future research. All claims of framework performance in this paper should be interpreted strictly within these defined boundaries. D. MAIN CONTRIBUTIONS AND PAPER ORGANIZATION The main contributions of this study are threefold: (1) a sys- tematic integration of high-fidelity digital twin modeling with physics-informed generative AI (PI-GAN) for ground motion synthesis—with validation limited to moderate-magnitude scenarios; (2) a full-chain probabilistic simulation workflow that couples seismic source generation, site response, struc- tural analysis, and infrastructure cascading failure within a unified digital twin environment—where the infrastructure and social modules remain conceptual at this stage; and (3) an empirical validation of the core modules (PI-GAN and damage cloud) using a real earthquake case (2021 Y angbi Ms6.4 earthquake), demonstrating the framework’s practical applicability and accuracy within the validated scope. Its core idea is to abandon the pursuit of predicting a ‘‘most likely’’ future, but instead systematically explore thousands of ‘‘possible’’ futures through computational experiments and statistically analyze their risk distribution. This study will elaborate on the framework’s architecture, key technologies, and implementation process, validate its core modules with empirical data, and discuss how it can provide revolutionary decision support for urban planning, resilience improvement, and precise emergency response—while clearly distinguish- ing demonstrated capabilities from conceptual extensions. INTEGRATION FRAMEWORK OF LARGE-SCALE SCENARIO ENSEMBLES AND DIGITAL TWINS The proposed prediction framework is essentially a complex system integrating advanced sensing, refined modeling, high- performance simulation, and intelligent decision-making. Its core lies in establishing a closed-loop, bidirectional inter- action between the physical city and its digital counterpart, where real-time or near-real-time data from the physical world (e.g., IoT sensor readings, InSAR deformation) are continuously ingested to update and calibrate the digital models, while simulation outputs from the digital twin (e.g., predicted damage states, cascading failure paths) are fed back to inform monitoring, planning, and emergency response in the physical city. This dynamic interaction enables a panoramic, probabilistic exploration of seismic risks via com- putational experiments. Logically, the framework is divided into four closely coupled layers, collectively forming a closed loop from real-world sensing to decision support.
