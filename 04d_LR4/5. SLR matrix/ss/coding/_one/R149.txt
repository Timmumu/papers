This paper presents a digital twin-based multi-objective optimization framework for identi - fying structural parameters of a cable-stayed bridge by matching its frequencies, mode shapes, and deflections. The framework integrates three techniques: (1) adaptive reference point relocation to maintain solution diversity across irregular Pareto fronts; (2) sensitivity-informed variation operators that concentrate exploration on pa - rameters with significant influence; and (3) hybrid local search incorporating quasi-Newton refinement for accelerated convergence. The framework was evaluated through implementation on a case study of a 580-meter- long cable-stayed bridge. Experimental results demonstrate up to a 52% reduction in parameter identification error and 25 – 35% reductions in total runtime compared with the Non-dominated Sorting Genetic Algorithm II (NSGA-II) and standard NSGA-III. Robustness analysis confirms the framework ’ s reliability with errors consis - tently below 3%. Introduction Cable-stayed bridges constitute a prominent category of long-span structures acclaimed for their superior structural efficiency, architec - tural aesthetics, and construction adaptability [1,2] . In practice, reliable bridge condition assessment is essential for ensuring operational safety and supporting risk-informed maintenance, because long-span cable- - stayed systems are highly sensitive to localized deterioration and stiff - ness/force redistribution in safety-critical components. Nevertheless, the inherent complexities of these structures, such as geometric non - linearities and material property uncertainties, pose considerable chal - lenges for accurate condition assessment. In long-term service, key components such as girders, stay cables, pylons, and anchorage systems inevitably deteriorate due to fatigue damage [3] , material aging, and environmental corrosion, which can reduce structural performance and trigger safety risks [4] . Since condition assessment fundamentally relies on comparing measured and predicted structural responses, establishing reliable Finite Element (FE) models and conducting effective parameter identification are critical but technically demanding tasks for integrity assurance and maintenance optimization [5 – 7] . Consequently, the fi - delity of bridge FE models governs the precision of structural response analysis and prediction capabilities [8,9] . The mechanical responses of cable-stayed bridges are governed by numerous interdependent parameters, such as material properties, stayed-cable pretension forces, tower stiffness, and bearing support conditions [7,10] . Conventional parameter identification methods have often relied on single-objective optimization frameworks [11] , wherein individual response metrics such as stayed-cable forces [12 – 14] or bridge deflections [15] serve as optimization targets. Although compu - tationally tractable, these approaches exhibit a fundamental deficiency: optimization of individual response parameters fails to ensure consis - tency across other critical structural behaviors, resulting in parameter estimates with limited adaptability and reliability. To overcome the above limitations, researchers have pursued multi-objective optimization methods to enable comprehensive param - eter identification strategies. Huang et al. [16,17] developed a bi-objective optimization framework incorporating both cable preten - sion forces and tower-deck deflections, whereas Peng et al. [18] formulated multiple objective functions encompassing static displace - ments and modal frequencies. These multi-objective approaches have demonstrated enhanced reliability through simultaneous consideration of multiple influential parameters [19 – 21] . However, they inevitably confront the curse of dimensionality, wherein exponentially increasing computational demands and conflicting objective functions significantly impede convergence efficiency and parameter estimation robustness. In response to these computational bottlenecks, data-driven methods have been introduced as practical alternatives. Wu et al. [22] developed FE-based Kriging surrogate models for parameter identification, Qiu et al. [23] proposed two-phase model updating strategies integrating multi-objective optimization with elimination and choice expressing reality III (ELECTRE-III) decision-making frameworks, and Xu et al. [24] implemented enhanced particle swarm optimization algorithms for vi - bration control objectives. Despite these advances, computational effi - ciency improvements come at the cost of vulnerability to measurement noises and uncertainties that are ubiquitous in real-world bridge moni - toring systems [25 – 27] . Collectively, these evolutionary developments reveal three persistent fundamental challenges: (1) high-dimensional parameter spaces (often exceeding 100 parameters) creating computational inefficiency and overfitting risks; (2) irregular, non-convex Pareto fronts from simulta - neous multi-objective matching requirements that conventional algo - rithms struggle to navigate; and (3) measurement uncertainties in field monitoring data that significantly compromise identification accuracy under realistic conditions. Addressing these challenges requires innovative optimization frameworks that transcend conventional limitations. Recent research reveals promising pathways via integrating reference-point based mechanisms [28,29] for maintaining solution diversity across irregular Pareto fronts, sensitivity analysis techniques [30] for efficient high-dimensional exploration, and hybrid optimization approaches [31, 32] for enhanced robustness. While conventional algorithms such as the Non-dominated Sorting Genetic Algorithm II (NSGA-II) [33,34] and Multi-Objective Evolutionary Algorithm based on Decomposition (MOEA/D) [35] exhibit limitations in high-dimensional scenarios [36] , the emergence of NSGA-III [37] with its reference-point mechanism represents a significant advancement for multi-objective optimization tasks. This transformative potential is evidenced by successful applica - tions across diverse engineering domains: Hernandez et al. [31] ach - ieved superior power transformer optimization through hybrid methods; Zhang et al. [32] overcame complex hydraulic parameter constraints; Sun et al. [30] integrated sensitivity analysis for high-dimensional motor optimization; and Li et al. [38] demonstrated advantages in structural bridge optimization compared with conventional algorithms [39] . The synergistic integration of these advanced techniques offers un - precedented capabilities: reference-point mechanisms employed in NSGA-III [28,29,37] ensure uniform exploration of non-convex Pareto fronts; sensitivity-informed approaches [30] conquer dimensionality curses by focusing on influential parameters while maintaining system fidelity; hybrid local-global strategies [31,32] provide robustness under realistic noise conditions through combined exploration and refinement. However, integrating these techniques into a cohesive framework spe - cifically tailored for cable-stayed bridge parameter identification re - mains an open challenge.
