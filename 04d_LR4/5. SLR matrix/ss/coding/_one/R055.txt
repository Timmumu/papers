Existing FL-HPO approaches (e.g., weight sharing and server-only selection) either incur iterative client-server loops or underutilize client heterogeneity, thereby raising privacy and governance concerns. We propose an asynchronous, one-shot local Bayesian optimization on each client using a small data subset, followed by loss-weighted aggregation of client-proposed hyperparameters on the server to produce a single global set for collaborative training. The pipeline is training-method agnostic and integrated into a Digital Twin (DT) platform for device registration, orchestration, and telemetry. Across public datasets (MNIST, EEGEyeState, Weather) and a private worker-safety dataset, our approach improves convergence and accuracy/MAE relative to simple averaging and reduces response time on Raspberry Pi clients, yielding an average improvement of 6.44% in end-to-end performance while remaining deployable on constrained AIoT hardware. In safety prediction, faster convergence and lower error enable earlier and more reliable hazard indicators, with on-device inference supporting near–real-time alerts to supervisors and wearables. We also discuss scalability, privacy of shared hyperparameters, and task complexity as directions for future work in safety-critical FL. Effective FL-HPO for safety must therefore minimize rounds, protect privacy, and remain robust under heterogeneous devices and data [46]. This creates a practical gap for safety-critical, resource-limited deployments. This study focuses on continuous safety-index prediction (site- and worker-level risk scoring) rather than on Personal Protective Equipment (PPE) compliance checks or unsafe behavior detection. While HPO is well explored in centralized settings, it is challenging in FL, where data remain distributed across clients [27]. Exhaustive or iterative HPO can be resource- intensive due to repeated training and communication [ 61]. Simple, parallelizable strategies (e.g., random search) are therefore strong baselines in FL [35], aligning with practical constraints in emerging applications (e.g., transportation). Related work in FL spans algorithmic advances, heterogene- ity handling, personalization, fairness, systems, and privacy [62]. However, comparatively fewer studies focus squarely on FL-HPO [ 25]. Federated Bayesian Optimization (FBO) [13] leverages cross-client information to improve a single participant, rather than yielding global hyperparameters for a collaborative model. Other efforts adjust specific hyper - parameters such as the learning rate in SGD-based FL [42]. FedEx draws on NAS weight-sharing to guide hyperparam- eter selection under successive halving [ 28], and FLoRa explores single-shot, server-side HPO [65]. Limitations of existing FL-HPO: Current FL-HPO approaches leave three practical gaps for safety-critical, edge deployments. First, many rely on iterative, multi- round tuning (e.g., Bayesian optimization loops or suc - cessive halving), which increases client–server traffic and latency. Second, weight-sharing/NAS-style methods (e.g., FedEx) often assume SGD-centric pipelines and exchange model artifacts that may be undesirable for privacy/gover - nance. Third, server-only single-shot schemes (e.g., FLoRa) reduce communication but can neglect client-side heteroge- neity during proposal, yielding suboptimal global settings under non-IID data. These gaps motivate a design that (i) performs lightweight client-local search once, (ii) aggre - gates proposals without exposing model weights, and (iii) remains optimizer-agnostic and heterogeneity-aware. We next categorize prior approaches to situate our method in context. We group prior work into four categories. (A) Cen- tralized HPO (grid, random, BO, evolutionary; multi-fidelity Hyperband/BOHB). Highly effective with full data access, but their iterative evaluations become impractical under FL’s communication and privacy constraints. (B) FL with NAS/weight sharing (e.g., FedEx and derivatives). Reduce search cost by sharing weights/architectures, yet typi - cally assume SGD-centric training and require exchanging model artifacts, which elevates privacy/governance risk and device overhead. (C) Adaptive/learning-based HPO for FL (RL, meta-learning, per-client tuning, clustering, encrypted aggregation). These approaches tailor hyperparameters to clients or rounds, but often retain multi-round communica - tion and, in many cases, rely on model access or heavier control logic that strains edge devices. (D) Single-shot or server-focused HPO for FL (e.g., FLoRa). Cuts communica- tion by collapsing to server-side selection; however, it can underuse client-side heterogeneity during proposal and may inherit privacy concerns from shared hyperparameters. In contrast to these, our approach is training-method agnostic (applicable to SGD and non-SGD) and aims to minimize communication and on-device compute by con - solidating client proposals into a single global hyperparam- eter set. We propose asynchronous, one-shot local Bayesian optimization on each client using a small local subset, and a loss-weighted, Top-K aggregation at the server to produce one global configuration without sharing model weights.
