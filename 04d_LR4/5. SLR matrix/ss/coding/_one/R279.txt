To address the low level of automation and insufficient adaption of the flight path in response to environmental obstacles, a method of automatic planning UAV inspection mission based on the Geometric Digital Twin (GDT) model and Voxelized Obstacle Information (VOI) is proposed. First, a method for shifting the Field of View (FOV) centroids in parallel is proposed to efficiently generate inspection waypoints. Second, a waypoints adjustment method based on environmental VOI of 3D point clouds is proposed to address the safety issues. Third, a method combining Genetic Algorithm (GA) with A* based on VOI is proposed for optimizing UAV flight path to avoid real-world obstacles. The feasibility of the proposed methods was verified in both an office building and a steel truss bridge. Compared to existing methods, the efficiency is significantly improved. 1. Introduction Periodic structural surfaces inspection is crucial for ensuring the structural safety of large-scale infrastructures such as buildings and bridges. Traditionally, such inspection has been largely conducted manually, which is time consuming and prone to errors. More recently, due to the agility and efficiency of Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs) in capturing surface images, some studies have begun to apply UAV tech- nology for structural surface inspection and shown promising results [ 1 – 3 ]. This process typically involves orienting the UAV camera towards the target structure where it captures inspection images of the visible coverage area on the structural surface, which is defined as the Field of View (FOV). During the process of using UAVs to fly around structures and acquire inspection image data, three main control indicators are emphasized [ 4 – 6 ]: (1) safety, to ensure that UAVs do not collide with obstacles; (2) comprehensiveness, to acquire complete images of the whole surface to avoid missing inspection; (3) efficiency, to avoid un- necessary flight burdens. When using traditional manual UAV flight, such multiple and possibly conflicting goals can be challenging to be achieved simultaneously, especially for large-scale and complex struc- tures [ 7 – 9 ]. To tackle this issue, some researchers have comprehensively reviewed and studied the pre-planning of rational UAV flight missions to enable automatic execution [ 10 – 12 ]. Compared to the manual control method, it has been shown that pre-planning can significantly enhance the safety and efficiency of the inspection [ 6 ]. For an effective pre- planning of flight mission, the first step is to generate suitable struc- tural inspection waypoints, which are the specified location coordinates where a UAV hovers and acquires images. Properly choosing of waypoints is crucial for the complete while economical covering of the structural surface. Naturally, a reference model to the real structure is necessary for determining the distribution of the waypoints. Such a reference model can be classified into five levels of detail (LoD) according to the Open Geospatial Consortium (OGC) standard for City Geography Markup Language (GML) 2.0 [ 13 ]. A building example is used to illustrate the different levels from LoD0 to LoD4, as shown in Fig. 1 . The LoD0 model [ 14 – 16 ] represents the structural projection on a 2D plane, which is typically acquired using footprint extraction methods [ 17 , 18 ]. Such a simple model can be used for the generation of waypoints that focus on the acquisition of rough top view images. To generate waypoints that focus on the acquisition of both rough facade and top view images, the LoD1 model [ 19 ], which is a block model derived by extending the LoD0 model vertically, can be used. To deal with this, the LoD2 model, in which the ac- curate geometric shape of the structure is represented, can be used. With openings in the structure, such as doors and windows, the LoD2 model can be further upgraded to LoD3 [ 21 ]. By further including details of the structural interior, the LoD4 model can be created [ 6 ]. From their characteristics it can be seen that it is challenging to use LoD0 and LoD1 models for effective structural surface inspection, while LoD2, LoD3 and LoD4 can serve as better reference models. Previously, some studies have generated waypoints based on models constructed by design drawings defined under LoD3, such as manually constructed Building Information Modeling (BIM) [ 22 – 25 ] and 3D geometric model [ 26 , 27 ]. While these models can serve as effective reference models can be automatically generated through methods such as deep learning [ 28 ] and progressive model-driven approach [ 29 ], their openings and inter- nal details are not essential information for external surface inspection waypoint generation. Moreover, the large amount of training data and modeling complexity required do hinder the automatic construction of the efficient reference models. Thus, it can be concluded that the LoD2 model is the most suitable choice for the task of external surface inspection. Some studies have utilized 3D point clouds to extract the geometric shape of the structure [ 4 , 30 – 32 ], voxelized it or adapt observation planes to the geometry of the scene for waypoints generation and iter- atively optimizing the number and positions of waypoints based on data quality [ 33 ]. The 3D point clouds can be produced by depth devices [ 34 , 35 ] and multi-view reconstruction [ 36 ], and extensive research has been conducted on the methods of point clouds generation [ 37 – 39 ] and factors affecting the quality of point clouds [ 11 , 12 , 40 ]. Processing 3D point clouds and voxelized models can effectively generate inspection waypoints. However, due to the inclusion of significant non-structural components and surrounding environments, it becomes challenging to focus on the structural surface. Additionally, since these methods involve a large amount of discrete data, they tend to be less accurate and efficient compared to waypoints generation using LoD2 models. Several studies have addressed waypoints generation based on reference models. Tan et al. [ 22 ] proposed a “ three-steps ” region divi- sion method. First, the polygonal surface of the building is divided into several rectangular regions, with the corner point of each rectangular region serving as initial point to create the first rectangle primitive. These primitives are then translated until them cover all rectangular regions on the surface, and the offset of each primitive ’ s center point is extracted to generate inspection waypoints for the surface. Additionally, by pre-dividing the plane into grids, a polygonal surface can be directly divided in a “ two-steps ” process. Yang et al. [ 41 ] used UAVs to collect overhead inspection images of debris fans, manually inputting the boundary information of the debris fan into the pre-divided grid and generating waypoints based on the grid ’ s center points, effectively covering the irregularly shaped debris fan. Yu et al. [ 15 ] also conducted research on the generation of overhead waypoints, using a four- neighborhood growing method to determine the areas to be covered and dividing the grid to generate waypoints. However, further research is needed on these methods to adapt them to the “ two-steps ” division of 3D polygonal surfaces of GDT model, thereby improving the efficiency of waypoints generation.
