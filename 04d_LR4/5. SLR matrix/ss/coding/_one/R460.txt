This study in - troduces a digital twin five-dimensional model framework specifically tailored for architectural heritage conservation, designed to facilitate a shift from reactive to proactive conservation strategies. Incorporating dimensions of physical entity, digital twin, twin data, service, and connection relationships, this framework enables real-time situational awareness and hyper-real- time virtual extrapolation. Through the reconstruction of the historical building information model and the development of both a structural linkage rule model and a displacement trend model based on mechanical properties, the framework offers a comprehensive digital represen - tation from geometric, physical, behavioral, and rule-based perspectives. Validated through a prototype system at the Yingxian Wooden Pagoda in China, a site susceptible to natural and anthropogenic hazards, the findings demonstrate that the digital twin effectively predicts and adapts to spatio-temporal dynamic changes in the pagoda ’ s structure, enhancing resilience and promoting sustainable heritage conservation. This research supports disaster risk reduction strategies, contributing to vulnerability analysis and resilience in heritage conservation amid increasing threats from climate change and other emergent risks. 1. Introduction As a non-renewable cultural resource, architectural heritage documents the architectural techniques and creative styles of many periods, embodying the cultural implications of social structures, religious beliefs, and lifestyles [ 1 ]. Nevertheless, due to variables such as material degradation and human intervention, some architectural historic sites are already compromised [ 2 , 3 ]. Consequently, transitioning conservation efforts from passive salvage restoration to proactive preventive maintenance and intervening prior to the onset of threats is essential [ 6 – 8 ]. Digital twin technology [ 9 ] offers a novel approach to precisely understanding architectural history by creating a virtual model that represents an entity ’ s complete life cycle state using data and model-driven simulation, monitoring, prediction, and optimization [ 10 , 11 ]. It can assess the condition of historical structures in real-time using multi-source heterogeneous data and execute hyper-real-time extrapolation, thereby enhancing building health management and minimizing human intervention [ 12 ]. The digital twin concept was initially introduced by Michael Grieves in 2003, characterized as a three-dimensional representation of entities, voxels, and their informational interconnections [ 13 ]. Subsequently, Negri et al. expanded this concept, highlighting the real-time connection between physical and virtual systems via data synchronization [ 14 ]. As research advances, the three-dimensional model is increasingly inadequate to fulfil complicated requirements. Tao Fei and colleagues developed a five-dimensional architecture encompassing physical entities, virtual entities, twin services, twin data, and connectivity linkages [ 15 ]. This design has significantly advanced the utilization of digital twin technologies across various study domains. Although digital twin technology has shown unique advantages in many fields, its application in architectural heritage conser - vation is still in the exploratory stage, especially in preventive conservation, and the theoretical construction and empirical analysis have not been systematized yet. Vuoto et al. explored its potential in conserving the structural integrity of architectural heritage but lacked the verification of its practical application [ 16 ].Tan et al. combined laser scanning with tilt photogrammetry tools for archi - tectural archaeology, but this approach is limited to specific heritage types [ 17 ]. There is no universal method for building digital twins of architectural heritage. Digital twins, as virtual entities, are digital representations of physical entities for understanding, predicting, optimizing and controlling natural objects. The universality of its construction method directly affects the application effect of the digital twin so - lution. In the face of complex building structures and variable environments, how to construct pervasive digital twins to realize real- time monitoring and early warning is a vital issue in current research [ 18 ]. Bordeleau et al. explored various model-driven engineering techniques used in the model building process, identified as potentially overcoming the challenges associated with digital twin modeling [ 19 ]. Concurrently, Rasheed et al. provide a comprehensive overview of the methods and techniques currently applied in constructing digital twin models [ 20 ]. They discuss the challenges encountered during the construction process in detail, offering specific recommendations and insights from the authors. Funari et al. proposed a parametric Scan-to-FEM framework and validated the application of this method in historical masonry structures through the S ˜ao Tocato Church in Portugal. However, the applicability to complex or irregular structures is limited [ 21 ]. Jouan et al. proposed a digital twin construction method based on the Historical Building Information Model (HBIM) to integrate multidimensional data to monitor changes and propose solutions [ 22 ]. The model (HBIM) digital twin construction method integrates multidimensional data to monitor changes and propose solutions. HBIM can accurately characterize complex geometrical structures and physical attributes of architectural heritage by integrating time di - mensions and multilevel information [ 23 ]. However, most current HBIM applications only serve as static models, failing to capture the dynamic processes of architectural change over time and multifactors and achieving proper preventive conservation. Colucci et al. investigated the interoperability of HBIM with 3D GIS, but their aim was limited to providing more comprehensive data documen - tation for heritage conservation [ 24 ]. This work aims to thoroughly investigate the methods for generating digital twins of architectural heritage utilizing the HBIM model, focusing on the four essential characteristics of "geometry-physics-behavior-rule." This will encompass the intricacies of architectural heritage in all dimensions, including structural modifications, physical conditions, behavioral responses, and adherence to regulations. Consequently, genuine preventive protection can be achieved, offering a novel perspective for architectural asset health monitoring and life cycle forecasting. 2. Methodlogy 2.1. Digital twin five-dimensional model The digital twin model of architectural heritage is an extensive data-driven framework designed to integrate the multi-physical, multi-scale, and multi-probability simulation processes throughout the entire life cycle of architectural heritage. It aims to compre - hensively capture data regarding the dynamic changes of architectural heritage over time and under various influences through multi- level real-time/quasi-real-time situational awareness and ultra-real-time virtual extrapolation, facilitating the transition from salvage to preventive conservation. Architectural heritage preventive conservation is a critical concern based on the notions above.
