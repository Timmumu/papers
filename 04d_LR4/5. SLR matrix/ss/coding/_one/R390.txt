Cured-in-place pipe (CIPP) lining is an emerging trenchless technology used to rehabilitate deteriorated pipes. However, the buckling behavior of CIPP liner-rehabilitated pipes is insufficiently investigated. This study presents an integrated framework for real-time structural health monitoring and damage detection for underground pipelines by leveraging the knowledge of distributed fiber optic sensors (DFOS), finite element analysis (FEA), deep learning (DL) algorithms, and digital twin. The study experimentally and computationally investigates the structural behavior of a single steel substrate and a CIPP-rehabilitated steel substrate under eccentric compression con - ditions. The application of DFOS enables the self-sensing function of the pipelines with high-resolution, continuous strain measurement. The finite element model and DL-based strain field correlation are employed to complement the function of DFOS from strain field monitoring to structural deformation assessment, facili - tating the development of virtual replicates of self-sensed pipes. The study further explores the location and severity of structural damage through numerical and theoretical strain analysis, enabling the detection of minor but critical defects. The developed digital twin framework enables real-time structural visualization and en - hances structural damage assessment. By accurately identifying the early-stage plastic region of deformation and quantifying crack severity, the framework supports predictive maintenance and improves pipeline reliability. This proactive approach improves the safety of underground pipelines, extends their service life, and reduces maintenance costs, demonstrating its practical potential for long-term pipeline infrastructure management. 1. Introduction Underground pipeline networks form the backbone of modern urban infrastructure, enabling the efficient transportation of water, gas, sewage, and various industrial fluids [1] . The underground pipeline systems are essential to economic growth, public health, and environ - mental protection. However, with the progression of time, underground pipelines become increasingly susceptible to deterioration due to ma - terial degradation, external loads, corrosion, and soil-structure in - teractions [2] . The failure of underground pipelines can result in severe economic losses, environmental contamination, service disruptions, and even loss of life. Consequently, ensuring the long-term structural integrity of underground pipelines has become a critical challenge in civil and infrastructure applications. Conventional methods, such as replacing deteriorated pipelines, often result in substantial inconvenience to local residents and industrial facilities due to service interruption. In contrast to conventional methods, various trenchless technologies have been developed to restore the function of pipelines while minimizing excavation and associated disruptions [3] . Among them, the cured-in-place pipe (CIPP) lining method has gained wide - spread recognition for its adaptability to different pipe materials and sizes, cost-effectiveness, and capacity to restore the structural integrity of deteriorated pipes [4] . The CIPP lining method involves inserting a resin-based liner mate - rial into an underground pipe. After being cured in place appropriately, the liner effectively forms a new structural layer within the host pipe [5] . Enabled by the enhanced structural integrity provided by the CIPP liner, this method enhances pipeline durability, improves hydraulic ef - ficiency, and extends the service life of the liner-rehabilitated pipe sys - tem. Common damage mechanisms include cracking, delamination, and deformation due to internal or external interferences, with buckling being one of the most critical failure modes [6–8] . Buckling in underground pipelines, whether orig - inal or rehabilitated with a liner, can be caused by soil loads, ground - water pressure, traffic-induced stresses, or vacuum-induced forces [9–12] . Since such failures often occur suddenly and can lead to critical structural instability, early detection and mitigation are essential for ensuring the pipeline’s reliability. Prior studies have evaluated the structural performance of CIPP liners through experimental testing and analytical or numerical modeling, including investigations of ovaliza - tion, local buckling instability, liner–host pipe interaction, and critical capacity [13–16] . These studies have improved the understanding of the progression of buckling from initial imperfection to post-buckling deformation in liner-rehabilitated pipes. However, existing in - vestigations generally rely on visual inspection or discrete sensors, which are unable to capture the distributed strain evolution that governs buckling initiation. Therefore, real-time tracking of buckling behavior and deterioration in liner-rehabilitated systems remains an open chal - lenge, due to the inaccessibility of pipelines and the complexity of their operating environment. Structural health monitoring (SHM) is an approach to assess the condition of critical infrastructure, providing valuable insights that can help detect early signs of damage and prevent catastrophic failures [17–19] . However, traditional SHM techniques face significant chal - lenges when applied to underground pipelines. Conventional point-based sensors, such as strain gauges or fiber Bragg grating (FBG) sensors, are difficult to deploy in underground environments and offer limited spatial coverage. Therefore, these sensors are not suitable for monitoring underground pipelines, where the locations of potential failures are unpredictable. To address these challenges, distributed fiber optic sensing (DFOS) has emerged as a promising solution to monitor the structural health condition of underground pipelines in real-time [20] . Unlike conventional sensors, DFOS enables spatially continuous strain measurements along the entire length of the optic fiber. This continuous coverage allows for real-time strain monitoring over a large area. Anomalous strain signals are indicative of structural irregularities, enabling both damage identification and localization. As a result, DFOS facilitates the timely detection of localized damage, enables the assess - ment of overall strain distribution, and monitors structural performance with higher resolution and accuracy. Additionally, DFOS offers several advantages, including its ability to work in harsh environments, its high sensitivity to strain variations, and its long-term stability, making it an ideal choice for underground applications. Recent studies have employed DFOS as a strain monitoring tool for various underground applications, including fractured steel pipelines subjected to operational pressure [21] , polyvinyl chloride liners under grouting pressure [22] , and corrugated steel culverts under traffic loading [23] . These previous studies demonstrate the capability of DFOS to provide continuous strain monitoring in underground structures, establishing a foundation for its effective integration into real-time SHM applications. The effectiveness of DFOS relies on its underlying principle of analyzing backscattered light along the optical fiber. In an optical distributed sensor interrogator, a laser source with a continuously varying frequency is injected into the fiber, and the backscattered Rayleigh signal is analyzed. Since the Rayleigh scattering pattern is unique to each segment of the fiber, any mechanical strain changes the local optical path, resulting in a shift in the backscattered signal. By comparing these shifts with the reference state, highly precise distributed strain measurements can be obtained at sub-millimeter-scale spatial resolution (SR). This technique makes Rayleigh-based DFOS particularly suitable for detecting fine-scale strain variations and tracking minor defect initiation and propagation. As a result, it is highly promising for SHM applications of underground pipes, where direct visual detection is often infeasible. DFOS provides a powerful tool for monitoring the structural health condition of underground pipelines. However, it is unrealistic to have DFOS fully cover the entire pipe due to the high cost of fiber optic sensors. Additionally, raw sensing data alone may not fully capture the complexities of pipeline behavior under real-world conditions. There - fore, in practical deployment, DFOS is installed on instrumented seg - ments that terminate at accessible interrogation points.
