modeling Continual surrogate learning Uncertainty quantification ABSTRACT Rockfill dams exhibit pronounced
stage-dependent deformation during construction, impoundment, and long- term operation, requiring monitoring-
updated deformation models for lifecycle prediction and digital twin- based safety assessment. However,
existing updating methods are often deterministic and stage-isolated, while conventional probabilistic
approaches insufficiently capture dependence among mechanically related constitu - tive parameters. For multi-
stage lifecycle analysis, these methods also lack cross-stage adaptability and often require repeated finite-
element simulations or full surrogate retraining, leading to high computational cost. This study proposes a
dependence-aware sequential probabilistic framework with posterior-guided continual surro - gate learning for
dynamic updating of rockfill dam deformation models. The framework progressively assimilates monitoring data
to track lifecycle parameter evolution, preserves empirical parameter dependence via Copula- enhanced prior
modeling within a sequential Bayesian updating scheme, and adaptively refines the surrogate by concentrating
new training samples in posterior-relevant regions while retaining historical deformation knowledge.
Application to the 303 m Lianghekou core-wall rockfill dam shows that the proposed method reduces the global
relative settlement prediction error from 37.4% to 9.7% and reduces the dominant computational cost of
lifecycle surrogate updating by 45.8% compared with full surrogate retraining. The updated model further
enables uncertainty-informed settlement prediction, spatiotemporal deformation reconstruction, and posterior-
informed diagnosis of safety-related deformation characteristics, providing an interpretable and efficient
approach for lifecycle deformation analysis and digital twin-based dam safety management. 1. Introduction
Rockfill dams are widely used in large hydraulic projects because of their adaptability to complex geological
conditions, use of locally available materials, and favorable seismic performance ( Ma and Chi, 2016a ; Zhang
et al., 2026 ). As large granular structures, their defor - mation behavior evolves markedly during staged
construction, reservoir impoundment, and long-term operation ( Sun et al., 2026 ; Yang et al., 2025 ).
Settlement is mainly governed by self-weight loading and compaction during construction, while wetting-induced
deformation, hydraulic loading, stress redistribution, and creep become increasingly important after
impoundment ( Fig. 1 ). For tall rockfill dams, such lifecycle-dependent deformation is closely related to
structural safety, seepage stability, and operational reliability, making reliable deforma - tion assessment
essential for dam safety management. Field monitoring provides direct information on the in-situ defor -
mation response of rockfill dams, but monitoring data are usually available only at discrete locations and
cannot directly characterize the full-field mechanical state. Finite element (FE)-based deformation analysis
provides a physics-based bridge between monitoring observa - tions and continuous deformation fields, making

intelligence, civil infrastructure is evolving toward intel - ligent operation supported by digital twin (DT)
technology ( Nav et al., 2025 ; Saif et al., 2024 ). For rockfill dams, a practical DT is not merely a static
replica or visualization tool, but an updatable computational model that can assimilate monitoring data,
evolve with the physical dam, and support lifecycle performance assessment. Existing monitoring-driven
deformation model updating methods have improved dam behavior interpretation and deformation predic - tion,
but several challenges remain for lifecycle applications. First, many back-analysis methods are deterministic
and stage-specific, identifying a single optimal parameter set for a given period while providing limited
information on parameter uncertainty and prediction reliability ( Ai et al., 2024 ; Li et al., 2024 ). Because
rockfill materials are inherently variable and monitoring data are limited and uncertain, uncertainty
quantification is important for assessing model confidence and sup - porting risk-informed decisions ( Chen et
al., 2025 ; Dai et al., 2025 ). Second, conventional probabilistic updating often assumes independent prior
parameters for simplicity ( Li et al., 2024 ; Zeng et al., 2024 ), although rockfill constitutive parameters
related to stiffness, volumetric deformation, strength, and nonlinear response are commonly correlated because
of shared material properties, compaction conditions, stress states, and testing procedures ( Ai et al., 2025
; Yang et al., 2025 ). Neglecting such dependence may reduce the interpretability of posterior parameter
evolution, especially when different parameter combinations can reproduce similar settlement responses ( Huang
et al., 2026 ). Third, lifecycle updating requires repeated model evaluations as new moni - toring data become
available and deformation mechanisms evolve. Direct FE-based Bayesian inference is computationally expensive,
while conventional surrogate-assisted approaches are usually developed for a single analysis stage or fixed
parameter domain ( Yu et al., 2025 ; Zhang et al., 2026 ). A surrogate trained in earlier stages may lose
accuracy in later stages, whereas full retraining requires additional FE samples and increases cumulative
computational cost. Continual learning provides a potential solution by allowing the surrogate to incorporate
new-stage information while retaining previously learned knowledge ( Delange et al., 2021 ; Kirkpatrick et
al., 2017 ; Van De Ven et al., 2022 ). To address these issues, this study develops a dependence-aware
sequential probabilistic framework with posterior-guided continual surrogate learning for lifecycle
deformation model updating of rockfill dams. The proposed framework treats constitutive parameters as stage-
wise effective material states and progressively updates their posterior distributions as monitoring data are
assimilated. A Gaussian Copula- based dependence model is embedded into the posterior-to-prior prop - agation
process, allowing posterior-informed marginal distributions from the previous lifecycle stage to be
reconnected through an empirical dependence structure. To improve computational feasibility, a deep neural
network surrogate is continually refined using posterior-guided FE samples, representative historical replay
samples, and Fisher- information-weighted regularization. This strategy enables the surro - gate to adapt to
current-stage deformation characteristics while retain - ing historically learned response mappings. The main
