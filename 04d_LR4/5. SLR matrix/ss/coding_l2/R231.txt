Generative adversarial network Unsupervised deep learning Damage detection ABSTRACT The progressive
degradation of bridge infrastructure poses significant risks to transportation safety, necessitating advanced
monitoring and management strategies. Digital Twins (DTs) have emerged as a transformative tech - nology for
real-time structural monitoring, offering a dynamic and data-driven representation of physical assets. When
combined with Deep Learning (DL), DTs enhance predictive capabilities by leveraging vast amounts of sensor
data to detect structural anomalies, optimize maintenance strategies, and improve decision-making processes.
This synergy enables a more accurate, automated, and cost-effective approach to infrastructure health
monitoring, ensuring timely interventions and prolonged service life. This paper presents a novel data- driven
DT framework for real-time bridge damage detection, incorporating two key innovations. First, a Multi-Domain
Fusion Autoencoder (MDFA) that integrates raw acceleration data with feature fusion from time, frequency, and
wavelet domains. Second, a synthetic anomaly simulation approach for evaluation. The frame - work employs a
transformer-based Generative Adversarial Network (GAN) to generate realistic synthetic signals, which are
modified to simulate various damage scenarios. Evaluations on Australia ’ s Werrington Bridge demonstrate the
MDFA ’ s ability to detect subtle anomalies often overlooked by conventional unsupervised DL methods. A
comparative analysis confirms its superior detection capabilities. Further validation on Austria ’ s S101
Bridge using real damage data successfully identifies and localizes all damage scenarios without requiring
additional optimization. These results highlight the efficiency of feature fusion techniques in improving
anomaly detection accuracy and suggest that synthetic anomaly simulation offers a cost-effective alternative
to laboratory testing for evaluating unsupervised damage detection approaches. 1. Introduction Bridge
infrastructures, as a critical component of the transportation network, are experiencing significant
deterioration due to increasing traffic volume and service load, ageing, and the impacts of aggressive
environmental factors associated with climate change. The progressive deterioration of bridge elements results
in extensive damage, leading to significantly increased repair or replacement costs. If left unaddressed, it
may ultimately cause catastrophic structural failure, resulting in severe economic losses and, tragically, the
loss of human lives. Therefore, the proper inspection and maintenance of bridge infrastructure through
Structural Health Monitoring (SHM) approaches have become increas - ingly important for ensuring structural
reliability and safety [ 1 – 3 ]. SHM systems play a crucial role in ensuring the safety and reliability of
bridge infrastructures by detecting damage and evaluating structural conditions. SHM approaches can be broadly
categorized into model- driven and data-driven methods. Model-driven approaches rely on physics-based models
and finite element analysis to predict structural behaviour, while data-driven methods leverage sensor data
and statis - tical techniques to identify patterns indicative of deterioration [ 4 , 5 ]. The increasing
complexity of structural systems and the vast amount of monitoring data have led to a growing reliance on
data-driven methods, particularly those incorporating Machine Learning (ML) techniques. ML techniques have
significantly transformed SHM by enhancing the ability to analyse large-scale monitoring data and have become

practical applicability to real-world cases remains limited [ 9 , 10 ]. In contrast, unsupervised learning
provides a flexible and adaptive method for extracting valuable insights, as it operates on unlabelled data.
Its ability to dynamically adjust to variations in data distributions and detect emerging trends makes it
efficient for real-time monitoring applications. Several research studies have explored unsupervised
techniques for the real time analysis of structural monitoring data. Despite the extensive progress made in
unsupervised damage detection in bridges, the extraction of meaningful results from extensive real-time
measurement data is a significant challenge, which affects the timely assessment of structural conditions and
the implementation of appro - priate remedial measures [ 9 ]. Digital Twins (DTs), as a powerful tool for
online monitoring and predictive maintenance, have gained significant popularity in bridge infrastructure
management. Various approaches, such as big data analysis, ML, and cloud/edge computing can be employed in DT
plat - forms to manage the increasing volume of input data, and facilitate continuous, and ideally real-time,
monitoring, which is crucial in iden - tifying defects or problems at an early stage [ 11 ]. By analysing the
collected data, DTs can identify potential issues within a system, enabling bridge management teams to make
informed predictive maintenance decisions. This enhances reliability, increases efficiency, and optimizes the
use of available resources [ 12 ]. The development of DTs mechanisms within Bridge Management Systems (BMS) in
recent years has provided valuable opportunities to address real time moni - toring challenge, particularly
through advancements in applying ML algorithms within DTs framework [ 13 ]. A DT model serves as a dynamic
virtual representation of physical infrastructure, providing a real-time reflection of its behaviour, current
condition, and performance. This synchronization between the virtual and physical entities is facilitated by
advanced systems with high-speed communication capabilities. These intelligent systems, encompassing advanced
technologies such as Deep Learning (DL) and data analytics, enable computers to execute tasks that usually
demand human intelli - gence. They achieve this by collecting and analysing large volumes of data from IoT-
enabled (Internet of Things) data collection methods and other sensors, to identify patterns in data structure
and make predictions with minimal human oversight [ 14 ]. Data-driven simulation models in DTs are developed
based on the validated data obtained from experiments/measurements on the actual structure and produce a model
to analyse the status of the bridge in real time. In data-driven models, data from the physical bridge is
collected using emerging sensor technologies and IoT devices. This data is then processed using Big Data
analytics and advanced ML techniques, to simulate the exact functionality of the bridge [ 15 ]. Consequently,
bridge DT is equipped with dynamic ML-based data-driven models that continuously updates using real-time data
analysis enabling the damage detection in real time. While significant advancements have been made in
developing of different virtual models within bridge DTs, such as geometric modelling via unmanned aerial
vehicles [ 16 , 17 ] and terrestrial laser scanning [ 18 , 19 ], as well as Finite Element Method (FEM) models
for numerical analysis [ 20 , 21 ], the incorporation of robust data-driven DT frameworks for long-term
