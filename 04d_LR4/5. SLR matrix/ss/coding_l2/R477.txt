konopskiprzemek@gmail.com Abstract This study presents a comparative analysis of six DFS implementations
representing differ- ent maturity levels and investigates the systemic gap between technological capabilities
and regulatory approaches. A structured narrative review with case-based analysis was conducted using the
Scopus database (2015–2026) with six targeted queries. The case selection followed the PICo protocol. An
original ten-criterion DFS maturity assessment rubric—grounded in the Technology Readiness Level (TRL),
Integration Readiness Level (IRL), and Digital Twin Maturity Model frameworks—was applied to all six cases.
Inter- rater validation yielded substantial agreement (κw = 0.797; unweighted κ = 0.674 [95% CI: 0.509,
0.839]). The results indicate a clear maturity gradient (Dimension X: 4–9 points; Dimension Y: 2–8 points).
Benefits reported in the analysed primary studies include up to a 55 s reduction in evacuation time, a 72%
improvement compared with static signage, and a 34-percentage-point increase in evacuation success rate under
simulation-based conditions. Five normative recommendations are proposed to address the structural regulatory
gap between current prescriptive frameworks and DFS deployment in Poland and the EU. This study argues that
prescriptive rules should remain the baseline, whereas complex facilities may adopt performance-based DFS
solutions, provided that equivalence to conventional protection levels is rigorously demonstrated. From a
sustainability perspective, the study frames DFS as a dynamic safety layer that supports occupant protection,
operational re- silience, and lifecycle adaptability in complex buildings exposed to uncertain fire and crowd
conditions. Keywords: sustainability; fire safety engineering; dynamic fire safety systems (DFSs);
performance-based design; smart buildings; digital twin; building information modelling (BIM); internet of

surveillance are expected to operate as a single coherent entity during an emergency. The traditional RBS
paradigm was founded on static fire scenarios developed in the design stage—documents that are rarely revised
after the building is handed over—while the actual behaviour of the building during a fire incident
progressively diverges from the original design assumptions. Building information modelling (BIM) has become
an established design standard; however, once a building is handed over, the BIM model is typically no longer
maintained. This is particularly important because BIM was originally conceived as a lifecycle-oriented
approach that can support planning, design, construction, and facility operations within a single digital
model [1]. This limitation is consistent with the broader literature, which shows that the implementation of
BIM in existing buildings remains constrained by the high effort required for model creation, the difficulty
of updating information, and the un- certainty of available building data [2]. The proliferation of Internet
of Things devices and continuous operational data streams has created the conditions necessary for the concept
of a living Digital Twin (DT)—a model continuously fed by sensor data. Integration of BIM with IoT
technologies enables real-time data exchange between a physical asset and its digital representation, forming
the technological foundation of Digital Twin systems [3]. This trend is consistent with recent review research
showing that IoT-enabled fire safety sys- tems increasingly integrate detection, localisation, and evacuation
functions through sensor networks, real-time data handling, and advanced analytics [4]. Artificial
intelligence adds predictive capability and dynamic evacuation routing. Recent studies have demonstrated that
intelligent evacuation guidance systems can dynamically generate safer and faster routes in large buildings by
combining hazard intensity, congestion data, and distributed intelligent indicators [5]. This observation is
consistent with recent research showing that digital twins can support building safety throughout the
lifecycle by integrating sensors, 3D visualisation, and data analytics for threat detection and monitoring [
6]. Together, these technologies constitute a responsive architecture: a building that actively responds to
hazards rather than passively awaiting the activation of predefined static scenarios. This fragmentation of
systems becomes particularly problematic under emergency conditions, where coordinated and timely decision-
making is critical to ensure occupant safety. The tension between technological capability and regulatory
constraints constitutes a critical and insufficiently addressed gap in contemporary fire safety engineering,
particu- larly in the context of integrating real-time data-driven evacuation systems into existing
prescriptive regulatory frameworks. This gap is particularly consequential in complex and high-occupancy
buildings, where static evacuation assumptions may not reflect dynami- cally evolving fire conditions and
occupant behaviour. The sustainability dimension of this problem should not be reduced to environmental
indicators alone. In complex buildings, sustainable performance also includes the capacity of the built
environment to maintain safe, reliable, and adaptable operation throughout its life cycle. From this
perspective, dynamic fire safety systems contribute to sustainability by transforming static fire safety
provisions into an operational layer capable of responding to changing hazard development, occupant
distribution, and system conditions. Such an approach links human-centred safety, resilience, and adaptive
facility management within a performance-oriented framework for sustainable building operation. 1.2.

solutions. This study aims to demonstrate that DFS solutions are already being tested or deployed in existing

research questions were formulated: (RQ1) How does the sensor → model → decision → actuator chain function in

can DFS solutions be transposed into the Polish legal system? The original contribution of this study is the

context of regulatory constraints. By doing so, the study also positions DFS maturity assessment as a tool for
