DIGITAL TWIN FOR UNDERGROUND STATIONS: IMPROVING DECISION MAKING FOR CONSTRUCTION LIFECYCLE Mengqi Huang1,
Jelena Ninić2, Qianbing Zhang1 1 Monash University, Australia; 2 The University of Nottingham, United Kingdom
ABSTRACT Challenges in the extraction and use of earth resources and spaces are encountered given a growing
worldwide population, rising infrastructures development, and widespread climate change. In Australia, mining
and construction are two major bases for economic growth while both being traditional hazardous and heavy
industries. A nation -wide infrastructure upgrade featuring large -scale underground development is underway,
the geological uncertainties and localisation difficulties of already laid infrastructure are associated with
challenges not seen in building construction. A safer and competent subterranean transport solution is yet
proposed in the context of sustainable developments. In light of this, geotechnical analysis as a fundamental
subject for develo ping and maintaining safe and sustainable use of underground space has huge potential to be
undertaken more intuitively considering the advancements in information management and visualisation. The PhD
work examines the state -of-the-art applications, limitations and future opportunities of Building Information
Modelling (BIM) and other computational techniques in the digitisation of tunnelling and underground
construction. The visualisation and interoperability facilitated by data-driven processes are espec ially
important to underground construction that engages interdisciplinary and multi-environment interaction. 1
INTRODUCTION After decades of development, digital engineering tools, particularly BIM and associated
processes and standards have been regularly used in the architecture, engineering and building (AEC)
industries (DAUB, 2019; Eastman et al., 2011). A key enabler of this transition is how information is handled,
which includes both geometries and properties. Correct use of information and exchange mechanism yields
efficiency that eventually converts to smooth project delivery and cost savings. In Australia, a nation -wide
infrastructure upgrade featuring large -scale underground development is underway, transport sector is
especially vulnerable to risks due to 25% increase of tunnelling projects in the next few years, which leads
to critical issues, majorly on tunnelling and ground movement (Infrastructure Australia, 2021). The research
focuses on improving efficiency and interoperability of cross -disciplinary and cross -phase applications for
station infrastructure, characterised by wide -span, complex structural form, and settlement -sensitive
properties. We propose a multi -module system that coordinates geographic information system (GIS), geological
interpretation, excavation modelling and BIM for a variety of engineering applications in underground
infrastructure projects. With the use case of geotechnical analysis, the multiple Level-Of-Details (LOD) BIM
models feature visual programming-enabled object-oriented modelling (ground, excavation, station parts and
support systems, e.g. rock bolt, lining, column) that are instantiated parametrically, both in geometrical
alterations, and semantic properties (e.g. ground properties), according to specific st ation designs. An
interactive platform that bridges information modelling and numerical modelling via automation to reduce
manual efforts and cost -bearing errors is developed. The platform prototype is essentially underpinned by the
digital twin concept that describe the situation where a physical entity is connected with a virtual
counterpart via data communications. The research is focusing on underground stations as they are important
infrastructures while possessing geometrical complexity and geotechnical challenges during construction.
Underground stations take a variety of forms corresponding to the characteristics of different construction
methods. Commonly used methods include cut-and-cover, full-face tunnelling boring machine (TBM) excavation,
and partial-face New Austrian Tunnelling Method (NATM) and its alternatives. The cut -and-cover method is
often suitable for shallow buried underground stations, and most of the structures are rectangular frame
structures or arch structures. Figure 1a -c show the cross-sectional view of the rectangular frame structure
of several stations, for example, the Beijing Station of Beijing Metro Line 2. The predominant feature of this
construction method is that the temporary retaining wall of the foundation pit and the side wall of the
station structure are combined into one structure. The underground stations constructed by the mining method
can be divided into single-arch stations, double-arch stations and separate stations according to different
geological conditions and usage requirements. Cross sections of a single arch could be consisted of one -,
three, or five -centre circles with smooth transition, and the platforms are often consisted of single-, or
double -column, with single -, or double -span. When space and load bearing criteria are set, single -column.

After understanding the characteristics of underground stations, the key components of the structure could be
established, which are important for developing the BIM -driven modelling process. The technical paper is
organised as follows. Section 2 introduces the methodology and workflow of the research. Section 3 details the
BIM -driven interoperability means developed to facilitate design -to-design efficiency. Section 4 discusses
the future perspectives of the research. Section 5 concludes the paper. The innovations of research present
herein are three -folded. First, we proposed an extension of station related systems in the primary open BIM
data schema – Industry Foundation Classes (IFC). Then, geometry instantiation and property assignment are
automated to facilitate modelling efficiency. Finally, an automatic information exchange mechanism between BIM
and geotechnical analysis was developed and tested on several use cases. The research outputs have been
preliminarily verified using the stations of Melbourne Metro Tunnel, and a wider industry application is
