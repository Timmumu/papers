AIoT Digital twin Fire safety management ABSTRACT High traffic flow in a confined tunnel makes fire safety a
critical issue. This paper proposed a digital twin framework for tunnel fire safety management in real-time,
driven by dynamic sensor data and AIoT technologies. A deep learning model trained by the Transformer network
and simulation dataset is used to predict real-time fire location and size. Then, the AI model is integrated
into a 3D digital twin platform developed by the game engine Unity 3D. The performance of the proposed digital
twin framework is demonstrated using numerical experiments and large-scale tunnel fire tests. Results show
that the established AI model achieved promising accuracy in predicting fire location and power for both
numerical and experimental data. The digital twin platform can also visualize the 3D fire scene that supports
evacuation, firefighting, and emergency rescue. This research demonstrates the feasibility of using a 3D
environment and digital twin in real-time fire safety management. 1. Introduction Driven by economic and urban
development, a large number of tunnels and underground facilities have been built to facilitate trans -
portation. However, the tunnel has an enclosed environment. In a tunnel fire incident, it is difficult for
people to evacuate from the narrow space with a single transferring direction ( Ingason et al., 2015 ). For
example, a tunnel fire occurred in the Shanxi Yanhou tunnel in 2014, 40 people were killed, and 12 people were
injured ( Zhang and Huang, 2022 ). Thus, fire safety management and emergency response for tunnels are
essential. Tunnel fire safety can be improved through several aspects, such as enhancing the safety education
of drivers to minimize traffic accidents, optimizing the fire safety design of tunnels, and improving
emergency response strategies. However, from the principle of safety management, some accident like collision
and fire in a tunnel is inevitable and un - predictable. Thus, to achieve proper fire safety, we need to
design a tunnel that can handle any possible fire scenarios ( Zhang et al., 2019 , 2021a ) and improve
emergency response to minimize fire casualties and damage through advanced technologies ( Ma and Wu, 2020 ;
Wang et al., 2015 ). Such a smart firefighting system can recognize and quantify fire timely and accurately
using neural networks and provide more fire management functions and visualization tools by combining digital
twin technologies ( Huang et al., 2022 ). To improve the fire safety of tunnel and underground space and
handle tunnel fires caused by vehicle collisions, several studies have been done to investigate the
application of the latest information tech - nologies and computational methods in tunnel firefighting. For
example, Wu et al. (2021) demonstrated the possibility of the Artificial Intelli - gence (AI) method to detect
and determine fire sources and hazards. An intelligent tunnel firefighting system based on IoT sensors and AI
methods is further proposed to identify fire scenarios in a real-time manner and demonstrated in a reduced-
scale tunnel model ( Wu et al., 2022a ). Guo et al. (2022) investigated tunnel fire scenario reconstruc - tion
and key fire parameters prediction by integrating tunnel fire in - formation and machine learning methods.

Digital Twin offers a promising solution by providing an executable and dynamic virtual representation of
real-world objects or activities, which reflects the geometrical or semantic properties and behavior patterns
of objects using the latest data communication and modeling techniques ( Qi et al., 2021 ). A digital twin
system consists of several key components, including physical entities, virtual models, services, fusion data,
and connections. These components work together to enable real-time integration and interoperation among
physical en - tities, virtual models, and services through fusion data ( Glaessgen and Stargel, 2012 ). The
framework of a digital twin system comprises five essential components. (1) Physical entities, representing
objects with specific functions within the real-world environment, serve as the foundation for the digital
representation created by the digital twin. (2) Virtual models, the digital equivalents of physical entities,
replicate the geometrical or semantic properties and behavior patterns of their real-world counterparts. (3)
Service layer, encompassing functions such as management, control, and optimization provided for stakeholders.
This enable stakeholders to interact with and utilize the digital twin system. (4) Fusion data, acting as the
core driver of the digital twin, inte - grating information from various sources to provide a compre - hensive
and accurate representation of the real-world environment within the digital twin system. (5) Connection
component, facilitating real-time integration and interoperation among physical entities, virtual models, and
ser - vices through fusion data. This ensures seamless operation of the digital twin system, providing timely
and relevant information to stakeholders. To enhance the fire safety of buildings or infrastructures like
tunnels, integrating digital twins with physical facilities or developing a digital twin system integrated
with current facility management systems is a feasible approach. This integration enables real-time
monitoring, accu - rate prediction, and effective management of fire incidents, ultimately improving overall
fire safety outcomes. However, several challenges persist in designing and developing a digital twin-enabled
safety management system for smart tunnels. Firstly, the deployment of IoT devices necessary for real-time
data collection in digital twin systems entails significant investment, leading to low cost-efficiency,
especially concerning fire emergency manage - ment alone. Addressing this challenge involves expanding the
func - tionalities of the tunnel digital twin system while enhancing its cost- efficiency. Secondly, ensuring
adequate coverage of critical fire infor - mation poses a challenge, particularly in long tunnels where
sensors located far from the fire may not receive sufficient data. Prioritizing sensors closer to the fire
source becomes essential to overcome this challenge effectively. Additionally, current deep learning models
used in fire prediction may not promptly process the limited data collected during fire situations. Developing
and applying new deep learning models to enhance fire information prediction based on the existing sensor
deployment is another pressing issue. Thirdly, previous experi - ments on fire prediction models have
