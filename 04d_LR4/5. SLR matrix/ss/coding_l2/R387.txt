the core nodes of transportation networks, bridges face critical challenges in their Structural Health
Monitoring (SHM) that are crucial for ensuring transportation safety and extending their service life.
Traditional SHM methods encounter high real-time data processing delays, inefficient fusion of multi-source
heterogeneous data, and insufficient synchronization between Digital Twin (DT) models and physical entities.
Existing DT architectures often lack a hierarchical edge-cloud collaboration mechanism, with fusion algorithms
confined to single-modality data, making it difficult to achieve both low latency and high accuracy in anomaly
detection. To address these issues, this study proposes a hierarchical edge-cloud collaborative DT
architecture, encompassing the physical layer, edge layer, cloud layer, DT layer, and application layer. The
core algorithms include: hierarchical multi-sensor fusion; a collaborative anomaly detection framework
combining edge-based attention 1D CNN with cloud-based variational autoencoder plus bidirectional long short-
term memory network; and Bayesian model updating for dynamic synchronization between virtual models and
physical entities. Experimental verification based on a 1:5 scaled steel simply supported beam shows that the
architecture achieves a low edge local task delay of 39.7ms and a cloud global task delay of 20.1ms; after
strain data fusion, the Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE) is reduced by an average of 62%, and the RMSE for
displacement reconstruction is reduced by 68%; the accuracy of anomaly identification reaches over 96%; and
Bayesian updating improves the accuracy of strain prediction by 40.1%. This study is the first to construct a
hierarchical edge-cloud collaborative DT architecture, filling the technical gap in hierarchical fusion and
collaborative computing for bridge SHM. It provides a data- driven intelligent solution for bridge lifecycle
health management and is of great significance for promoting the digital transformation of transportation
infrastructure. Keywords Bridge structural health monitoring, Edge-cloud collaboration, Digital twin, Multi-

8:938 1 Introduction Bridges, as critical nodes in transportation networks, depend on Structural Health Mon -
itoring (SHM) to ensure safety and extend service life. Rising traffic loads and environ - mental erosion
amplify deterioration risks: timber bridges’ lifespan hinges on humidity and material selection; irregular
slab bridges need seismic optimization due to com - plex earthquake-induced mechanical behavior; suspension
bridges face vortex-induced vibration (VIV) threats; foundation scouring requires real-time depth monitoring;
inte - gral bridges’ end displacement is distorted by soil compaction, challenging traditional models.
Traditional SHM methods have limitations: direct monitoring disrupts traffic, indirect methods are affected by
vehicle frequencies and road roughness; bridge weigh - ing-in-motion (BWIM) systems ignore lateral effects
leading to weight errors; long-term monitoring of external prestressed tendons lacks effective tools;
heterogeneous sensor network coverage optimization remains unresolved; data imbalance reduces anomaly
detection accuracy. A systematic review highlights interdisciplinary collaboration as key for efficient
systems but notes persistent challenges: high costs, insufficient standardiza - tion, and multi-source data
handling issues [1]. Digital Twin (DT) technology provides a dynamic, data-driven solution for bridge SHM,
enabling precise monitoring and prediction via real-time physical-virtual inter - action. A data-driven DT
framework combined with deep learning integrates multi- domain features for damage detection, but real-time
virtual response synchronization requires efficient computing engines [ 2]. Geometric DT model
construction—tradi - tionally manual—can be automated using parametric prototypes generated from point cloud-
extracted parameters. Integrating Bridge Information Modeling (BrIM) with Decision Support Systems (DSS)
enhances asset management efficiency. Bayesian model updating with seismic data reduces model uncertainty,
while accurate material charac - terization lays the foundation for virtual model precision. Cross-
disciplinary DT appli - cations also offer insights for data analysis and optimization. Edge-cloud
collaborative architecture is critical to resolving DT computing latency and resource constraints. Traditional
cloud-centric setups suffer from high latency, but edge-cloud collaboration allocates real-time tasks to edge
devices and offloads complex computations to the cloud, enabling low latency, low carbon footprint, and cost-
effective operation [ 3]. This architecture requires addressing data security and privacy: secure
deduplication with blockchain ensures confidentiality; lightweight federated learning aggregates edge data
while protecting privacy [4]; attribute-based keyword search resists guessing attacks for secure sharing. Task
offloading strategies optimize computational task allocation, and cross-domain applications demonstrate edge-
cloud collaboration’s potential in resource optimization and latency reduction [5]. These technologies support
real-time updates and efficient operation of bridge DT systems, balancing computing power, latency, and energy
consumption. Multi-sensor fusion and anomaly detection are core components of edge-cloud col - laborative DT
architectures, addressing data sparsity, noise, and incompleteness. Fusion methods include: Adversarial
Autoencoder (AAE) + CycleWGAN to convert sparse strain data into dense; Multi-Domain Fusion Autoencoder (MDFA)
integrating time/ frequency/wavelet-domain features for higher accuracy; Zero-mean Normalized Cross-
correlation (ZNCC) with weighted data to fuse lateral position info; wavelet analysis + bi- pass technology
for baseline-independent monitoring; hybrid time–frequency analysis Page 3 of 32 Liu Discover Applied Sciences
