infrastructure. Orthotropic steel decks (OSDs) have become the preferred structural form for long-span steel
bridges owing to th eir high strength-to-weight ratio, modular construction efﬁciency, and favorable
structural performance 1. However, welded joints in OSDs are highly susceptible to fatigue damage under
repetitive traf ﬁc loading, posing a serious threat to structural integrity2. Fatigue cracking at critical
connections, particularly deck-rib and diaphragm-rib welds3,4, has emerged as one of the predominant failure
modes in aging infrastructure. Field investigations indicate that steel bridges with over 10 years of service
can develop more than 2000 fatigue cracks per structure. Such extensive damage directly undermines load-
bearing capacity and has become a critical bottleneck for safe and reliable service 5. Conventional structural
health monitoring (SHM) systems focus on global bridge responses such as displacement and vibration6. While
effective for detecting global anomalies, they a re limited in identifying localized fatigue damage in OSDs,
where the initiation sites are spatially unpredictable and temporally uncertain 7. Fixed sensor networks for
full-bridge coverage are often economically infeasible and still fail to ensure complete detection8.
Consequently, engineers still rely heavily on manual inspection for fatigue evaluation. This process requires
temporary scaffolding, visual crack iden- tiﬁcation by trained inspectors, and manual documentation. Within
con- ﬁned steel box girders, such procedures are inefﬁcient, prone to subjective error, and limited in
inspection frequency9. Recent advances in robotics have created new opportunities for auto- mated bridge
inspection. Various robotic platforms have been proposed, including bio-inspired climbing robots 10, unmanned
aerial vehicles 11, ground vehicles12, and magnetic adhesion climbing robots13. Among them, magnetic wheeled
robots are particularly promising for navigating inside steel box girders due to their stability and access to
weld details14.T oe n h a n c e detection accuracy and reduce operator dependence, camera-based inspection has
