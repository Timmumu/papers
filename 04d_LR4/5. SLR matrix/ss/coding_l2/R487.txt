Computer Science, University of Victoria, Victoria, Canada ABSTRACT Recent urban practice has highlighted the
need for safe and efficient pedestrian movement in urban settings. Urban planners and policymakers are tasked
with ensuring that individuals can navigate cities while avoiding crowding and minimizing safety risks. Among
the various proposed solutions to achieve these planning objectives, enhancing public space adaptability to
changing circumstances, such as fluctuations in pedestrian demand and crowding, has garnered significant
interest. However, constrained municipal budgets limit the feasibility of large-scale, capital-intensive
upgrades to pedestrian infrastructure. As a result, there is an urgent need for a short-term, temporary, and
cost-effective strategy to redesign pedestrian public space. This paper introduces a novel tactical urban
planning approach, combining evidence-based urbanism. Using a campus digital twin system as an urban
simulation platform, this study presents a unique evidence-based planning approach to improve pedestrian
spacing on sidewalks by dispersing pedestrians without overly interfering with existing infrastructure. As
interventions within existing pedestrian public spaces, one-way sidewalk systems and building entrance control
are considered. Through the proposed agent-based simulation of the campus digital twin system, the
optimization of their application and the demonstration of its effectiveness are achieved. The outcome of the
simulations verifies that these measures can enhance pedestrian spacing by dispersing them without causing
substantial adverse effects. While these findings stem from experiments conducted on a particular campus,
limiting their generalizability, they still hold significance as they verify the potential for practical
implemen - tation of tactical urban planning concepts utilizing a digital twin system for urban environments.

Providing citizens with a high-quality walking environ - ment is essential in contemporary cities (Gehl 2013
). In public spaces that attract large crowds, effective man - agement of high-density pedestrian flows is
critical for safety, comfort, and the overall performance of public space (Dufour et al. 2025 ; Schneider et
al. 2021 ; Zhang et al. 2023 ). Although several studies have explored different urban planning interventions
for crowding and pedestrian management, there is a noticeable lack of attempts to combine tactical urbanism
with evi - dence-based planning approaches. In this paper, we aim to address this gap by proposing a tactical
urban planning approach to improve pedestrian movement in high-density urban settings, utilizing evidence-
based planning principles and implementing a simulation in a digital twin of a university campus as a case
study. Urban planning and design, which involves techni - cal and political processes aimed at shaping the
physical layout, land use, and built environment of human settlements (Taylor 1998 ), has evolved over time,
giving rise to various innovative approaches. One such trend is tactical urban planning, which is an agile,
low-cost, and scalable method that facilitates rapid implementation and iterative adjustments to urban design.
This tactical approach addresses the limitations and challenges of traditional urban plan - ning that may be
hindered by institutional capacity and complex procedures (Alonso et al. 2018 ). Evidence-based planning, on
the other hand, is an essential aspect of urban planning that ensures inter - ventions are grounded in robust
data and analysis for evaluating alternative and optimizing planning sce - narios, forecasting the effects of
diverse interventions on urban spaces (Alonso et al. 2018 ; Cheshmehzangi 2016 ; Chung and Choo 2011 ; Davis
et al. 2006 ; Moonen et al. 2012 ; Mueller, Klein, and Hof 2018 ). Recently, digital twin technology, a

are the primary infrastructure enabling pedestrian mobility. In commercial districts or public parks, walking
on sidewalks can itself be recreational; however, in most settings sidewalks primarily serve a utilitarian
mobility function (Kang et al. 2017 ; Southworth 2005 ; Yin et al. 2023 ). From this functional perspective,
the effective management of high-density pedestrian flows on side - walks is essential. Such management
ensures comfor - table walking conditions in everyday circumstances and can prevent crowd-related safety
accidents during temporary events (Asadi-Shekari, Moeinaddini, and Shah 2014 ; Still et al. 2020 ). Moreover,
as widely demonstrated during the COVID-19 pandemic, when many cities emphasized physical distancing, high-
density pedestrian management also plays a critical role in reducing transmission risk during infectious-
disease outbreaks (Chu et al. 2020 ; Li, Yin, and Chen 2023 ; Psyllidis et al. 2023 ; Xiao et al. 2022 ). In
this study, among various approaches to high- density pedestrian management, we focus on pedes - trian
spacing. As seen in critical cases such as the strict lockdown policies during the COVID-19 period, redu -
cing the number of pedestrians through demand con - trol can be the most effective measure in emergency
situations. However, this is a very exceptional case. In this study, we discuss ways to control pedestrian den
- sity while accepting the demand for sidewalk use as it is in everyday life. We define this practice as pedes
- trian spacing, which refers to reducing close contact among pedestrians to lower the pedestrian density
experienced by individuals, leading to comfortable and safe walking. Despite numerous studies examining urban
plan - ning interventions for crowding and pedestrian man - agement, research that combines tactical urbanism

structure. We employ evidence-based planning principles and implement a novel simulation at digital twin of a
university campus as a case study to assess the effectiveness of these measures. Considering this context, our
