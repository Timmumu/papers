Accepted: December 2nd, 2025 ABSTRACT Despite the digitalization of architecture, engineering, and
construction, construction-site safety is still limited by fragmented data, non-computable regulations, and
slow decision processes. This paper presents DTSafe-O, a semantic digital twin for safety management that
integrates an ontology formalized in Web Ontology Language 2 (Description Logic profile), guided by Semantic
Web Rule Language and Shapes Constraint Language and connecting Internet of Things sensors, building
information modeling, and regulatory documents. The contribution is twofold: (a) a computable formalization of
safety-critical requirements enabling explainable and verifiable inferences and (b) a validation pipeline
combining reasoning, compliance checking, and performance metrics, supported by replicable materials in a
public repository. Results show that merging digital twins with semantic technologies improves consistency,
timeliness, and auditability of safety decisions, laying the ground for scalable industrial adoption. The
study also discusses limitations and opportunities for extending the approach to infrastructure domains and
advancing spatiotemporal reasoning and uncertainty management. KEYWORDS Digital Twin, Building Information
Modeling, Internet of Things, Ontology, Construction Safety Web Ontology Language INTRODUCTION Safety on
construction sites represents a primary challenge for the construction industry, marked by elevated worker
risks and considerable repercussions of accidents on project timetables, costs, and reputation.
Notwithstanding substantial investments in training, equipment, and safety protocols, the 2 International
Journal on Semantic Web and Information Systems Volume 21 • Issue 1 • January-December 2025 prevalence of
fatal and severe accidents is alarming, underscoring the necessity for new and effective management and
prevention techniques (Lorusso & Celenta, 2023). In recent years, digital twin (DT), a dynamic and current
digital representation of a physical object or environment, has emerged as a viable alternative for proactive
safety management on building sites, due to the capacity of DTs to amalgamate data from diverse sources
(Internet of Things [IoT] sensors, drones, cameras, BIM [building information modeling]), continuous and
comprehensive monitoring of the building site can be conducted, anticipating dangerous scenarios and
initiating prompt and precise actions (Hagedorn et al., 2023a; Hong & Teizer, 2025). The objective of this
project is to design, formalize, and validate Digital Twin Safety Ontology (DTSafe-O), a semantically driven
DT for construction site safety management, which can integrate diverse data sources (sensors, video
analytics, BMS [building management systems]/SCADA [supervisory control and data acquisition] systems,
wearables), information models (BIM), and regulatory documentation into a queryable and explicable knowledge
graph. The proposal aligns with research on developing ontologies in the architecture, engineering, and
construction (AEC) sector and the increasing integration of BIM, IoT, and the Semantic Web (Casillo, Colace,
et al., 2024; Colace et al., 2014). Still, it seeks to address the deficiency in comprehensive solutions
tailored for temporary or mobile construction sites, where safety necessitates prompt reasoning, regulatory
traceability, and interoperability with construction site operations (Farghaly et al., 2023; Pauwels, Zhang,
et al., 2017). The contribution includes a domain ontology for safety featuring Semantic Web Rule Language
(SWRL) rules and Shapes Constraint Language (SHACL) shapes that delineate obligations and operational
relationships; a integration mechanism with Semantic Sensor Network ontology (SSN)/sensor, observation,
sample, and actuator (SOSA), Industry Foundation Classes Web Ontology Language (ifcOWL), and Brick Schema
(BRICK) standards, as well as Information Delivery Specification (IDS) profiles and Information Container for
Linked Document Delivery (ICDD) containers for the exchange and storage of information packages; a
experimental validation utilizing synthetic and field data with metrics for correctness, coverage, and
performance; and replicability materials (ontology, shape, p) (Colace et al., 2013).This system enables the
conversion of raw signals into substantiated and verifiable judgments, consistent with the regulatory
framework and quantifiable through operational key performance indicators (KPIs; Teizer et al., 2022). The
work falls within the European framework for health and safety protection on construction sites defined by
Directive 89/391/European Economic Community (EEC) and Directive 92/57/EEC on temporary or mobile construction
sites, implemented in Italy by Legislative Decree 81/2008 (Title IV). With regard to systems management, the
principles of International Organization for Standardization (ISO) 45001 are relied upon. With regard to the
interoperability of information, reference is made to the ISO 19650 standard for information management, the
IDS for the definition of information requirements and the ICDD (ISO 21597) for the traceable delivery of
documents. This work addresses four research questions (RQ) based on these objectives. • RQ1, computable
safety modeling: What ontological constructs and patterns, such as Web Ontology Language 2, Description Logic
profile (OWL 2 DL), SWRL rules, and SHACL constraints, are essential and adequate to accurately represent the
safety requirements derived from Directive 92/57/EEC, Legislative Decree 81/2008, and ISO 45001, while
maintaining explainability and verifiability? • RQ2, integration and interoperability: To what degree does
alignment with SSN/SOSA, BRICK, and ifcOWL, along with the application of IDS profiles and ICDD containers,
facilitate an end-to-end observe–deduce–validate process among sensors, BIM, and documents, thereby minimizing
ambiguity and enabling automated checks? • RQ3, effectiveness and performance: To what degree does the
DTSafe-O instance facilitate prompt and dependable inferences on actual streams, such as coverage of
competency questions (CQs), SHACL compliance of the assertional box (A-Box), latency, and classification
efficacy, and what 3 International Journal on Semantic Web and Information Systems Volume 21 • Issue 1 •
