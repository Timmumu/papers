revised form 23 March 2026; accepted 8 May 2026 Available online 22 July 2026 Abstract Eﬀective management and
safe operation of tunnel infrastructure are critically challenged by the persistent vehicle tracking across
non-overlapping camera domains and the lack of real-time situational awareness for emergency incidents. This
work proposes a dynamic digital twin framework to create a high-ﬁdelity virtual representation that
synchronously updates with physical tunnel environments by leveraging existing Closed-Circuit Television
(CCTV) networks. The core is a novel Tunnel Re-identiﬁcation Model (TRIM) that can create discriminative
feature embeddings for seamless vehicle tracking and re-identiﬁcation across multi-camera domains. Comparative
validations unequivocally demonstrate that the TRIM signiﬁcantly outperforms Convolutional Neural Networks
(CNNs) and standard Vision Transformer backbones, achieving a state-of-the-art mAP@10 of 0.684. Analysis of
attention heatmaps reveals its ability to gen- erate a comprehensive and granular feature representation,
focusing on preserving high-quality characteristics for speciﬁc vehicle iden- tity. Furthermore, the emergency
response ability of the digital twin is augmented with a vision-based ﬁre detection module to real-time
localize and visualize emergent tunnel ﬁre events in the digital twin. By consolidating and distilling vision
data from CCTV cameras into an intuitive representation, the proposed framework enhances situational judgment
for operators and signiﬁcantly reinforces the safety resilience of tunnel infrastructure without requiring
extensive hardware redevelopment. Keywords: Digital twin; Vehicle re-identiﬁcation; Emergency response; Traﬃc
management; Smart tunnel 1 Intr oduction As fundamental transportation infrastructure, road tun- nels are the
key nodes of city operation and personnel interaction. Due to the inherently semi-enclosed environ- ments and
complicated facilities of tunnels, traditional tun- nel operation and maintenance mostly rely on isolated
monitoring systems and preset management plans ( Liu et al., 2024 ). Operational data, including videos,
textual report s, and binary sensor signals, commonly lags behind changes in real-world tunnels, and its
presentation is not in a straightforward and easy-to-understand format. Oper- ation and maintenance teams
necessitat e information- intensive distillation and analysis of heterogeneous data from tunnel sensor
networks ( Yin et al., 2020). Traditional tunnel operation lacks responsiveness to the dynamic changes in
traﬃc ﬂow and the complexity of unexpected events, with critical issue s such as insuﬃcient perception
capabilities, delayed decision-making responses, and high operational costs ( T. Li et al., 2024 ). ⁎

reﬂect changes of the real world remains the focus for tunnel digital twins ( Liu et al., 2021). Current
solutions have limited practicality because of the lack of cross-domain perception and the incompati- bility
of multi-source sensor data. This data silo degrades tunnel digital twins into low time-sensitivity systems
with poor interaction between physical devices and virtual mod- els. One of the challenges lies in
persistently tracking vehi- cle ﬂow inside tunnels, as well as immediately reacting to emergency incidents
such as ﬁres ( Gao et al., 2021 ). Given these gaps, this study proposes a dynamic tunnel digital twin
framework for simultaneously updating vehicle behaviors and reﬂecting occurrences in accordance with the
physical world. Rather than functioning as a fully auto- mated closed-loop digital twin, the proposed
framework serves as an advanced data fusion and perception layer within the comprehensive digital twin
architecture ( Chang et al., 2024 ). By employing a Transformer-based re- identiﬁcation model (TRIM) to
conduct visual feature extraction and matching, the constant tracking and re- identiﬁcation (Re-ID) of the
same vehicle from multiple camera areas can be performed within the full-domain dig- ital twin. Along with the
regular traﬃc monitoring func- tion, a vision-based ﬁre detection unit is coupled into the digital twin, which
provides accurate ﬁre coordinates and risk estimations. This dynamic tunnel digital twin is estab- lished
directly on Cl osed-Circuit Television (CCTV) net- works, meaning it can be conveniently deployed in existing
and upcoming tunnels without redevelopment. By consolidating multi-camera vision data, it establishes a
fundamental data-fusion platform, thereby providing the essential prerequisite data infrastructure for future
intelligent decision-making and automated facility control. 2 Literature revie w 2.1 Digital twin in tunnel
lifecycle Over the period of tunnel construction, uncertain and changeable geological conditions will be
encountered, which are gradually exposed as excavation or blasting pro- gresses ( Desai et al., 2022 ).
Through integrating on-site sensing methods, including image recognition, oblique photography, laser scanning,
and various physical sensors, digital twins are allowed to quickly and accurately access geotechnical and
structural responses, thereby supporti ng tunnel construction ( L. Zhang et al., 2024 ). Considering that
tunnel construction involves multidisciplinary knowl- edge that contains mechanics, thermodynamics, geology,
and materials science, digital twins present computability and pr edictability to simulate multi-physics
coupling and avoid potential structural failures with ﬁne-grained descrip- tions ( Fang et al., 2024 ). During
the operation and maintenance phase, digital twins serve as a comprehensive and intelligent management
platform, providing a holistic and real-time overview of the tunnels’ performance and environment. By
integrating with disparate systems, such as electromechanical equipment ( Yu et al., 2023), lighting controls
(Shen et al., 2022), traﬃc signals (Zhao et al., 2022 ), and structural health mo nitor- ing (Wang et al.,
2025 ), digital twins create a uniﬁed repre- sentation of tunnel operational conditions. A practical
exempliﬁcation involves designing a digital twin framework to ad opt surveillance videos to reconstruct an
immersive virtual tunnel and couple with tunnel facilities ( Z. Wu et al., 2022 ). This enables operators to
predict the future performance of these assets and minimize the losses of traf- ﬁc accidents, moving beyond
reactive problem-solving to a more predictive and optimized management paradigm ( Khan, 2025 ). Historic traﬃc
patterns can be superposed onto this digital twin model, allowing for the proactive adjustment of traﬃc
managem ent strategies at the speciﬁc time point to ensure smooth and eﬃcient transit ( Rezaei et al., 2 023).
