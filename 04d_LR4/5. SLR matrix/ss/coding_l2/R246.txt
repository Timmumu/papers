I.Introduction Bridges are critical components of transportation networks, and their long-term performance
strongly depends on effective inspection and maintenance strategies. Aging infrastructure, increasing traffic
demand, and environmental exposure have significantly intensified the need for reliable and timely bridge
condition assessment (Agredo Chávez et al., 2024; Mishra et al., 2022). Conventional bridge inspections are
predominantly based on manual visual surveys, which are time-consuming, costly, and often limited by
accessibility and safety constraints, particularly for hard-to-reach components such as bridge decks, girders,
bearings, and piers (Gucunski et al., 2015; Ye, 2020, 2022). In recent years, unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs)
have emerged as a promising alternative for bridge inspection, offering improved accessibility, reduced
inspection time, and enhanced safety for inspectors (Aliyari, 2022; Ayele, 2020). Equipped with high-
resolution cameras and stabilized platforms, UAVs enable close-range visual inspection without the need for
scaffolding, traffic closures, or specialized access equipment (Ameli, 2022; Biscarini, 2020). Consequently,
UAV-based inspection has been increasingly adopted in both research and practice for bridge condition
assessment and monitoring. Alongside the growing use of UAVs, significant research efforts have focused on
automated damage detection using computer vision and artificial intelligence techniques. Deep learning-based
methods have demonstrated strong capabilities in detecting cracks, spalling, corrosion, and other surface
defects from UAV-acquired images (Hu et al., 2024; Kao, 2023; Luciw et al., 2024). While these approaches This

interpretation of inspection data, they largely assume that inspection data are already available and of
sufficient quality. The flight planning process that determines where and how inspection data are collected
often remains manual or only loosely defined. Existing UAV flight planning strategies for infrastructure
inspection are commonly based on generic coverage path planning or viewpoint optimization principles
originally developed for mapping, photogrammetry, or robotic exploration tasks (Aela, 2024; Fang, 2024;
Hildmann & Kovacs, 2019). These methods typically aim to maximize surface coverage or minimize flight time and
energy consumption. Although effective for geometric data acquisition, such approaches do not explicitly
account for bridge maintenance objectives, such as the prioritization of critical structural components or the
need for consistent inspection viewpoints across multiple inspection cycles (Falorca, 2021; Ri, 2024). More
recent studies have attempted to tailor UAV path planning to bridge inspection scenarios by considering
environmental constraints, complex geometries, or specific structural configurations. For example, research
has addressed UAV navigation under bridge decks with limited GNSS availability (Shults et al., 2023), wind-
aware path planning for long-span bridges (Xu, 2021), and surface-specific inspection of bridge substructures
(Omar & Nehdi, n.d.). Despite these advances, most existing approaches remain predominantly geometry-driven,
focusing on obstacle avoidance and surface coverage rather than inspection relevance from a maintenance
perspective. Parallel to developments in UAV inspection, the field of bridge maintenance has seen increasing
interest in knowledge-based systems and decision support tools. Knowledge graphs, ontologies, and data-driven
frameworks have been proposed to organize maintenance knowledge, defect characteristics, and decision rules in
a structured manner (Gao et al., 2024; Hoang, 2021). More recently, advanced reasoning frameworks integrating
large language models with bridge maintenance knowledge graphs have demonstrated the potential to support
expert-level maintenance decision-making (Wang et al., 2026). These studies highlight the importance of domain
knowledge in guiding maintenance actions but generally focus on diagnosis and recommendation rather than the
operational execution of inspections. As a result, a critical gap remains between maintenance knowledge and
UAV-based inspection planning. Current UAV inspection workflows rarely incorporate maintenance knowledge, such
as component criticality, historical damage patterns, or inspection requirements, into the path planning
process. Consequently, inspection routes are often inconsistent across inspection cycles, limiting their
usefulness for long-term monitoring and maintenance planning (Yeum & Dyke, 2015). To address this gap, this
study proposes a knowledge-based approach to UAV path planning for bridge inspection and maintenance. The
proposed approach integrates maintenance knowledge with bridge geometry to define inspection objectives and
generate UAV flight paths that are inspection- oriented, maintenance-relevant, and repeatable. By shifting the
focus from generic coverage to knowledge-informed inspection planning, the proposed framework aims to support
continuous bridge inspection and provide a practical foundation for long-term maintenance-oriented UAV
deployment. II.Background and Related Work 2.1 UAV-Based Bridge Inspection in a Maintenance Context Over the
