Digital twins ABSTRACT Bridge infrastructures are inevitably exposed to a wide range of deterioration
mechanisms and external damages over their service life, which can gradually impair structural integrity,
compromise functionality, and increase safety risks. These challenges, coupled with the growing complexity of
infrastructure systems and limited financial resources, highlight the need for implementing intelligent and
proactive asset management solutions. To address these needs, this paper presents a Reinforcement Learning-
based Decision Support System (RL-DSS) framework for bridge maintenance optimization, leveraging a Multi-
Objective Deep Centralized Multi-Agent Actor-Critic (MO-DCMAC) algorithm that is designed for application
within bridge Digital Twin environments. The proposed RL-DSS implements a three-objective MO-DCMAC framework
that models maintenance cost, structural failure risk, and serviceability as separate objectives within a
vector-valued reward formulation, while employing a fixed linear utility function for policy optimization. The
proposed framework was evaluated through three real-world bridge case studies, each representing different
service conditions, to demonstrate its capability in providing long-term maintenance planning. Its performance
was benchmarked against multiple established rule-based policies commonly used in practice. The results
revealed that the MO-DCMAC agent achieved the highest overall utility score, demonstrating superiority by
generating more refined and practical maintenance policies. By actively penalizing high-frequency site visits
and strategically balancing intervention costs with risk mitigation, the RL agent prioritizes fewer but more
impactful repair actions. The proposed RL-DSS reflects an adaptive, informed, and cost-effective decision-
making process that captures the complexity of real-world maintenance scenarios, surpassing the limitations of
traditional rule-based approaches. 1. Introduction Sustainable transportation systems are widely recognized as
critical foundations for the development of resilient and sustainable societies, and are closely aligned with
the principles of smart cities ( Moslem et al., 2024 ; Saha et al., 2024 ). Achieving these sustainability
objectives relies heavily on the implementation of effective infrastructure asset man - agement systems, which
enable decision-makers to apply, data-driven, timely and predictive maintenance strategies supported by
advanced intelligent control and autonomous management approaches ( G ¨orçün et al., 2025 ; Sood, 2024 ; Tang
et al., 2025 ). In this context, bridges serve as fundamental components of transportation infrastructure and
the broader built environment. However, their structural condition de - teriorates over time due to various
factors, including environmental exposure, traffic-induced loading, material degradation and aging. Without
appropriate monitoring and management, these factors can lead to reduced structural capacity and
serviceability, which may necessitate major rehabilitation, repair, or even demolition, resulting in increased
costs, prolonged traffic disruptions, and elevated safety risks ( Wu et al., 2021 ). Therefore, considerable

). Over the years, a range of maintenance approaches has been devel - oped to enhance infrastructure longevity
and reliability. Proactive maintenance strategies aim to prevent deterioration through timely and planned
interventions. One common approach is time-based mainte - nance, which involves performing scheduled repairs
at predetermined intervals, regardless of the structure's actual condition. Although straightforward to
implement, time-based methods often lead to in - efficiencies by triggering unnecessary maintenance actions (
de Jonge et al., 2017 ). In contrast, condition-based maintenance, which relies on observed or predicted
structural states derived from inspection data or analytical models, tends to be more efficient than time-
based main - tenance due to its reliance on inspection information and tailoring in - terventions to the
asset's actual condition and performance needs ( Zonta et al., 2020 ). However, the rapid expansion of
transportation infrastructure and advances in technology have considerably reshaped the landscape of bridge
management. Preventive maintenance, particularly for bridges with different structural forms, could be a
challenge for engineers, especially in the absence of a structured, data-informed decision-making frameworks (
Brighenti et al., 2024 ; Rashidi et al., 2016a ). This is mainly because strategic bridge management and
maintenance extends beyond routine preventive maintenance needs; it requires prioritizing long-term safety,
operational functionality, and economic sustainability ( Frangopol and Kim, 2022 ). Given the substantial
financial and envi - ronmental implications associated with replacing deteriorating bridge structures,
extending the service life of existing bridges has become a more cost-effective and sustainable alternative.
In recent decades, decision-making in bridge asset management has become progressively more complex, largely
due to structural com - plexities and the integration of advanced digital and communication technologies.
These advancements have expanded the range of potential management solutions, complicating the identification
of optimal stra - tegies for asset managers. As a result, a number of specialized Bridge Management Systems
(BMSs), such as PONTIS ( Thompson et al., 1998 ), DANBRO( Andersen, 1990 ) and BRIDGIT ( Hawk and Small, 1998
), have been developed to support systematic maintenance planning and asset preservation. These systems
integrate data inputs, regulatory standards, and specialized software tools to facilitate tasks such as
maintenance planning, prioritization of interventions, and lifecycle cost optimization ( Brighenti et al.,
2024 ; Rashidi, 2013 ). Emerging technologies including Bridge Information Modelling (BrIM), Digital Twins
(DTs) and Deep Learning (DL) are further advancing the BMS capabilities. By enabling the digital
representation of assets and integrating real-time monitoring data with predictive models, these technologies
allow for reliable assessment of structural health and more informed decisions regarding the timing and scope
of maintenance activities ( Mousavi et al., 2025a , 2025b ). Consequently, transportation authorities around
the world are increasingly adopting these systems to address the growing complexity of infrastructure
management and improve the efficiency and effectiveness of bridge maintenance practices ( Shahrivar et al.,
2025 ). Despite significant advancements in development of bridge DTs, application of Decision Support Systems
(DSS) into bridge management systems has been rarely considered in bridge DTs ( Mousavi et al., 2024 ).
