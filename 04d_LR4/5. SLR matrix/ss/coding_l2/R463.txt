Technology, Xuzhou 221116, China * Correspondence: zhangxin1994@cumt.edu.cn Abstract Actual construction
errors and accumulated damage seriously affect the spatial structural form of the tensile membrane structure,
making it difficult to characterize the full-life spatial form of the physical tensile membrane structure
based on the original theoretical model. There are still significant challenges in achieving the virtual–real
correspondence of digital twin results based on the original theoretical design model. It is necessary to
propose a high- precision digital twin model construction method that adapts to the full life cycle of the
tensile membrane structure. For this reason, a refined digital twin model reconstruction and morphological
deviation visualization method for the umbrella-shaped tensile membrane structure combined with 3D point
clouds, computational geometry algorithms, and 3D3S finite element simulation modeling is proposed in this
paper. Firstly, three-dimensional point cloud data of the umbrella-shaped membrane structure test bench were
acquired, and the point cloud data were accurately registered based on the Iterative Closest Point (ICP)
algorithm. Secondly, the physical membrane surface reconstruction was obtained by applying the Screened
Poisson Surface Reconstruction (SPSR) algorithm. Subsequently, the 3D3S theoretical model of the umbrella-
shaped tensile membrane structure was updated according to the contour of the measured membrane surface
reconstruction model. Finally, global spatial geometric morphological deviation was compared among the
theoretical models and the reconstructed physical model. Furthermore, different environmental load
combinations were analyzed to support hazard warning of the refined digital twin model of the physical
umbrella-shaped tensile membrane structure. The results show that the maximum spatial form deviation of the
original theoretical model was 43.56 mm, whereas the maximum form deviation of the updated theoretical model
was only 0.05 mm. The updated theoretical model accurately reflected the spatial form characteristics of the
physical membrane structure and satisfied the requirement for digital twin physical–virtual consistency. In
addition, the global stress distribution between the original/updated theoretical models differed markedly,
and the updated theoretical model is more suitable for identifying weak areas and evaluating safety
performance of the actual umbrella-shaped tensile membrane structure under different ultimate environmental

morphological deviation; environmental load analysis; tensile membrane structure 1. Introduction A digital
twin is a virtual counterpart of a physical entity that supports bidirec- tional and real-time data mapping
and seamless switching between physical and virtual domains [1,2]. Grieves [1] first proposed a three-
dimensional model integrating the phys- ical entity, the virtual entity, and their interactive connection.
With the development of application demands, digital twin models have been continuously updated for different
application scenarios [ 2]. Tao [ 3] proposed a five-dimensional digital twin model and systematically
analyzed its application domains, levels, disciplines, dimensions, generality, and functions. Owing to its
integrated monitoring, control, and prediction capabilities, dig- ital twin technology has become prominent in
manufacturing [4]. Against the background of domestic intelligent development strategies, digital twin
technology has gradually ex- panded from manufacturing to the construction industry and has become one of the
key technologies supporting the transformation of traditional construction toward intelligent construction
[5]. Although research on digital twins in the construction field has grown rapidly, existing studies remain
highly fragmented and regionally oriented [6]. Congiu [7] developed a digi- tal twin system framework to
support facility managers in decision-making by effectively integrating Building Information Modeling (BIM)
and Internet of Things (IoT). For intelli- gent construction services, Pan [5] developed a detailed digital
twin framework containing physical models, virtual models, and connected data by integrating BIM, IoT, and
data min- ing (DM) techniques. Oti [8] used laser scanners and cameras to acquire real-time building data and
established a building energy-consumption feedback loop, which reduces the gaps between different building
life-cycle stages through continuous correction of design and facility management in the digital twin
environment. Yu [9] proposed an intelligent operation and maintenance scheme for urban roads based on multi-
source spatiotemporal monitoring data. Song [10] developed a conceptual framework that integrates digital
twins with BIM, aiming to extend BIM applications from design and construction stages to the en- tire life
cycle of bridge engineering through real-time data interaction and intelligent asset management. Based on data
integration, synchronization, and analysis, Lu [11] designed a building–city digital twin model to support
anomaly detection, environmental monitoring, maintenance optimization, prioritization, and energy planning. Qi
[12] proposed a digital twin framework for transmission towers by integrating BIM, IoT, and the Finite Element
Method (FEM), which supports analysis of structural health monitoring and visual warning under wind loads and
flood scouring. These studies indicate that the integration of digital twins with DM, IoT, BIM, and FEM can
enable multi-source data fusion and in-depth data mining, and further reduce carbon footprints, improve indoor
environmental quality, and enhance resource utilization and energy efficiency [13,14]. Although existing
studies show that digital twins are evolving from static BIM toward dynamic decision-support systems,
simulation modeling methods remain central to their implementation. Digital modeling technology is a core
method for virtual-environment modeling and is also a primary approach for digital twin modeling. Based on
geometric modeling technology, a virtual mapping platform for physical entities can be established and formed
as data carriers by constructing geometric models of various physical entities. Digital twins currently have
moved from theoretical research to engineering applications, https://doi.org/10.3390/app16157658 Appl. Sci.
