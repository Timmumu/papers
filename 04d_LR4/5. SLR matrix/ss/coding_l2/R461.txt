time-frequency image analysis Railway track monitoring ABSTRACT Predictive maintenance is a key enabler of
automated Structural Health Monitoring (SHM) for safety-critical infrastructure, supporting reliable operation
under increasing service demands. This paper presents an inte - grated Digital Twin – Artificial Intelligence
(DT – AI) framework for the automated condition assessment of bonded Insulated Rail Joints (IRJs) and adjacent
sleepers. A high-fidelity Digital Twin is developed to simulate joint behaviour under varying preload and
support degradation scenarios, generating physically consistent datasets across representative loading
conditions. Deep Convolutional Neural Networks (DCNNs) and traditional Machine Learning (ML) classifiers are
trained on time – frequency representations of selected predictive indicators to identify and classify
degradation states. Results demonstrate that the proposed physics-informed DT-driven framework achieves high
diagnostic accuracy and strong generalization capability, with DCNNs models out - performing conventional
classifiers. By integrating physics-based simulation with automated data analytics, the proposed framework
advances scalable, data-driven SHM and predictive maintenance strategies, for the intel - ligent automation of
railway infrastructure management. 1. Introduction Modern railway networks are advanced technological systems
that underpin sustainable mobility, fostering social cohesion, economic growth, and regional integration [1] .
By enhancing safety and capacity while promoting a modal shift from road to rail, efficient rail systems
contribute to reduced congestion and greenhouse gas emissions, sup - porting circular economy objectives [2] .
Achieving these goals requires not only infrastructure expansion but also improved reliability, resil - ience,
and optimized lifecycle management of critical assets. In this context, the digitalization of railway
infrastructure, through Digital Twins (DTs), the Internet of Things (IoT), and Artificial Intelli - gence
(AI)-based predictive tools, represents a transformative paradigm for asset management [3] . These
technologies enable real-time moni - toring, automated anomaly detection, and Predictive Maintenance (PdM),
moving beyond traditional time-based scheduling. Their stra - tegic importance is reflected in large-scale
national and European ini - tiatives, such as the Italian project MOST — Centro Nazionale per la Mobilit `a
Sostenibile [4] , Spoke 4 Railway Transport, funded under the National Recovery and Resilience Plan (NRRP) and
the Next Generation EU (NGEU) programme [5] , which promote advanced digital solutions for sustainable railway
transport. Among safety-critical railway components, bonded Insulated Rail Joints (IRJs) remain essential in
conventional signalling systems until the transition to European Rail Traffic Management System/European Train
Control System (ERTMS/ETCS) and Radio Block Center (RBC) technologies, is fully completed. While ensuring
electrical insulation and mechanical continuity, IRJs introduce structural discontinuities relative to
continuous welded rail, making them inherently susceptible to degradation. Failures may compromise signalling
integrity, reduce track stiffness, and adversely affect operational safety. Periodic inspection-based
maintenance is increasingly inadequate, as IRJ dete - rioration results from complex interactions among cyclic
loading, bolt preload loss, environmental effects, and support degradation [6] . Despite robust design, bonded
IRJs are prone to progressive bolt loosening, cracking, and wear. Loss of bolt preload increases vertical
deflection and may induce bolt – hole clearance undetectable until cracking occurs [7] . Their reduced bending
stiffness amplifies defor - mation under train passage, accelerating deterioration and increasing maintenance
costs [8,9] . Adjacent sleepers also play a critical role: stiffness discontinuities around joints intensify
impact loads and stress concentrations [10] , contributing to ballast degradation [11] and * Corresponding

While DTs have been applied to railway bridges, switches, and rolling stock [19,20] , and previous studies
have examined bolt degradation, IRJ behaviour, or sleeper stiffness separately, no work has systematically
integrated their coupled interaction within a unified DT – AI framework for automated diagnostics. Traditional
methods, such as isolated com - puter vision or conventional sensors, often rely on heuristic thresholds and
manual inspections, limiting scalability and real-time applicability. To address these limitations, the
present study develops a high- fidelity, physics-informed Digital Twin that reproduces the coupled mechanical
behaviour of IRJs and adjacent sleepers under both nominal and controlled degradation scenarios that cannot be
safely reproduced in the field. DT-generated signals, specifically vertical displacement and gap value. i.e.
the longitudinal displacement between adjacent rails, are transformed into time – frequency representations
and analysed using Deep Convolutional Neural Networks (DCNNs) and traditional Machine Learning (ML)
classifiers for automated condition assessment. The main contributions of this work are: • A unified physics-
informed Digital Twin reproducing the coupled me - chanical behaviour of bonded IRJs and adjacent sleepers
under varying bolt-preload and support-stiffness conditions. • A structured definition of structural health
classes grounded in me - chanically interpretable degradation mechanisms. • A systematic comparative
assessment of time – frequency-based deep learning models (STFT-CNN and WT-CNN) and a traditional ML decision-
tree classifier across six predictive maintenance scenarios. • A methodology for quantifying the detectability
limits of various degradation modes, linking classification performance to underlying mechanical phenomena and
providing a foundation for future maintenance prioritization. From an automation perspective, the proposed
framework estab - lishes a closed-loop, decision-oriented monitoring workflow in which DT simulations generate
labelled degradation scenarios that are mapped to measurable indicators through AI-based classifiers. By
embedding mechanical knowledge within the diagnostic logic, the methodology reduces reliance on manual
inspections and heuristic thresholds, enabling scalable predictive maintenance aligned with digital trans -
formation objectives. The decision-oriented monitoring workflow re - duces human dependency by automating the
classification of degradation states, thus enabling data-driven maintenance decisions and mitigating
operational bottlenecks throughout the infrastructure lifecycle. The remainder of the paper is structured as
follows: Section 1 pro - vides an overview of the relevant background literature, defines the research gap,
and positions the present study. Section 2 describes the DT and AI modelling framework, shown in Fig. 1 .
