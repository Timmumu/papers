ABSTRACT
Rockfill dams exhibit pronounced stage-dependent deformation during construction, impoundment, and long- 
term operation, requiring monitoring-updated deformation models for lifecycle prediction and digital twin- 
based safety assessment. However, existing updating methods are often deterministic and stage-isolated, while 
conventional probabilistic approaches insufficiently capture dependence among mechanically related constitu -
tive parameters. For multi-stage lifecycle analysis, these methods also lack cross-stage adaptability and often 
require repeated finite-element simulations or full surrogate retraining, leading to high computational cost. This 
study proposes a dependence-aware sequential probabilistic framework with posterior-guided continual surro -
gate learning for dynamic updating of rockfill dam deformation models. The framework progressively assimilates 
monitoring data to track lifecycle parameter evolution, preserves empirical parameter dependence via Copula- 
enhanced prior modeling within a sequential Bayesian updating scheme, and adaptively refines the surrogate 
by concentrating new training samples in posterior-relevant regions while retaining historical deformation 
knowledge. Application to the 303 m Lianghekou core-wall rockfill dam shows that the proposed method reduces 
the global relative settlement prediction error from 37.4% to 9.7% and reduces the dominant computational cost 
of lifecycle surrogate updating by 45.8% compared with full surrogate retraining. The updated model further 
enables uncertainty-informed settlement prediction, spatiotemporal deformation reconstruction, and posterior- 
informed diagnosis of safety-related deformation characteristics, providing an interpretable and efficient 
approach for lifecycle deformation analysis and digital twin-based dam safety management.
1. Introduction
Rockfill dams are widely used in large hydraulic projects because of 
their adaptability to complex geological conditions, use of locally 
available materials, and favorable seismic performance ( Ma and Chi, 
2016a ; Zhang et al., 2026 ). As large granular structures, their defor -
mation behavior evolves markedly during staged construction, reservoir 
impoundment, and long-term operation ( Sun et al., 2026 ; Yang et al., 
2025 ). Settlement is mainly governed by self-weight loading and 
compaction during construction, while wetting-induced deformation, 
hydraulic loading, stress redistribution, and creep become increasingly 
important after impoundment ( Fig. 1 ). For tall rockfill dams, such 
lifecycle-dependent deformation is closely related to structural safety, 
seepage stability, and operational reliability, making reliable deforma -
tion assessment essential for dam safety management.
Field monitoring provides direct information on the in-situ defor -
mation response of rockfill dams, but monitoring data are usually 
available only at discrete locations and cannot directly characterize the 
full-field mechanical state. Finite element (FE)-based deformation 
analysis provides a physics-based bridge between monitoring observa -
tions and continuous deformation fields, making monitoring-driven FE 
model updating an important approach for interpreting dam behavior,

and artificial intelligence, civil infrastructure is evolving toward intel -
ligent operation supported by digital twin (DT) technology ( Nav et al., 
2025 ; Saif et al., 2024 ). For rockfill dams, a practical DT is not merely 
a static replica or visualization tool, but an updatable computational 
model that can assimilate monitoring data, evolve with the physical 
dam, and support lifecycle performance assessment.
Existing monitoring-driven deformation model updating methods 
have improved dam behavior interpretation and deformation predic -
tion, but several challenges remain for lifecycle applications. First, many 
back-analysis methods are deterministic and stage-specific, identifying a 
single optimal parameter set for a given period while providing limited 
information on parameter uncertainty and prediction reliability ( Ai 
et al., 2024 ; Li et al., 2024 ). Because rockfill materials are inherently 
variable and monitoring data are limited and uncertain, uncertainty 
quantification is important for assessing model confidence and sup -
porting risk-informed decisions ( Chen et al., 2025 ; Dai et al., 2025 ). 
Second, conventional probabilistic updating often assumes independent 
prior parameters for simplicity ( Li et al., 2024 ; Zeng et al., 2024 ), 
although rockfill constitutive parameters related to stiffness, volumetric 
deformation, strength, and nonlinear response are commonly correlated 
because of shared material properties, compaction conditions, stress 
states, and testing procedures ( Ai et al., 2025 ; Yang et al., 2025 ). 
Neglecting such dependence may reduce the interpretability of posterior 
parameter evolution, especially when different parameter combinations 
can reproduce similar settlement responses ( Huang et al., 2026 ). Third, 
lifecycle updating requires repeated model evaluations as new moni -
toring data become available and deformation mechanisms evolve. 
Direct FE-based Bayesian inference is computationally expensive, while 
conventional surrogate-assisted approaches are usually developed for a 
single analysis stage or fixed parameter domain ( Yu et al., 2025 ; Zhang 
et al., 2026 ). A surrogate trained in earlier stages may lose accuracy in 
later stages, whereas full retraining requires additional FE samples and 
increases cumulative computational cost. Continual learning provides a 
potential solution by allowing the surrogate to incorporate new-stage 
information while retaining previously learned knowledge ( Delange 
et al., 2021 ; Kirkpatrick et al., 2017 ; Van De Ven et al., 2022 ).
To address these issues, this study develops a dependence-aware 
sequential probabilistic framework with posterior-guided continual 
surrogate learning for lifecycle deformation model updating of rockfill 
dams. The proposed framework treats constitutive parameters as stage- 
wise effective material states and progressively updates their posterior 
distributions as monitoring data are assimilated. A Gaussian Copula- 
based dependence model is embedded into the posterior-to-prior prop -
agation process, allowing posterior-informed marginal distributions 
from the previous lifecycle stage to be reconnected through an empirical 
dependence structure. To improve computational feasibility, a deep 
neural network surrogate is continually refined using posterior-guided 
FE samples, representative historical replay samples, and Fisher- 
information-weighted regularization. This strategy enables the surro -
gate to adapt to current-stage deformation characteristics while retain -
ing historically learned response mappings.
The main contributions of this study are summarized as follows: 
(1) A lifecycle-oriented sequential probabilistic updating formula -
tion is developed for rockfill dam deformation models. By 
updating stage-wise effective constitutive parameter distribu -
tions rather than a single deterministic parameter set, the 
formulation enables progressive monitoring-data assimilation 
and uncertainty-informed deformation assessment across con -
struction, impoundment, and operation stages.
(2) A dependence-preserving posterior-to-prior propagation strategy 
is introduced for mechanically related rockfill parameters. By 
reconnecting posterior-informed marginal distributions through 
an empirical Gaussian Copula dependence structure, the pro -
posed approach retains prescribed statistical dependence among 
constitutive parameters and improves the interpretability of 
posterior parameter evolution.
(3) A posterior-guided continual surrogate learning strategy is pro -
posed for efficient cross-stage model updating. The strategy uses 
posterior information to guide incremental FE sampling and 
combines response-stratified replay with Fisher-weighted regu -
larization to balance current-stage adaptability and historical 
knowledge retention.
(4) The proposed framework is validated using the 303 m Lianghe -
kou core-wall rockfill dam. The case study evaluates posterior 
parameter evolution, independent settlement prediction, surro -
gate updating performance, uncertainty decomposition, and 
safety-oriented deformation diagnosis based on the posterior- 
updated FE model, demonstrating its capability for lifecycle 
performance assessment of tall rockfill dams.

Section 5 presents the validation, comparative analysis, and discussion. 
Section 6 concludes the paper.

2. Related works
2.1. SHM-oriented FE deformation model updating of rockfill dams
Deformation monitoring is a central component of structural health 
monitoring (SHM) for rockfill dams ( Ma & Chi, 2016b ), because set -
tlement and displacement responses directly reflect staged construction, 
self-weight compaction, reservoir impoundment, wetting deformation, 
and long-term creep. However, monitoring data are usually available 
only at discrete locations and cannot directly describe the full-field 
mechanical state of the dam. Therefore, an important task in 
SHM-oriented safety assessment is to assimilate field observations into 
finite element-based (FE-based) deformation analysis models, so that 
monitored responses can be transformed into interpretable material 
parameters, deformation fields, and safety-related indicators.
FE-based deformation analysis provides the mechanistic basis for 
interpreting dam behavior (K. Wang et al., 2024 ; W. Zhou et al., 2011 ). 
By incorporating nonlinear constitutive models and simulating staged 
construction, reservoir loading, wetting, and creep, FE models can 
reproduce the main deformation characteristics of rockfill materials. 
Monitoring-assisted parameter back-analysis is widely used to reduce 
discrepancies between simulations and observations, while 
optimization-based methods improve parameter identification effi -
ciency for deformation prediction and safety assessment ( Jia & Chi, 
2015 ; Z. Li et al., 2024 ).
Recent studies have enhanced monitoring-driven model updating 
through multi-source data integration, representative monitoring-point 
selection, response reconstruction, and surrogate-assisted inverse anal -
ysis, improving both reliability and computational efficiency ( Ai et al., 
2024 , 2025 ; Huang et al., 2026 ). Meanwhile, probabilistic updating 
methods have been introduced to quantify parameter and prediction 
uncertainties by integrating prior information with monitoring data 
( Coletti et al., 2024 ; Ding et al., 2025 ; L. Zhang et al., 2018 ). Repre -
sentative SHM-oriented deformation analysis and model updating 
studies are summarized in Table 1 .
As summarized in Table 1 , existing studies have advanced from FE- 
based mechanism interpretation to monitoring-driven model updating 
and uncertainty-informed deformation assessment. However, lifecycle 
SHM of rockfill dams still faces several limitations: many back-analysis 
and surrogate-assisted methods remain deterministic; probabilistic 
updating often neglects correlations among physically coupled 
constitutive parameters; and most surrogate-assisted strategies are 
developed for fixed domains or individual stages, whereas deformation 
mechanisms and credible parameter spaces evolve continuously from 
construction to long-term operation.
2.2. Digital twin-enabled lifecycle performance assessment of rockfill 
dams
Digital twin technology is reshaping infrastructure management by 
integrating physical assets, monitoring data, computational models, and 
decision-support functions into an evolving cyber-physical system 
( Talasila et al., 2026 ). Recent studies show that infrastructure assess -
ment is moving from static visualization toward multi-source data 
integration, dynamic state perception, uncertainty-aware prediction, 
and lifecycle decision support ( Nakamura et al., 2025 ; X. Wang et al., 
2025 ). Advances in physics-guided machine learning and 
sparse-sensor response reconstruction further support the inference of 
unobserved structural states from limited monitoring data ( Ai et al., 
2026 ).
This paradigm is particularly relevant to rockfill dams, whose 
deformation behavior evolves under staged construction, reservoir 
impoundment, wetting effects, stress redistribution, and long-term 
creep. Thus, a practical digital twin for rockfill dams should go 
beyond a virtual replica or monitoring dashboard, and should provide 
an updatable deformation analysis model for tracking material-state 
evolution, reconstructing spatial deformation characteristics, quanti -
fying prediction uncertainty, and supporting lifecycle performance 
assessment. Recent studies have explored digital twin applications for 
deformation–seepage analysis, physics-constrained safety assessment, 
and full-field deformation reconstruction of high earth-rock dams ( Ai 
et al., 2026 ; Huang et al., 2026 ; Tian et al., 2025 ). However, most 
existing studies remain oriented toward specific functions or represen -
tative stages. The continuous linkage among monitoring data assimila -
tion, deformation model evolution, and lifecycle safety assessment has 
not yet been fully established, indicating the need for digital twin 
models that can evolve consistently with the dam state throughout 
construction, impoundment, and operation.
3. Methodology for sequential probabilistic lifecycle updating
3.1. Framework overview
To support lifecycle deformation analysis of rockfill dams, this study 
develops a sequential updating framework for probabilistically tracking 
material parameters and adaptively refining deformation analysis 
models. By progressively assimilating monitoring data from construc -
tion to impoundment and operation, the framework enables 
uncertainty-informed evaluation of deformation behavior and dam state 
under evolving conditions. The overall workflow is shown in Fig. 2 .
The framework starts from dam geometry, material properties, 
environmental loads, and in situ deformation monitoring data. Based on 
the dam geometry and loading conditions, a high-fidelity finite element 
(FE) model is constructed to simulate the main deformation mechanisms 
of rockfill dams, including staged construction, reservoir impoundment, 
wetting deformation, and creep. By mapping constitutive parameters to 
deformation responses, the FE model serves as the physical forward 
model for assimilating monitoring data and updating the material 
constitutive parameters that govern deformation behavior, thereby 
supporting subsequent deformation assessment.
At each lifecycle stage, the effective constitutive parameters are 
updated through sequential probabilistic inference. The posterior dis -
tribution from the previous stage is propagated as informative prior 
knowledge, while newly acquired monitoring data further constrain the 
current parameter distributions. A dependence-informed prior con -
struction is introduced to retain prescribed statistical dependence 
among mechanically related parameters, improving the interpretability

The posterior distribution also guides surrogate evolution. New FE 
samples are generated within the posterior high-probability region, 
rather than repeatedly sampled from the original global parameter 
domain, and are evaluated by the high-fidelity FE model. Together with 
representative historical samples, these new samples are used to incre -

the surrogate to focus on the parameter region most relevant to the 
current dam state while retaining previously learned deformation- 
response mappings, thereby reducing unnecessary FE simulations and 
avoiding full retraining at each stage.
Through this monitoring-driven process, the framework enables 
progressive uncertainty reduction, probabilistic deformation prediction, 
and posterior-informed deformation assessment over the dam lifecycle. 
The updated parameter distributions and reconstructed deformation 
responses provide a computational basis for digital twin-enabled dam 
management, including uncertainty tracking, deformation-state updat -
ing, and safety-oriented decision support.
3.2. Physics-based deformation forward model
The deformation behavior of rockfill dams is governed by material 
nonlinearity, staged construction, reservoir impoundment, wetting ef -
fects, and long-term creep. To represent these mechanisms within the 
proposed updating framework, a physics-based finite element (FE) 
deformation model is established as the forward model that maps 
constitutive parameters and loading conditions to deformation re -
sponses ( Tomassini et al., 2025 ; W. Zhou et al., 2011 ).
The deformation of rockfill dams is generally composed of static, 
creep, and wetting deformation ( Cheng et al., 2023 ; T. Zhou et al., 2025 ; 
W. Zhou et al., 2011 ), which act alternately or in combination during 
construction, impoundment, and long-term operation, thereby govern -
ing the overall deformation evolution of the dam. To represent these 
mechanisms, constitutive models commonly used in engineering prac -
tice are adopted to describe the nonlinear behavior of rockfill materials 
under different conditions. Specifically, static deformation is modeled 
by the Duncan – Chang E-B model (K. Wang et al., 2024 ), creep defor -
mation by an exponential decay model (G. Li et al., 2004 ), and wetting 
deformation by a three-parameter wetting model ( Jia et al., 2018 ). In 
this study, these three constitutive models are jointly employed as the 
governing equations of the deformation analysis model, and the asso -
ciated parameters and their physical meanings are summarized in 
Table 2 .
Within the sequential updating framework, the constitutive param -
eters of the deformation model are treated as stage-wise effective ma -
terial parameters rather than fixed lifecycle constants. By assimilating 
field deformation monitoring data, the probabilistic updating process 
identifies parameter distributions that better represent the current me -
chanical state of the prototype dam. The updated parameter distribu -
tions quantify material-state uncertainty and provide the basis for 
posterior deformation prediction, full-field response reconstruction, and 
safety-oriented deformation assessment.

In this study, the Gaussian Copula is not used as a standalone static 
prior, but as a correlation-reconnection mechanism in the sequential 
updating process. It reconnects evolving posterior-informed marginal 
distributions into a joint prior at each lifecycle stage, so that marginal 
uncertainty can be progressively updated by field monitoring data while 
prescribed empirical correlations among mechanically related parame -
ters are retained. This strategy reduces the tendency of correlated pa -
rameters to evolve in a decoupled manner because of compensation 
effects in displacement-based back analysis, and leads to more stable 
and physically interpretable parameter evolution during sequential 
updating.
The correlation matrices derived from the 48-dam database are used 
as empirical prior information rather than exact project-specific mate -
rial laws for the LHK dam. Project-specific deformation observations are 
subsequently assimilated through the likelihood function at each life -
cycle stage, allowing the posterior distributions to be updated by field 
monitoring data. In addition, the Gaussian Copula primarily captures 
monotonic dependence through correlation matrices and may have 
limited capability in representing tail dependence or more complex 
nonlinear dependence structures. More flexible models, such as t -Cop -
ulas or vine Copulas, could potentially provide richer descriptions under 
extreme safety states, although their reliable identification typically 
requires larger databases or more project-specific observations. This 
issue will be investigated in future work.
The posterior samples generated from this sequential inference are 
further used for uncertainty propagation and for defining the posterior 
high-probability region that guides incremental FE sampling in the 
continual surrogate learning strategy described in Section 3.4 .
3.4. Posterior-guided continual surrogate learning strategy
3.4.1. DNN surrogate modeling and Continual learning strategy
In sequential probabilistic updating of rockfill dam deformation 
models, repeated evaluations of the high-fidelity FE model are required 
for likelihood calculation, posterior sampling, and uncertainty propa -
gation. Directly embedding the nonlinear three-dimensional FE model 
into Bayesian inference would lead to prohibitive computational cost ( Ai 
et al., 2024 ; Yu et al., 2025 ). Therefore, a DNN-based surrogate model is 
introduced to approximate the mapping from uncertain constitutive 
parameters and spatiotemporal loading information to deformation 
responses.
The surrogate is formulated as a multi-output regression model

( Fig. 4 ). Its inputs include the updated constitutive parameters and the 
corresponding spatiotemporal features of the monitoring or prediction 
locations, while its outputs are the deformation responses at represen -
tative monitoring points or structural locations. The network adopts a 
fully connected feedforward architecture with batch normalization, 
LeakyReLU activation, and dropout regularization, which improves 
training stability and generalization under limited FE samples. Once 
trained, the surrogate replaces the FE model during Bayesian sampling 
and enables efficient posterior inference.
However, a static surrogate trained only once is insufficient for 
lifecycle deformation analysis. During construction, reservoir 
impoundment, and long-term operation, the relevant parameter domain 
and deformation response pattern change as the dominant mechanisms 
evolve from self-weight compaction to wetting-induced deformation 
and creep. A surrogate trained only on earlier-stage samples may lose 
accuracy in later stages, whereas full retraining at every lifecycle stage 
requires additional FE simulations and increases cumulative computa -
tional cost.
To address this issue, the surrogate is updated in a continual-learning 
manner. Continual learning provides a feasible strategy for sequentially 
incorporating new information while preserving previously learned 
knowledge, aiming to balance model plasticity and stability ( Delange 
et al., 2021 ; Parisi et al., 2019 ). In the context of lifecycle dam anal -
ysis, this enables the surrogate to continuously adapt to newly generated 
FE samples and evolving deformation patterns without losing predictive 
capability for earlier stages. Specifically, the surrogate trained at the 
previous lifecycle stage is inherited as the initial model for the current 
stage and is incrementally updated to absorb newly available sample 
information while maintaining consistency with previously learned 
response characteristics. This strategy allows the surrogate to adapt to 
current-stage deformation features while retaining previously learned 
response mappings. The detailed implementation of this 
lifecycle-adaptive updating strategy is described in Section 3.4.2 .
3.4.2. Posterior-guided continual surrogate updating
In the proposed framework, the posterior distribution obtained from 
Bayesian updating is used not only for uncertainty quantification, but 
also as an adaptive learning signal for surrogate evolution. Instead of 
generating new FE samples from the original global parameter bounds, 
the incremental sampling domain is defined by the posterior high- 
probability region of the previous lifecycle stage. In this study, this re -
gion is taken as the 90% credible interval of each updated parameter and 
is restricted within the physically admissible parameter space. Latin 
hypercube sampling (LHS) is then performed within this posterior- 
defined domain to maintain space-filling coverage and avoid excessive 
concentration around posterior modes. In this way, new FE simulations 
are concentrated in the parameter region most relevant to the current 
dam state, enabling local refinement of the surrogate with fewer addi -
tional FE samples and reducing extrapolation risk during lifecycle 
transitions.

4.2. Finite element modeling and physics-based simulation
A three-dimensional finite element (FE) model was developed in 
ABAQUS to simulate the stress-deformation evolution of the LHK core- 
wall rockfill dam during construction and operation. A user-defined 
material subroutine (UMAT) was implemented to capture the 
nonlinear constitutive behavior of rockfill materials. The model explic -
itly represents the dam zoning, including the impervious core, filter, 
transition, and rockfill zones. The computational domain consists of 
150,776 elements and 146,691 nodes, primarily discretized using eight- 
node hexahedral elements. Considering the nearly 300 m dam height, 
complex zoned configuration, and representative monitoring sections 
used for deformation updating, the adopted mesh resolution is consis -
tent with the discretization level commonly used in large-scale three- 
dimensional analyses of rockfill dams ( Ai et al., 2025 ; Ren et al., 2025 ; 
Yu et al., 2025 ). The material zoning and FE mesh are shown in Fig. 6 (c) 
and Fig. 7 (a), respectively.

4.3. Lifecycle staging and monitoring data partitioning
To implement sequential probabilistic updating, the continuous dam 
construction – operation process was divided into four representative 
lifecycle stages according to the documented construction and 
impoundment schedule of the LHK dam. Phase I (December 2019) cor -
responds to a filling elevation of 2755 m, approximately three-quarters 
of the dam height, when deformation was mainly governed by self- 
weight loading and static compaction. Phase II (December 2021) rep -
resents dam completion, with deformation still dominated by static 
response. Phase III (July 2023) corresponds to the initial operation 
period, during which reservoir impoundment induced wetting defor -
mation in the upstream rockfill. Phase IV (October 2024) represents the 
first attainment of the normal pool level, when sustained high-water 
exposure led to coupled wetting and creep deformation.
High-quality in situ monitoring data are essential for model updating 
and uncertainty quantification (G. Ma et al., 2024 ). The monitoring 
dataset used in this study consists of multi-source in situ deformation 
measurements collected from embedded instruments distributed across 
the main rockfill zones and the core wall. Before Bayesian updating, a 
monitoring-data filtering procedure was applied to remove low-quality 
or redundant records and retain measurements with higher informa -
tion content for model updating ( Ai et al., 2024 ). Candidate monitoring 
points were evaluated using quality and representativeness indicators, 
and the selected monitoring points, together with their 
instrument-specific nominal precisions used in the likelihood formula -
tion, are summarized in Table 3 .
To provide an independent benchmark for posterior predictive 
evaluation, 10 monitoring points with complete lifecycle records were 
reserved as validation data. These records were excluded from likeli -
hood construction, parameter updating, and model calibration, and 
were used only for subsequent comparative validation. The remaining 
monitoring points were used for sequential Bayesian calibration.

4.4.2. Implementation of sequential Bayesian updating
The sequential Bayesian updating framework was implemented in 
Pyro, with posterior inference performed using the No-U-Turn Sampler 
(NUTS). At each lifecycle stage, the uncertain constitutive parameters 
and residual model discrepancy were jointly sampled. The trained sur -
rogate model was used for likelihood evaluation to avoid repeated direct 
FE simulations during MCMC sampling, and automatic differentiation 
was employed to compute the gradients required by NUTS.
The likelihood variance incorporated four uncertainty components: 
observation error, surrogate approximation error, FE discretization 
error, and residual model discrepancy. The observation error was 
assigned according to the nominal precision of each monitoring instru -
ment, while the surrogate error was estimated from the independent 
test-set performance of the surrogate model at each lifecycle stage. The 
FE discretization error was specified as 50 mm, corresponding to a 
conservative representative value of approximately 1%-2% of the 
maximum lifecycle settlement response. The residual discrepancy was 
treated as an unknown parameter and inferred jointly with the consti -
tutive parameters.
For each lifecycle stage, four independent Markov chains were run in 
parallel. Each chain included 1000 warm-up steps and 2000 posterior 
sampling steps. The target acceptance probability was set to 0.8, and the 
maximum tree depth was set to 8. Sampling convergence was assessed 
using the Gelman – Rubin statistic and effective sample size. The main 
NUTS-MCMC settings and error-term definitions are summarized in 
Table 5 .
