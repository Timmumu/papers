ABSTRACT
Tunnel facility management (FM) is crucial for ensuring safety, efficiency, and resilience of tunnel infrastructure. 
Current FM practices, such as reactive and preventive maintenance, have limitations - reactive maintenance can't 
prevent failures, and preventive maintenance can't predict asset maintenance needs, leading to costly and in -
efficient processes. In addition, existing computerized tunnel FM systems face various challenges, including the 
lack of integrated real-time monitoring information, visualization of assets in a three-dimensional (3D) en -
vironment, supporting predictive maintenance, and scaling into long infrastructure with complicated spatio -
temporal relationships. This study addresses these limitations by proposing a data-driven digital twin (DT)-based 
framework that supports predictive maintenance to improve tunnel FM processes and enhance resilience. The 
proposed framework consists of six layers, allowing the integration of data from monitoring system, FM system, 
and building information modeling (BIM) models. The framework proposes a flexible tunnel data model and 
classification system that hierarchically divides the tunnel models, ensuring an efficient data connection from 
the physical twin to the DT. The system was implemented in a tunnel case study that generates maintenance 
plans and work orders using historical and current condition monitoring data, and the 3D visualization tech -
nology suggests maintenance and repair processes, making the FM decision process more effective. The proposed 
system detected and predicted the twin state based on a data-driven analysis, and the prediction accuracy of the 
machine learning models was sufficiently high for use in real scenarios to make FM plans in advance and prevent 
asset failures. The proposed framework is contributing to the infrastructure resilience by enhancing the tunnel 
system ability to predict the maintenance tasks and prevent failures using data-driven DT technology.
1. Introduction
The increasing population and urbanization of cities, have driven 
attention to underground infrastructures such as tunnels. An under -
ground tunnel system consists of a variety of structural elements, in -
cluding shotcrete, concrete linings, drainage, and other functional fa -
cilities, such as lighting, fire protection, plumbing, and ventilation. 
These elements are surrounded by a nonhomogeneous complex geolo -
gical environment ( Sharafat et al., 2021 ). During operations, tunnel 
components face various defects like deformation, cracking, leakage, 
service disruptions, and equipment failures, necessitating inspections 
and maintenance for optimal service. Developing a resilient system for 
tunnel maintenance tasks can significantly reduce the risk of expected 
failures ( Dui et al., 2025 ). Currently, reactive and preventive main -
tenance approaches are used for the facility management (FM) of tun -
nels. Reactive maintenance is adopted after the failure of an asset, 
whereas preventive maintenance is a schedule-based method that 
enables an FM team to perform maintenance tasks at predefined in -
tervals. However, these maintenance methods have limitations. Re -
active maintenance doesn't prevent facility failures, and preventive 
maintenance can't forecast facility conditions for proactive scheduling 
of repairs or extending its lifespan. ( Hosamo et al., 2023 ). Hence, these 
maintenance approaches should be changed to smart maintenance 
systems to address their shortcomings. A predictive maintenance ap -
proach can be adopted to enhance the resilience that uses historical 
data from the systems to predict the condition of the asset so that 
maintenance actions can be planned in advance ( Gbadamosi et al., 
2021 ).
In addition to these maintenance approaches, computerized systems 
are used to manage the FM data during FM activities. One such system 
is the computerized maintenance management system (CMMS), which 
stores historical, daily operational and maintenance (O&M), work or -
ders, and other FM-related data. However, CMMS has limitations, in -
cluding difficulties in locating the facility, extracting the required FM

challenges in integrating real-time monitoring information and visua -

building information modeling (BIM) have been integrated with such

one-sided decisions.
The digital twins (DTs) approach can be adopted as a comprehen -
sive smart solution ( Vivi et al., 2019 ). Due to the emergence and ben -
efits, numerous researchers have provided definitions and interpreta -
tions ( Khan et al., 2025; Lu and Brilakis, 2019 ). Several review papers 
summarizes, DT definitions, challenges, and opportunities across in -
dustries ( Babanagar et al., 2025; Liu et al., 2023; Opoku et al., 2021; Qi 
et al., 2021; Semeraro et al., 2021 ). Most definitions share three fun -
damental components of building DT: (1) physical components, (2) 
digital components, and (3) connections. Physical components re -
presents the actual objects or process, digital components replicates the 
objects in 3D environment, and connections facilitates to connect the 
physical and digital components using hardware and software tools 
( Tao et al., 2019 ).
The digital representations of physical components has been cate -
gorized into three categories ( Babanagar et al., 2025 ): (1) digital model, 
(2) digital shadow, and (3) digital twin. The digital model depicts only 
the digital replica of the physical components, which is achieved 
through BIM techniques. The digital shadow is the next stage, where the 
information from the physical twin have a uni-directional flow with the 
digital model. Digital twin, on the other hand, have a bi-directional 
flow of information between physical and digital components. For op -
erational stage, a DT simulates physical infrastructure/assets in a vir -
tual environment by integrating and processing dynamic real-time FM 
information in an integrated environment ( Lo et al., 2021 ). To max -
imize the benefits of the collected data, DTs develop machine learning 
(ML) and artificial intelligence (AI) models to predict the future of the 
infrastructure ( Hallaji et al., 2022 ).
Within this context, Li et al. (2024) proposed a four-level maturity 
framework for DT: descriptive twin, reflective twin, predictive twin, 
and prescriptive twin. Cotoarbă et al. (2025) linked these maturality 
level with the digital representations of the physical components. For 
instance, descriptive twin represents the physical components in a di -
gital environment, which corresponds to the digital model. Ad -
ditionally, the reflective twin depicts the current known state of the 
physical components linked with digital twin through uni-directional 
flow of information developing a digital shadow of the system. Pre -
dictive and prescriptive twins are high-level DTs, not only monitoring 
the physical components but also predicting the state and optimizing 
the process/actions. Predictive twin forecast the twin state, while pre -
scriptive twin optimizes the process using the predictions.
For tunnels, achieving a predictive twin requires integration of data 
from multiple systems, including IoT sensors, databases, BIM models, 
and historical systems. Due to the elongdated nature of the tunnels, 
developing a single tunnel model is not efficient since the tunnel state 
and predictions may vary along their alignment. A partitioned model, 
dividing the tunnel into different sections based on the data collections, 
state variation, and complexity of the system can contribute to achieve 
an accurate DT system. Partitioning the tunnel into manageable sec -
tions enables localized monitoring, anomaly detection, and main -
tenance forecasting at the component level. This allows more granular 
insights and a detailed breakdown of tunnel conditions, facilitating 
proactive and targeted maintenance. Combining IoT data, ML-driven 
predictive analytics, and a partitioned tunnel model leads to a higher 
DT maturity level and overcomes the shortcomings of prior FM ap -
proaches. Researchers have applied DTs to various operational stages of 
O&M activities, such as defect detection ( Omer et al., 2019 ), asset 
monitoring ( Ozturk, 2021 ), anomaly detection ( Lu et al., 2020b ), au -
tomatic control ( Akanmu et al., 2014 ), structural diagnosis 
( Tahmasebinia et al., 2019 ), CO

ciency by reducing time and cost, increasing accuracy, and improving 
communication during FM activities ( Zhao et al., 2022 ). However, 
applying DTs to tunnel O&M is challenging because of the complex 
nature of tunnel structures. Tunnel maintenance tasks are more com -
plicated than other infrastructure projects because they involve long 
underground infrastructures with complex interactions and de -

projects also present additional difficulties such as unpredictable geo -

2021; Wu et al., 2022 ). In addition, integrating and visualizing data 
from various sources, such as-built tunnels, the environment, main -

partitioned model is achieved, integrating mutli-source data with the 
DT; system is difficult in real-time. Additionally, the data update re -
quires the system to achieve and reflect the updated state and predic -
tions demanding a computationally efficient system.
In a recent study, a knowledge-driven framework was developed to 
propose a DT solution for tunneling the O&M process ( Yu et al., 2021 ). 
However, the proposed framework lacks self-learning capabilities that 
rely on expert knowledge and domain expertise and ignores environ -
mental data, which are essential for tunnel DT, particularly in the O&M 
stage. Existing studies also lack a partitioned tunnel data model for 
localized monitoring and a specialized classification system tailored for 
tunnel FM. Most importantly, tunnels require predictive maintenance 
strategies that can automatically detect and predict the anomalous 
conditions of an asset and take timely action, which are not addressed 
in the above frameworks. On the other hand, in a data-driven DT fra -
mework, maintenance actions can be predicted through the analysis of 
continuous data using advanced data analysis and ML algorithms that 
can significantly overcome these issues.
To address the issues and achieve a predictive twin for tunnel 
maintenance, a system is needed that segment the tunnel model into 
different units, defines a classification system to integrate the data ac -
cording to the tunnel components, where each tunnel components in -
tegrates their own data from multiple systems, and support state pre -
dictions using prediction model. This study aims to develop and 
implement a novel data-driven DT framework that integrates data from 
multiple system, proposes a unified classification system, that connect 
physical and virtual tunnel twin data, and uses ML to predict the twin 
maintenance state to improve the tunnel FM process. The contributions 
of the proposed research are as follows: 
(1) This research enhances resilience in tunnel FM by proposing a data- 
driven DT-based framework for tunneling FM. The framework col -
lects real-time O&M data of tunnel assets, integrates them with their 
digital replicas in the DT environment, and uses ML algorithms that 
learn from the collected data and predict the asset state at the 
component level, making it easier to perform operational analyses 
and plan maintenance actions.
(2) This paper proposes a flexible segmented tunnel data model that 
hierarchically divides tunnel components to efficiently locate and

2. Literature review
2.1. Tunnel FM
During the operational phase, inspection and maintenance activities 
play vital roles in the functioning of tunnel facilities ( Fan and Zhou, 
2021 ). Reactive and preventive maintenance are the commonly 
adopted strategies ( Hallaji et al., 2022 ). Reactive maintenance, also 
called corrective maintenance, is an unplanned maintenance activity 
adopted after damage occurs to an asset to recover its condition and 
make it functional. Reactive maintenance does not address the root 
cause of equipment failure. Therefore, the same problems can occur 
repeatedly, leading to increased downtime and repair costs. Scheduling 
repairs can also be difficult because unexpected breakdowns can occur 
at any time. These types of maintenance are avoided for assets that have 
a significant impact because they lead to high costs and service delays. 
Hence, preventive maintenance strategies have been adopted to miti -
gate the effects of reactive maintenance. These are preplanned or 
scheduled maintenance activities that check the status of an asset at 
regular intervals and prevent failures ( Sun et al., 2021 ). While pre -
ventive maintenance activities are substantially better than corrective 
maintenance, failures can still unexpectedly occur and assets may be 
replaced while still in a healthy state, as future conditions are not ac -
curately predicted ( Hosamo et al., 2022 ).
The predictive maintenance strategy employs data-driven analysis 
to predict the asset state. It uses continuous monitoring data from IoT 
sensors and past data from FM databases to predict asset conditions 
( Hallaji et al., 2022 ). Predictive maintenance enables the detection of 
anomalous asset behavior in near real-time, facilitating timely decision- 
making ( Fan and Zhou, 2021 ). Although some researchers have created 
frameworks for predicting the maintenance requirements of individual 
machines and MEP components in buildings, these frameworks are only 
suitable for single-component models ( Cheng et al., 2020; Hallaji et al., 
2022 ). Tunnel facilities are complex multicomponent systems, and 
frameworks designed for single components are often inadequate ( Jia 
et al., 2022 ). Furthermore, these studies focused solely on predicting 
the state of an individual component with little attention paid to the 
classification and visualization of physical entities into various main -
tenance categories. Consequently, the development of specialized pre -
dictive maintenance frameworks tailored to the unique characteristics 
of tunnel systems is necessary for effective maintenance management.
In addition to the maintenance process, tunnel systems face severe 
problems because of the use of traditional computerized FM systems. 
Current tunnel FM systems use FM data from multiple heterogeneous 
sources with complicated spatiotemporal relationships, mostly managed 
in multiple-format systems ( Chen et al., 2020 ). For example, if an asset 
requires maintenance, the historical information or manufacturing data 
and properties of the asset are retrieved from the CMMS, whereas the 
location or space information is collected from the space management 
system (SMS), each with a different data format and structure. In addi -
tion, manual queries for retrieving essential information are key pro -
blems that cause service delays. One of the main challenges with these 
systems is their lack of integration with other systems, particularly those 
that provide real-time monitoring and advanced visualization. This 
integration is critical for enabling operators and managers to better un -
derstand asset conditions and performance. In recent years, BIM has been 
used to enhance visualization during the tunnel FM stage ( Chen et al., 
2020 ). However, these studies typically focused on enhancing the vi -
sualization capabilities and did not fully address the challenge of in -
tegrating real-time condition data or supporting predictive maintenance. 
These problems indicate that tunnels require an integrated 3D-based 
digitalized maintenance management system that integrates real-time 
status information and predicts the asset state to support predictive 
maintenance and improve maintenance strategies.
2.2. DT for tunnels FM
The DT is a fast-growing technology in the civil engineering field, in 
which a physical asset has a digital counterpart that reflects its char -
acteristics and information management ( Opoku et al., 2021 ). DT is 
considered a promising digital technology that integrates static and 
dynamic information, connecting physical and digital assets using ad -
vanced digital and sensor technologies such as BIM, laser scanners, 
cameras, and IoTs. In the FM stage, any information required for the O& 
M activities can be efficiently retrieved from the DT ( Jiang et al., 2022 ). 
At its optimum, DT offers historical and current information about in -
frastructure as well as contextual details, such as environmental and 
geological data, which can have a more significant impact during op -
erational use ( Wu et al., 2022 ).
DTs are created for asset monitoring by collecting real-time data 
from the physical twin to receive updated information owing to var -
iations in the physical space within the tunnel. In the past, studies have 
been conducted to monitor underground infrastructure. For example, 
temperature and humidity in subway stations have been monitored 
using BIM and IoT systems ( Marzouk and Abdelaty, 2014 ). In another 
study, a BIM-based preliminary framework was proposed for sustain -
able utility tunnel O&M. However, these studies only focused on phy -
sical space monitoring, ignoring the spatial structure and monitoring of 
existing assets within the physical space. The decision regarding the 
monitored data was based on engineering expertise without a detailed 
data-driven analysis, making the systems highly subjective.
Valuable insights can be gained by analyzing the DT data. For ex -
ample, the as-built geometric information from DT has been used for 
bridge defect detection, retrofitting, and inspection that greatly im -
proves the detection and retrofitting processes ( Xu and Turkan, 2019 ). 
Bridge structural performance has been visualized using BIM and a 
large amount of IoT sensor data, which has improved effective decision- 
making capabilities ( Boddupalli et al., 2019 ). In addition, efforts have 
been made to analyze non-geometric information from the past and 
present for asset-state detection and prediction. For example, the vi -
bration frequency over time has been used to detect anomalous pump 
behaviors ( Lu et al., 2020b ). A data-driven framework has been pro -
posed for the predictive maintenance of MEP components and pumps to 
improve dynamic maintenance plans ( Cheng et al., 2020; Hallaji et al., 
2022 ).
Growing research on DT applications that provide a high-fidelity 
digital environment shows that smart infrastructure and maintenance 
strategies can be adopted efficiently in an updated manner. For ex -
ample, several ML algorithms can be used to predict asset states. 
Recently, artificial neural networks (ANN) are utilized to predict non -
linear time-series trends using data from databases and monitoring 
devices ( Cheng et al., 2020 ). An ANN has an advantage over traditional 
statistical methods for learning the nonlinear features of time-series 
data, and works efficiently in predicting an asset state. Similarly, sup -
port vector machines (SVM) have been used as decision support tools 
for predicting asset conditions ( Fan and Zhou, 2021 ). The precision of 
machine learning algorithms depends on the training dataset utilized 
for model training and improves with an increased number of datasets. 
In addition, the types of input parameters were selected for each 
component based on their physical twin characteristics.

Since DT are designed to mirror their physical counterparts, the 
attributes of the physical twin inevitably influence the architecture and 
design of DTs. In other words, the features and properties of a physical 
asset have a direct impact on the structure and function of its corre -
sponding DT. Therefore, it is essential to consider the characteristics of 
the physical twin while developing a DT to ensure that it accurately 
represents the real-world asset and can effectively replicate its behavior 
and performance. Tunnels are complex and elongated infrastructures 
surrounded by geological entities, and their DT can significantly im -
prove the productivity during FM tasks, particularly during the O&M 
phase. However, the application of DTs in tunnels is challenging and 
vastly different from that in buildings, bridges, and roads, which re -
quire specialized analysis techniques ( Jiang et al., 2022 ). The spatial 
structure, data collection, requirements for FM tasks (reactive, pre -
ventive, and predictive tasks), and the structure of data for creating a 
dynamic database in tunnels are unique. Currently, the application of 
DTs in tunnel O&M, particularly in supporting predictive maintenance 
strategies, has not been thoroughly examined. Hence, this article pro -
poses a framework that implement a DT for tunnel FM.
3. Research problem and methodology
Current FM systems have several limitations that impede their 
ability to effectively maintain tunnel facilities. First, maintenance ac -
tivities are reactive and conducted after facility failures occur, which is 
uneconomical and time-consuming, leading to increased downtime and 
reduced productivity. Secondly, routine inspections are conducted, 
which are planned based on manufacturing or construction data, and 
decisions are made using theoretical data or expert judgment instead of 
actual measured data (condition monitoring) from the field. These in -
spections may not capture the actual condition of the assets, leading to 
inadequate maintenance and increased risk of asset failure. Third, 
multiple computerized systems are used for FM data management, 
which often result in service delays in fulfilling requests, increasing 
downtime, and decreasing efficiency. Furthermore, data collected 
during facility inspection or maintenance are stored in fragmented and 
disconnected systems, making them difficult to access and analyze. 
Additionally, the collected data alone are insufficient for effective 
tunnel maintenance planning and execution. Advanced visualization, 
ML, and data analytics functions, are necessary for condition mon -
itoring, condition assessment, performance prediction, and decision 
support. Hence, the maintenance strategy must be changed from the 
traditional to a smart maintenance approach to address these limita -
tions (As-is vs. To-be in Fig. 1 ).
A data-driven DT-based system that can support 3D visualization, 
integration of real-time data, asset state prediction, and failure classi -
fication, is required to ensure better tunnel maintenance. The system 
presented in Fig. 1 is expected to enhance maintenance strategies 
adopting real-time conditions from sensors and other platforms to 
predict the maintenance state, enabling the detection and prevention of 
asset failures before they occur. Furthermore, maintenance quality can 
be improved by providing maintenance teams with 3D visualization, 
generating digital maintenance plans and work orders, and ensuring 
that maintenance tasks are consistently performed and at a high stan -
dard. A framework is proposed in this study to achieve the system 
shown in Fig. 1 (To-be system), which is shown in Fig. 2 . The DT fra -
mework proposed in this paper primarily comprises data acquisition, 
transmission, modeling, integration, analysis, and application layers. 
The details of each layer are as follows.
3.1. Data acquisition layer
Data acquisition layer collects and stores the data required for the 
operational stage of the tunnel. It collects design, construction, and 
operational phase information, such as day-to-day maintenance and 
operation data, through various methods, including monitoring and 
manual inspection, to gather the operational status of the tunnel 
system. Data are collected, including evaluations of the structural per -
formance and defects, maintenance records of each component, life 
cycle evaluations, quality control, major and minor repairs, replace -
ments, recommended upgrades, and contracts with contractors and 
installation companies for day-to-day operation and maintenance ac -
tivities. The as-built information on civil, structural, and functional 
systems, was monitored in real-time to provide a reliable, safe, and 
efficient level of service.
The tunnel FM data for DT are divided into physical and digital data. 
The physical data include tunnel physical entities, data from installed 
IoT monitoring devices, and manual inspection and maintenance data, 
which are available in various formats. Real-time IoT operational data 
are collected for physical spaces, structural entities, and functional 
components to ensure safe and effective tunnel services.
The digital data are obtained from the BIM tunnel models and the 
FM system. BIM models contain physical tunnel geometrical and non- 
geometrical information that portray the as-built condition. The FM 
system includes historical tunnel maintenance-related information, 
such as component age, installation date, installation cost, time of in -
spection, maintenance per year, and cost of maintenance.
3.2. Data processing/transmission layer
The transmission layer acquires physical and digital data from the 
data source layer and provides them to the other layers. It transmits 
physical data received from IoT sensors, such as temperature, humidity, 
vibration, airflow, velocity, and settlement, as well as manual inspec -
tion and maintenance data in various formats, from the physical part of 
the tunnel to the digital part. In addition, the transmission layer 
transfers digital data, such as geometrical and non-geometrical tunnel 
information from BIM models and historical maintenance data from the 
FM system, to the data modeling layer. This layer plays a crucial part in 
linking the physical and digital parts of the tunnel DT and requires 
various communication technologies, including short-range and wide- 
coverage access networks, to ensure efficient and reliable data transfer. 
Moreover, the transmission layer can use an application programming 
interface (API) to enable seamless and secure data exchange between 
systems, such as historical data from databases or BIM data, to the DT 
system.
3.3. Digital modeling layer
Digital modeling layer is used to build the virtual counterpart of 
each physical facility in a digital environment. It leverages BIM cap -
abilities to model virtual assets, supplementing nongeometric in -
formation that is significant for O&M operations. Furthermore, it de -
velops a relational database that collects real-time data through a 
transmission layer that can be integrated with the BIM for asset status 
visualization. A flexible partitioned 3D-based data model is proposed 
for tunnel assets, making it easy for stakeholders to efficiently locate, 
link, retrieve, and update FM information during maintenance activ -
ities. A voxel-based representation is used for the tunnel surrounding 
the geological environment to show nonhomogenous characteristics for 
comprehensive tunnel DT. The BIM virtual tunnel assets and FM se -
mantic data are visualized in a digital environment to analyze the op -
erational state of the tunnel elements along with the surrounding en -
vironment in more detail to make better decisions. The details of 
creating the 3D environment are provided in the next section.
3.4. Data/model integration
Data/model layer integrates tunnel multisource spatiotemporal data 
into a single collaborative updated platform. In this context, it in -
tegrates BIM data and historical tunnel FM information with current 
monitoring and O&M information. The data and models in the

3.5. Analytical layer
Analytical layer leverages data analytics and ML to make data-

3.6. Application layer
The application layer is the implementation layer that enables 
connections between the stakeholders and the tunnel DT system. It 
provides different services to facility managers, which serve as a basis 
for decision-making. The proposed DT system supports tunnel main -
tenance activities such as reactive, preventive, and predictive main -
tenance. In reactive maintenance, after a failure has occurred in an 
asset, the DT service creates an alarm by visualizing the asset and au -
tomatically creates a work order retrieving the maintenance informa -
tion and other historical working conditions of the asset, including 
installation date, manufacturer information, reason for failure, mate -
rials, and properties. These details can help FM managers to repair and 
maintain faulty components. Furthermore, it keeps the system updated 
by incorporating the details of the faulty component into a database 
that can be used for future predictions.
Managers use DT services in preventive maintenance to determine 
the date, location, and maintenance assets. The managers click on the 
maintenance asset and list all the required details. This service further 
helps assess the health status of each planned segment by integrating 
real-time information through condition monitoring. Most importantly, 
the maintenance assets and surrounding environment represented by 
the as-built voxels are visualized in the DT system for a better under -
standing of the task. The predictive maintenance services are used to 
predict the future state of an asset. It predicts the maintenance state of a 
facility using ML and data analytics based on the collected data. In 
addition, it detects anomalous behavior of an asset from real-time IoT 
data.

web-based tunnel FM system that links the digital and physical twins for FM 
decision-making. It connects the digital and physical twins realizing in -
formation exchange among the BIM virtual models, IoT-based monitoring 
system, and FM database. In addition, the system provides a user-friendly 
digital platform that supports visualization, information integration, and 
analysis, generates task alerts and work orders, assigns FM tasks and re -

The DT framework first develops BIM virtual models represented by 
a partitioned model (explained in Section 4.1). Each partitioned seg -

Subsequently, a DT system is developed that contains historical FM 
information along with information from the BIM model and real-time

Real-time monitoring data are obtained from physical spaces as well as 
from individual critical components. Information from BIM, historical 
FM, and monitoring systems is integrated with the developed database. 
To address the issue of integrating and synchronizing information from 
multiple systems, a unique identifier (ID) is used. A classification 
system specialized for the unique characteristics of the tunnel system 
was proposed in this study for efficient information exchange among 
the systems (detailed in Section 4.3). The classification system is de -
veloped based on the partitioned model, where each tunnel segment/ 
component and their corresponding data from the three systems share 
same ID. The proposed DT system uses data collected from various 
sources and applies ML algorithms to predict the asset state (detailed in 
Section 4.5). The model and information are visualized in a DT 
environment, which makes it easy for managers to make FM decisions 
(see Section 4.4). Finally, a web-based user interface based on a typical 
client/server (C/S) was designed with advanced functions to support 
FM tasks and provide updated real-time information to end users. This 
interface retrieves data from the central database utilizing IDs, guar -
anteeing that modifications in BIM, FM, or IoT sources are consistently 
and very instantaneously represented across all views.
4.1. BIM models
BIM provides a virtual environment that plays a significant role in the 
tunnel DT. In this context, BIM plays a twofold role. First, it serves as the 
input data for the DT. Second, it functions as a graphical representation of 
the results obtained from the aforementioned procedure, thereby providing 
visualization. For optimal performance, the proposed framework requires 
access to a comprehensive tunnel BIM database containing all the relevant 
information. Creating a holistic tunnel model, including ground, structural,

4.2. Tunnel facility classification system
For efficient information exchange between the BIM model, mon -
itoring system, and developed FM database, a relationship must be 
established. The developed DT system proposes a simplified unique ID 
based on a partitioned 3D data model. The proposed partitioned data 
model is based on a dynamic tree model, which forms the foundation 
for developing unique identifiers for link systems. The proposed unique 
IDs are combinations of English letters and Arabic numerals. It consists 
of four levels starting with the tunnel number, partition or segment 
number, component type (civil, structural, and functional), and asset 
name and type. Fig. 6 presents the classification method for developing 
unique IDs and an example of a ventilation fan as the functional com -
ponent in the 12th segment of No. 05 tunnel. This ID serves as the 
primary key in FM relational database tables, is connected as metadata 
in sensor data streams, and is included as a property in BIM models. 
Cross-referencing between systems with various data structures and 
update frequencies is made possible by this consistent ID model.

4.4. Condition prediction
The aim of predicting tunnel facility conditions is to provide deci -
sion support for maintenance planning to avoid asset failures. The 
process of condition prediction using ML consists of the following steps 
( Fig. 9 ): (a) dataset collection, (b) selection of an ML algorithm, (c) 
prediction of the asset state, and (d) decision making. The dataset for 
the proposed DT system is collected from three systems: a database, 
BIM models, and sensor data. The database provides historical FM in -
formation such as past maintenance records, work order information, 
and inspection data. It captures degredation trends and intervention 
histories. BIM provides as-built information such as facility type, loca -
tion, material data, strength data, geometrical data, and construction or 
install information. These attributes reflects the physical and design- 
related factors influencing settlement and deterioration. The sensor 
data include real-time information collected by the system and previous 
data stored in the system, which directly capture the as-built opera -
tional status of the components. Information is collected and analyzed 
for each planned segment. The segmented model allows prediction of 
each component individually allowing more granular insights. Each 
category of the features add values for the prediction model develop -
ment. For instance, FM data reflects the past deterioration cycles, BIM 
provides physical and spatial information of assets, and sensor data 
reflect the operational status.
Every feature vector utilized for ML prediction is indexed by the ID 
and time to guarantee that the dataset compiled from BIM, FM, and IoT is 
synchronized and consistent. By doing this, data drift and misalignment 
are avoided and all predictor variables are guaranteed to correlate to the 
same physical asset and time period. Before training and prediction, pre- 
processing scripts make sure that FM logs (low-frequency), IoT readings 
(high-frequency), and BIM attributes (static) are correctly integrated by 
ID and arranged chronologically. Additionally, when fresh IoT data be -
comes available, the model may be incrementally retrained using this ID- 
based synchronization, preserving historical consistency.
For the predictions, support vector machine (SVM) and artificial 
neural network (ANN) were selected as the primary algorithms. These 
two are selected for their appropriateness in the non-linear and multi -
modal data pattern such as those in tunnels (e.g., settlement data). SVM 
is effective for small and noisy datasets, which is common in construction 
datasets. Since, our dataset contains multiple parameters from multi -
source ( Fig. 9 ), SVM can also captures nonlinear relationship between 
input parameters and output parameters using kernel-based mapping. In 
our study, we used radial basis function (RBF) because of its strong 
performance in nonlinear regression tasks. The regularization parameter 
(C) and kernel coefficient (gamma) were tuned through a grid search 
combined with 5 fold cross validation on the training dataset.

5. Case study
A tunnel case study was conducted to implement the proposed data- 
driven DT-based FM system. The case study tunnel was in an opera -
tional state and consisted of several pieces of electromechanical 
equipment intended to eliminate harmful gases. Simultaneously, the 
tunnel is exposed to settlement that can cause structural damage be -
cause it is located on soft ground. The maintenance in the case study 
tunnel is based on manual scheduling and using traditional FM system 
that lack 3D visualization and real-time monitoring of the physical as -
sets. In addition, the system has no functions to predict the asset state 
and enable effective preventive maintenance. This case study was se -
lected to implement the proposed data-driven DT framework, which 
connects physical and virtual tunnel twins and improves the FM deci -
sion-making process.
5.1. DT-based FM system implementation
The implementation of the proposed DT system comprises the fol -
lowing steps: (a) virtual modeling of existing facilities, (b) development 
of a DT-based FM interface, (c) integration of virtual and physical data 
with the developed FM system, and (d) scenario implementations.
The first step is to build a virtual twin model that virtualizes all the 
facilities inside the tunnel and inputs the FM-related attributes and 
information. Autodesk Revit software was used to develop the virtual 
twins using the BIM method according to the LODs defined in Table 1 . 
The integration of design and construction information was achieved 
and is discussed in our previous article ( Sharafat et al., 2021 ). The 
virtual twin information is integrated with the DT system (explained in 
Section 5.2 ). A DT system was developed for the case study tunnel 
based on the framework described in the Methodology section. The 
proposed web-based graphical user interface (GUI) for the DT system is 
shown in Fig. 10 .
The GUI of the proposed DT-based FM system included a tree 
structure for efficient management of the tunnel entities, a 3D view of 
the BIM model, and information collected through the sensors and FM 
database. The right part of the GUI presents the tree structure, which 
demonstrates that the system hierarchically stores and manages tunnel 
assets. A hierarchical structure was developed for the case study using 
the proposed DT system (Section 4.1). The first level of the structure 
divides the tunnel into several parts because it is a geographically long 
distributed project. The second level of the tree structure includes in -
formation on the different structural and functional facilities located 
and installed in a specific section. The system is sufficiently flexible 
such that other levels can be achieved according to the conditions, 
depending on the management purpose. The GUI contains a menu bar
