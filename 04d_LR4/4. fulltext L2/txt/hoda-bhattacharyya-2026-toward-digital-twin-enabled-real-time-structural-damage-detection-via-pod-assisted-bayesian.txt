Abstract
Accurate and timely detection of structural damage is essential for ensuring the safety and integrity of civil infrastruc-
ture. While Bayesian filtering has been widely adopted in structural health monitoring (SHM) for estimating system
states and parameters, full-scale parameter estimation can often result in biased outcomes and significant computational
costs. T o overcome these limitations, this study proposes a framework toward digital twin-enabled SHM by integrating
proper orthogonal decomposition (POD) with Bayesian filtering. The framework uses displacement and excitation mea-
surements from the system to estimate states in real-time, updated proper orthogonal mode (POM) slopes, and the
location and severity of damage. Structural damage localization is achieved by continuously monitoring changes in the
slope of the first POM, which is updated in real time using an online singular value decomposition (SVD) algorithm.
Simultaneously, the Unscented Kalman Filter (UKF) estimates system states and selectively updates stiffness parameters,
focusing only on degrees of freedom identified as damaged by comparing the evolving POM slopes with a baseline refer-
ence derived from the healthy structure. This coordinated operation of online SVD and UKF enables efficient, accurate,
and robust assessment of structural condition in real time. The proposed framework addresses three critical stages of
SHM that is, detection, localization, and quantification. Its performance is validated through comprehensive numerical
simulations on an eight-story shear frame under various damage scenarios, including adjacent-story, non-adjacent, and
progressive damage cases. Furthermore, validation on a laboratory-scale three-dimensional steel frame confirms its
practical applicability. The results demonstrate that the proposed approach can reliably localize and quantify multiple
damage events, offering a scalable solution for real-time SHM.

Introduction
A digital twin (DT) is a virtual replica of a physical
system that uses real-time sensor data to reflect its
behavior and evolving state. Advances in the Internet
of Things (IoT), machine learning, and computational
modeling have established DTs as transformative tools
across engineering disciplines. Glaessgen and Stargel
1
define a DT as ‘‘an integrated multiphysics, multiscale,
probabilistic simulation of an as-built system that uses
the best available physical models, sensor updates, fleet
history, etc., to mirror the life of its corresponding
physical twin.’’ A DT typically integrates three
core components: physical system data, physical or
surrogate models, and synchronization mechanisms to
align the virtual and physical domains.
2,3 Applications
span manufacturing, 4 healthcare,5 smart cities, 6,7
environmental monitoring,8 and civil infrastructure.9–11 In
civil infrastructure, DTs s how significant promise for
structural health monitoring (SHM), where integrated
data-driven and physics-based methods enable continuous
assessment of structural integrity.
Among various SHM approaches, vibration-based
techniques are widely adopted in SHM due to their
non-invasive nature and sensitivity to structural
changes. These methods rely on dynamic

Modal-based damage detection remains popular for its
simplicity, interpretability, and effectiveness. While
shifts in natural frequencies can signal damage by indi-

14 therefore, mode shapes and

Early studies 15 demonstrated that curvature mode
shapes exhibit strong sensitivity to local stiffness reduc-

Other studies have validated the effectiveness of
curvature-based methods through extensive numerical
and experimental investigations, further establishing
their robustness for practical damage detection appli-
cations.
16 Later investigations 17 compared curvature-
based indicators with damage locating vector methods
and confirmed the strong localization capability of cur-
vature features across different damage scenarios.
Subsequent research has further refined curvature-
based approaches through enhanced numerical and
analytical formulations to improve damage localiza-
tion accuracy under weak damage scenarios.
18 Reviews
have emphasized the importance of such mode-shape-
based indicators in capturing subtle integrity
changes.
19–23 Their use spans various structures,
including beams, 24–26 shear buildings, 27 laminated
plates,28 and general plate structures. 29 Techniques
such as modal strain energy with hybrid optimization
have enhanced damage detection in steel frames, 30
while mode shape slope and curvature-based formula-
tions have enabled localization and severity estimation
of damaged zones. 31,32 Perturbation-based and slope
ratio approaches further highlight strong correlations
between damage and changes in the fundamental mode
shape.27,32 Although higher modes offer increased sen-
sitivity to localized damage, 33,34 their extraction in
real-world conditions remains challenging.35
Extending the concept of mode shape-based damage
detection, data-driven methods have been introduced
that utilize similar spatial indicators such as slope and
curvature but apply them to statistical vibration modes
extracted directly from measured data. One such tech-
nique is proper orthogonal decomposition (POD),
which yields a set of orthogonal spatial patterns known
as proper orthogonal modes (POMs). POM derivatives
(slope and curvature) effectively highlight localized
damage through abrupt spatial variations. Similar to
mode shape curvature in traditional analysis, POM
curvature enhances sensitivity to damage. For instance,
POD has been integrated with optimization for dam-
age localization in shear type buildings
36 and with
POMs to identify damage in composite beams, 37 treat-
ing POMs as dynamic invariants to reduce
environmental influence. POM-based features have
been implemented in a field-programmable gate array-
enabled unsupervised SHM framework for a bridge
structure, demonstrating their effectiveness in identify-
ing structural damage.
38 A baseline-free method using
POM changes in guided wavefields showed high accu-
racy across damage scenarios. 39 POMs from frequency
response functions have also proven effective in beam
damage detection.40 Additionally, POD combined with
neural networks has enabled damage detection in
bridges and buildings, demonstrating strong adaptabil-
ity across structural types and data conditions.
41–43
Although modal and POD-based techniques effec-
tively detect structural damage, their offline nature lim-
its real-time SHM applications. To overcome this,
Bayesian filtering methods offer recursive, real-time
estimation of states and parameters.
9,44,45 The Kalman
Filter (KF) 46 provides optimal estimation for linear
systems with known inputs and sparse measurements
and is widely applied in response reconstruction and
state estimation.
47–49 However, its linearity assumption
limits use in nonlinear systems. The Extended Kalman
Filter (EKF) addresses this by linearizing dynamics
around current estimates.
50 EKF has been used for
fatigue life prediction in plates under coupled loading 51
and for modal identification in systems with tuned
mass dampers, 52 though its performance degrades in
strongly nonlinear cases. To improve robustness, the
Unscented Kalman Filter (UKF) was developed,
53–55
employing the unscented transform (UT) to capture
nonlinearities more accurately without linearization.
UKF maintains recursive estimation and suits non-
linear SHM applications. Recent developments include
DT frameworks using KFs for time-variant model
identification via GSINDy,
56 validated on the Lorenz
system and an industrial hydrotreating unit. Other
DTs using KFs have been proposed for state monitor-
ing and anomaly detection,
57 and for joint input-state-
parameter estimation via nonlinear minimum variance
unbiased filters validated with vision-based
measurements.
58
Hierarchical Bayesian filtering using augmented
Kalman filters (AKF) and particle filters (PF) has been
integrated with parametric reduced-order models to
enable joint state, input, and parameter estimation
under output-only measurements.
59 AKF has also been
coupled with a substructuring approach to estimate
strain responses at unmeasured locations of a roll-
ercoaster connection for remaining fatigue life predic-
tion.
60 Gaussian process latent force models have been
employed for virtual sensing in wind turbine towers,
providing stable and accurate estimation of dynamic
strains and damage-equivalent loads compared with
conventional KF formulations.
61 Despite their advan-
tages, Bayesian filters face challenges as the number of

unknown parameters increases, often leading to biased
and unstable estimates. 62 To address this, POD has
been integrated with Bayesian filtering for dimension-
ality reduction. A combined POD–Bayesian filtering
framework was proposed for real-time SHM in a
reduced-order space,
63 where a KF updated the POMs
and an EKF estimated states and parameters. Damage

Similarly, a real-time SHM method using online singu-

operating in the reduced space. While effective for
tracking stiffness changes, these approaches estimate in
a transformed domain, lacking direct physical inter-

Traditional Bayesian filtering techniques, though
effective for real-time state and parameter estimation,
are computationally intensive as they track all para-
meters continuously. Similarly, existing POD Bayesian
frameworks rely on reduced-order models lacking
physical interpretability. To overcome these limita-
tions, this study presents a framework toward DT-
enabled SHM by combining online POD with Bayesian
filtering for real-time SHM. Unlike conventional POD
approaches, the method uses online SVD to extract
POMs and track their slopes relative to a healthy base-
line as damage-sensitive features in online manner.
Damage detection triggers localized Bayesian para-
meter estimation, reducing computational cost and
retaining physical interpretability. This adaptive strat-
egy enables efficient, real-time monitoring and accurate
localization of structural damage, as demonstrated
through numerical and experimental validation.

stiffness parameters at the identified damage locations.
This targeted strategy avoids full parameter estimation,
reduces computational cost, and improves accuracy in
real-time SHM.

Online SVD
When dealing with dynamic data, updating POD or
recomputing the entire SVD from scratch is computation-
ally expensive and time-intensive. Traditional SVD also
requires storing and processing the entire dataset simulta-
neously, further increasing computational demands. To
address these challenges, researchers have demonstrated
that online SVD is an effective solution.
69–72
Online SVD initializes the decomposition using a
small subset of data in the conventional manner. As
new data become available, it updates existing SVD
results rather than re-computing them from the begin-
ning. This incremental approach significantly reduces
computational costs and resource requirements. This
sequential updating method has proven effective in var-
ious applications involving continuous data streams.
By leveraging prior calculations instead of restarting
the process for each update, online SVD enhances effi-
ciency in handling evolving datasets.

POD-assisted UKF algorithm
This study proposes a novel methodology that inte-
grates POD with the UKF for real-time damage locali-
zation and quantification in dynamic systems. The
Hoda and Bhattacharyya 7
overall flow of the proposed approach is illustrated in
Figure 1. A healthy POM slope is considered as the
baseline and can be computed using either numerically
simulated responses or experimental data.
Although POD is generally considered insensitive to
loading conditions, 63 minor variations in the POMs
can still occur due to changes in excitation and system
uncertainties. To account for this variability, a number
of simulations are conducted under different realiza-
tions of zero-mean white Gaussian excitation with unit
covariance. For each simulation, the first POM is
extracted and its slope is computed. The baseline POM
slope ( _f) is then defined as the mean of all computed
POM slopes plus four times their standard deviation.
This statistically informed threshold ensures a conser-
vative and robust reference, allowing clear differentia-
tion between normal structural variability and changes
indicative of damage.
In the online setting, POD is employed to compute
POMs and compute its spatial derivative in real time
using an online SVD approach. In this study, only the
first POM is used, and references to POMs henceforth
refer specifically to this mode unless otherwise stated.
Damage localization is achieved by comparing the
evolving POM slope ( _U ) during system operation with
the established baseline. Deviations beyond the base-
line indicate potential damage, and the associated
DOFs are flagged as affected as shown in Figure 1. The
UKF operates in parallel with the online SVD, initially
estimating only the structural states (e.g., displacements
and velocities). Once damage is detected, the UKF
dynamically augments its state vector to include stiff-
ness parameters corresponding only to the affected
DOFs. This selective augmentation avoids unnecessary
parameter estimation across all DOFs, thereby reduc-
ing computational burden and maintaining numerical
stability. The state dimension increases adaptively
based on the number of damaged locations detected in
real time. This adaptive estimation strategy offers sig-
nificant advantages over conventional approaches. The
proposed framework significantly reduces computa-
tional cost by avoiding full-parameter estimation. Its
real time capability facilitates continuous monitoring
and timely detection of structural changes. The com-
bined use of online SVD for damage localization and
UKF for selective parameter estimation ensures

This approach effectively addresses three out of the
four standard stages of SHM: detection, localization,
and quantification excluding only the prediction of
remaining useful life. By focusing estimation efforts
solely on the affected DOFs, the framework strikes a
compelling balance between computational efficiency
and accuracy, rendering it highly suitable for real time
SHM applications. Furthermore, to clarify the effi-
ciency of the proposed method, we report the computa-
tional effort of the two main components of the
framework in the Appendix section. Although both
components, (i) the online SVD for updating the
POMs and (ii) the UKF for estimating state and stiff-
ness parameters, operate simultaneously within the
proposed framework, they contribute to the total cost
in different ways. The POMs update requires repeated
decomposition of the response data; therefore, a com-
parison of the cost of full SVD with the online SVD is
presented. The full SVD becomes increasingly expen-
sive as the response matrix grows, whereas the online
SVD updates the basis using only the newest snapshot
and avoids storing past data. The second component is
the state and parameter-estimation step. The computa-
tional cost associated with the classical UKF and the
POD-assisted UKF is presented. The computational
cost of the UKF increases cubically with the number of
parameters being estimated. The classical UKF updates
all stiffness parameters, while the POD-assisted updates
only those associated with the detected damage. This
leads to a smaller augmented state and reduction in
computation cost.

Conclusions
This study presents a real-time framework for struc-
tural damage detection and state estimation by com-
bining data-driven damage localization with Bayesian
filtering, with direct relevance to DT-enabled SHM.
Specifically, damage was identified by monitoring
changes in the slope of the first POM, which was
updated continuously using an online SVD algorithm.
Simultaneously, UKF was employed to estimate the
full structural state and update stiffness parameters
only at the localized damage locations. This parallel
operation of damage localization and parameter esti-
mation enabled real time and efficient monitoring of
the system. Compared to traditional methods that
required full parameter estimation or relied on prede-
fined damage models, the proposed approach offered
several key advantages. First, localizing damage prior
to estimation reduced computational complexity and
enhanced numerical stability. Second, selective updat-
ing of parameters improved robustness in the predic-
tion of parameters. Lastly, the real-time integration of
online SVD and UKF supported early detection and
continuous monitoring, making the framework well-
suited for real-time SHM applications.
The effectiveness of the methodology was demon-
strated through both numerical and calibrated three-
dimensional steel building studies. The numerical
investigation included an eight-story shear frame sub-
jected to various damage scenarios, such as adjacent,
non-adjacent, and progressi ve stiffness reductions.
The calibrated study was carried out on a six-degree-of-
freedom lumped mass model derived from a
laboratory-scale three-dimensional steel building. To
emulate realistic field conditions, white Gaussian noise
was added to the displacement responses, with the noise
variance set to 5% of the signal’s variance. Despite the
presence of measurement noise, the framework consis-
tently achieves accurate damage localization and
reliable estimation of the system states and stiffness
parameters, demonstrating its practical relevance for
continuous structural condition assessment.
A key limitation of the present framework is the
assumption that the system is fully observable in terms
of displacement responses. In real-world applications,
such complete measurement coverage may be challen-
ging due to restrictions on sensor deployment, instru-
mentation cost, and accessibility. Nevertheless,
emerging non-contact measurement technologies par-
ticularly computer vision–based methods offer a pro-
mising pathway to obtain full displacement data with
minimal physical instrumentation. In addition to this
measurement limitation, the current implementation
assumes that the input excitation is known, which may
not always hold under ambient or operational loading.
Future work will focus on relaxing both assumptions
by integrating sparse-sensor strategies, computer
vision-based displacement acquisition, and input esti-
mation techniques. Further developments will also
explore incorporating multiple POMs to enhance loca-
lization sensitivity and broaden the applicability of the
framework to more complex structural configurations.
