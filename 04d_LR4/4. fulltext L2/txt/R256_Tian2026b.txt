ABSTRACT
Digital twin technology has emerged as a promising paradigm for advancing instrumentation and measurement 
in dam safety monitoring. However, existing models often face significant cross-domain discrepancies between 
numerical simulations (source domain) and in-situ measurement data (target domain), which constrain pre -
diction accuracy and generalization under varying operating conditions. To address these challenges, this study 
proposes a novel cross-domain adaptive digital twin framework that integrates graph attention networks, gating 
mechanisms, and feature alignment strategies to enhance measurement consistency and predictive reliability. 
Multi-source numerical simulation domains are systematically constructed to enrich input variability and 
improve measurement-domain coverage. The proposed framework is comprehensively validated through abla -
tion studies and comparisons with six state-of-the-art baseline models, achieving superior accuracy with mean 
errors as low as 0.44 mm and 0.99 %, thereby demonstrating excellent measurement precision. Furthermore, 
validation under extreme water-level and temperature scenarios confirms strong generalization, with the in -
clusion of extreme-condition samples further reducing errors and mitigating prediction degradation at critical 
monitoring points. The results show that the proposed approach provides a physically consistent, generalizable, 
and robust digital twin measurement framework for dam structures, enabling reliable real-time deformation 
monitoring and safety evaluation. These findings establish a foundation for intelligent, large-scale, and multi- 
condition applications of digital twins in civil infrastructure monitoring and measurement science.
1. Introduction
With the continuous advancement of digital technologies driving the 
transformation and upgrading of hydraulic engineering, the construc -
tion of high dams is facing unprecedented technical challenges and 
demands [ 1,2 ]. Along with the in-depth implementation of China ’ s 
Western Development Strategy, a series of ultra-high dams have been 
successively constructed and put into operation, such as the Jinping-I 
Arch Dam (305 m), the Lianghekou Dam (292 m), the Baihetan Arch 
Dam (279 m), and the Shuangjiangkou Dam (305 m) [ 3,4 ]. These pro -
jects are characterized by massive dam volumes, enormous reservoir 
capacities, and high stress levels, which impose stringent requirements 
on real-time monitoring and safety assessment [ 5 – 7 ]. However, as dam 
structures become increasingly complex and large in scale, the limita -
tions of traditional monitoring and management approaches in terms of 
data acquisition, information integration, and state prediction are 
becoming more prominent. Therefore, the adoption of advanced digital 
twin technology to achieve comprehensive real-time monitoring and in- 
depth data analysis is expected to provide strong support for accurate 
perception of structural states, enhanced safety assurance, and opti -
mized operational decision-making [ 8,9 ].
Structural Health Monitoring (SHM) methods have been widely 
applied in the safety evaluation of dams and can be generally classified 
into data-driven and mechanism-driven models [10] . Data-driven ap -
proaches, including Hydrostatic-Seasonal-Time (HST) models and ma -
chine learning techniques, focus on statistical correlations, while 
mechanism-driven models rely on finite element analysis and reli -
ability theory to assess structural risk [11] . Recent advances in deep 
learning have enhanced the modelling capacity of traditional HST 
methods [12] , with techniques such as clustering, support vector

represent temporal and spatial correlations but remain constrained by 
data availability and interpretability. Consequently, existing data- 
driven approaches are limited to localized monitoring points and 
cannot fully reflect the global behavior of large-scale dams, under -
scoring the need for an integrated digital twin framework that fuses 
simulation and monitoring data for comprehensive dam safety 
assessment.
In the context of Industry 4.0, digital twin technology provides a 
novel solution for global real-time monitoring of dams [ 17,18 ]. By 
integrating Building Information Modelling (BIM) and SHM technolo -
gies, digital twins enable the construction of high-fidelity virtual rep -
licas of engineering structures and facilitate the perception of structural 
states and the prediction of potential damage [ 19 – 21 ]. Through the 
combination of physical models, sensor data, and artificial intelligence 
techniques, digital twins realize real-time synchronization of physical 
system states and trend prediction [ 22,23 ]. The core architecture con -
sists of physical entities, digital models, data transmission, real-time 
interaction, and intelligent analysis [24] . The introduction of deep 
learning and reinforcement learning endows the system with adaptive 
learning capabilities and enables intelligent decision-making and opti -
mization [25] . A major advantage of digital twins lies in their ability to 
perform testing and optimization within a low-cost and low-risk virtual 
environment, thereby reducing uncertainties during the operation and 
maintenance of actual engineering projects [ 26,27 ].
In recent years, the concept of the digital twin has evolved rapidly 
across various engineering disciplines, highlighting the integration of 
physical modelling, numerical simulation, and data-driven learning. As 
noted by de Wilde, the boundaries between building performance 
simulation, machine learning, and digital twins are conceptually

At present, research on digital twin models for dam structures is still 
at an early exploratory stage [33] . Existing studies are mainly based on 
Geographic Information Systems (GIS) and BIM to construct geospatial 
digital twin models of dams and river systems [34] . Although such 
methods can achieve high-precision digitalization of engineering 
structures, their practical application remains limited due to the lack of 
real-time dynamic responses to environmental changes. Other scholars 
have applied vision-based stereo camera systems for real-time defor -
mation monitoring of gates. While such approaches provide significant 
value for external safety assessment [ 35,36 ], they fail to capture changes 
in the internal structural state and therefore offer limited support for 
comprehensive structural health monitoring [ 37 ].
Some studies have constructed digital twin models based on nu -
merical simulation within the Finite Element Method (FEM) [ 38,39 ]. 
However, such approaches often require extensive calibration of mate -
rial parameters and structural simplifications [ 40,41 ], and involve 
computationally demanding iterative equation solving [ 42 ], which 
constrains both efficiency and accuracy. To overcome these challenges, 
recent studies have proposed FEM-deep learning-coupled digital twin 
frameworks for analyzing dam deformation, seepage, and stress evolu -
tion, demonstrating the feasibility of physics-informed digital twins for 
dam safety monitoring [ 43,44 ]. These models combine data-driven and 
physics-based approaches. Nevertheless, their accuracy is still restricted 
by domain discrepancies between the numerical simulation source 
domain and the monitoring data target domain, as illustrated in Fig. 1
[45] . Furthermore, the extrapolation and generalization capabilities of 
data-driven models remain to be validated [44] . Therefore, digital twin 
methodologies in the field of dam structural safety monitoring still face 
critical technical bottlenecks, and their construction methods require 
further investigation.
At present, cross-domain adaptive modelling from numerical simu -
lation source domains to monitoring data target domains in dam digital 
twins remains largely unexplored. Although studies in related subfields, 
such as dam image recognition, have attempted to employ transfer 
learning [ 46 – 48 ] and meta -learning [49] to achieve domain adaptation 
and generalization to mitigate data scarcity, existing work has primarily 
focused on pre-training and fine-tuning frameworks for cross-site crack 
detection [ 50,51 ] or condition monitoring [ 52,53 ]. Due to the absence 
of quantitative characterization of cross-domain discrepancies and high- 
dimensional feature alignment, the applicability of these methods to 
cross-domain modelling in digital twins is still limited [54] .
In summary, current digital twin models of dam structures are pri -
marily constructed based on numerical simulation and deep learning. 
The accuracy of these models is affected by cross-domain discrepancies 
between simulation-based source data and monitoring-based target 
data. At the same time, the extrapolation and generalization capacity of 
deep learning approaches remains uncertain. To address these issues, 
the present study proposes a cross-domain adaptive method to mitigate 
discrepancies between source and target domains, thereby improving 
the accuracy of digital twin models established through numerical 
simulation and deep learning. In addition, the proposed method is

3. Case study
3.1. Project overview
In this study, as shown in Fig. 4 , the DS Dam and PB Dam, both 
located on the mainstream of the Dadu River in Sichuan Province and 
serving as important components of cascade hydropower projects, are 
selected as validation cases. The DS Dam is a 210 m-high double- 
curvature arch dam, forming the 14th cascade hydropower station on 
the Dadu River and functioning as a daily regulation reservoir. The dam 
has a total installed capacity of 2,600 MW, with a normal reservoir water 
level of 1,130.00 m and a dead water level of 1,120.00 m. Safety 
monitoring is conducted using the plumb-line method, with multiple 
monitoring points arranged at typical dam sections (#6, #10, #14, #19, 
#24) as well as in the grout galleries at the dam abutments. Monitoring 
results show that the reservoir exhibits a high frequency but small 
amplitude of daily water-level fluctuations. The deformation of the dam 
is influenced by both the periodic effects of temperature and water level 
and a long-term gradual trend.
The PB Dam is a rockfill dam with a gravel-soil core wall, with a 
maximum height of 186 m and a total storage capacity of 5.337 billion 
m
3
, serving as an annual regulation reservoir. The dam crest elevation is 
856.00 m, with a crest length of 540.5 m. It adopts a composite slope 
configuration, with a cross-section consisting of a gravel-soil core wall, 
filter layers, transition zones, and outer rockfill zones. Cable-type hori -
zontal displacement meters are installed within the dam body to monitor 
layered displacement in the downstream transition zone and the rockfill 
zone of the core wall. A total of 40 sensors, organized into eight sets 
(CH1-CH40), are deployed, with some installed in Section 210 + at el -
evations of 808 m, 758 m, and 731 m. Monitoring data reveal a clear 
periodic horizontal deformation pattern strongly correlated with reser -
voir water-level fluctuations, as well as an irreversible gradual defor -
mation trend, reflecting the time-dependent behaviors of the fill 
materials, such as creep and consolidation.
3.2. Datasets description
As shown in Table 1 , this study employs a total of eight datasets. 
Specifically, the DS Dam includes five source-domain datasets and one 
target-domain dataset, while the PB Dam includes one source-domain 
dataset and one target-domain dataset. For the DS Dam, Source 
Domain I is constructed by inverting only transient parameters. Source 
Domain II involves inversion of both transient and creep parameters, 
targeting monitoring points PL10-2, PL10-1, PL14-1, PL14-2, PL14-3, 
PL14-4, PL19-1, and PL19-2. Source Domain III also inverts both tran -
sient and creep parameters, but targets monitoring points PL10-1, PL14- 
1, PL14-2, PL14-3, PL14-4, and PL19-1. Source Domain IV is generated 
by combining Source Domains I, II, and III, whereas Source Domain V 
extends Source Domain IV by incorporating extreme hydraulic condi -
tions (check flood level and dead water level) and extreme temperature 
conditions (mean temperature ± 20

the DS Dam is constructed from longitudinal displacement monitoring

3.3. Baseline models
To comprehensively validate the effectiveness of the proposed cross- 
domain adaptive modelling method, this study selected ten represen -
tative comparison models, which were categorized into two groups 
based on task characteristics. The first group consists of four ablation 
models, designed to evaluate the contribution of each key component: 
Ablation Model I removes both the CORAL and MMD losses; Ablation 
Model II removes the GAT-based feature encoder; Ablation Model III 
removes both the CORAL and MMD losses as well as the GAT-based 
feature encoder; Ablation Model IV [ 43,44 ] is based on Ablation 
Model III but uses Source Domain I (while all other models use Source 
Domain IV). The second group consists of six mainstream baseline 
models for dam structural health monitoring, including LSTM [58] , GRU 
[64] , GCN [ 57 ], Transformer [49] , GAT [ 57 ], and TGCN [65] , which 
serve as benchmarks against traditional machine learning and deep 
learning approaches. Table 2 presents the parameter configurations of 
all models, where the baseline model parameters are selected under 
computational constraints to ensure optimal configurations, thereby 
guaranteeing fairness and consistency in experimental conditions.
4. Results
This experiment is conducted under the Windows 10 system envi -

The server configuration follows: CPU is Intel Core i7-13700, GPU is 
Nvidia RTX 4070SUPER. In the experiment, the learning rate is set to 
0.001, the batch size is 64, the optimizer selects the Adam optimizer to

performs well on the PB Dam but moderately on the DG Dam. These

different neural network architectures can be uncertain, while a high-

intervals of MAE = 0.35 ± 0.09 mm and RMSE = 0.40 ± 0.09 mm on the 
DG Dam dataset, outperforming all baselines. This indicates that the 
performance gains are statistically significant and the model exhibits 
high robustness against random fluctuations in network initialization 
and optimization. Such repeated experimental validation confirms that 
the proposed cross-domain adaptive framework maintains consistent 
accuracy across stochastic trials, reinforcing the model ’ s reliability for 
real-world digital twin deployment in dam monitoring and safety 
assessment.

4.4. The proposed model has generalization ability under extreme 
working conditions
To further evaluate the generalization capability of the proposed 
model under extreme conditions, two categories of extreme scenarios 
were designed in this study. The first category concerns water level 
conditions, including the check flood level and the dead water level. The 
second category relates to temperature conditions, in which the average 
temperature was increased or decreased by 20 ℃ . Based on these set -
tings, a source domain V that includes extreme conditions was con -
structed and compared with source domain IV, which does not 
incorporate extreme conditions. The validation results are shown in 
Figs. 18 – 20 . As illustrated in Fig. 18 , the model was evaluated under 
both routine monitoring and extreme conditions for source domain IV 
and source domain V. The results indicate that after introducing samples 
from extreme conditions, the model achieved a significant improvement 
in prediction accuracy under routine conditions, with the average MAE, 
RMSE, and MAPE reduced by 20.45 %, 13.95 %, and 22.22 %, respec -
tively. Meanwhile, under source domain IV, the prediction accuracy 
under extreme conditions decreased at several individual monitoring 
points such as PL16-1 and PL19-1, suggesting limitations in the model ’ s 
generalization ability. In contrast, under source domain V, the overall 
accuracy of the model was further improved, with the average MAE, 
RMSE, and MAPE reduced by 33.33 %, 23.26 %, and 30.16 %, respec -
tively, and the improvements under extreme conditions were more 
pronounced. As shown in Fig. 19 , the predicted process lines under both 
source domain IV and source domain V were closely aligned with the 
measured values and the simulated values under extreme conditions, 
with only minor discrepancies. Furthermore, Fig. 20 presents the three- 
dimensional visualization results, which demonstrate that the model 
predictions were consistent with physical laws and that the overall dif -
ferences remained within a controllable range. In summary, the pro -
posed model exhibits strong generalization performance under extreme 
conditions, and the inclusion of extreme condition samples in the source 
domain can effectively enhance both prediction accuracy and cross- 
domain adaptability.
5. Discussion
Building upon previous LSTM-FEM coupled digital twin models 
[ 43,44 ] for high arch and earth-rock dams, this study redefines the 
boundary between data-driven prediction and physics-based simulation 
by introducing a cross-domain adaptive learning framework. The pri -
mary limitation of existing dam digital twins lies in the significant 
discrepancy between numerical simulations and field monitoring data, 
which constrains prediction accuracy and generalization under extreme 
or rare operating conditions. To address this, multiple high-fidelity 
source domains are constructed by systematically varying material 
properties, boundary conditions, and load scenarios in the finite element

simulations. This approach effectively expands the feature space of the 
source domain, reducing the domain gap and enhancing the model ’ s 
ability to generalize across different structural and operational 
conditions.
In addition to improving predictive accuracy, the proposed graph 
attention-gated network architecture introduces a new level of inter -
pretability. By capturing spatial dependencies among monitoring points 
and structural nodes, the attention mechanism quantifies the relative 
influence of each node on the predicted deformation or stress field, 
while the gating mechanism regulates the propagation of information 
over space. This enables the model to maintain stability and physical 
consistency even under sparse sensing or noisy measurement conditions. 
Importantly, this interpretable design allows engineers to gain insights 
into the internal decision-making process of the digital twin, bridging 
the gap between “ black-box ” neural network predictions and physical 
understanding.
From a broader perspective, this study establishes a generalizable 
framework for physics-informed domain adaptation in dam digital 
twins, integrating multi-source learning with mechanistic reasoning. 
The findings demonstrate that high-quality, diversified source domains 
and adaptive feature alignment are critical for achieving both accuracy 
and robustness, particularly under extreme loading or failure conditions. 
Moreover, the framework provides a foundation for future extensions 
that incorporate emerging technologies. For instance, generative AI 
methods, such as diffusion-based models, could augment source do -
mains with synthetic but physically consistent deformation fields, 
improving robustness in data-scarce or hazardous scenarios. Alignment 
with Industry 5.0 principles, including human-centric AI and industrial 
metaverse applications, could transform the digital twin into an inter -
active, human-in-the-loop decision-support tool, enabling real-time 
feedback, immersive visualization, and collaborative diagnostics. 
Finally, the framework may be extended to semi-physical commis -
sioning environments, allowing real-time, closed-loop validation by 
integrating live sensor data with simulated responses. These de -
velopments collectively position the proposed digital twin framework as 
a scalable, interpretable, and robust approach for next-generation cyber- 
physical-human systems in hydraulic engineering.
6. Conclusion
This study proposes a novel cross-domain adaptive digital twin 
modelling framework for high arch and earth-rock dams, integrating 
GAT, gating mechanisms, and feature alignment strategies to improve 
prediction accuracy and generalization across domains. The main find -
ings are summarized as follows:
(1) The proposed model achieves superior prediction accuracy 
compared with ablation and baseline models. On average, the model 
reduces MAE by 22.81 %, 43.59 %, 45.68 %, and 87.02 % relative to 
ablation variants, and by 29.03 % and 14.05 % compared with state-of- 
the-art baselines on concrete and earth-rock dams, respectively. These 
results confirm the effectiveness of the GAT-based architecture, source- 
domain construction, and cross-domain feature alignment in enhancing 
digital twin accuracy.
(2) The study demonstrates that constructing high-quality source 
domains is crucial for cross-domain modelling. Multi-source domain 
transfer (Source Domain IV) outperforms single-source domains,

distribution discrepancies between simulation and monitoring data. 
This highlights the importance of incorporating diverse simulation 
scenarios in digital twin modelling.
(3) Validation under extreme water level and temperature conditions 
confirms that the proposed model possesses strong generalization 
capability. Incorporating extreme-condition samples (Source Domain V) 
further improves accuracy, reducing MAE, RMSE, and MAPE by 33.33 
%, 23.26 %, and 30.16 %, respectively, and effectively alleviating pre -
diction degradation at critical monitoring points.
Overall, the proposed framework provides an effective and physi -
cally consistent digital twin modelling approach for dam structures, 
offering robust adaptability across domains and under extreme oper -
ating conditions. Future research will focus on enhancing the fusion of 
multi-source monitoring information, quantifying cross-domain differ -
ences between numerical simulations and actual monitoring, verifying 
the generalization of structural damage, integrating domain knowledge, 
and constructing intelligent agents to support large-scale, real-time dam 
safety monitoring.
