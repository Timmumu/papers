ABSTRACT
Digital twin technology is vital in hydraulic engineering for real-time visualization, performance analysis, and 
risk management of water infrastructure systems. This paper proposes a digital twin model for deformation and 
seepage analysis of high earth-rock dams, integrating deep learning with the Finite Element Method (FEM). Key 
contributions include a sensor-based monitoring point model with time-variant update and extrapolation ca -
pabilities and a point-to-domain model that achieves full-domain monitoring predictions from point-level 
monitoring by learning the node relationships generated by FEM using neural networks and dynamic moni -
toring loss functions. Applied to a 186-m dam, the model achieves an average error of 3.17 %, improving 
deformation prediction accuracy by 19.44 % and simulation accuracy by 64.42 %. This approach facilitates real- 
time monitoring, predictive analysis, and early warnings, making it a powerful tool for hydraulic engineering 
safety. Future work will focus on exploring three-dimensional high-precision modeling and advancing data 
fusion techniques.
1. Introduction
Earth-rock dams are widely utilized in global water conservancy 
projects due to their low cost, easy access to local materials, adaptability 
to diverse topographical and geological conditions, simplicity of con -
struction, and excellent seismic performance [ 1 , 2 ]. Driven by the rapid 
development of hydropower in China, numerous high earth-rock dams 
are already in the design and construction stages, such as the Gushui 
Dam (305 m), the Shuangjiangkou Dam (315 m), the Rumei Dam (315 
m), and the Lianghekou Dam (295 m) [ 3 , 4 ]. These large-scale water -
shed-controlling high earth-rock dams are often located in mountainous 
canyon regions, where they face complex environmental conditions, 
such as large water level fluctuations, significant stresses, and thick 
overburden layers [ 5 , 6 ]. Real-time monitoring and safety analysis of 
deformation and seepage for high earth-rock dams in these areas are 
crucial for preventing structural failure and ensuring safe operation.
Traditionally, real-time monitoring of the structural health of high 
earth-rock dams has relied on data-driven models based on sensor 
monitoring points, such as the Hydrostatic-Season-Time (HST) model, 
which is limited by the linear assumptions and fixed parameters, 
reducing accuracy in capturing complex, non-linear behaviors and dy -
namic changes in dam safety [ 7 – 10 ]. With advancements in machine 
learning, particularly deep learning, more powerful models with supe -
rior modeling and feature extraction capabilities have been introduced 
for dam safety monitoring [ 11 – 13 ]. However, capturing the complex 
relationships between dam deformation and various environmental 
factors, as well as modeling temporal features, remains a challenge for 
both the statistical models and shallow machine-learning approaches 
[ 14 ]. Thus, deep learning models, represented by neural networks, have 
emerged to effectively address issues such as multi-feature relationships 
and multi-point correlations in dam safety monitoring. For example, 
Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) like Long Short-Term Memory 
(LSTM) [ 15 , 16 ] and Gated Recurrent Units (GRU) [ 17 ] have been 
employed to capture the long-term futures of dams. Graph Neural Net -
works (GNNs) have shown particular promise in capturing the complex 
spatial dependencies of sensor location graphs [ 18 ], with models such as 
Graph Convolutional Network (GCN) being applied by researchers to 
develop multi-point monitoring models for dam structural health

[ 19 , 20 ]. Additionally, attention mechanisms, known for their powerful

Transformer-based models [ 22 ] in dam safety monitoring applications.
Although deep learning models exhibit exceptional nonlinear 
modeling capabilities for high earth-rock dam structures, their extrap -
olation performance under extreme water level conditions such as dead 
water level and check flood water level remains limited. Pre-training 
methods based on transfer learning show promise in providing prior 
knowledge to deep learning-based monitoring models for extreme and 
previously unobserved conditions. Transfer learning leverages knowl -
edge learned in one domain to improve learning tasks in the same or 
different domains, thereby enhancing model generalization [ 23 ]. In the 
field of dam safety monitoring, transfer learning methods based on pre- 
training and fine-tuning have been primarily employed to address 
challenges such as insufficient labeled crack images of dams [ 22 , 24 ] and 
high earth-rock particle fragmentation [ 2 ], which often lead to poor 
model generalization. By utilizing prior task knowledge, transfer 
learning not only enhances prediction accuracy but also improves 
overall efficiency [ 25 ], leading to a significant improvement in the 
reliability and accuracy of models used in dam safety monitoring [ 26 ].
The aforementioned dam monitoring model based on single or 
multiple sensor-based monitoring points is insufficient for assessing the 
structural health of high earth-rock dams in complex environments [ 27 ], 
as an evaluation of the overall structural condition of the project may be 
indispensable. Alternatively, the digital twin model offers a promising 
solution [ 28 ]. It refers to the interaction between a virtual model and 
real-time data of physical entities, processes, or systems, establishing a 
digital, dynamically synchronized mapping relationship. This model can 
reflect the state, behavior, and changes of the physical object in real- 
time and, through simulation, prediction, and optimization functions, 
enable intelligent monitoring, analysis, and decision support for 
physical systems [ 29 ]. It includes three core components: the physical 
entity, its digital representation, and the data flow and feedback con -
necting them, supported by data sensing technologies, artificial intelli -
gence capabilities, and high-speed networks [ 30 ]. However, the 
construction of existing digital twin models for dam structures primarily 
relies on numerical simulation frameworks based on Finite Element 
Model (FEM) [ 31 , 32 ], which often requires extensive material param -
eter calibration, structural simplifications, and iterative equation solv -
ing [ 33 ], limiting both timeliness and accuracy [ 34 ]. Additionally, some 
researchers have developed geographic information system-based geo -
spatial digital twin models for dams and river systems, achieving high- 
precision digitalization of engineering structures [ 35 ], but these 
models cannot dynamically respond to environmental changes in real- 
time. In other fields, the development of digital twin models for small- 
scale physical systems is often based on numerical simulations, with 
machine learning models facilitating the connection between physical 
and digital environments [ 36 – 38 ]. Therefore, key obstacles include the 
construction of physical-digital models, the integration of sensor data, 
and the establishment of robust connections and feedback loops be -
tween the physical environment and the digital model. It reveals a 
critical gap in the availability of a reliable digital twin model for dam 
structural safety monitoring. Transitioning from traditional monitoring 
point models to digital twin models for dam structures remains a sig -
nificant challenge that requires urgent investigation.
Therefore, this paper proposes a deformation-seepage digital twin 
model for high earth-rock dams, which integrates deep learning and 
FEM, for the first time. Firstly, a novel monitoring model with time- 
update and extrapolation capabilities under extreme water level con -
ditions is constructed using GCN and transfer learning. Subsequently, by 
integrating LSTM with the FEM, monitoring point data from the moni -
toring point model can be mapped to full-domain monitoring

2. Theory of monitoring traditional earth-rock dams
Whether employing traditional statistical models or machine 
learning models, most existing dam monitoring models are constructed 
based on the HST influence factor theory [ 39 ]. The HST model catego -
rizes dam deformation into two types: reversible and irreversible. 
Reversible deformation is induced by fluctuations in external environ -
mental loads, including changes in hydrostatic water level [ 41 ] and 
temperature [ 42 ]. In contrast, irreversible deformation is attributed to 
nonlinear effects such as creep, which can potentially compromise the 
structural integrity [ 40 ]. The formula for the HST model is typically 
expressed as follows [ 43 ]:

3. Finite element method
The FEM, as one of the commonly used numerical analysis models for 
calculating stress and strain in complex nonlinear structures [ 44 ], is 
widely applied in modern dam engineering [ 45 ]. The fundamental 
concept of the finite element method involves dividing a complex 
structure into a finite number of non-overlapping elements that are 
interconnected [ 46 ]. For simulating the dams ’ instantaneous deforma -
tion, the Duncan-Chang E -B model is commonly used [ 47 ]. Creep cal -
culations often rely on the empirical creep constitutive models [ 48 ]. In 
the analysis of seepage in earth-rock dams, it is generally assumed that 
the movement of seepage water conforms to Darcy ’ s law. Based on this 
principle, seepage control equations are formulated [ 49 ].
4. Proposed model
Section 4 introduces the Dam Neurons Digital Twin Model, a digital 
twin model for high earth-rock dams that integrates deep learning, 
transfer learning, and FEM. The model dynamically predicts seepage 
and deformation states under varying environmental and operational 
conditions, enabling real-time monitoring, safety evaluation, and early 
warning.

functions. The monitoring point model predicts structural response 
changes in the dam by sensing environmental variations. Its predicted 
values are then input into the point-to-domain mapping model to ach -
ieve full-domain monitoring predictions from point-level monitoring. To 
better illustrate this approach, inspired by the bionic concept of infor -
mation transmission between neuronal cell bodies and synapses, the 
monitoring point model and the point-to-domain mapping model are 
referred to as the “ cell body model ” and “ synapse model ” , respectively. 
Finally, the integration of the cell body model and dendrite model en -
ables real-time perception, prediction, and early warning [ 50 ] of 
seepage and deformation in the dams, while further assessing structural 
safety through slope stability analysis. The main contributions of this 
paper are as follows: 
• For the first time, a new digital twin model suitable for high earth- 
rock dams is proposed.
• A new integrated monitoring model for seepage and deformation 
sensor points with time-variant updating and extrapolation capa -
bilities under extreme water level conditions is introduced.
• A model for jointly mapping numerical simulations and monitoring 
data to physical entities is proposed.
• The model presented in this paper is applied to a real-world high 
earth-rock dam project, with extensive accuracy and applicability 
verification conducted.
4.2. Cell body model
The principle of the cell body model is shown in Fig. 2 . First, a feature 
extraction module based on GCN extracts the influence factor features. 
To couple the dams ’ seepage and deformation futures, separate feature 
extractors for seepage and deformation sensor points are constructed, 
and feature fusion is performed using pre-trained models. The hydraulic 
load features and soil-rock creep futures output from the FEM are used to 
apply physical constraints to the deformation sensor feature extractor, 
enhancing the model ’ s ability to extrapolate structural responses under 
extreme high-water conditions. Finally, transfer learning is applied to 
update the time-variant effects of the feature decoder, mitigating per -
formance degradation caused by issues such as particle fragmentation.

4.3. Synapse model
The synapse model principle is illustrated in Fig. 3 . This section 
presents the synapse model, which employs an LSTM network to learn 
information transmission relationships among measurement points. A 
loss function is designed to optimize the transformation from sensor data 
to a digital twin, with a dynamic monitoring loss to adjust for FEM 
discrepancies. Finally, the model integrates seepage and deformation 
predictions to construct a comprehensive digital twin model for high 
earth-rock dams.

employed to learn the node information transmission relationship.

the input gate, the forget gate, and the output gate. These gates control 
the writing, retention, and output of information, respectively. The core

5. Project case
This section analyzes the PB Hydroelectric Station using monitoring 
data and finite element modeling. It examines displacement and seepage 
trends, develops an FEM for structural analysis, and evaluates model 
accuracy with key metrics. Various baseline models are compared to 
validate the proposed model.
5.1. Project overview
The PB Hydroelectric Station is a large-scale hydropower project 
primarily focused on power generation, with additional benefits in flood 
control, sediment retention, and other integrated uses. The dam type is a 
gravel-filled earth-core rockfill dam, with a top elevation of 856 m, a 
maximum dam height of 186 m, a normal reservoir water level of 850 m, 
and a dead water level of 790 m. As shown in Fig. 4 , this paper focuses 
on the typical section of PB Dam at the elevation 0 + 210, where a group 
of horizontal displacement meters using tensile lines is arranged at the 
downstream filter layers, transition layers, and rockfill body at eleva -
tions 808 m, 758 m, and 731 m. These meters are numbered from CH1 to 
CH16, with a total of 16 measurement points. Currently, there are three 
monitoring points for seepage pressure on the downstream dam slope of 
this section, numbered P22, P29, and P32. The remaining measurement 
points are either out of service or have been discontinued.
5.2. Data sets
This paper creates a dataset using deformation and seepage moni -
toring data from the PB Dam ’ s 0 + 210 section, spanning from May 21, 
2012, to May 23, 2024, along with the finite element simulation data.

5.3. Finite element model
In the FEM, the actual conditions such as the dam ’ s partitioning 
layout and the thick covering layer of the foundation are taken into 
account. Additionally, based on the results of in-situ drilling tests, the 
model divides the dam into an upper and lower zone, with the dead 
water level at 790 m as the boundary. The simulation range extends 2 
times the dam height upstream, downstream, and below the dam 
foundation. The model consists of 6572 elements and 13,348 nodes, 
with the finite element mesh and material division shown in Fig. 6 (a). 
The PB dam is divided into the upstream embankment zone, transition 
layer, filter layer, gravel soil core wall, downstream secondary 
embankment zone, and downstream main embankment zone. This paper 
focuses on the transient and creep key parameter inversion of the main 
embankment, secondary embankment, core wall, and covering layer of 
the typical section. The parameter values for these are shown in Table 2
and Table 3 , while the designed permeability coefficients for each 
partition of the PB dam are shown in Table 4 . From Fig. 6 (b), it can be 
observed that the settlement curve calculated by the FEM matches well 
with the measured values of the monitoring points.

6. Analysis of results
This experiment is conducted under the Windows 10 system envi -
ronment using Pycharm IDE and Pytorch2.5.1 based on Python3.10. The 
server configuration follows: CPU is Intel Core i7 – 13,700, GPU is Nvidia 
RTX 4070SUPER. In the experiment, the learning rate is set to 0.001, the 
batch size is 64, the optimizer selects the Adam optimizer to update the 
network parameters, and the number of iterations is set to 500.
6.1. Analysis of the accuracy of the proposed model
To validate the accuracy of the deformation and seepage pressure 
results generated by the proposed model for the cross-section of high 
earth-rock dams, the predicted values at sensor-monitored nodes were 
compared with the actual measured values, as well as with the FEM 
results and predictions from the ablation model of the proposed model. 
It is important to note that, to simplify numerical simulation, the 
deformation results compared here represent incremental displacements 
relative to the baseline measurement taken on May 21, 2012.
As shown in Fig. 7 , the MAE, RMSE, and MAPE of predictions for all 
deformation monitoring points in the 0 + 210 cross-section were 
compared among the three models. The proposed model achieved the 
best accuracy, with average improvements over the FEM of 64.28 %, 
59.37 %, and 64.42 % in terms of MAE, RMSE, and MAPE, respectively. 
This demonstrates that integrating actual monitoring data significantly 
enhances the simulation accuracy of the FEM. Similarly, compared to 
the ablation model using only the LSTM, the proposed model improved 
the average accuracy of all monitoring points by 33.31 %, 33.32 %, and 
32.55 % in terms of MAE, RMSE, and MAPE, respectively. This indicates 
that the dynamic monitoring loss function effectively integrates and 
mutually corrects the FEM with actual monitoring data. From the 
perspective of individual monitoring points, the proposed model 
consistently achieved the best prediction accuracy across all points, 
highlighting its strong generalization capability. Furthermore, the per -
formance improvement was particularly pronounced for monitoring 
points near the dam crest compared to the other two models. This

6.2. Analysis of the accuracy of the cell body model
To analyze the primary sources of prediction errors in the proposed 
model and assess the effectiveness of the cell body model, the prediction 
accuracy of the cell body model for points CH1-CH16 and P10-P32 was 
compared with baseline models in terms of MAE, RMSE, and MAPE, as 
shown in Fig. 12 and Fig. 13 . The results indicate that the proposed 
model achieved the best average prediction accuracy across all points, 
with improvements of 14.03 %, 16.05 %, and 19.44 % in MAE, RMSE, 
and MAPE, respectively, compared to the GCN model. This demonstrates 
that the proposed model which integrates seepage and deformation 
features, significantly enhances the prediction accuracy of the cell body 
model. Furthermore, compared to the results in Fig. 7 , the MAE, RMSE, 
and MAPE of the cell body model decreased dramatically by 90.35 %, 
89.70 %, and 90.85 %, respectively, suggesting that the prediction errors 
of the proposed model are minimally related to the cell body model it -
self. Fig. 13 further shows that the proposed model achieves the best 
prediction accuracy for the majority of monitoring points, highlighting 
its strong generalization capability.
Fig. 14 and Fig. 15 illustrate the comparison between the predicted 
value trajectories and the measured value trajectories of the cell model 
and baseline models for selected deformation and seepage pressure 
monitoring points on the test set, accompanied by corresponding 
residual analyses. The results show that the proposed model effectively 
captures the changing trends of both deformation and seepage pressure 
measurements, achieving high prediction accuracy. Compared with 
other models, the proposed model demonstrates superior predictive 
performance for the selected deformation and seepage pressure points. 
Moreover, the residual distribution is relatively stable, indicating that 
the proposed model exhibits robust predictive capabilities.
6.3. Analysis of the synapse model accuracy
To investigate the primary sources of error in the proposed model 
and evaluate the accuracy of the synapse model, the synapse model was 
used to fit the horizontal displacement, settlement displacement, and 
seepage pressure calculated by the FEM for 3238 nodes. The error results 
on the test set are presented in Fig. 16 , Fig. 17 , and Fig. 18 . The analysis 
reveals that the synapse model achieves average MAPE values of only 
0.041 %, 0.008 %, and 1.031 % for horizontal displacement, settlement 
displacement, and seepage pressure, respectively, indicating its capa -
bility to effectively fit the FEM ’ s simulation of the cross-section of high 
earth-rock dams. Combined with the previous analyses, it can be 
concluded that the primary source of error in the proposed model stems 
from discrepancies between the trend variations in the FEM ’ s simula -
tions and the actual monitoring data. While the proposed model can 
partially correct the FEM ’ s deviations by incorporating real monitoring 
data, its digital twin accuracy for the cross-section of high earth-rock 
dams remains fundamentally dependent on the FEM ’ s simulation 
accuracy.

7. Conclusions
This paper presented a deformation-seepage digital twin model for 
high earth-rock dams based on deep learning and FEM and applied it to a 
high earth-rock dam project. The main conclusions were as follows: 
1. A monitoring point model was developed using GCN and transfer 
learning, enabling time-updating and extrapolation under extreme 
water level conditions. Furthermore, an innovative point-to-domain 
model integrated LSTM with the FEM to map monitoring point data 
onto two-dimensional cross-sectional information, with a dynamic 
loss function proposed to effectively fuse sensor data and FEM 
outputs.
2. The proposed model can effectively achieve the digital twin of the 
deformation-seepage for the cross-section of high earth-rock dams, 
with an average error of only 3.17 % between the predicted defor -
mation and actual monitoring data. Compared with the traditional 
numerical simulation model, it can significantly improve simulation 
accuracy, with the deformation simulation accuracy increasing by 
64.42 %. Furthermore, the dynamic monitoring loss method pro -
posed in this paper can effectively improve the fusion prediction 
accuracy of the FEM and real monitoring data, with deformation 
prediction accuracy improving by 32.55 %.
3. The proposed prediction model for monitoring points achieved the 
best prediction accuracy and better generalization compared with 
baseline models. It was shown that the proposed deformation- 
seepage coupling prediction method can effectively improve the 
prediction accuracy of the synapse model by 19.44 %, as compared 
to the ablation model. Through accuracy analysis of the cell body 
model and synapse model, it was found that the main source of error 
in the proposed model arose from inconsistencies between the FEM 
simulation results and actual monitoring patterns. If precise simu -
lation can be achieved, high-precision digital twins can be realized.
The model proposed in this paper can only preliminarily implement 
the deformation-seepage digital twin for cross-sections of high earth- 
rock dams. Issues such as how to achieve a three-dimensional high- 
precision digital twin, the influence of the number of sensor monitoring 
points on digital twin accuracy, how to reduce the limitations of nu -
merical simulations on digital twin accuracy, and computational models 
for slope stability analysis based on twin data, still need to be addressed 
in future research.
