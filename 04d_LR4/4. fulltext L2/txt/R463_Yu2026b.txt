Abstract
Actual construction errors and accumulated damage seriously affect the spatial structural
form of the tensile membrane structure, making it difficult to characterize the full-life spatial
form of the physical tensile membrane structure based on the original theoretical model.
There are still significant challenges in achieving the virtual–real correspondence of digital
twin results based on the original theoretical design model. It is necessary to propose a high-
precision digital twin model construction method that adapts to the full life cycle of the
tensile membrane structure. For this reason, a refined digital twin model reconstruction and
morphological deviation visualization method for the umbrella-shaped tensile membrane
structure combined with 3D point clouds, computational geometry algorithms, and 3D3S
finite element simulation modeling is proposed in this paper. Firstly, three-dimensional
point cloud data of the umbrella-shaped membrane structure test bench were acquired,
and the point cloud data were accurately registered based on the Iterative Closest Point
(ICP) algorithm. Secondly, the physical membrane surface reconstruction was obtained by
applying the Screened Poisson Surface Reconstruction (SPSR) algorithm. Subsequently, the
3D3S theoretical model of the umbrella-shaped tensile membrane structure was updated
according to the contour of the measured membrane surface reconstruction model. Finally,
global spatial geometric morphological deviation was compared among the theoretical
models and the reconstructed physical model. Furthermore, different environmental
load combinations were analyzed to support hazard warning of the refined digital twin
model of the physical umbrella-shaped tensile membrane structure. The results show
that the maximum spatial form deviation of the original theoretical model was 43.56 mm,
whereas the maximum form deviation of the updated theoretical model was only 0.05 mm.
The updated theoretical model accurately reflected the spatial form characteristics of the
physical membrane structure and satisfied the requirement for digital twin physical–virtual
consistency. In addition, the global stress distribution between the original/updated
theoretical models differed markedly, and the updated theoretical model is more suitable
for identifying weak areas and evaluating safety performance of the actual umbrella-shaped
tensile membrane structure under different ultimate environmental loads.

1. Introduction
A digital twin is a virtual counterpart of a physical entity that supports bidirec-
tional and real-time data mapping and seamless switching between physical and virtual
domains [1,2]. Grieves [1] first proposed a three-dimensional model integrating the phys-
ical entity, the virtual entity, and their interactive connection. With the development of
application demands, digital twin models have been continuously updated for different
application scenarios [ 2]. Tao [ 3] proposed a five-dimensional digital twin model and
systematically analyzed its application domains, levels, disciplines, dimensions, generality,
and functions. Owing to its integrated monitoring, control, and prediction capabilities, dig-
ital twin technology has become prominent in manufacturing [4]. Against the background
of domestic intelligent development strategies, digital twin technology has gradually ex-
panded from manufacturing to the construction industry and has become one of the key
technologies supporting the transformation of traditional construction toward intelligent
construction [5].
Although research on digital twins in the construction field has grown rapidly, existing
studies remain highly fragmented and regionally oriented [6]. Congiu [7] developed a digi-
tal twin system framework to support facility managers in decision-making by effectively
integrating Building Information Modeling (BIM) and Internet of Things (IoT). For intelli-
gent construction services, Pan [5] developed a detailed digital twin framework containing
physical models, virtual models, and connected data by integrating BIM, IoT, and data min-
ing (DM) techniques. Oti [8] used laser scanners and cameras to acquire real-time building
data and established a building energy-consumption feedback loop, which reduces the
gaps between different building life-cycle stages through continuous correction of design
and facility management in the digital twin environment. Yu [9] proposed an intelligent
operation and maintenance scheme for urban roads based on multi-source spatiotemporal
monitoring data. Song [10] developed a conceptual framework that integrates digital twins
with BIM, aiming to extend BIM applications from design and construction stages to the en-
tire life cycle of bridge engineering through real-time data interaction and intelligent asset
management. Based on data integration, synchronization, and analysis, Lu [11] designed a
building–city digital twin model to support anomaly detection, environmental monitoring,
maintenance optimization, prioritization, and energy planning. Qi [12] proposed a digital
twin framework for transmission towers by integrating BIM, IoT, and the Finite Element
Method (FEM), which supports analysis of structural health monitoring and visual warning
under wind loads and flood scouring. These studies indicate that the integration of digital
twins with DM, IoT, BIM, and FEM can enable multi-source data fusion and in-depth data
mining, and further reduce carbon footprints, improve indoor environmental quality, and
enhance resource utilization and energy efficiency [13,14].
Although existing studies show that digital twins are evolving from static BIM toward
dynamic decision-support systems, simulation modeling methods remain central to their
implementation. Digital modeling technology is a core method for virtual-environment
modeling and is also a primary approach for digital twin modeling. Based on geometric
modeling technology, a virtual mapping platform for physical entities can be established
and formed as data carriers by constructing geometric models of various physical entities.
Digital twins currently have moved from theoretical research to engineering applications,

and their successful implementation depends on the construction of digital twin models
adapted to specific objects [15]. Liu [16] proposed a digital twin modeling method based on

integrates quaternion semantic diffusion with parameter diffusion to improve the efficient
exchange, retrieval, and reuse of digital twin models. Arvanitis [18] proposed a robust and

and digital twin life-cycle analysis. Hu [21] constructed an integral blisk digital twin model,

proposed a real-time displacement perception method, and constructed a theoretical dis-
placement perception model based on multi-point camera observations, which enables one
to drive the twin model to map the real state of the physical environment and supports
intelligent algorithms for manufacturing-state prediction and control.
Compared with conventional building structures, inspection and evaluation tech-
nologies for the tensile membrane structure remain relatively underdeveloped, making it
difficult to provide long-term and effective technical support for safe operation. Analysis
of the above studies indicates that the primary task in implementing a digital twin is to
create an accurate reconstructed digital twin model for the application object. An accurate
structural twin model has become an indispensable tool for assessing load-bearing capacity,
safety, and maintenance during the structural full life cycle [ 23,24]. In order to conduct
reliable load-effect analysis and structural safety assessment for tensile membrane struc-
tures, the key requirement is to establish an accurate reverse-modeled digital twin model
that conforms to the spatial form of the real in-service structure. Up to now, a common
problem in the design and construction of domestic tensile membrane structures is the
disconnection between construction and structural design, which leads to serious accumu-
lation of construction errors. In addition, environmental corrosion, material aging, load
fatigue, and overload effects caused by emergencies may occur during service life, resulting
in damage accumulation and resistance degradation and thus significantly affecting the
structural spatial geometric form. Therefore, when structural performance analysis and
safety inspection are carried out for a physical tensile membrane structure, the original
theoretical design model can no longer adequately represent the physical structure. Digital
simulation results based on the original theoretical design model may differ considerably
from those of the actual structure, making it difficult to construct a high-fidelity model and
achieve physical–virtual consistency. Therefore, it is necessary to use intelligent monitoring
methods to perform refined reconstruction of the physical tensile membrane structure, and
to support full-life-cycle morphology monitoring and safety inspection.
To address these challenges, this paper integrates three-dimensional point clouds,
3D3S modeling, geomagic modeling, and FEM environmental load analysis to investigate
refined digital twin model reconstruction, spatial form deviation visualization, and nonlin-
ear structural analysis under different load combinations for an umbrella-shaped tensile
membrane structure. This work can provide a reference for the full-life-cycle monitoring of
digital twins in tensile membrane structures. The main contributions are as follows:

2. Methodology
The tensile membrane structure has the typical characteristics of a large-scale and
complex configuration. Traditional measurement methods struggle to meet the data require-
ments of full scale and high accuracy. 3D reverse modeling based on 3D point cloud can not
only achieve fast and comprehensive spatial information collection, but also provide data
support for structural construction quality control, deformation analysis, and structural
health monitoring. It can efficiently generate digital twin reconstruction models that are
consistent with physical entities, which provides a data foundation and model basis for
performance evaluation and optimization design of existing structures.
2.1. 3D Point Cloud Registration Methodology
An independent three-dimensional coordinate system is established when an object
is scanned using a laser scanning system. In this coordinate system, the laser scanner
is generally taken as the coordinate origin o, the laser scanning direction is defined as
the Y axis, the axis in the scanning plane perpendicular to the Y axis is defined as the X
axis, and the Z axis is perpendicular to the scanning plane. α is the horizontal scanning

a reference direction, and typically rotating about the vertical axis of the scanner. θ is the
vertical scanning angle, representing the elevation or depression angle of the laser beam
relative to the horizontal plane. The horizontal scanning angle and vertical scanning angle
θ can then be obtained using a precision-clock-controlled encoder. Each coordinate point

2.2. Three-Dimensional Membrane Reconstruction Theory
Membrane surface reconstruction of the umbrella-shaped tensile membrane structure
is based on the three-dimensional point cloud geometric topology information of the phys-
ical membrane surface to reconstruct the spatial geometric form characteristics. Poisson
surface reconstruction algorithms are widely valued for high-fidelity scene reconstruction
capabilities [26]. The main idea of the Poisson surface reconstruction algorithm is to estab-
lish an intrinsic relationship between the scanned object point cloud sample set S and the
scalar indicator function χ of the scanned object, so that the surface reconstruction can be
transformed into a benign sparse dynamic Poisson equation solving problem, and then the
reconstructed target object can be obtained by extracting the isosurface. Briefly, Poisson
surface reconstruction algorithms transform the reconstruction into a global problem, and
complete the spatial reconstruction of the directed point set by solving the Poisson function

2.3. Finite Element Theoretical Model Modification Theory
Finite element model modification is usually achieved by optimizing model param-
eters to ensure that the predicted finite element results are consistent with the measured
target results within the allowable deviation range. The model modification in classical
structural dynamics is often based on modal test results such as frequency and mode shape.
However, the large-span tensile membrane structure adopts coated fabric membrane mate-
rials and auxiliary support structures to form a stress-controlled spatial structure through
the tensioning method, as shown in Equation (11). From the perspective of structural
mechanics, the core difference between tensile membrane structures and traditional rigid
structures lies in their highly nonlinear mechanical characteristics of shape changing with
stress. The spatial geometric configuration state of the membrane surface is an equilibrium
form constrained by both prestress and boundary conditions. Therefore, the measured
three-dimensional point cloud of the membrane surface is not only geometric data, but
also indirectly reflects the comprehensive influence of various factors such as construction
errors, material degradation, and prestress loss.

2.4. Global Deviation Analysis Between the Theoretical Reference Model and the Physical
Measurement Model
Geomagic software, version 2014 has been widely used in research fields such as
3D metrology, detection, and deviation quantification due to its powerful 3D computing
capabilities. Geomagic software also developed software interfaces for CAD and CAM,
which can achieve data sharing and fast processing between multiple software through
setting plugins, and complete 2D or 3D geometric measurement graphic comparison.
When conducting global deviation analysis on two models in Geomagic software, the
essence is to analyze their geometric distance fields in three-dimensional space. When
comparing the two models in three dimensions, geomagic software calculates a deviation
value for each data point in the measurement data; that is, each measurement point has a
measurement position Pm. By projecting the direction, the reference position Pr is found,

is then calculated, and the mathematical expressions are shown in Equations (14) and (15).

achieved. In this paper, an umbrella-shaped tensile membrane structure was used as the
research object, and a digital twin test platform was established in this work. The refined

As shown in Figure 7 and Table 3, the point cloud data of each station has achieved
good coincidence in the overall spatial position when the registration of membrane point
cloud data obtained from different measurement stations is completed based on the ICP
algorithm. The point cloud distribution in the main area of the membrane surface is
continuous, and the outer boundary and central opening contour are clear. The spatial
curvature and overall undulating shape of the membrane surface are well preserved in the
lateral view. This also indicates that the ICP algorithm can effectively eliminate the initial
position and pose differences between the clouds of multiple measurement stations, and
the registered point cloud basically reflects the real spatial shape of the umbrella-shaped
tensile membrane structure, which can provide a reliable data basis for the membrane
surface reconstruction and morphological deviation analysis.
It can also be found from Figure 7 that a small number of discrete points and reduced
local coincidence can still be observed near the local edge, the central opening and the area
with sparse point clouds of the membrane surface. The main reason for this phenomenon
is the lack of complete neighborhood geometric constraints in the edge and opening area
of the membrane surface, and the limited visible range and effective overlapping area of
different stations at these positions. This makes it difficult for the ICP algorithm to establish
a stable and unique point correspondence. Because the ICP algorithm relies on the nearest
neighbor correspondence and aims to minimize the overall distance error, local low overlap,
non-uniform density and abnormal points may make some point clouds converge to the
local optimal position and present local discrete phenomena.
4.2. Membrane Surface Reconstruction in the Umbrella-Shaped Tensile Membrane Structure
After obtaining the complete point cloud data of the registered membrane surface, the
three-dimensional model of the membrane surface is required to be reconstructed based
on the spatial geometric features. The SPSR algorithm is adopted as the main algorithm
for the three-dimensional reconstruction of the membrane surface in the umbrella-shaped
tensile membrane structure in this paper. The corresponding parameter settings and
membrane surface three-dimensional reconstruction process are shown in Figure 8. The
TPSR algorithm and the SPSR algorithm were adopted to perform membrane surface
reconstruction of the umbrella-shaped tensile membrane structure. The corresponding
reconstruction results are shown in Figures 9 and 10 and Table 4.
As illustrated in Figure 9, the TPSR algorithm can effectively reconstruct the spatial
geometry of the membrane surface during 3D reconstruction, and the overall membrane
surface reconstruction is relatively smooth and stable. However, the TPSR algorithm has
certain shortcomings in expressing membrane surface boundaries and local details. Due to
the emphasis on global smoothness in this method, it is easy to weaken the local geometric
changes in the original membrane surface point cloud during the reconstruction process,
which results in excessive smoothing of the membrane surface edge region, and incomplete
preservation of some boundary undulations and local curvature features. It is worth noting
that both boundary morphology and local curvature changes are important features for the
umbrella-shaped tensile membrane structure, which reflect the spatial morphology of the
membrane surface. Therefore, the TPSR algorithm has certain limitations in high-precision
representation of the spatial geometric form of the membrane surface.

low radius variation coefficient and height standard deviation, with a computation time of
only 16.23 s. Therefore, considering reconstruction completeness, geometric accuracy, and
computational efficiency comprehensively, a depth of 12 and a voxel size of 0.005 constitute
the optimal parameter combination.

4.3. Finite Element Theoretical Model Modification for the Umbrella-Shaped Tensile
Membrane Structure
3D3S is a piece of domestic engineering software dedicated to the analysis and design
of spatial structures [28,29]. It offers capabilities for parametric modeling, automatic form-
finding, and nonlinear finite element analysis of cable–membrane structures. The software
can account for the tensile properties of membrane materials, the cable–membrane coupling
effect, and structural large-deformation behavior. It is an ideal analytical tool for simulating
the mechanical response of tensile membrane structures under prestress and external
loads. In this study, a computational model of the umbrella-shaped tensile membrane
structure is established using 3D3S software. Then, form-finding analysis and load-response
analysis are carried out to verify the geometric rationality of the updated theoretical model
and the mechanical performance of the umbrella-shaped tensile membrane structure.
Three-dimensional point cloud scanning technology can effectively complete the three-
dimensional form reconstruction of the umbrella-shaped tensile membrane structure. The
theoretical model, the physical structure, and the three-dimensional point cloud model of
the umbrella-shaped tensile membrane structure investigated in this paper are shown in
Figure 12.
As shown in Figure 12, it is worth noting that obvious deviations existed between
the measured three-dimensional reconstructed model and the theoretical model of the
umbrella-shaped tensile membrane structure. At this stage, only relying on the theoretical
model could not accurately characterize the spatial form of the physical tensile membrane
structure, nor could it be used to construct its digital twin model. Consequently, the spatial

to analyze. Therefore, the theoretical model of the umbrella-shaped tensile membrane

listed in Table 2, membrane form-finding was performed again on the basis of the original
3D3S theoretical form-finding framework. During the re-form-finding process, the external

4.4. Global Form Deviation Analysis Between the Theoretical Design Model and the Actual

To investigate spatial geometric form deviation between the theoretical/physical mod-
els, Geomagic software is used for verification. Firstly, the .stl file of the reconstructed

Subsequently, the encapsulated .stl file of the umbrella-shaped tensile membrane structure

as “TEST”, and the .stp theoretical model file was set as “REF”. The best-fit alignment and
3D comparison functions in Geomagic Control were then used to complete the morpho-
logical deviation analysis among the physical tensile membrane structure and the origi-
nal/updated theoretical models. The comparison results are shown in Figures 15 and 16.
