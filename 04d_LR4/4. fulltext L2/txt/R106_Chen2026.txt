ABSTRACT
Shield attitude control is vital for tunnel safety, yet existing single-scene models lack generalization and real-time 
adaptability across diverse geologies. Herein, a digital twin (DT)-driven intelligent control framework integrating 
the improved black kite algorithm (IBKA), natural gradient boosting (NGBoost), and NSGA-III is proposed to 
achieve automated, optimal attitude management. Using a Wuhan metro underpass as a case study, the 
framework ’ s performance was validated across typical geological conditions. Key results demonstrate that (1) the 
IBKA-NGBoost prediction model achieves an optimal accuracy-efficiency trade-off for small-sample data under 
two geological conditions, with R2values exceeding 0.91 for seven attitude indicators and a training time of only 
29.2 s; (2) the integrated IBKA-NGBoost-NSGA-III framework enables adaptive multiscenario optimization, 
reducing deviations of the seven attitude parameters by an average of 69.3%; adjustment of the 12 key con -
struction variables yielded a maximum improvement of 84.8%; and (3) embedding this algorithm into a DT 
system enhanced real-time regulatory capability, supporting adaptive adjustment for improved construction 
guidance and safety. Consequently, the proposed method enables adaptive adjustment, thereby supporting 
construction guidance and safety management.
1. Introduction
1.1. Research background
Metro tunnels play crucial roles in transportation and have become 
important infrastructure for cities [1 – 3] . However, during the con -
struction process of tunnel projects, they not only face challenges from 
complex and variable environments but also are affected by uncertain 
factors such as human operations, which makes the shield position 
difficult to control during tunnel construction [4 – 7] . Furthermore, as 
shield tunneling technology continues to mature, the safety standards 
for tunnel construction have substantially increased [8,9] . Therefore, in 
the construction of shield tunnels, maintaining control over the position 
of the shield requires special attention [10] . If the shield attitude is not 
well controlled, a series of problems, such as rising, deviation, snake 
movement, displacement and cracking [11,12] , which significantly 
affect the safety and quality of tunnel construction, can occur [13] . 
Moreover, several typical engineering accidents highlight the 
importance of controlling the attitude of the TBM [14,15] . Therefore, 
dynamic anticipation of the shield attitude and optimized control of the 
attitude deviation that is generated in the excavation route are highly 
important [9,16] .
1.2. Related research
1.2.1. Related research on prediction
To achieve shield tunneling attitude control, scholars have con -
ducted extensive research. The existing methods for predicting the 
attitude and position of tunnel boring machines include primarily 
theoretical derivation, experimental methods, numerical simulation, 
and artificial intelligence algorithms. In terms of theoretical formulas, 
Liu et al. [17] integrated acceleration, momentum, and kinematic 
equations and azimuth equations to establish a dynamic mathematical 
model for TBM attitudes to achieve control of TBM trajectory tracking. 
Shen et al. [18] used equivalent springs to model the interplay between 
shield tunneling and soil, which resulted in a method for calculating the 
shield attitude. Huang et al. [19] introduced shield attitude into the

large-scale model experiments. As examples of numerical simulation 
methods, Shen et al. [21] studied excavation mechanics and limit ranges 
during the shield attitude adjustment process, and Sun et al. [22]
examined how various soil parameters and burial depths affect the 
correction torque and shield attitude. They suggested employing atti -
tude adjustment methods by controlling tunnel parameters. However, 
the factors that influence the movement behavior of tunnel boring ma -
chines are complex and varied, with coupling relationships between 
them. Considering real geological conditions and the complex factors 
that influence shields in theoretical and experimental methods is diffi -
cult. Predictions that are obtained under simplified conditions cannot 
provide operators with accurate guidance and are costly. Numerical 
simulation methods involve long modeling cycles, and the boundary 
conditions and parameter values are highly dependent on experience, 
which makes updating them in real time challenging during tunneling. 
Additionally, forecasting the orientation of a TBM is a real-time and 
dynamic highly nonlinear challenge that encompasses numerous inter -
related input and output datasets. Traditional analysis methods often 
lack adaptability to typical geological conditions and provide insuffi -
cient real-time prediction capabilities and accuracy in cases with com -
plex nonlinear engineering issues [15] . To overcome these limitations, 
data-driven approaches have emerged as a powerful alternative, shift -
ing the focus from rigid theoretical derivations to adaptive learning from 
massive engineering datasets.
With advances in artificial intelligence, machine learning (ML) 
methods provide more innovative, scientific, and precise solutions for 
solving complex nonlinear engineering problems [23] . They have been 
extensively applied in engineering research for prediction, analysis, and 
optimization [24–26] . Liu et al. [27] integrated excavation efficiency 
and cost objectives to investigate the regulation of TBM parameters 
using an improved backpropagation neural network (BPNN) algorithm. 
Pan et al. [28] applied the support vector machine (SVM) algorithm to 
predict surface subsidence in conjunction with the Hongmei South Road 
Tunnel West Line project. SVMs are good at handling similar features, 
but their ability to deal with complex features and data gaps is limited. 
The BPNN has low efficiency and poor generalization ability. Ke Lei 
et al. [29] utilized the Transformer model to achieve rock classification 
in TBM tunnels. While deep learning architectures, particularly 
Transformer-based models, have gained prominence in time-series 
forecasting due to their self-attention mechanisms [30,31] , they often 
require massive datasets to achieve convergence and avoid overfitting. 
In shield tunneling projects, high-quality labeled data for specific 
geological strata often exhibit “small-sample” characteristics, which 
limits the efficacy of data-hungry Transformer-based models. Further -
more, the high computational demand of Transformers can hinder the 
real-time feedback required for DTs. The natural gradient boosting 
(NGBoost) algorithm is a probability-based boosting algorithm that was 
proposed by ML researchers Tony Duan et al. in 2019 [32] . It can 
address challenges such as nonlinearity, data noise, and complex 
dependence relationships [33] . Several scholars have applied it for 
prediction in the engineering field [34–36] . Liu constructed a BO- 
NGBoost prediction model for predicting the deformation of existing 
tunnels [37] . Zhou proposed a BO-NGBoost prediction model that out -
puts corresponding safety control strategies on the basis of the safety risk 
level of the shield approaching the structure [38] . Cao et al. used the 
NGBoost algorithm to accurately predict the optimal amount of sup -
plementary cementitious material, which served as a supportive tool for 
reducing the production costs and carbon emissions of segment concrete 
[33] . By employing the NGBoost technique, this method iteratively re -
fines the parameter probability distributions, thereby facilitating accu -
rate probabilistic estimation of the total thrust and torque [39] . They 
reported that the probabilistic prediction model based on NGBoost 
exhibited superior predictive performance compared with conventional 
deterministic ML models with the same quantity of or fewer data. In 
complex engineering systems and high-dimensional feature spaces, to 
explore patterns, a large number of data samples are usually required 
[40] . However, herein, the number of available samples is only 
approximately 1,000. The small sample size in this study is relative to 
tens of thousands or even hundreds of thousands of data records in some 
cases. As a probabilistic gradient-boosting model, NGBoost can increase 
robustness against both ground disturbances and equipment errors [41] . 
Building on previous research and analysis, in this study, the NGBoost 
algorithm is selected to model the intricate nonlinear relationships be -
tween the shield orientation and excavation-related parameters. How -
ever, the performance of such boosting models is highly sensitive to their 
configuration, necessitating a robust optimization strategy to ensure 
global convergence in complex search spaces.
Hyperparameters significantly influence the accuracy of NGBoost 
model predictions. To optimize these hyperparameters, techniques such 
as grid search and random search are frequently used to identify the best 
combination [42–44] . Many scholars have proposed the use of genetic 
algorithms and particle swarm optimization for hyperparameter selec -
tion. However, because both are population-based optimization tech -
niques, they necessitate an adequate number of initial training points for 
effectively searching for optimal solutions, which can lead to lower 
training efficiency [45] . To address these constraints, IBKA is an effi -
cient algorithm that can discover the optimal solution with the fewest 
objective function evaluations, thereby greatly promoting convergence 
speed and population diversity. It significantly outperforms all the 
currently popular competitive optimizers on most test functions. IBKA 
employs chaotic initialization and opposition-based learning to generate 
a highly diverse initial population that covers the solution space, thus 
avoiding the local-optimum traps that are caused by traditional random 
initialization. This strengthens the global exploration of the shield spa -
tial–temporal search space and provides a better starting point for 
determining NGBoost hyperparameters such as the learning rate and

1.2.2. Related research on multiobjective optimization
Constructing the multiobjective optimization (MOO) decision model 
to find the optimal attitude is the next key issue. To effectively solve 
multidimensional objective optimization problems, Deb [47] introduced 
a widely distributed reference point instead of a crowding degree on the 
basis of the NSGA-II framework, which resulted in a new generation 
algorithm, NSGA-III. By preserving the key features and benefits of the 
NSGA-II algorithm, the NSGA-III algorithm increases population di -
versity and improves the ability to discover superior Pareto-optimal 
solutions in multiobjective scenarios, which makes it particularly 
adept at addressing high-dimensional multiobjective problems [48,49] . 
Wen Xu et al. [12] used BO-LGBM-NSGA-III to achieve MOO for surface 
subsidence, cutterhead wear, and shield operation energy consumption. 
Chen Hongyu et al. [3] used BO-CatBoost-NSGA-III to achieve dynamic 
MOO for tunneling energy, speed and tool wear. NSGA-III predefines a 
set of uniformly distributed reference points to steer the search toward 
diverse trade-offs on the Pareto front and keep the algorithm from 
becoming trapped in local optima [50] . However, at present, research on 
the optimization of shield parameters and attitude is mainly focused on 
the algorithms used, with limited attention given to the integration of 
optimization models and physical entities [51] .Therefore, it is necessary 
to establish a framework that can not only accurately predict attitude 
but also convert modeling insights into reliable automated control 
strategies.
The emergence of digital twin (DT) technology is regarded as a 
promising approach to bridging the aforementioned issues. DT tech -
nology enables interactions among simulation, optimization, and feed -
back, and this capability creates new opportunities for intelligent 
control in tunnel construction [52] . Herein, a new method combining 
the IBKA-NGBoost-NSGA-III intelligent hybrid algorithm with the DT 
platform is proposed to promote intelligent adaptive attitude adjust -
ment. Currently, research is mainly focused on the application of arti -
ficial intelligence methods in specific geological settings [7] , and 
emphasis is placed on the application of construction parameters based 
on digital twins [40] ; however, research on the application of DTs for 
attitude control and evaluation is relatively scarce. This study combines 
MOO and a DT to explore the optimization and control of shield machine 
attitude under different geological conditions, and the implementation 
mechanism of the DT is systematically evaluated to promote DT 
deployment in the shield construction safety field.
1.3. Gaps and contributions of this study
1.3.1. Research gaps
Previous traditional methods and data-driven research have 
improved the reliability and efficiency of tunnel excavation. This is 
substantive progress in this field, but specific constraints have also been 
identified: 
(1) Emphasis on individual geological conditions: Prior research has 
often focused on specific types of geological conditions and has 
not succeeded in developing a universal model that is applicable 
to multiple geological scenarios. A comprehensive strategy is 
essential for addressing the complex geological challenges in 
tunnel engineering.
(2) Methodological variation: There is still no consensus regarding 
the adoption of deep learning methods that are related to shield 
spatial – temporal analysis or the application of ensemble algo -
rithms, which depend on the sample size and research objectives. 
The differences among these methods may affect the consistency 
and validity of the research results.
(3) Lack of adaptive optimization regulation and evaluation mecha -
nisms: The current data-driven methods use existing data for 
prediction, which results in a lag effect. The objective of the en -
gineering problem of determining the shield attitude should focus 
on optimization rather than prediction. Prediction is merely the 
logic for achieving the objective of control. Therefore, adaptive 
decision regulation for MOO and a DT evaluation mechanism are 
needed to effectively develop real-time intelligent DTs.
(4) Underused technological progress: In previous research, sensor 
data, ML, the Internet of Things, and other emerging technologies 
have rarely been used to develop an intelligent decision-making 
framework for the adaptive attitudes of shield tunnels using 
DTs. Integrating these technologies can increase the efficiency, 
precision and initiative of decision-making. This study focuses 
primarily on investigating the predictive regulation of a single 
geological condition rather than adaptive optimization decision- 
making [14] .
1.3.2. Motivation and contributions
During shield tunneling, the attitude of the machine easily becomes 
unstable and deviates under dynamically changing, complex geological 
conditions; thus, rapid response and timely control adjustments are 
essential. Moreover, as many as seven objectives must be simultaneously 
considered for attitude regulation. Therefore, determining which 
method to adopt and how to bridge the resulting research gaps are key 
issues that must be addressed. On the basis of the research status and 
problem-solving methods analyzed, the fundamental research inquiries 
of this study focus on the following: 
(1) How can an intelligent prediction framework be constructed 
without a large amount of sample data to accurately describe the 
complex nonlinear relationship between the shield attitude and 
influencing factors?
(2) How can the significance of parameters be determined and 
assessed, how can the key control parameters that influence the 
shield orientation during construction be identified, and how can 
effective control of the shield attitude be achieved?
(3) How can MOO be conducted on the basis of key parameters to 
achieve real-time dynamic adaptive control of the shield attitude 
under varying geological conditions?
Given the above problem analysis, herein, a DT platform with an 
intelligent IBKA-NGBoost-NSGA-III optimization control framework for 
the tunneling attitude is presented. The aim of this study is to provide a 
feasible method for adaptive real-time intelligent dynamic control of the 
shield attitude. To address the aforementioned gaps, the following 
contributions are made: 
• A novel DT-driven framework for adaptive shield attitude control: A 
closed-loop intelligent framework is proposed by integrating IBKA- 
NGBoost and NSGA-III, enabling real-time dynamic interaction be -
tween physical tunneling entities and virtual models to achieve 
automated attitude management across varying geological 
conditions.
• High-efficiency predictive modeling via IBKA-NGBoost under small- 
sample constraints: By optimizing the NGBoost algorithm with the 
IBKA, the model achieves excellent uncertainty quantification and 
generalization. It yields high precision (R

2. Methodology
To address the high-dimensional MOO problem related to the atti -
tude of the TBM and achieve intelligent decision-making in the 
tunneling system, in this study, a high-dimensional MOO method for the 
shield attitude that is based on the DT platform integrated with the 
IBKA-NGBoost-NSGA-III algorithm is developed. The primary process is 
outlined as follows: First, a framework of the shield attitude DT system is 
established, and the physical entity is mapped to a virtual model. On this 
basis, data are obtained through the DT platform to offer numerical 
sources for intelligent decision-making. Second, intelligent prediction is 
achieved through the integration of intelligent modules. Finally, control 
from the virtual entity to the entity is achieved, and the shield attitude is 
controlled. Moreover, the performance of the TBM is improved. The 
intelligent online real-time adaptive optimization and decision-making 
process is displayed in Fig. 1 .
2.1. Establishment of the DT model for shield attitude control
2.1.1. DT framework
Attitude control in shield tunneling using DTs is a stage in which 
shield tunneling is simulated in a virtual construction environment. 
Herein, DTs are introduced into shield tunneling attitude analysis, and 
an intelligent shield tunneling attitude analysis framework that is via 
DTs is proposed, which provides a visual decision-making environment 
for controlling the orientation of a TBM. The framework of DTs, which 
includes the following main parts, is shown in Fig. 2 . 
1. System Architecture and Components
(1) Physical layer
The physical layer consists of the physical entities, surrounding 
construction facilities, data collection sensors, and activities of con -
struction personnel. Its main functions include completing the basic 
construction tasks of the shield machine, providing parameters for twin 
body modeling, and collecting and transmitting data. To obtain various 
parameters, the shield machine is equipped with different types of 
sensors. For instance, the soil chamber pressure is monitored by pressure 
sensors installed at different positions (upper part, lower part, left side, 
and right side) of the soil chamber. The corresponding variables are: X1 
(upper part), X2 (lower part), X3 (left side), and X4 (right side). The 
comprehensive automated monitoring system can monitor and record 
the various attitude parameters of the shield machine in real time, 
including all the attitude parameters in this study. Additionally, the data 
acquisition frequency is set to 30 s per collection sequence and the In -
dustrial Ethernet data communication protocol is used to ensure the 
reliable acquisition of real-time data. 
(2) Digital layer
This layer includes virtual models of all the physical layer compo -
nents and maps the behavior of the physical layer. This involves estab -
lishing models for the appearance and rules of physical systems, 
including prediction and optimization algorithms, along with control -
ling physical shield tunneling construction and managing project 
engineering-related information. The construction process of the virtual 
model includes 3D geometric modeling of the shield machine based on 
Unity 3D, as well as modeling of behavior rules. The IBKA-NGBoost- 
NSGA-III algorithm is encapsulated as a callable microservice and 
embedded into the digital thread. 
(3) User layer
The user domain of DTs includes various participants who can make

2.1.2. Physical-to-virtual reflection: Establishment of a DT model
The sensors in the TBM persistently track and record the operating 
status throughout the excavation process, and the data are processed by 
the acquisition system. These sensors capture important information 
such as the shield excavation time, construction progress, construction 
parameters, and shield attitude. At present, the attitude and construc -
tion parameters are displayed on the screen in the control room of the 
TBM and can be remotely viewed for monitoring. However, the lack of 
intuitive real-time dynamic visualization functions hinders real-time 
analysis of the machine's operating status and safety, which results in 
an inability to fully understand the substantial volume of data generated 
in the TBM excavation process. To address this challenge, a DT platform 
was created as an effective means for reflecting physical entities. The 
mapping between the shield physical object and the virtual 
representation is shown in Fig. 3 .
2.1.3. Operational mechanism of the DT system
Based on the aforementioned DT platform, real-time intelligent 
control of the shield attitude is carried out. The operational mechanism 
of the DT system is as follows.
Step 1: Real-time Sensing. Multisource operational and attitude data 
are synchronously acquired in the physical layer using TBM-embedded 
sensors, including earth pressure cells, thrust jack transducers, and the 
guidance system. Following preliminary noise reduction via the local 
PLC, data packets are uploaded to the DT database at 1-second intervals 
(1 Hz) through an industrial Ethernet backbone, establishing a robust 
high-fidelity nexus between the physical and digital domains.
Step 2: Intelligent Prediction. Serving as the “ intelligent brain ” , the 
digital layer first performs preprocessing on the incoming streaming 
data. The system then invokes the pretrained IBKA-NGBoost algorithm 
module, which not only predicts the current attitude deviation based on 
historical tunneling data but also provides an uncertainty estimation of 
the prediction through its probabilistic output characteristics. Compared 
to traditional point-estimate prediction methods, this probabilistic 
paradigm aids in identifying latent risks and establishing a more scien -
tific basis for threshold judgment. An early warning is triggered when 
the predicted deviation probability exceeds the 80% confidence limit.
Step 3: Intelligent Optimization. Upon completion of the prediction, 
the system implements real-time MOO using the IBKA-NGBoost-NSGA- 
III framework. The optimization primarily aims to minimize attitude 
deviations while simultaneously enhancing the tunneling efficiency of 
the TBM process. By exploring the predefined search space of opera -
tional parameters, the algorithm identifies a set of Pareto-optimal so -
lutions. The digital layer then filters the most compatible recommended 
parameters from the Pareto set, tailored to the current geological con -
ditions, to serve as the basis for correction.
Step 4: Decision Feedback. The final optimization recommendations

2.1.4. Feature selection and sample preprocessing
1. Parameter variable selection
Herein, four shield machine position indicators are selected as the 
output variables of the model: the horizontal deviation of the shield 
machine incision (HDSI), the vertical deviation of the shield machine 
incision (VDSI), the horizontal deviation of the shield tail (HDST), and 
the vertical deviation of the shield tail (VDST). Additionally, three angle 
indicators are chosen: the pitch attitude angle (PAA), the rolling attitude 
angle (RAA), and the horizontal attitude angle (HAA). The HDSI rep -
resents the horizontal distance between the center of the TBM cutter -
head and the design tunnel axis (DTA) at a given moment. A positive 
deviation is recorded when the cutterhead center deviates to the right of 
the DTA. Similarly, the VDSI indicates the vertical distance between the 
nucleus of the cutterhead and the design tunnel axis (DTA), where a 
positive deviation signifies an upward shift toward the DTA. The same 
logic applies to the HDST and VDST. The HAA, which is also known as 
the yaw angle, is the deflection angle of the TBM in the horizontal di -
rection and describes the variation in the tunneling route in the hori -
zontal direction. This angle requires frequent correction control. The 
PAA represents the vertical attitude of the shield machine, specifically 
its deflection angle while tunneling vertically. Owing to its own gravity, 
the “ top-heavy and bottom-light ” phenomenon of the shield machine 
bowing downward can occur, which necessitates precise control of the 
PAA variation. The RAA is the rotation angle of the TBM around its axis 
because of the force from the cutterhead's rotation during propulsion. 
The RAA is defined as positive when the TBM rotates clockwise around 
its axis. To ensure normal forward tunneling of the shield machine, the 
RAA must be kept within a small range. As tunneling progresses, the 
attitude and position deviate from the DTA, and the seven considered 
parameters also change continuously. To accurately predict and control 
the shield position and attitude, precise measurements of these attitude 
indicators are needed [15,53] . Geological parameters and shield con -
struction factors are the key factors that influence the attitude and 
positioning of the shield machine. Owing to the short excavation section 
under the tunnel, the burial depth and geometric shape of the tunnel 
remain almost unchanged, and their effects can be ignored. On the basis 
of expert experience and the literature [9,14,15] , in this study, the soil 
warehouse pressure — upper, soil warehouse pressure — lower, soil 
warehouse pressure — left, soil warehouse pressure — right, threshold 
difference between upper and lower jacks, left and right jack thrust 
difference, travel difference, articulation cylinder stroke difference 
(horizontal), articulation cylinder stroke difference (vertical), articula -
tion oil pressure, cutterhead rotation speed, cutterhead torque, 
advancing speed, grouting pressure, grouting volume, spiral rotation 
speed, screw torque, internal friction angle, soil cohesion, compression 
modulus, and other input parameters are selected.

2.3.3. TOPSIS approach for deriving the optimal solution
The Pareto-optimal solutions that are generated by NSGA-III form a 
collection that effectively addresses MOO requirements. However, 
practical tunneling operations necessitate a single decision-making 
strategy to guide excavation activities. When the final optimal solu -
tion is selected from the Pareto front, various multicriteria deci -
sion ‑ making methods, such as those that are based on fuzzy logic or 
utility theory, are available. However, fuzzy logic requires the mem -
bership functions to be manually defined, which introduces strong 
subjectivity, and utility theory requires the decision ‑ makers ’ utility 
curve to be obtained in advance, which makes real ‑ time construction on 
site difficult. In this study, the TOPSIS method is adopted for the 
following reasons: First, the Pareto solution set that is generated in this 
research consists of precise numerical objectives; thus, no fuzzification is 
needed. Second, the “ closeness to the ideal solution ” concept of TOPSIS 
is intuitive and can provide a clear ranking of compromise alternatives. 
Third, TOPSIS has been widely applied to shield tunneling construction 
problems, and the use of TOPSIS to screen the optimal solution can offer 
valuable insights for similar projects [70,71] . Therefore, TOPSIS is 
considered the most suitable approach for addressing the deci -
sion ‑ making problem in this study. This method evaluates each candi -
date by measuring its distances to both the positive (ideal) and negative 
(nonideal) reference points. This dual-proximity assessment enables 
systematic ranking of trade-off solutions under complex geological and

3. Case study
3.1. Background
In this study, intelligent control and decision-making for shield 
attitude in a specific section of the Wuhan Metro Tunnel in China are 
used as a case study. This study focuses on the underpass construction 
between Julong Avenue Station and Tangyunhai Station of the Wuhan 
Metro Line 7 project, which intersects with Line 2. At the underpass 
location, the existing pipeline segment features an inner diameter of 6 m 
and is buried at a depth of 8.5 – 9.92 m and situated in cohesive soil that 
is interspersed with crushed stones. In contrast, the pipe segment for 
Line 7 features an outer diameter of 6.2 m and is buried at a depth of 
18.62 m and positioned within a layer of cohesive soil that is mixed with 
crushed stones and moderately weathered dolomite. Notably, the sep -
aration between the underpass section and the existing line is merely 
2.7 m.
The construction of this tunnel project has high requirements for the 
attitude and position of the TBM, and new intelligent technologies for 
predicting the attitude of the TBM are necessary. The shield machine 
attitude parameters and geological environment around the tunnel are
