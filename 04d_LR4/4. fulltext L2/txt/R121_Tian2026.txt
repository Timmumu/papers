ABSTRACT
Structural health monitoring of high earth-rock dams is essential for ensuring operational safety under complex 
hydro-mechanical conditions. However, existing digital twin approaches mainly focus on state prediction and 
lack capabilities for intelligent fault reasoning and engineering decision-making. To address this issue, this study 
proposes a digital twin-driven intelligent fault diagnosis framework integrated with Large Language Models 
(LLMs). Multi-source monitoring data, finite element simulations, and transfer learning are combined to 
construct multi-physics digital twins of deformation, seepage, and stress fields. A robust gradient estimation 
method and a real-time slope stability model are developed to identify critical failure indicators, including 
seepage anomalies, cracking risks, hydraulic fracturing, and slope instability. Furthermore, a data semantici -
zation engine is introduced to transform numerical fault information into engineering-oriented natural-language 
descriptions, which are subsequently analyzed by a dam-specific LLM for root-cause reasoning and mitigation 
recommendation generation. The proposed framework is validated using a 186 m-high core-wall rockfill dam 
with long-term monitoring data. The results demonstrate that the method can accurately reconstruct multi- 
physics fields, effectively identify potential structural risks, and provide interpretable decision-support infor -
mation, showing strong potential for intelligent safety management of large hydraulic infrastructures.
1. Introduction
Earth-rock dams have been widely constructed and applied world -
wide due to their strong adaptability to complex foundation conditions, 
the possibility of using locally available materials, and their excellent 
seismic performance [1] . In recent years, China has successively con -
structed or planned several high earth-rock dams with heights on the 
order of 300 m, such as Shuangjiangkou, Lianghekou, and Rumei [2,3] . 
However, during long-term operation, high earth-rock dams are sub -
jected to complex working conditions, including high water pressure, 
high stress, and particle crushing [4] . Structural failures induced by 
seepage and deformation still occur from time to time [5 – 7] . Therefore, 
carrying out Structural Health Monitoring (SHM) for high earth rock 
dams is of great engineering significance and can effectively safeguard 
public safety and property [8,9] .
As illustrated in Fig. 1 , with the continuous advancement of moni -
toring technologies, dam operation has generated massive volumes of 
monitoring data characterized by multiple types and scales [10 – 12] . In 
this context, digital twins of high earth-rock dams can effectively predict 
full-field responses [13,14] , such as deformation and seepage fields, 
thereby enabling multi-source data fusion. However, current SHM 
methods for high earth-rock dams based on digital twins still face the 
following challenges: (1) Existing digital twin-based multi-physical-field 
fault diagnosis approaches remain largely undeveloped. Traditional 
fault diagnosis methods are limited in their ability to perform real-time 
and full-field multi-physical-field diagnosis [15] . (2) Fault analysis and 
response planning still heavily rely on the knowledge and experience of 
human engineers, lacking systematic approaches for automated fault 
cause identification and decision-making [16] .
To improve the automation of SHM for high earth-rock dams, this 
study proposes a data-mechanism-knowledge-driven framework based 
on digital twins and Large Language Models (LLMs). The framework is 
built on multi-source monitoring data, uses digital twins to achieve full 
domain mapping of multi-field coupled states, and relies on LLMs to 
accomplish fault type diagnosis, cause analysis, and mitigation recom -
mendation generation. In this way, interpretable and interactive

2. Relevant work
Existing SHM methods for high earth-rock dams can be broadly 
classified into data-driven models and mechanism-driven models [17] . 
Data-driven approaches, such as statistical models based on the 
Hydrostatic-Seasonal-Time (HST) theory and various machine learning 
and deep learning methods, have achieved notable progress in defor -
mation time-series feature extraction [18] . Mechanism-driven ap -
proaches rely on finite element models to perform stress, seepage, and 
stability analyses, often combined with engineering reliability methods 
to quantify failure risks [19,20] . Recurrent neural networks, including 
Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) and Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU), are 
capable of capturing nonlinear temporal dependencies in high-dam 
deformation [21 – 23] , while Graph Neural Networks (GNN) can model 
spatial relationships among monitoring points [24,25] . However, these 
methods are primarily dependent on limited monitoring data and are 
unable to achieve full-domain state characterization of dams. They are 
further constrained by cross-domain discrepancies, limited interpret -
ability, and insufficient engineering operability. As a result, their 
application in identifying complex multi-field coupling behaviors and 
failure modes of earth-rock dams remains limited.
With the advancement of Industry 4.0 and intelligent infrastructure 
concepts [26,27] , digital twin technology has provided a new paradigm 
for full-domain perception and multi-field coupling analysis of high 
earth-rock dams [13,28] . Digital twins establish a dynamic connection 
between physical and virtual systems through the integration of physical 
entities, Building Information Modelling (BIM), and Finite Element 
Method (FEM) simulations, thereby enabling full-domain and 
multi-physics analysis [29] . In recent studies, BIM models have been 
increasingly adopted as centralized information carriers for geometry, 
material properties, monitoring layouts, and operation records, while 
FEM models provide physically interpretable responses for deformation, 
seepage, and stress evolution under complex hydro-mechanical condi -
tions [30] . The interoperability between BIM and FEM further improves

By integrating physical entities, numerical simulation models, sensor

enable synchronized mapping and predictive analysis between physical 
and virtual systems [35,36] . This paradigm has been validated in 
structures such as bridges, tunnels, and hydraulic infrastructures

calibration of material properties and boundary conditions, and 
cross-domain discrepancies between numerical simulations and field 
monitoring data persist, which limits the accuracy of twin fields [14,41] . 
Moreover, current digital twin frameworks remain insufficient in se -
mantic understanding, fault interpretation, knowledge reasoning, and 
engineering measure recommendation, and thus cannot yet achieve true 
intelligent decision making.
Meanwhile, LLMs, with their robust capabilities in natural language 
understanding and logical reasoning, are rapidly emerging as core 
technologies for intelligent infrastructure monitoring [42,43] . When 
fine-tuned with domain-specific knowledge bases, LLMs can transform 
high-dimensional digital twin outputs into engineering-oriented se -
mantic descriptions, automatically generating fault inferences and 
mitigation recommendations [44,45] . By incorporating 
retrieval-augmented generation or knowledge graphs [46] , compliance 
with industry standards and engineering logic is significantly enhanced, 
enabling digital twins to transition from numerically driven analysis to 
knowledge-driven reasoning [47 – 49] . However, a critical review reveals 
that these emerging digital twin-LLM integrated frameworks are pre -
dominantly applied to structures with relatively straightforward 
boundary conditions, such as standard building frames or logistical site 
operations [50,51] , or treat the LLMs merely as an external 
natural-language chatbot for database querying, rather than a deeply 
coupled reasoning engine for multi-physics interpretation [52,53] . For 
high earth-rock dams, which are characterized by multi-physics and 
multi-scale coupling, the integration of digital twins and LLMs is still at 
an early exploratory stage, and systematic framework design and engi -
neering validation remain lacking.
Overall, digital twins provide high-precision virtual representations 
of multi-field coupled states in dams, while LLMs offer engineering 
knowledge, natural language reasoning, and human-machine interac -
tion capabilities. How to construct a deeply integrated intelligent agent 
that unifies data, physical mechanisms, and knowledge to enable intel -
ligent decision making for the structural safety of earth-rock dams re -
mains a critical research gap and constitutes the core problem addressed 
in this study.
3. Proposed method
To enhance the knowledge, reasoning, automation, and intelligence 
of existing earth-rock dam digital twin models and to improve the 
timeliness and effectiveness of structural fault response, this study 
proposes a hybrid framework integrating digital twins with LLMs. The 
overall framework consists of three interconnected stages, as illustrated 
in Fig. 2 , where the output of each stage serves as the input for the 
subsequent analysis and reasoning process. First, a multi-physics digital 
twin predictive model is developed by combining deep transfer learning 
with physics-based finite element simulations. By integrating moni -
toring data, hydraulic conditions, and numerical simulation results, the 
model reconstructs coupled multi-field states of the dam, including 
deformation, seepage, and stress fields. The generated multi-physics 
responses provide both data-driven and mechanically interpretable 
representations of the structural state, which serve as the foundational 
input for subsequent fault diagnosis. Second, an intelligent fault

states obtained from the digital twin model. Critical safety-related in -

hydraulic loads, and operational conditions, are fed into the LLM-based 
reasoning module. By incorporating external knowledge bases and task- 
oriented fine-tuning, the LLM performs knowledge-driven reasoning, 
root cause analysis, and mitigation strategy generation, thereby 
enabling intelligent decision support for earth-rock dam safety

real-time acquisition of multi-field information, including deformation, 
seepage, and stress monitoring data, as shown in Fig. 3 . In combination

monitoring technologies, the perceptual information is categorized into

local monitoring points to the entire dam body, this study employs 
source domain data obtained from finite element numerical simulations 
and target domain data from sensor monitoring. A digital twin model is 
constructed by integrating these data with transfer learning techniques.

3.2. Intelligent structural fault diagnosis
The structural fault diagnosis primarily processes the deformation 
twin field, seepage twin field, and stress twin field transmitted from the 
multi-physics digital twin and transforms them into corresponding fault 
types, as shown in Fig. 4 . Based on statistical analysis of typical failure 
types in earth-rock dams and structural mechanistic reasoning, struc -
tural faults in earth-rock dams can be classified into two categories: the 
impervious zone and the rockfill zone. In the impervious zone, the main 
fault types include seepage failure, uneven deformation, and hydraulic 
fracturing, which are evaluated using hydraulic gradient, deformation 
gradient, and effective stress, respectively. In the rockfill zone, the pri -
mary fault type concerns slope stability, with evaluation indicators 
including deformation rate and the factor of safety for slope stability. To 
achieve high-precision analysis of these structural safety indicators, this 
study develops real-time gradient computation models based on the 
deformation twin field, seepage twin field, and stress twin field, as well 
as a real-time slope stability computation model. These models support 
accurate identification of fault types and risk assessment, which can be 
expressed as follows:

The intelligent decision-making module processes fault information

serves as a critical bridging component within the proposed framework. 
Its primary role is to map heterogeneous fault-related information, 
including structural failure types, hydraulic loads, spatial coordinates, 
and dam segments, into structured variables that are explicitly associ -
ated with physically meaningful entities of the dam system. In this sense, 
it is not a simple text conversion module, but a constrained semantic 
mapping process. The generated variables are required to satisfy pre -
defined physical and engineering consistency constraints, ensuring 
alignment with structural mechanics interpretations and monitoring 
system definitions. Only such standardized and physically interpretable 
representations are passed to the subsequent reasoning stage. In this 
study, the LLM module is not used as a general-purpose conversational 
chatbot. Instead, it is configured as a task-oriented reasoning engine that 
accepts structured semantic inputs and outputs constrained engineering 
interpretations, including fault diagnosis explanations and correspond -
ing mitigation suggestions. The model operates on structured inputs 
rather than free-form dialogue, ensuring consistency with engineering 
semantics. Overall, the data semanticization engine ensures that multi- 
source monitoring data are consistently anchored to the dam ’ s phys -
ical structure, enabling reliable downstream reasoning and decision- 
making.

The dam-specific LLM serves as the core intelligent unit for fault type 
identification, root cause analysis, and mitigation strategy generation. 
This model consists of two main components: a domain knowledge base 
and a Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA)-based fine-tuning module. The 
knowledge base provides structured domain expertise, while the LoRA- 
based fine-tuning ensures consistency in the input-output formats and 
improves the model ’ s ability to generate task-specific responses. At the 
knowledge construction level, the LLM relies on a systematically inte -
grated, dedicated dam knowledge base K , including engineering design, 
construction, and monitoring reports. In addition to project-specific 
documentation from the case study dam, the knowledge base also in -
corporates authoritative industry standards, including Specifications for

3.4. Case study
To validate the effectiveness of the proposed method, this study se -
lects a high-core-wall rockfill dam with comprehensive monitoring data 
as the research subject. The dam is located in the Dadu River basin and is 
a large-scale hydropower project primarily used for power generation, 
while also serving flood control, sediment retention, and other inte -
grated functions.
3.5. Project description
The PB Dam is a gravel-core rockfill dam with a maximum structural 
height of 186 m, defined as the elevation difference between the foun -
dation level (670 m) and the dam crest (856 m). The normal reservoir 
water level is 850 m, the flood-season operating limit is 841 m, and the 
dead water level is 790 m, resulting in a maximum drawdown depth of 
60 m. The total reservoir capacity is 5.337 billion m³ , and it functions as 
a partially regulated reservoir. As shown in Fig. 6 A , the layered dis -
placements of the downstream transition material and the upstream and 
downstream directions of the rockfill body are monitored using hori -
zontal extensometers. In the cross-section at chainage 0 + 240 m, 
sixteen monitoring points are arranged at elevations of 808 m, 758 m, 
and 731 m, numbered CH1 to CH16. Piezometers P10 to P32 are 
installed at the junctions between the core wall, downstream dam body,

A numerical model is established using the finite element method in 
ABAQUS. A two-dimensional plane strain model is adopted based on the 
dam design drawings, with material zoning implemented as shown in 
Fig. 6 B . The geometry of the model is directly derived from engineering 
design documents, ensuring consistency with the actual dam configu -
ration. The simulation domain extends to twice the dam height in the 
upstream, downstream, and foundation directions to minimize bound -
ary effects. Appropriate boundary conditions are applied, including 
prescribed hydraulic head boundaries at the upstream and downstream 
reservoirs and flux-controlled seepage boundaries at the foundation. The 
finite element mesh consists of 6572 elements and 13,348 nodes. Ma -
terial parameters [13] are determined based on design values and 
further refined using time-varying parameter inversion, and multiple 
parameter sets are used to construct the source-domain simulation 
dataset.
The transient deformation behavior is modeled using the Duncan- 
Chang constitutive model, while time-dependent creep effects are rep -
resented using a seven-parameter creep model. The creep model cap -
tures the macroscopic time-dependent deformation caused by particle 
rearrangement and crushing, although it does not explicitly describe the 
progressive degradation of strength-related parameters. To account for 
this limitation, the internal friction angles of the zoned materials used in 
the slope stability analysis are represented as time-varying parameters 
through period-based parameter inversion [54,55] . In this process, the 
parameter evolution is fitted using an appropriate degradation function, 
such as an exponential function, according to the historical inversion 
results and monitoring trends. This approach does not involve real-time 
parameter adjustment of the numerical model, but rather provides a 
formalized description of long-term material degradation. As a result, 
the model can better reflect irreversible processes such as particle 
crushing and consolidation, thereby improving its ability to capture 
long-term slope stability evolution, as shown in Fig. 6 C .
3.6. Dataset description
Based on the actual monitoring configuration of deformation, 
seepage, and stress at the PB Dam (as summarized in Table 1 ), this study 
constructed the target-domain dataset using the monitoring records 
from extensometers, piezometers, and earth pressure cells collected 
between 28 May 2012 and 23 May 2024. The measured data from 
various monitoring instruments were used as inputs, while the digital- 
twin predictions of the full-domain physical fields of the PB Dam were 
used as outputs. The dataset was randomly divided into training, vali -
dation, and test sets with a ratio of 0.7:0.1:0.2. Meanwhile, a series of 
operating-condition simulations was generated using the established 
finite element model, incorporating both design parameters and 
parameter-inversion results. These simulations formed the source- 
domain dataset, which was employed to enhance the model ’ s general -
ization capability and improve the reconstruction accuracy of the twin 
fields. The dam-specific LLM was fine-tuned based on DeepSeek-V2.5. A 
total of 108 high-quality samples were used for the fine-tuning process.

4. Results and analysis
The experiments were conducted in a Windows 10 operating system 
environment using the PyCharm IDE and the PyTorch 2.5.1 platform 
based on Python 3.10. The server hardware configuration included an 
Intel Core i7 – 13700 CPU and an NVIDIA RTX 4070 SUPER GPU. During 
the digital twin modelling process, the learning rate was set to 0.001, the 
batch size was 64, and the Adam optimizer was used to update the 
network parameters. The training was performed for 500 iterations. To 
quantitatively evaluate the accuracy and performance of the digital twin 
model, three metrics were selected: Mean Absolute Error (MAE), Root 
Mean Squared Error (RMSE), and Mean Absolute Percentage Error 
(MAPE) [56] .
4.1. Accuracy of the digital twin model
To evaluate the accuracy of the proposed method in digital twin 
modelling, the test set from December 31, 2021, to May 23, 2024, was 
selected to systematically compare the predicted deformation, seepage, 
and stress twin fields with the corresponding measured monitoring data. 
By examining the consistency with physical mechanisms, the variation 
trends of monitoring time histories, and quantitative indicators such as 
MAE, MAPE, and RMSE, the model accuracy was thoroughly assessed. 
Fig. 7 A presents the deformation, seepage, and stress twin fields of PB 
Dam at the time instant of December 31, 2021. The results indicate that 
all three twin fields accurately capture the actual deformation patterns, 
hydraulic gradient distribution, and stress transfer behavior, and their 
spatial characteristics are consistent with the theoretical trends of nu -
merical simulation. This demonstrates that the model can effectively 
reconstruct the internal physical evolution of the dam. Fig. 7 B compares 
the predicted twin curves with the measured time histories at selected 
monitoring points. It can be observed that the deformation prediction of 
CH4 matches the measured data well; although other deformation 
points, such as CH1, exhibit certain deviations, their overall trends 
remain consistent, and the discrepancies mainly arise from cross-domain 
differences. Nevertheless, the prediction accuracy is significantly 
improved compared with traditional numerical simulation methods [28, 
13,14] . The fitting performance of seepage and stress points is generally 
good, with the variation trends closely following the measured data, 
except for slight deviations near E16.
Fig. 7 C presents the statistical results of MAE, RMSE, and MAPE for 
all monitoring points in the test set. The average MAE values for 
deformation, seepage, and stress points are 6.63 mm, 1.88 m, and 13.72 
kPa, respectively. Since the pore pressure is close to zero at certain 
times, the MAPE of seepage points is not representative; however, the 
average MAPE of deformation and stress points reaches 1.12% and 
1.80%, indicating low overall prediction errors. In summary, the digital 
twin model developed using the proposed method exhibits high accu -
racy and can meet the requirements for real-time full-domain structural 
safety assessment of dams.

5. Discussions
The hybrid framework integrating digital twin and LLM technologies 
proposed in this study provides a practical solution for SHM and oper -
ational management of high earth-rock dams. By combining multi- 
physics digital twin prediction with knowledge-driven reasoning, the 
framework improves the analysis capability for complex monitoring 
data and enhances the interpretability of diagnostic results. In engi -
neering practice, the framework can continuously integrate monitoring 
data with digital twin simulation outputs to identify abnormal structural 
behavior, generate interpretable diagnostic reports, and support routine 
dam safety assessment and maintenance management. During emer -
gency conditions, such as abnormal seepage or deformation evolution, 
the system can rapidly infer potential failure modes and provide tar -
geted mitigation recommendations, thereby assisting engineers in 
timely operational decision-making. Owing to its modular architecture 
and compatibility with monitoring systems and numerical simulation 
models, the proposed framework also shows potential for broader 
application to other large hydraulic infrastructures and intelligent 
infrastructure monitoring scenarios.
Digital twin methods, as advanced monitoring tools in dam engi -
neering, can, through the combination of finite element analysis and

deformation, seepage, and stress behaviors [13,28] . However, there

diagnosis model for high earth-rock dams based on digital twin outputs. 
This model, relying on gradient computation and slope stability analysis,
