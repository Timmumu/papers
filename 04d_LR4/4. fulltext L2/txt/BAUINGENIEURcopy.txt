ABSTRACT Artificial Intelligence (AI) is now playing a cruci-
al role not only in everyday life, evidenced by the booming ap-
plication of Large Language Models (LLMs) such as the Gene-
rative Pretrained Transformer (GPT), but also in its potential to 
transform traditional industries like civil engineering. This 
work examines the application of novel AI tools to enable Digi-
tal Twins (DT) for engineering structures, providing a compre-
hensive solution for the life-cycle management. A comprehen-
sive state-of-the-art review is conducted to explore existing 
advancements in sensing, inspection, and simulation that are 
fundamental to the development of digital twins. Building on 
this knowledge, a framework is proposed to define DT for En-
gineering (DT4ENG) based on their emphasis and data flow, 
including forward DT , backward DT , and DT -informed decision 
making. Following this, a case study on floating offshore wind 
turbine (FOWT) structures demonstrates the application of 
DT4ENG in a specific domain, with findings that have broader 
implications for the life-cycle management of engineering 
structures. The present study reveals that the AI enables digital 
twins to effectively identify potential structural issues, predict 
deterioration, and suggest timely maintenance interventions. 
This approach enhances the accuracy of structural health as-
sessments, optimises resource allocation, and minimises 
downtime. By translating the capabilities of digital twins into 
actionable strategies, the research highlights their potential to 
significantly improve the life-cycle management of enginee-
ring infrastructure. In general, these advancements promise a 
new era of intelligent maintenance strategies, offering increa-
sed safety, extended service life, and cost-effectiveness. The 
proposed DT4ENG is set to become a standard in the traditio-
nal industry, driving a shift towards more sustainable, resi-
lient, adaptive, and intelligent structures.

1 Introduction
The next-generation engineering structure is on a key turning 
point, with increasing demands for improved life-cycle design, 
construction and maintenance to ensure safety, efficiency, sustai-
nability and resilience. The growing complexity of environments, 
coupled with aging infrastructure systems, underscores a shift 
from traditional open-loop methods to more proactive, predicti-
ve, and site-specific strategies. Advances in sensing technology, 
inspection methods, and simulation capabilities provide a robust 
foundation of reliable data and models, essential for understan-
ding the current state and predicting the future performance of 
civil engineering structures [1]. For instance, Sensing technolo-
gies have improved infrastructure monitoring by using tools such 
as fibre optics, drones, and wireless sensor networks, which con-
tinuously assess structural conditions [2]. These technologies of-
fer online data collection on stress, strain, vibration, and environ-
mental effects, allowing for immediate fault identification [3]. 
Advanced inspection techniques like ultrasonic pulse velocity and 
ground-penetrating radar further enhance infrastructure health 
assessments. Contact-less measurement methods, including visual 
inspection systems with convolutional neural networks, have sig-
nificantly improved the accuracy and efficiency of structural as-
sessments[4, 5]. The rapid development in computational power 
and advanced simulation methods has greatly enhanced the mo-
delling of complex civil infrastructure. Simulations powered by fi-
nite element analysis and computational fluid dynamics allow en-
gineers to predict structural behaviour under various conditions 
[6]. Structural control, particularly vibration control, has advan-
ced through vibration-based energy harvesting, providing benefits 
in energy efficiency and structural stability [7]. 
More important, the growth of artificial intelligence (AI) has 
enabled new possibilities for Digital Twins for Engineering 
(DT4ENG). Digital twins [8] are dynamic, virtual replicas of 
physical assets, providing real-time views of their state and per-
formance [9]. By integrating predictive analytics, continuous mo-
nitoring, and regular inspections, DT4ENG supports a more in-
formed and adaptive maintenance approach [10]. Within the core 
of the DT4ENG lies the Artificial Intelligence (AI) algorithm, e.g. 
machine learning, deep learning, reinforcement learning, etc., 
which analyse data from sensors and inspections to detect pat-
terns, predict failures, and plan maintenance actions [11]. To this 
end, DT4ENG represents a significant advancement for civil en-
gineering maintenance. These systems enhance the precision and 
timeliness of maintenance interventions, extend asset service life, 
optimise resource use, and reduce the eco-impact of maintenance 
actions [12]. Additionally, DT4ENG serves as a tool for training 
and decision support, allowing engineers to explore scenarios, as-
sess strategy impacts, and make well-informed decisions with up-
to-date information and sufficient what-if simulations [13]. 
Specifically, ageing infrastructure is particularly vulnerable to de-
terioration, which can compromise structural integrity and safety 
[14]. Various efforts have highlighted the importance of predic- 
tive maintenance strategies in extending the lifespan of such 
structures. Especially, factors like corrosion-fatigue and dynamic 
loads contribute significantly to the degradation of structural 
components, emphasising the need for advanced diagnostic and 
predictive tools to manage these issues effectively [15, 16].

safety. The social acceptance module emphasises the AI in inte-

As discussed above, the future engineering structure, especially 
the wind turbine structure, demands an enhanced, data-model in-
tegrated approach to life-cycle management, driven by advance-
ments in sensing, inspection, and simulation technologies. The 
application of DT4ENG signifies a pivotal shift that has the po-
tential to revolutionise the management of infrastructure assets. 
This work explores the role and potential of AI-enhanced 
DT4ENG in improving the maintenance of civil engineering 
structures. It begins by detailing digital twins from three per-
spectives: forward DT, backward DT, and DT-informed decision 
making. Following this, a case study on forward DT in floating 
offshore wind turbine structures is presented, providing a snap- 
shot of state-of-the-art applications and their benefits. Finally, the 
work concludes with insights into future directions and the 
broader implications of this transformative technology.
2 General Perspectives of DT4ENG
2.1 Forward Digital T wins
Forward DT represent a cutting-edge approach in engineering 
maintenance, combining real-time data acquisition with advanced 
simulations and predictive analytics. This novel method enhances 
structural serviceability, sustainability and resilience by providing 
a continuous, comprehensive view of a structural current conditi-
on and future performance. The detailed depiction of forward di-
gital twins, as shown in Figure 2, outlines a comprehensive pro-
cess beginning with the real-time data acquisition directly from 
structures and advancing towards an improved assessment of 
structural health state. This framework exemplifies the integra- 
tion of multiple advanced methodologies, from site-condition 
monitoring that continuously tracks external conditions affecting 
the structure to sophisticated data analysis techniques that inter-
pret complex datasets. The process starts with the collection of 
site-condition data, including wind and wave measurements, 
using technologies like LiDAR (Light Detection and Ranging) 
and other sensors. These monitoring data is fed into multi-phy-
sics simulation tools, which simulate interactions between various 
components of the wind turbine under environmental excitations. 
The results from the above simulations are processed with load 
transfer from the structure to component, and then to the detail, 
by analysing stress ranges and distributions. Following the load 
analysis, a deterioration model is applied to predict structural de-
terioration over time, considering factors such as material 
roughness, critical crack size, growth rates, and failure criteria. 
This model integrates multiple disciplines like electrochemistry, 
damage mechanics, and fracture mechanics. Finally, probabilistic 
models predict the growth of damage over time, simulating the 
progression of cracking and assessing the likelihood of different 
failure modes, accounting for uncertainties in material properties, 
environmental conditions, and operational loads.
In general, forward DT of engineering structures signify a ru-
dimentary shift in structural condition assessment by introducing 
a proactive model that combines site-specific real-time monito-
ring, numerical simulations and prediction models [17]. Utilising 
state-of-the-art sensing technologies, these systems continuously 
collect data on structural behaviour and site-conditions, which is 
processed using advanced simulation models and algorithms to 
predict responses to various excitation and envisage possible de-
terioration modes [18]. The core of forward DT lies in their abi-
lity to simulate future states based on current conditions, employ-
ing algorithms like computational mechanics, material science, 
and electrochemistry to evaluate structural behaviour, anticipate 
degradation, and predict service life [19]. Through the further in-

2.2 Backward Digital T wins
In contrast to the prediction-focused forward DT, backward 
DT perform a prognostic function for structures. These DTs are 
crucial for synthesising prediction and inspection results, thereby 
providing a comprehensive understanding of structural conditi-
ons. The backward DT is founded on the convergence of pre-
diction models, inspection result and monitoring data. Besides in-
tegrating prediction models and monitoring data, it typically in-
cludes historical data from periodic and non-regular inspections 
[21]. Unlike forward digital twins, which primarily predict future 
states, backward digital twins initially focus on diagnostic analy-
sis, which then informs and supports updated predictions. As il-
lustrated in Figure 3, the backward DT for wind turbine struc- 
tures begins with model-data integration, where observed 
structural conditions are aligned with their digital replica. This 
process involves continuous monitoring and periodic inspections. 
Advanced algorithms, such as Physics-Informed Neural Networks 
(PINNs) [22] and Dynamic Bayesian Networks (DBNs) [23], 
can be employed to analyse the relationships between state, re-
sponse, and measurement, updating the a priori model into an a 
posteriori counterpart. Accordingly, this model-data integrated 
approach provides an updated understanding of structural beha-
viours and integrity by identifying differences between model 
predictions and exact observations and/or measurements.
As discussed above, backward digital twins are particularly ef-
fective in identifying patterns and damage that might not be im-
mediately visible through conventional monitoring techniques. 
Say, these analyses can uncover inconsistencies between expected 
and actual structural responses and observations under various 
environmental conditions. Such results are crucial as they prompt 
a re-examination of the predictive models and may lead to neces-
sary adjustments in the DT to mirror the structural state and be-
haviour accurately. Additionally, by integrating inspection data, 
backward DT expand the database. Inspections can reveal early 
signs of damage that sensors may have missed, requiring updates 
to the DT to account for these findings. Therefore, backward DT 
act as a dynamic feedback system, continually enhancing the ac-
curacy of the structural model.
2.3 Digital T wins-Informed Decision Making
In general, the forward and backward DT provide in-depth in-
sights into the current condition and anticipated evolution of en-
gineering structures. These insights enable informed decision-ma-
king to optimise maintenance policies [24]. The essence of this 
type of optimisation lies in striking a balance between risk miti-
gation and cost reduction, achieved by the intelligent interpreta- 
tion of insights derived from both model and data. Figure 4 illus-
trates the core engine of this process, including a dynamic 
POMDP (Partially Observable Markovic Decision Process, shown 
in Figure 4a), and reinforcement learning with muti-agent 
(shown in Figure 4b) to support the solution of the dynamic 
POMDP. The dynamic POMDP begins with an initial state of the 
structure, followed by an action, such as a maintenance interven-
tion or operational adjustment, which then transit the structure 
to a new state. The outcome of this intervention, whether bene- 
ficial or detrimental, serves as a reward or penalty within a rein-
forcement learning framework, guiding the selection of subse-
quent actions. The reinforcement learning framework [25] in- 
volves a Global A3C network that replicates its parameters to 
multiple worker agents. Each worker interacts with the environ-
ment, taking actions and receiving states and rewards. These in-
teractions are recorded in a training set, which is used to update 
the global network, reducing the effects of high uncertainty.
In this context, the decision-making is not only about selecting 
the right action but also determining the optimal timing (When), 
identifying the most critical maintenance needs (What), and se-
lecting the most effective intervention methods (How) [26]. The 
DT projects the future condition of the structure based on its 
current state and simulate various scenarios to predict the out- 
comes of different actions. The optimisation planning component 
leverages the intelligence of DT. By utilising insights from both 
forward and backward DT, owners can develop maintenance 
schedules, allocate resources effectively, and prepare for future 
challenges. Scenario simulations allow exploration of different de-
cision outcomes, identifying the most cost-effective strategies 
while maintaining a low risk of structural failure.

3 Digital T wins-boosted deterioration 
prognosis of floating offshore wind turbine 
structures: a case study
To illustrate the practical application and benefits of 
DT4ENG, a detailed case study is presented in this section, focu-
sing on the corrosion-fatigue (CF) deterioration of high-strength 
bolts in the ring-flange connections of floating offshore wind tur-
bines (FOWTs) on modular energy islands (MEIs) [27]. The 
MEI is designed to harness the abundant natural resources found 
in ocean depths, such as wind, tidal, and solar energy. However, 
this pioneering approach presents unique engineering challenges, 
including the increased susceptibility of high-strength bolts in the 
ring-flange connections of wind turbine towers to corrosion- 
fatigue (CF) deterioration [28]. According, the case study has 
been carried out to elucidate the CF deterioration mechanisms af-
fecting bolts in floating offshore wind turbines (FOWTs) on 
MEIs by integrating material testing data, site-specific environ-
mental conditions, a probabilistic CF (PCF) model, and advanced 
multi-physics simulations.
The research incorporates wind-wave data from the Gulf of 
Mexico [29] into the multi-physics simulation tool OpenFAST 
[30]. As illustrated in Figure 5, this data is processed through the 
PCF model to predict the structural deterioration evolution. The 
assessment highlights the stochastic nature of CF, leveraging ma-
terial test data and site-specific conditions to improve analysis ac-
curacy.

4 Conclusions
Based on the above efforts and discussions, several major fin-
dings can be drawn. 
•The AI boosts Digital Twins for Engineering (DT4ENG), 
which emerges as a transformative solution for life-cycle ma-
nagement of engineering structures. This provides a proactive 
and predictive approach that is crucial for addressing the chal-
lenges posed by aging infrastructure exposed to complex ser-
vice conditions.
•Advances in sensing technologies, inspection methods, and si-
mulation capabilities have established a robust and reliable 
framework. This framework is essential for understanding, 
predicting, and managing the performance of engineering 
structures through DT4ENG.
•A case study on the modular energy island (MEI) concept de-
monstrates the effectiveness of DT4ENG in predicting corro- 
sion-fatigue (CF) deterioration in floating offshore wind tur-
bines. The study highlights the importance of integrating pre-
dictive models, material data, and monitored site-specific con-
ditions into probabilistic simulations.
•The insights gained from this work advocate for an integrated 
prediction-monitoring-inspection framework. This approach 
supports a proactive maintenance methodology that prioritises 
high-risk failure modes, ensuring the structural integrity, 
functionality, and durability.
While the integration of DT4ENG shows great promise, there are 
still challenges to overcome, including the high initial setup costs, 
the need for continuous data acquisition and processing, and the 
complexity of integrating various data sources and models. 
Future efforts are highly suggested on developing more cost-ef-
fective solutions for deploying DTs, enhancing the interoperabili-
ty of different systems and data formats, and advancing machine 
learning algorithms to improve accuracy and efficiency. Additio-
