ABSTRACT
Tunnel boring machines (TBMs) often experience attitude deviation during excavation, impacting the stability 
and safety of tunnel construction. Traditional attitude adjustment relies on manual adjustment, which has a 
lagging effect. Therefore, the study combines a digital twin platform and a hybrid intelligence algorithm to 
enable real-time adjustment of the shield attitude deviation. The particle swarm optimization and random forest 
(PSO-RF) algorithm is first used to make accurate predictions of the shield attitude. Shapley additive explana -
tions (SHAP) is subsequently employed to identify the key construction parameters. Then, based on these pa -
rameters, a control system for the shield attitude is designed in conjunction with a digital twin (DT) technique. A 
case study of China ’ s Guiyang Metro Line 3 demonstrates the following: (1) The PSO-RF model achieves high 
accuracy, with R² values ranging from 0.916 to 0.943 for six shield attitude targets. (2) The key shield parameters 
are continuously optimized and adjusted within the control range to achieve shield attitude control. (3) The 
digital twin system provides real-time attitude warnings and parametric inference, significantly improving TBM 
performance and safety. In this paper, a novel method of combining predictive modeling and the DT platform is 
proposed. Under the proposed intelligent method, the attitude deviation of a TBM during tunneling was 
significantly reduced.
1. Introduction
With the growing demand for underground transportation systems, 
tunnel construction faces numerous challenges [1] , particularly in 
maintaining shield attitude control. Shield tunnels have become the 
preferred method for underground tunnel construction because they 
adapt to diverse geological conditions. The shield construction process 
for rail transit is extremely complex and involves many construction 
techniques [2] . Shield attitude control while tunneling is key to ensuring 
sufficient tunnel construction quality [3,4] . During shield construction, 
the shield tunnel axis is affected by external factors such as excavation 
surface disturbances, shield quality changes and shield operation diffi -
culties. As a result, the axis direction of the tunnel is difficult to control, 
and the tunnel can easily deviate from the designed route [5] ; moreover, 
the shield advancing attitude can tilt up or become deviated or

issues. Some typical engineering cases also illustrate the challenges for

the defects caused by these accidents incurred significant economic 
losses and postponed the construction period by two months. Given 
these challenges, developing intelligent and timely methods for shield 
attitude adjustment remains a crucial research focus.
Ensuring the quality of shield construction necessitates precise shield 
attitude adjustment and control. At present, this adjustment process is 
predominantly manual, but because this adjustment needs to occur at 
the onset of deviation, timely control is difficult [7] . Hence, leveraging 
actual engineering monitoring data to predict the shield attitude is a 
beneficial approach for efficient and timely adjustment and control. In 
the past few decades, many studies have explored attitude prediction in 
shield construction. These methods fall into two categories: empirical 
formulas and numerical simulations. In terms of empirical formulas, 
Festa et al. [8] constructed an empirical formula based on a mechanism 
data analysis of shield construction and actual shield driving processes 
to control attitude deviation during shield construction. Liu et al. [9]
established acceleration, momentum, kinematic and orientation equa -
tions for a tunnel boring machine (TBM) and performed position pre -
diction and adjustment. The above research was based on empirical 
formulas. Although such approaches are simple and fast, it is difficult to 
accurately control a TBM in complex terrain and on steeply curved 
sections during construction [10] . In terms of numerical simulation 
methods, Sun et al. [11] used finite element simulations to analyze the 
shield correction process, examining how correction moments influence 
shield attitude and assessing the effects of different soil properties and 
burial depths on both parameters. These studies were based on a 
traditional numerical simulation method, which has the advantages of 
reflecting the physical laws governing tunnel construction and simu -
lating the experimental scenario with high accuracy. However, numer -
ical simulation methods often overlook potential coupling relationships 
between factors influencing shield attitude, while also suffering from 
excessive assumptions, high computational costs, limited factor 
consideration, and an inability to perform dynamic real-time calcu -
lations — leading to relatively low accuracy in shield attitude analysis 
and prediction [12] .
In recent years, mainstream prediction research has shifted toward 
machine learning (ML) algorithms, driven by their potent data analysis 
capabilities and capacity to thoroughly explore potential coupling re -
lationships among various influencing factors [13 – 16] . Li et al. [17]
developed a variety of intelligent algorithms to propose a prediction 
model for estimating the penetration rate of TBMs under different 
geological conditions and achieved good application results in actual 
projects. Harandizadeh et al. [18] proposed a hybrid intelligent model 
called ICA-ANFIS-PNN, which provides an accurate, effective and 
applicable technique for the performance prediction of TBMs. Parsajoo 
et al. [19] used an artificial bee colony algorithm to optimize the pa -
rameters of an adaptive neuro-fuzzy inference system (ANFIS) and 
accurately predicted the brittleness index of rock samples from a project 
in Malaysia. Bardhan et al. [20] used a novel ensemble strategy based on 
input – output amalgamation techniques to predict the performance of 
TBMs and verified its effectiveness in a project conducted in Turkey. Xu 
et al. [21] constructed a database based on a project in Malaysia and 
accurately predicted the penetration rate of TBMs through supervised 
ML techniques. These studies demonstrate the potential of ML models 
for application in real engineering projects.

To adjust attitude in shield tunneling, many scholars have adopted 
different methods of identifying key operating parameters of shield 
tunneling and have adjusted the shield attitude on the basis of the 
identified operating parameters. After shield attitude prediction is per -
formed, the effective adjustment and control of the shield attitude are 
vital for guiding engineering practice. Many researchers have reported 
that adjusting construction parameters is an effective means of con -
trolling the shield attitude and ensuring construction safety [29] . Xie 
et al. [30] adjusted the shield attitude by changing the thrust distribu -
tion in the jack working area. Wang et al. [31] introduced a multi -
cylinder shield control system that operates according to a 
leader–follower control strategy, where the system manages the shield 
attitude by manipulating the length of each motion chain. Wu et al. [32]
established a connection between the shield jack oil pressure and shield 
attitude via historical engineering data through an ML algorithm, 
allowing for the control of the shield attitude by adjusting the oil pres -
sure. Pan et al. [33] proposed an AutoDL framework for the dynamic 
prediction and multiobjective optimization of the TBM excavation atti -
tude, which increases the reliability and efficiency of tunnel excavation. 
The adjustment of the shield tunneling attitude depends on precise 
predictions. However, practical engineering cases often pose challenges 
for shield attitude control because of the uneven mass distribution, 
operational complexities, and varying frictional resistance across sec -
tions. In addition, current shield attitude control methods rely on the 
experience of the shield driver, introducing a lag effect in the manual 
adjustment process. The shield needs to operate for a certain distance or 
time before the machine’s motion path changes. To enable intelligent 
real-time shield attitude adjustment, an algorithm-physical interaction 
platform must be developed to enhance TBM excavation attitude 
monitoring and control.
In summary, the main research issues are as follows: (1) How can an 
index system for the influencing factors of the shield attitude be estab -
lished, and on this basis, how can data be collected and processed to 
obtain valid data sample sets? (2) How can the shield attitude and 
nonlinear mapping relationship between shield parameters be estab -
lished to quickly and accurately predict the shield attitude? (3) How can 
the importance of shield parameters be evaluated, the key control pa -
rameters in construction be defined, and effective shield attitude control 
be realized? This research incorporates a digital twin model for the real- 
time monitoring of the TBM excavation posture and utilizes a particle 
swarm optimization and random forest (PSO-RF) model to map the 
complex relationship between the operational parameters and the TBM 
posture. In addition, the parameter control range for the effective 
adjustment of the TBM excavation posture under local conditions is 
identified. This paper seeks to provide a new paradigm for collaborative 
decision making between operators and intelligent algorithms to pro -
vide a feasible method for adaptive shield attitude control.

2. Research significance
Existing methods have yielded significant results in predicting 
changes in the attitudes of shield machines, thereby providing guidance 
for the on-site construction of shield tunnels. However, prior research 
has focused predominantly on predicting shield attitudes, while 
neglecting the subsequent control based on these predictions. When a 
shield machine initiates excavation, changes in its attitude arise from 
complex interactions between the machine and the surrounding ground, 
which are influenced by multiple parameters, including the shield pa -
rameters and geological properties. This dynamic process is highly 
intricate, rendering precise analysis through theoretical and numerical 
methods very challenging. Although numerous ML methods have been 
employed to predict issues related to attitude changes during shield 
excavation, limited research has identified key parameters based on 
these predictions, and no study has yet established a collaborative 
decision-making mechanism that integrates intelligent algorithms with 
manual operations.
A new approach is necessary to more effectively predict and provide 
just-in-time control of the changes in the shield attitude during exca -
vation. Therefore, this paper proposes a dynamic prediction and control 
method for shield attitudes, which integrates a high-performance PSO- 
RF model and a digital twin platform. The key contributions include:
(1) Effective fusion of data from multiple sources. By analyzing 
the mechanisms of shield attitude deviation, this study identifies influ -
encing factors, constructs a comprehensive index system, and pre -
processes the data to create an effective sample dataset.
(2) Establishment of high-performance predictive models. An 
ML algorithm is employed to achieve accurate shield attitude prediction, 
with the PSO-RF algorithm validated for its effectiveness in model 
construction.
(3) Exploration of collaborative decision-making mechanisms 
between operators and intelligent models. Using the SHAP method, 
key parameters significantly impacting shield attitude are identified. 
These parameters are then dynamically adjusted and optimized within a 
digital twin framework to guide real-time attitude control.
In summary, this study integrates digital twin technology with shield 
attitude adjustment processes to design a control system. The proposed 
framework not only improves predictive accuracy but also identifies 
critical control parameters, offering a solution for intelligent shield 
attitude management.
3. Methodology
Due to the complexity of TBM operations and their working envi -
ronment, multiple coupling factors make it challenging to establish 
precise theoretical descriptions or numerical models. To address this, 
this study proposes a shield attitude prediction model using a PSO-RF 
algorithm, with attitude control achieved through iterative adjust -
ments of key construction parameters. The implementation of this 
approach is based on the Python platform; the detailed flowchart is 
given in Fig. 1 . The approach has three main steps: data acquisition and 
preprocessing, shield attitude prediction, and key construction param -
eter optimization. The specifics of each step are discussed in detail in the 
following sections: 3.1, Data collection and preprocessing; 3.2, Devel -
opment of the shield attitude prediction model; and 3.3, Shield attitude 
control on the basis of key construction parameters.
3.1. Data collection and preprocessing
The sensors embedded in the TBM provide real-time monitoring of 
operational status during excavation. However, some of the collected 
data values will be abnormal, and this will have a negative impact on the 
modeling of shield posture prediction. Consequently, data preprocessing 
is conducted to extract valuable information and transform it into a 
format suitable for model development. This preprocessing involves 
outlier cleaning, filling, normalization, and Pearson correlation analysis.

3.1.1. Outlier cleaning
Because the sensors and data acquisition instruments used in 
tunneling systems are always operating, the data collected include not 
only the data acquired under normal shield working conditions but also 
those acquired during shutdowns. The data from the shutdown state 
have no reference value regarding the operating state in the tunneling 
system, and the data from the shutdown and normal operation states are 
generally mixed together, which greatly affects model construction and 
accuracy. To address this, an abnormal data filter is introduced to 
improve analysis precision. Abnormal data can be categorized into 
nonworking shutdown data and abnormal data. These two types of data 
need to be identified and treated in different ways.
The data acquired in a shutdown state are collected mainly when the 
cutterhead is replaced or when the shield is stopped or maintained. The 
nonworking state of a TBM can be identified according to the relevant 
construction parameters. In this work, four major construction param -
eters are selected as indices: the gross thrust, cutterhead torque, advance 
speed and cutterhead speed. When one of the four construction pa -
rameters is equal to zero, the shield can be considered to be in a 
nonworking state. The corresponding discriminant function is as follows

3.2.2. Particle swarm optimization algorithm
The PSO algorithm is utilized to determine the optimal hyper -
parameters for the ML algorithm. While alternative parameter optimi -

ability to avoid local optima.

3.3.2. A digital twin system for driving shield attitude control
The sensors installed on the TBM monitor and record real-time 
excavation data, which is processed by the onboard acquisition sys -
tem. These records — containing shield parameters and excavation sta -
tus — form the foundation for a three-dimensional digital twin platform. 
Physical equipment can be converted into digital models, which should 
reflect the actual characteristics of the modeled TBM. By combining the 
digital twin model with real-time monitoring data from the TBM and 
integrating intelligent modules, reliable estimates of the TBM excava -
tion posture can be obtained.
By leveraging engineering monitoring data, the shield attitude can be 
accurately predicted through a mapping relationship between the target 
attitude and influencing factors. This prediction capability enables 
shield attitude control. Through SHAP analysis, considering the pro -
ject ’ s actual conditions and control requirements, key construction pa -
rameters during shield tunneling are selected for optimization and 
adjustment. This iterative process continues until the shield attitude 
conforms to the defined control specifications, ensuring the safety of 
tunneling operations. The twin shield attitude control system is subse -
quently created from the shield attitude control method and digital twin 
technology. Serving as a valuable addition to the existing shield oper -
ating system, the digital twin model significantly increases the intelli -
gence and automation of shield attitude control. This approach enables 
shield attitude control on the basis of dual physical and data inputs, 
providing a comprehensive solution.

4.1. Background
Guiyang Metro Line 3 construction commenced in late 2018, with an 
estimated total investment of 32.231 billion yuan. This line is an 
important transportation route for promoting the balanced and coordi -
nated development of the northern and southern regions of the city and 
for achieving green, low-carbon transportation and environmental 
protection in the western urban agglomeration. On this line, the tunnel 
from the Sifanghe Road Station to Zaojiaojing Station (SZ) spans 
YDK24 + 511.010 to YDK25 + 628.283, covering a total distance of 
1117.209 m. It features a maximum longitudinal slope of 25.144 ‰ and 
burial depths ranging from 12.69 to 21.72 m. Shield construction in this 
region employs an earth pressure balancing (EPB) TBM with a diameter 
of 6470 mm. The EPB-TBM is equipped with numerous sensors that 
continuously monitor its operating status. All operating parameter data 
are collected and recorded by the data acquisition system. The tunnel in 
this area mainly passes through medium-weathered dolomite that is 
partially insulated and partially hydrated, leading to complex hydro -
geology. The dolomite in this region has a compressive strength of 
45 – 78 MPa, a soil cohesion of approximately 200 kPa, and an internal 
friction of approximately 30
◦
. Shield tunneling in such locally hydrated 
and complex formations complicates shield control. The shield 
tunneling process in rail transit construction is highly complex and re -
quires precise control of the shield attitude within the challenging 
environment of the SZ construction zone. The project plan map, the 
composite EPB-TBM used in the project, the construction situation in -
side the tunnel and the interface of the shield attitude monitoring system 
are shown in Fig. 4 (a)~(d), respectively.

The sensors equipped in the EPB-TBM can monitor real-time exca -
vation conditions, with collected data processed by the onboard system 
and exported as CSV files organized by ring number. These files contain 
critical parameters including the excavation date and time, ring number, 
construction parameters, navigation parameters, etc. Based on literature 
review, key input parameters were selected to optimize model training 
efficiency, avoiding redundancy. The bottom of the cylinder of the 
shield thrust system rests on the lining segment of the upper ring, the 
jacking force generated by the segment is used for the segment, and the

geological parameters and tunnel burial depth. Geological parameters 
reflect soil pressure in the shield environment. During shield attitude 
adjustment, the TBM interacts with surrounding soil mainly via shear 
and compression forces [53] . As the tunnel burial depth increases, the 
thrust of the TBM needs to increase to overcome the soil pressure, which 
poses certain challenges for the stability of the shield attitude [54] .
In this paper, an input index system for shield attitude is introduced 
based on the previous mechanism analysis. The identified indices 
include jack stroke (JS), jack thrust (JT), shield tail clearance (STC), 
chamber earth pressure (CEP), rotation speed of the screw conveyor 
(RSSC), cutterhead torque (CT), cutterhead speed (CS), advance speed 
(AS), grouting pressure (GP), gross thrust (GT), shield muck (SM), foam 
agent (FA), internal friction angle (IFA), soil cohesion (SC), and tunnel 
burial depth (TBD). Among these, JS, JT, CEP, RSSC, CT, CS, AS, GP, and 
GT are the TBM ’ s active control parameters that can be directly adjusted 
by operators. Conversely, STC, SM, and FA are passive control 
parameters beyond the operators ’ direct control. HDSI, VDSI, HDST, 
VDST, HAA, and PAA serve as descriptors of the shield ’ s position state 
and are the most critical control indicators for shield operation, as 
shown in Fig. 5 . Consequently, these variables are selected as the output 
variables for the prediction model. A comprehensive dataset for rings 
201 – 400 was generated during TBM operation and collected, 
comprising data for a total of 200 rings.

4.4. Shield attitude prediction via the PSO-RF
4.4.1. Hyperparameter setting
Optimizing the hyperparameters of the ML model is crucial for 
improving the accuracy of predictions before applying ML algorithms to 
predict shield attitudes [55] . Improperly formulated hyperparameter 
values may increase the number of calculations, prolong the training 
time and overfit the model. Therefore, it is necessary to set the hyper -
parameters of the ML model reasonably. When the RF algorithm is 
employed to train samples, four hyperparameters in the algorithm 
should be optimized: (1) the number of decision trees (n_estimators), 
which determines the prediction accuracy and computational 
complexity of the RF model; (2) the maximum depth of the decision tree 
(max_depth), which is related to the complexity of the RF model struc -
ture and is a key hyperparameter to prevent overfitting of the RF model; 
(3) the minimum number of samples required for splitting internal nodes 
(min_samples_split), which prevents the model from being too sensitive 
to noisy data; and (4) the minimum number of leaf nodes (min_sam -
ples_leaf), which controls the purity of leaf nodes and can improve the 
generalization of the RF model.
To maximize the RF prediction model ’ s accuracy, the PSO fitness is 
set as the training model ’ s MSE loss function. The particle population 
number is 20, the maximum number of iterations is 100, the learning 
factors are both 0.5, and the inertia weight is 1. When the fitness of the 
particles becomes stable or the number of iterations reaches the 
maximum, the iterative cycle is terminated, and the optimal hyper -
parameters are obtained.
Before the parameters are optimized, it is crucial to establish the 
initial optimization range for the hyperparameters. The setting range of 
the model hyperparameters depends on the specific research objective. 
On the basis of the parameter ranges provided in previous studies [29, 
56,57] , combined with specific case backgrounds, the parameter search 
ranges for the relevant hyperparameters in this study are determined. 
The initial ranges of n_estimators, max_depth, min_samples_split and 
min_samples_leaf are [50,150], [20,500], [2,5] and [1,5] , respectively.

4.5. Optimization and improvement of the shield attitude
4.5.1. SHAP analysis of the predictive models
The influence of input parameters like jack stroke on shield attitude 
is complex and interrelated, making single-parameter qualitative anal -
ysis inadequate for capturing real-world construction dynamics or 
identifying key factors. To address this, the SHAP algorithm can be 
employed. By considering the shield attitude as the prediction index, the 
SHAP algorithm facilitates the evaluation of primary and secondary 
factors of the shield parameters. This method enables the selection of 
pertinent shield construction parameters for attitude optimization, 
providing a robust theoretical foundation for rail transit.
While importance rankings provide insights into the significance of 
input parameters, they do not inherently reveal the specific direction of 
influence of each parameter on the model ’ s prediction results. Analyzing 
importance rankings alone may not fully clarify the decision-making 
mode of the model. This limitation becomes apparent for shield drivers 
seeking a deeper understanding of the model ’ s operational rules, 
creating a gap in trust between humans and the machine model.
SHAP provides another visual interpretation scheme. Features are 
ordered vertically by importance, with each sample plotted horizontally 
according to its Shapley value. Values left of the centerline (negative) 
reduce model output, while those right of the centerline (positive) 
enhance it. Color intensity reflects feature magnitude, with red indi -
cating higher values and blue lower ones, revealing how each parameter 
influences predictions. Fig. 11 shows the global summary of the SHAP 
values and the feature importances of the average SHAP values of the 15 
most critical parameters.
According to the global summary diagram of the SHAP values drawn 
via calculation, JS, JT, STC, CEP, CT, CS and GT exert the most signif -
icant influences on the shield attitude prediction results. Therefore, to 
control the shield attitude, special attention should be given to these 
parameters.
The results show that (1) the impact of JS on the shield attitude 
becomes more pronounced with increasing JS. JS-L and JS-R affect 
mainly the HDSI, HDST and HAA, and JS-L and JS-R have opposite in -
fluences. JS-U and JS-D affect mainly the VDSI, VDST and PAA, and JS-U 
and JS-D have opposite influences. In addition, the impacts of JT and 
STC on the shield attitude are similar to that of JS. An excessive dif -
ference in JS and JT will lead to uneven segment damage, which will 
seriously interfere with the normal shield attitude during construction. 
An STC value that is too low during construction can lead to interference 
between the shield tail and the segment, hindering shield advancement 
and potentially causing segment dislocation damage. Conversely, 
excessive STC increases the grouting between the tunnel surface and 
soil, significantly affecting the load on the surrounding soil. Both sce -
narios significantly impact the shield attitude. These findings align with 
prior studies. Zhou et al. proved that JT is positively correlated with the 
shield tail deviation [58] . Wang et al. identified JS and CEP as key 
influencing factors in shield attitude deviation [59] . Wada et al. [60]
reported that STC significantly impacts the shield attitude.
(2) CEP, CT, CS and GT are the four most important parameters. The 
same parameter has different effects on different shield attitude targets. 
The side on which the SHAP value of the GT is less than 0 is represented 
mainly by red and purple points, indicating that the influence of the GT 
on the HDSI is reversed; that is, with increasing GT, the smaller the 
corresponding SHAP value is, the greater the influence on the shield 
attitude. The influence of CEP on the HDSI is complex; the red and blue 
points are on the side with SHAP values less than 0, and the purple 
points are on the side with SHAP values less than 0. The HDSI first in -
creases with increasing CEP; when the HDSI increases within a certain 
range, it begins to decrease with further increases in CEP. The sample 
point concentration areas of the other shield parameters are closer to 
0 than those of CEP, CT, CS and GT are, indicating that these parameters 
have a certain impact on the shield attitude, but the impact is small. 
During construction, a CEP that is too small will cause the tunnel face to 
collapse and subsequently cause surface settlement, whereas a CEP that 
is too large will cause ground uplift. Changes in CT and CS cause 
improper shield movement, resulting in attitude deviation. Adjusting 
the GT can affect the AS and prevent the attitude instability caused by 
propulsion being too fast or too slow.

4.5.2. Shield attitude optimization control
Based on SHAP and influence rule analyses of input parameters, key
