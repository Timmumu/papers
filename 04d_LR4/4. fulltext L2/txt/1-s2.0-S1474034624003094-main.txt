ABSTRACT 
Large-scale hydro-steel structures (LS-HSS) are pivotal in hydraulic engineering, boasting high lift, expansive 
apertures, and discharge capacities. Conventional methods of operation and maintenance for LS-HSS require a 
digital revolution to ensure safety, reliability, and durability. This study introduces a novel approach, harnessing 
the power of a digital twin (DT) to integrate physical and virtual elements in LS-HSS operation and maintenance 
seamlessly. A digital twin modeling framework of LS-HSS operation and maintenance is first presented, incor -
porating five levels of a physical entity, a virtual entity, DT data, services, and connections. Subsequently, a DT- 
driven intelligent operation and maintenance (DT-IOM) platform is constructed and applied, which features real- 
time mapping, closed-loop control, dynamic health assessment, and intelligent decision-making. This platform 
provides a pathway for digitally transforming conventional LS-HSS operation and maintenance and paves the 
way for a new era of intelligent infrastructure management. The proposed approach has been successfully 
implemented and validated with multiple engineering projects, including radial gates of reservoir spillways, 
plain gates of flood discharge orifices, and emergency gates of pumped storage power stations. The impressive 
results demonstrate a significant anomaly response and processing efficiency improvement of over 60%. 
Moreover, the platform has substantially reduced field inspection time and equipment failure rate by approxi -
mately 50% and 20%, respectively. These tangible benefits further highlight the platform ’ s functional advan -
tages, including its capability for IoT connection, modeling and integration, virtual-real interaction, 2D/3D 
visualization, and service expansion. The results demonstrate the unique features in the closed loop of percep -
tion, analysis, decision-making, and optimization for DT-IOM, especially for integrating data, models, knowl -
edge, and services in LS-HSS intelligent operation and maintenance. 
1. Introduction 
With the rapid advancement of emerging information technologies, 
such as the Internet of Things (IoT) [1] , Big Data [2] , and Artificial In -
telligence (AI) [3] , there is currently active exploration in the research 
field to finely describe, accurately diagnose, and intelligently make 
decisions for operational processes of objects in the physical world by 
continuously enhancing digital mapping capabilities [4] . During the 
global transition to industrial intelligence, the digital twin (DT) is a 
unified entity encompassing both the physical object and its digital 
representation, enabling seamless interconnectivity, fostering collabo -
ration, and co-evolving. It possesses characteristics such as the inte -
gration of virtual and real, real-time interaction, iterative optimization, 
and data-driven approach [5] , thus attracting extensive attention. 
The integration and widespread adoption of digital technology in 
conventional sectors have led to the progressive utilization of digital 
twin technology for engineering structures [6,7] . This has particularly 
resulted in significant implications for aging, antiquated, and hazardous 
structures. Therefore, in the context of extending the service life of hy -
draulic engineering projects and the increasingly prominent demands 
for safety monitoring and health management of engineering facilities 
and equipment [8] , the integration of digital twin technology into the 
conventional operation and maintenance process is gradually being 
implemented to ensure comprehensive safety throughout the entire 
service life. However, relevant research in hydraulic engineering pri -
marily focuses on areas such as engineering construction management 
[9 – 11] , dam safety monitoring [8,12,13] , hydroelectric generating units 
[14,15] , and water resource management [16,17] . Insufficient attention

2. Literature review 
2.1. Digital twin theory and implementation approach 
During a presentation on product lifecycle management (PLM) in 
2003, Grieves [30] initially introduced the notion of the digital twin. 
With the continuous expansion of theoretical and technological ad -
vancements in physical information perception, virtual model valida -
tion, and data algorithm integration, as well as the increasing demand 
for application requirements [31], the development and application of 
digital twin technology have gradually extended from its initial focus on 
military and aerospace fields to a more comprehensive range of civilian 
sectors. Moreover, it has deeply integrated with IoT, Big Data, and AI 
[31]. However, one of the primary challenges in implementing digital 
twin technology within engineering is attaining a seamless and 
compatible connection between the physical and virtual domains [32]. 
Therefore, scholars worldwide have conducted extensive studies on 
digital twin theory and practical aspects to align with the emerging 
patterns and evolving requirements of digital twin research and 
application. 
In the domain of modeling methods for digital twin, the primary 
emphasis lies in general models such as the C2PS reference model for 
digital twin architecture [33], object-oriented reference models [34], 
six-layer architecture model for digital twins [35,36], and universal

In the management of digital twin data, the primary areas of interest in 
ongoing research encompass techniques for data acquisition, methodolo -
gies for data interchange, facilitation of data collaboration, and enhance -
ment of data presentation. A method for constructing data was introduced 
by Kong et al. [48] , which encompasses modules for representing, orga -
nizing, and managing the data. Wang et al. [49] put forward a data 
acquisition method based on a Programmable Logic Controller (PLC) to 
enable real-time monitoring of traditional machinery. Tong et al. [50] 
introduced a data exchange method based on the MTConnect protocol.

and modeling twin data, utilizing AutomationML. Related research also 
includes blockchain-based digital twin data security sharing and man -
agement methods [27,28] and augmented reality (AR) technology-based 
twin data visualization methods [29] . Additionally, domestic and inter -
national researchers have explored various approaches to interactive 
digital twin. These encompass a framework grounded in virtual reality 
(VR), AR, and mixed reality (MR) for establishing connections with digital 
twin [51] , a blockchain-based methodology for facilitating data interac -
tion [52] , an implementation of interactive digital twin utilizing model- 
driven software engineering [53] , a natural language approach to 
engaging with digital twin [54] , techniques for human – machine interac -
tion in the context of digital twin [55] , an integrated multi-model dynamic 
process modeling mechanism for enhancing interaction capabilities [56] , 
as well as the integration between physical production systems and their 
corresponding digital twin [57] . Moreover, the application of digital twin- 
based services, including monitoring [49] , assessment [58] , simulation 
[59] , forecasting [60] , optimization [61] , decision-making [62] , and 
control [63] , has been extensively researched by both domestic and in -
ternational scholars. Furthermore, it finds applications in various fields 
such as aerospace engineering [64] , intelligent manufacturing [61] , smart 
cities development [65] , transportation systems [58] , the energy sector 
[62] , and the equipment industry [66] .

2.2. Digital twin applications for engineering structures 
In recent years, the integration and widespread adoption of digital 
technology in conventional sectors have led to the progressive utiliza -
tion of digital twin technology for engineering structures. This has 
particularly resulted in significant implications for aging, antiquated, 
and hazardous structures. Long et al. [10] performed an extensive ex -
amination of existing literature to understand how the utilization of 
digital twin can enhance operational intelligence in the building con -
struction sector. The findings indicated that existing research primarily 
concentrated on applying digital twin in the construction and operation/ 
maintenance stages while neglecting its potential application in the 
planning/design and demolition/recovery stages. Kang et al. [6] intro -
duced a digital twin-based approach for monitoring the health of 
bridges, which utilizes multimedia knowledge to synchronize virtual 
and real spaces. By collecting data from sensors installed in actual sys -
tems, this method can accurately reflect the current state of the bridge. 
As a result, it can reduce maintenance costs and prolong the lifespan of 
bridges. Shim et al. [7] proposed adopting a digital twin model concept 
to enhance decision-making reliability and optimize bridge maintenance 
processes in their innovative approach toward the next generation of 
bridge maintenance systems. Additionally, the digital twin is employed 
for risk monitoring and process optimization control in construction and 
transportation engineering domains. Ku ˇsi ´c et al. [67] focused on simu -
lating microscopic motorway scenarios while integrating real-time data 
seamlessly into system functioning. They introduced a new approach to 
modeling motorway traffic and demonstrated it using the Geneva mo -
torway ’ s digital twin model (DT-GM), which remains continuously 
synchronized. Jiao et al. [11] proposed a novel and effective framework 
for forecasting worker movements in construction sites, leveraging 
cloud-edge cooperation to anticipate the trajectories of workers. This 
approach ensures data privacy and minimizes computational load while 
achieving accurate results. 
Meanwhile, the issues of safety monitoring and health management 
for engineering facilities and equipment are becoming increasingly 
prominent as the service life of hydraulic engineering projects extends 
[8] . Therefore, digital twin technology is progressively integrated into 
operational and maintenance procedures, guaranteeing all- 
encompassing safety across the lifespan. Zhang et al. [8] proposed a 
novel online learning algorithm called AF-OS-ELM-MVE to address dam 
deformation monitoring challenges. Shi et al. [12] put forward the uti -
lization of the digital twin approach for managing hydraulic engineering 
operations to attain intelligent operational procedures, accurate regu -
lation, and reliable maintenance in hydraulic engineering. Li et al. [68] 
introduced a digital twin-based intelligent monitoring system architec -
ture to expand the scope of digital twin applications and enhance the 
overall lifespan safety management of dam operations. Wang et al. [14] 
proposed a maintenance model based on digital twin for electrome -
chanical devices to predict health status. Liu et al. [16] described an 
innovative framework for digital twins, which can be a robust tool for 
assessing the present conditions of canals and forecasting future trends. 
This framework significantly improves security and efficiency in 
extensive water diversion projects. With the advancement of informa -
tion technology, there has been continuous improvement in the 
digitization, networking, and intelligence levels within hydraulic engi -
neering. The utilization and exploration of digital twin technology are 
experiencing significant growth. Earlier studies have explored the 
application and effects of the digital twin in hydraulic engineering, 
primarily focusing on areas such as dam safety monitoring, electrome -
chanical devices, and water resource management. Insufficient attention 
has been given to the digital transformation pathway of conventional 
operation and maintenance for LS-HSS and the integration methods of 
data, models, knowledge, and services driven by digital twin technol -
ogy. In addition, the actual operating conditions and demands for 
different engineering structures in situ are complex, discrepant, and 
changing. Therefore, a comprehensive DT-driven LS-HSS intelligent 
operation and maintenance (DT-IOM) platform is essential. 
In summary, the current methodologies do not encompass a 
comprehensive framework of digital twin modeling for LS-HSS opera -
tion and maintenance. Moreover, exploring integrating methods of data, 
models, knowledge, and services driven by digital twin technology is 
essential. Subsequently, establishing a comprehensive DT-driven intel -
ligent operation and maintenance (DT-IOM) platform becomes crucial in 
promoting the digital transformation of conventional LS-HSS opera -
tions. We believe that this study will provide valuable references in the 
future. 
3. Framework of digital twin-driven LS-HSS operation and 
maintenance 
The digital twin represents a significant technological advancement 
enabling seamless interaction and integration between physical and 
virtual environments, thereby driving widespread adoption across 
diverse domains. In Grieves ’ s research [30] , the initial concept of digital 
twin encompassed physical entities, virtual entities, and their inter -
connectedness. However, the emergence of digital twin models has led 
to new trends and demands because of application needs and advance -
ments in theoretical technologies. In this study, the intelligent operation 
and maintenance framework for LS-HSS is designed with a five- 
dimensional digital twin model [32] , including physical entity, virtual 
entity, DT data, services, and connections (see Fig. 2 ). The digital twin 
modeling framework for LS-HSS operation and maintenance focuses on 
integrating data, models, knowledge, and services. 
Then on this basis, to provide a pathway for digitally transforming 
conventional LS-HSS operation and maintenance, a DT-driven LS-HSS 
intelligent operation and maintenance (DT-IOM) platform features real- 
time mapping, closed-loop control, dynamic health assessment, and 
intelligent decision-making is constructed and applied. The platform 
architecture comprises four layers: physical entity layer, digital twin 
layer, service layer, and connection layer, as shown in Fig. 3 . The 
perception and processing of data offer crucial information for real-time 
bidirectional mapping and interoperability. The connection in real-time 
between the digital twin layer and service layer offers data assistance for 
immediate control directives. Ultimately, the intelligent perception and 
optimal control of the LS-HSS can establish a closed-loop proactive 
safety assurance and predictive maintenance mode with the stages of 
perception, analysis, decision-making, and optimization. 
3.1. Physical entity layer 
The physical entity layer comprises essential elements of LS-HSS and 
subsidiary facilities: major structures (panel, gate leaf, girder, radial 
arm, etc.), hoists (hydraulic station, hydraulic cylinder, control system, 
etc.), and hydraulic structures (lock chamber, sluice pier, wing wall, 
etc.). These components enable the LS-HSS to undertake vital functions 
such as flood management, water supply for diverse purposes, irrigation 
facilitation, power generation, and navigation in hydraulic engineering.

3.2. Digital twin layer 
The data platform, model platform, and knowledge platform 
constitute the digital twin layer. The data platform has functions, 
including data aggregation, management, mining, and services. It in -
corporates functional components such as PLCs, industrial computers, 
and sensors for acquiring data and facilitating communication. These 
components enable the platform to achieve data acquisition, interaction, 
and real-time monitoring between the tangible and digital domains. It 
integrates the operation and maintenance data and the digital twin data 
to establish a cohesive foundation of unified information. The operation 
and maintenance data mainly include design, monitoring, and mainte -
nance data. The data of the digital twin encompass information on 
mechanism analysis, mathematical statistics, simulation analysis, iter -
ative optimization, and decision-making. Once cleaned, these diverse 
data sets are promptly stored in the database to supply the application 
layer with real-time data support. 
Simultaneously, the model platform is a digital reconstruction of LS- 
HSS, encompassing a virtual depiction of the major structures, hoists, 
hydraulic structures, etc. Based on a three-dimensional geometric model 
and employing intelligent sensor recognition, mechanical analysis, and 
simulation fusion, a digital twin model for LS-HSS is ultimately con -
structed. Hence, the digital twin model possesses identical geometric 
measurements, positional data, and behavioral information as tangible 
equipment to ensure efficacy and accuracy. Its objective is to establish a 
more realistic connection between operational and maintenance tasks in 
both physical and virtual domains. Meanwhile, a knowledge platform, 
including standard specifications, expert knowledge, contingency plans, 
maintenance experiences, rules, etc., is established to support intelligent 
decision-making. The data platform, model platform, and knowledge 
platform collectively constitute the geometries, physical properties, 
behaviors, and rules of the digital twin model. Additionally, the digital 
twin layer facilitates the synchronization of data and model platforms 
through integration and interconnection. This enables seamless 
integration, promotes analysis, provides virtual mapping in real-time, 
provides statistical insights, and enhances efficiency in digital opera -
tions and maintenance activities. 
3.3. Connection layer 
The connection layer (CN) enables seamless communication between 
different platform components, including the connection from the 
physical entity layer to the digital twin layer and the service layer. In this 
procedure, real-time collection of physical entity data is facilitated by 
utilizing various sensors, embedded systems, data acquisition cards, etc., 
with data as a medium. The acquired information is subsequently 
transmitted to the data platform via protocols like MTCconnect, OPC- 
UA, and MQTT [69] . Following processing on the data platform, the 
processed instructions or information can be relayed to the physical 
entity for operational optimization through protocols such as OPC-UA, 
MQTT, and CoAP [69] . At the same time, real-time storage of 
simulation-related and service data is facilitated in the data platform 
through database interfaces like JDBC and ODBC [69] . Simultaneously, 
dynamic utilization of fusion data, associated data, historical data, rule 
data, and commonly used algorithms and models enables real-time 
simulation. Furthermore, it provides support for service operation and 
optimization.

4. Key enabling technologies 
Successful implementation of a DT-IOM platform for LS-HSS neces -
sitates utilizing DT-driven data, models, knowledge, and services inte -
gration methods. The primary focus is overcoming technical obstacles 
related to data acquisition and analysis, real-time mapping, online

4.1. Data service system of LS-HSS intelligent operation and maintenance 
platform 
Data plays a crucial role in facilitating and serving as a vital element 
within the DT-IOM platform for LS-HSS. The virtual models can 
comprehend the condition, forecast, approximate, and scrutinize dy -
namic alterations of physical entities by utilizing sensory data obtained

4.2. Real-time mapping and online monitoring system of LS-HSS 
intelligent operation and maintenance platform 
Fig. 7 illustrates the virtual-real interaction mechanism. The physical 
entity performs the data acquisition process in real-time and then 
transfers the acquired data to the data service system for processing and 
storage. The integration of virtual and physical is achieved through 
continuous access to the data service system, enabling the digital twin 
platform to acquire real-time data. The digital twin platform encom -
passes services including real-time data-driven operational status 
monitoring, online assessment, rehearsal of LS-HSS operating condi -
tions, and intelligent maintenance decision-making. 
The services are achieved through a combination of methods, 
including analysis of determinants impacting health status, multi-sensor 
information fusion data acquisition, real-time safety assessment, and 
knowledge-driven process control optimization. This serves the dynamic 
evaluation and accurate prediction of safety elements during LS-HSS 
operation. The framework for real-time data-driven operating status 
monitoring is as follows in Fig. 8 and is comprised of the subsequent 
procedures: 
(1) Analysis of determinants impacting health status. 
The design and processing quality influence the strength, stiffness, 
and stability of LS-HSS. Meanwhile, these mechanical properties can be 
impacted by environmental conditions, hydraulic conditions, equipment 
operation, and maintenance, leading to performance degradation and 
potential safety hazards. The conventional LS-HSS operation and 
maintenance often takes insufficient consideration of the relationship 
between various indicators, resulting in a single-dimensional evaluation 
(such as structural stress – strain or vibration) of equipment operation 
safety. As a result, the accuracy and efficiency of identifying safety risks 
and health conditions during equipment operation are affected. 
Therefore, in this paper, the health evaluation indexes of LS-HSS are 
established through a comprehensive analysis of safety risk factors in the 
design, manufacturing, operation, and maintenance processes. These 
primarily include Mechanical Performance Indicators (MPI), such as 
strength, stiffness, and stability; Working Performance Indicators (WPI), 
which encompass vibration, tilt angle, control parameters, hydrody -
namics, and environmental parameters; Operational Maintenance In -
dicators (OMI), which cover regulations compliance, operational 
proficiency, and the maintenance and repair status of LS-HSS.

above in both structural mechanics and data science dimensions

4.3. Dynamic health assessment system of LS-HSS intelligent operation 
and maintenance platform 
Considering the effects of changing circumstances over time and 
maintenance procedures on the structure and operational efficiency of 
the LS-HSS, it is essential to conduct health assessments throughout the 
lifecycle to ensure its reliability and safe operation. Fig. 9 illustrates the 
process of dynamic health assessment of the DT-IOM platform. As in the 
case of a radial gate, the process begins in the virtual space by con -
structing a finite element model that includes geometric and physical 
data based on the original design drawings. During this construction, 
various factors, such as external driving forces, environmental distur -
bances, operational mechanisms, constraint relationships, and behavior 
and rules, influence the LS-HSS during its service life. Therefore, 
response surface methodology [72] is incorporated into the finite 
element model to facilitate model updating, ensuring its accuracy 
following the operational process. The flow chart depicting the response 
surface methodology for the dynamic health assessment system can be 
observed in Fig. 10 . 
(1) The main parameters that influence the response of LS-HSS, 
including material parameters (elastic modulus, density, Poisson ’ s 
ratio) and geometric parameters (length, thickness, etc.), should be 
initially identified. The stress and vibration data obtained from real-time 
monitoring technology in Sections 4.1 and 4.2 should be the target 
function for modifying the finite element model. The optimization 
objective is to minimize the disparity between the calculated values of 
the modified finite element model response and experimental 
measurements. 
(2) The Central Composite Design (CCD) method is utilized for the 
experimental design of parameter samples, generating sample points. 
The F-test is employed to analyze the parameters ’ significance, and 
higher-significance parameters are selected for response surface fitting.

(3) The input to each neural network is either the opening or current
