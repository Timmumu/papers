ABSTRACT
Traditional experience-based methods for controlling the attitude of pipe-jacking machines frequently result in 
notable deviations from the planned trajectory, posing risks such as excessive jacking forces and structural 
damage to segments. Achieving precise control of machine attitudes is vital for large-section pipe-jacking pro -
jects. This paper presents a mechanics-informed digital twin framework designed to reduce trajectory deviations 
by leveraging intelligent control systems. A dynamic control platform processes diverse real-time data from 
construction operations, issues immediate risk alerts and suggest corrective forces derived from a theoretical load 
model. As demonstrated in a super-large rectangular pipe-jacking project in China, this approach effectively 
predicted attitude-related moments and provided dependable guidance for trajectory corrections, enhancing 
construction safety and efficiency. Building on this progress, combining mechanics-informed digital twin 
approach with machine learning techniques for attitude prediction and control is expected to further enhance the 
intelligence and adaptability of rectangular pipe-jacking operations.
1. Introduction
Rectangular underground structures offer over 20 % higher spatial 
utilization compared to circular ones, helping to reduce overall con -
struction costs and providing significant green and low-carbon benefits 
[ 1 , 2 ]. As a result, rectangular pipe-jacking technology has rapidly 
developed and is increasingly applied in various urban underground 
projects [ 3 , 4 ]. To meet growing engineering demands, the size of rect -
angular pipe-jacking machines has continued to increase, placing higher 
requirements on control accuracy and construction reliability [ 5 , 6 ]. 
During pipe-jacking operations, the machine is expected to advance 
precisely along the Designed Tunnel Axis (DTA). However, due to un -
even mass distribution, complex geological conditions, and varying 
control parameters, the machine often deviates from the DTA, failing to 
meet required construction tolerances [ 7 – 9 ]. Although operators may 
frequently perform attitude adjustments, current experience-based 
control methods rely heavily on manual operations and are prone to 
delayed responses, making it difficult to effectively reduce tunnel de -
viation [ 9 – 11 ].
Tunnel deviation leads to increased jacking resistance, which may 
exceed the machine ’ s designed capacity and cause significant con -
struction risks [ 12 ]. Excessive jacking force on the pipe segments can 
also result in cracking, structural damage, and joint leakage, posing 
serious safety threats [ 13 ]. These challenges are particularly prominent 
in large-section rectangular pipe-jacking, where multi-cutter designs 
and heterogeneous soils significantly complicate attitude control 
[ 14 – 16 ]. Furthermore, during construction near existing structures, 
deviations in the machine ’ s attitude and position may lead to collision 
risks. For instance, in twin or multi-tunnel projects, excessive tunnel 
deviation can widen the gaps between adjacent tunnels, making it 
difficult to form an integrated structure [ 17 ]. Therefore, it is imperative 
to introduce informative and digital technology to achieve precise and 
real-time attitude control of rectangular pipe-jacking machines, thereby 
ensuring construction accuracy, reducing trajectory deviation, and

The attitude control of pipe-jacking and shield machines has 
attracted significant research attention in recent years. To enhance the 
precision control of the machine’s attitude, the force analyses of 
machine-soil interactions in shield tunnelling have been studied, and the 
accuracy of proposed theoretical models has been validated through 
engineering cases [ 18 , 19 ]. While theoretical models have enhanced our 
understanding of machine–soil interactions, they lack real-time appli -
cability. Additionally, there are substantial differences in the forces 
acting on rectangular pipe-jacking machines compared to circular shield 
machines. For example, the multi-cutter design leads to more uneven 
forces between the front and rear, making the machine prone to nose- 
diving during construction. The uneven distribution of soil chamber 
pressure further complicates attitude adjustments for rectangular pipe- 
jacking machines [ 5 , 20 ]. On the other hand, to improve the real-time 
attitude control for shield machines, machine learning algorithms 
have been used to predict the attitude and motion trajectory during 
shield tunnelling [ 21 ]. Shen et al. [ 22 ] proposed a framework for shield 
attitude prediction using a random forest deep learning method, inves -
tigating the correlation between input parameter combinations and 
prediction accuracy. Zhou et al. [ 11 ] developed a deep learning model to 
achieve real-time prediction of shield machine attitude and position 
under complex environments. Although these studies have improved the 
real-time attitude prediction for rectangular pipe-jacking construction, 
aiding operators in making corrective decisions, such decisions remain 
largely experiential and retain significant uncertainty. Therefore, 
establishing a correction decision platform based on theoretical force 
analysis models of the machine, combined with real-time position, 
attitude, and geological conditions, can provide operators with real-time 
decision support for corrections and is expected to significantly improve 
the accuracy of rectangular pipe-jacking machine control [ 23 ].
Digital Twin (DT) technology, through the real-time mapping of 
physical entities and virtual models [ 24 ], contributes to intelligent 
construction and cost-effective management in civil engineering. This 
technology has been promoted and applied in civil engineering [ 25–27 ], 
providing a new approach for establishing a real-time correction deci -
sion platform for rectangular pipe-jacking machines. Wu et al. [ 28 ] 
proposed a digital twin framework based on Bayesian optimization 
categorical boosting and a multi-objective evolutionary algorithm based 
on decomposition, which effectively helps to control the attitude of 
large-diameter slurry shields. Ye et al. [ 29 ] developed a digital twin- 
based platform for smart early warning and risk management, which 
has been successfully applied in engineering practice. However, the 
application of digital twins in the field of underground engineering is 
still in its infancy [ 30 , 31 ], and it has not yet been applied to real-time 
prediction and auxiliary correction decision-making for the attitude of 
rectangular pipe-jacking machines.
The application of DT technology in pipe-jacking construction faces 
challenges similar to those in shield tunnelling: insufficient real-time 
data acquisition and processing, issues with the immediacy and stabil -
ity of data transmission, complex interactions among geotechnical, 
mechanical, and structural components, and difficulties in achieving 
efficient collaborative decision-making between human operators and 
intelligent platforms [ 32 ]. Zhang et al. [ 33 ] proposed a digital twin- 
based intelligent monitoring and control method for shield tunnelling 
that utilizes deep learning algorithms to optimize tunnelling parameters; 
however, their approach does not effectively resolve the issue of real- 
time attitude correction for shield machines. Moreover, data-driven al -
gorithms like deep learning demand high-quality, high-volume data and 
often fail to capture the underlying physical mechanisms, making their 
outputs difficult to interpret or validate and thus less suitable for real- 
time guidance in shield tunnelling [ 34 , 35 ]. Additionally, the results 
from these models are typically based on data from specific conditions, 
which limits their generalisability to other engineering projects [ 34 ]. 
Consequently, researchers have increasingly turned to physics-based 
models to guide tunnel boring machine operations, a strategy that 
may better address the challenge of real-time attitude correction for 
pipe-jacking machines under complex geological conditions [ 36–38 ].
This paper explores applying mechanics-informed DT technology to 
enable the intelligent attitude control of large rectangular pipe-jacking 
machine. The proposed DT framework significantly enhances the effi -
ciency and accuracy of attitude control by integrating real-time moni -
toring, risk warning, and recommended correction thrust for the 
articulated cylinders. The key innovations of this paper are as follows: 
(1) a mechanics-informed DT framework is proposed to achieve real- 
time intelligent control of the pipe-jacking machine’s attitude; (2) a 
soil-machine interaction load model is established and integrated into 
the DT framework, providing a theoretical foundation for attitude con -
trol during pipe-jacking construction; (3) A DT-based intelligent control 
platform is developed, enabling dynamic visualization of the pipe- 
jacking process, risk warnings, and correction force recommendations 
through computational algorithms.
The rest of this paper is structured as follows: Section 2 outlines the 
engineering background. Section 3 details the architecture of the DT- 
based intelligent control platform for the pipe-jacking machine’s atti -
tude. Section 4 explains the theoretical analysis model for the attitude of 
the rectangular pipe-jacking machine used in this paper. Section 5 pre -
sents a case study of the platform’s application in a practical tunnel 
construction project. Section 6 concludes the paper.
2. Engineering background
The Shasan Station, located in the Bao’an District of Shenzhen, 
China, is a two-story underground island station. As shown in Fig. 1 , the 
two ends of the station were built using the open-cut method, while the 
middle part of the station, which is 70 m long and passes under a large 
concrete box culvert, was constructed using the twin closely-spaced 
rectangular pipe-jacking method [ 17 ]. As shown in Fig. 2 (a), the 
cross-section of the station during construction is 13.53 m high and 22.6 
m wide, divided into two small sections: the left-line and right-line 
substructures, with a close spacing of 5 cm. Each substructure is built 
in sequence with a super-large rectangular pipe-jacking machine. The 
left- and right-line substructures are then connected to form an inte -
grated underground space for operational use through structural trans -
formation, as shown in Fig. 2 (b). As shown in Fig. 3 , the pipe-jacking 
machine used is 13.55 m high and 11.295 m wide, comprising two 
smaller pipe-jacking machines: the upper and lower submachines. The 
machine is equipped with 45 articulation cylinders to assist with attitude 
correction, arranged in the middle of the machine. These cylinders are 
divided into six groups to improve control efficiency, with their pa -
rameters listed in Table 1 .
During rectangular pipe-jacking, the machine typically exhibits a 
snake-like motion (see Fig. 4 (a)). Therefore, the primary challenge in 
constructing subway stations using the closely-spaced pipe-jacking 
method lies in the high-precision control of the machine’s attitude and 
alignment. This is especially critical when the right-line tunnel is jacked 
closely to the left-line tunnel. Additionally, for super-large rectangular 
pipe-jacking machines, the uneven distribution of soil strata within the 
excavation area can easily cause an unbalanced force on the machine, 
exacerbating its deviation. Deviation of the machine towards the left- 
line tunnel can easily lead to collision risks, while deviation away 
from the left-line tunnel can widen the gap between the two tunnels, 
making it difficult to form an integrated station structure (see Fig. 4 (b)). 
Vertical misalignment of the machine can also cause elevation differ -
ences between the left and right tunnels, leading to water leakage and 
reducing the station’s usable space, thereby severely impacting the 
station’s functionality. According to design requirements, the net dis -
tance between the left- and right-line tunnels must be controlled within 
10 cm. However, it is difficult to effectively control the machine’s atti -
tude within the design requirements if using the traditional feedback- 
based control method [ 2 , 11 ]. Therefore, a digital twin approach for 
the attitude control of rectangular pipe-jacking machines is considered,

3. Digital-twin framework for the intelligent control of 
machine ’ s attitude
To achieve intelligent attitude control in pipe-jacking operations, 
this section presents a digital twin (DT) framework that integrates real- 
time data acquisition, virtual modeling, and intelligent decision-making. 
The framework consists of a physical layer, a digital layer, and a service 
layer, enabling real-time interaction between physical systems and vir -
tual models. Section 3 details the functions of each layer and their roles 
in supporting accurate attitude prediction and control.
3.1. Framework design
To reduce the trajectory deviation of the rectangular pipe-jacking 
machine, this paper developed an intelligent control platform based 
on DT technology. According to the DT concept, which comprises five 
components: physical entity, virtual entity, connections, data, and ser -
vices [ 8 , 39 ]. The platform ’ s key functions include data visualization, 
risk warning, and intelligent decision-making for deviation correction. 
The framework design of this platform is illustrated in Fig. 5 . By inte -
grating geological information, various monitoring data of the machine, 
and other relevant details, the platform aids managers in real-time site 
information management and provides risk warnings and guidance for 
timely attitude adjustments by users. The platform operates as follows: 
first, monitoring data from the pipe-jacking construction is automati -
cally collected through a multi-function collection base station and 
various sensors. This information is then wirelessly transmitted from the 
base station to the data center and the platform. A virtual model of the 
tunnel construction, including the pipe-jacking machine, surrounding 
soil, and tunnel structure, is created, and dynamic mapping between the 
construction site and the virtual model is achieved based on the 
collected data. With the input of dynamic data, the attitude of the 
rectangular pipe-jacking machine can be simulated using the established 
theoretical analysis model (introduced in Section 4 ). As a result, the

3.2. Physical layer
The physical layer serves as the foundation of the DT system, 
focusing on the real-time collection and transmission of key construction 
data. This section outlines its structure and core functions.
3.2.1. Multi-information of pipe-jacking construction
Pipe-jacking construction is a complex process involving various 
types of information, including geological data, tunnel structure details, 
and specifications of the pipe-jacking machine. To effectively reduce 
trajectory deviation, it is essential for the platform to collect and inte -
grate these diverse data types in a timely manner, thereby providing a 
fundamental information source for accurate control and adjustment.

Since tunnel structural data are less utilized in this paper — primarily

Regarding data acquisition, geological data were primarily obtained 
through borehole sampling, while pipe-jacking machine data were 
collected via the tunnelling system and guidance system and transmitted

In terms of data storage, geological data were stored in a constructed

For data processing, to improve the retrieval efficiency of geological 
data, the original data were preprocessed by segmenting the geological 
model into smaller zones along the pipe-jacking direction. A weighted 
algorithm was applied to generate stratum information for each zone, 
enabling efficient real-time access. As for pipe-jacking machine data, 
preprocessing methods such as data extraction and outlier detection 
were employed to ensure data validity.
3.2.2. Real-time data acquisition and transmission
To effectively acquire construction site information, real-time data 
collection is achieved through multiple base stations with distinct 
functions, all connected to a central gateway that manages and controls 
terminal equipment. Various sensors, including those integrated into 
different systems of the pipe-jacking machine and the automatic guid -
ance system, are linked to the collection base stations to gather real-time 
data from the construction site. Data are collected at the second-level 
interval, ensuring real-time acquisition of operational parameters. The 
collection base station is responsible for aggregating diverse information 
during pipe-jacking, while the transmission base station handles both 
uplink and downlink data transmission and control.
The hardware layout at the construction site is illustrated in Fig. 6 . 
Sensors and theodolites are strategically installed on the construction 
site, and laser targets are positioned on the pipe-jacking machine. Most 
of the sensors installed on the machine are connected to the control 
platform via wire transmission, which ensures high transmission speed 
and stability [ 32 ]. Additionally, these sensors are calibrated both in the 
factory and on-site following assembly, further enhancing the reliability 
and stability of the data and effectively resolving issues related to real- 
time data acquisition and transmission. Collected data is transmitted to 
the collection base station via a wireless link and subsequently sent to 
the data center through the transmission base station. Theodolites, laser 
targets of the automatic guidance system, and sensors on various sys -
tems of the pipe-jacking machine are installed and calibrated based on 
the specific conditions of the tunnel construction. Additionally, manual 
checks of the machine ’ s position and attitude are performed at regular 
intervals to verify the data measured by the automatic guidance system. 
To ensure uninterrupted operation of the acquisition system, a contin -
uous power supply is maintained, and relevant equipment is routinely

3.3.2. Real-time interaction
The real-time data and 3D models of pipe-jacking construction dis -
played on the platform, as shown in Fig. 7 , are synchronized with the 
construction process through the data acquisition system. Multiple 
monitoring points are established, including those for the attitude of the 
pipe-jacking machine and the earth pressure at the excavation face. 
Users can access the platform via the network to obtain real-time 
monitoring data. Furthermore, when the monitoring data exceeds pre -
defined warning thresholds, the platform will display information 
regarding the risk warning level and the corresponding response plans. 
Based on this risk warning mechanism and response plan, the platform’s 
computer system can automatically generate guidance parameters for 
the intelligent control of the machine’s attitude, which are then pro -
vided to operators in the control room.
3.4. Service layer
The service layer utilizes theoretical load model and real-time data to 
support functions such as predictive simulation and intelligent control. 
This section introduces how these services assist operators in making 
data-informed decisions for accurate machine attitude adjustment.
3.4.1. Parallel deduction service
To aid users in assessing the risk warning levels, a risk warning 
system has been integrated into the platform’s software, designed ac -
cording to current specifications [ 7 ]. As shown in Fig. 8 , the risk warning 
workflow utilizes two control indexes: the pipe-jacking machine’s de -
viation and its change rate. Notably, if the sign of the change rate is 
opposite to that of the deviation, it indicates that the deviation of the 
pipe-jacking machine will decrease, and the risk level associated with 
the change rate is categorized as green. The pipe-jacking machine’s 
deviation is determined based on real-time monitoring data, while its 
change rate is influenced by the machine’s attitude.
The risk warning level is determined using these two control indexes. 
Users can preset standard or threshold values for the deviation and 
change rate risk levels in the software according to relevant risk man -
agement specifications. Consequently, the platform can provide real- 
time risk warnings based on the monitoring data, which are displayed 
in red, orange, yellow, or green to indicate different risk levels, as shown 
in Fig. 9 .
As shown in Fig. 10 , the pipe-jacking construction process is divided 
into two stages: the stoppage and advancement stages. During the 
advancement stage, the pipe-jacking machine moves forward, and soil 
extracted during excavation is conveyed to the muck bucket. The ma -
chine will stop when the muck bucket is full, and the muck bucket will 
then be transported to the ground surface to unload the muck. Fig. 10 (c) 
illustrates that parallel deduction is performed during the stoppage 
stage. Based on the geological conditions and construction parameters 
from the previous advancement stage, the attitude of the pipe-jacking 
machine for the next stage is predicted using the theoretical analysis 
model (introduced in Section 4 ). This allows the operator to evaluate 
risks and make timely adjustments. The predicted risk warning level for 
the next advancement stage is determined in conjunction with the ma -
chine’s deviation, and intelligent decisions are made based on the pre -
dicted risk level.
3.4.2. Intelligent decision service
As illustrated in Fig. 10 (c), intelligent decision-making is based on 
the predicted risk warning level and the corresponding response plan, 
which guides the intelligent control of the pipe-jacking machine’s atti -
tude for the following advancement stage. The workflow of the response 
plan is detailed in Table 2 , where the parameters are selected based on 
the Chinese pipe-jacking specification [ 40 ]. Depending on the risk 
warning level, various response strategies are employed, including ad -
justments to monitoring frequency and deviation correction angles. The 
attitude angle of the machine for the next advancement stage is deter -
mined to minimize trajectory deviation based on the correction angle 
and attitude angle from the previous stage. The relationship between 
various machine attitude angles and rectification moments can be 
determined using a load model that accounts for soil-machine interac -
tion. This model leverages sensor data, including soil chamber pressure, 
articulation cylinder thrust, and geological distribution parameters, to 
compute the correction force in real time, as discussed in Section 4 . 
Consequently, guide parameters for intelligent attitude control, such as 
the rectification moments for the articulation cylinders arranged in 
groups, are calculated automatically using the theoretical analysis 
model based on the attitude angle. These parameters are then displayed 
on the intelligent decision interface of the platform. Operators in the 
control room can use these guide parameters to precisely adjust the 
articulation cylinders, thereby achieving accurate control of the ma -
chine’s attitude.

5. Case study and validation
To assess the efficacy of the suggested intelligent control platform 
that integrates the load model, the left-line tunnel of Shasan Station was 
chosen as a case study. The main parameters are presented in Table 4 . 
The data acquired by the sensors of the machine are initially stored in 
the local database of the intelligent control platform. Subsequently, the 
data processing module embedded in the control platform performs 
preprocessing operations, including data extraction, outlier detection, 
and data transformation, to generate a refined dataset. The refined 
dataset is then fed into the embedded prediction algorithm, which is 
designed based on the load model of the pipe jacking machine. The 
predicted attitude parameters are validated against predefined safety 
thresholds and instantaneously transmitted to the risk warning module 
and intelligent decision control unit for auxiliary attitude correction. 
Specifically, by embedding this model into the intelligent platform, the 
platform can output shield attitude based on input soil and operating 
parameters, enabling risk warnings. Additionally, it can input shield 
attitude (such as rectification angles calculated from response strategies) 
and other parameters to output predicted rectification moments, 
thereby facilitating intelligent decision-making.

5.1. Data base
The geological profile of the left-line tunnel is shown in Fig. 21 . 
Based on the geological survey findings, the overlying strata at the 
construction site consist of plain fill and silty clay, while the underlying 
strata are sandy clay, fully weathered mixed granite, and highly 
weathered granite. Due to these uneven strata, controlling the attitude 
of the pipe-jacking machine is challenging, and the machine tends to 
deviate from the DTA. Detailed mechanical and physical characteristics 
of the strata have been presented in Table 5 , which were primarily ob -
tained from the detailed investigation phase of the Shasan Station 
project through borehole exploration. Parameters concerning the rect -
angular pipe-jacking machine and the left-line tunnel are detailed in 
Section 2 .
Operation parameters of the machine were collected using various 
sensors during pipe jacking, as illustrated in Section 3.2 . Attitude pa -
rameters of the pipe-jacking machine were collected using an automatic 
guidance system. These data were transmitted to the data center of the 
platform. Subsequently, several data preprocessing methods, including 
data extraction, outlier detection, and data transformation, were applied 
to the operating and attitude parameters of the machine. The data was 
processed centrally every minute during the jacking process, and the 
preprocessing typically took less than 1 s. No significant delays were 
observed during the data processing on the platform.
5.1.1. Data extraction
The operating and attitude parameters from the advancement stage 
were collected for preprocessing. Since the machine stopped during 
muck outward transportation stage, the advancement process was 
discontinuous. Consequently, these parameters during the stoppage 
stage were set to zero. First, the data for the advancement stage were 
extracted from the monitoring data. Advancement Distance ( Ad ) was 
used to identify the pipe-jacking construction stage in the operating 
parameters, which was recorded at a regular intervals Δ t . If Ad ( t + Δ t ) – 
Ad ( t ) = 0, the data belong to stoppage stage; otherwise, the data belongs 
to the advancement stage.
The data from the advancement stage were used as the initial dataset 
for preprocessing, which included outlier detection and data trans -
formation. However, the first 2.5 % and last 2.5 % of the data, recorded 
during the transition between the advancement and stoppage stages, 
were excluded due to their high volatility.
5.1.2. Outlier detection
Outliers are some extreme values that markedly differ from the 
trends observed in the data after preprocess. Due to sensor malfunctions 
and unstable construction conditions, outliers can represent errors in the 
monitoring data, often characterized by sudden and substantial in -
creases or decreases. Consequently, it is essential to identify outliers 
using statistical methods. In this paper, the Interquartile Range (IQR) 
was employed to detect these anomalies.

parameters for the same construction process, average values corre -
sponding to specific advancement distances were calculated. In this 
paper a short construction process was defined as the machine 
advancing a distance of 10 mm. The average values for this process were

5.2.3. Risk warning analysis
The risk waring function of the established platform is shown in 
Fig. 25 . At the start of jacking the left-line tunnel, the machine was right- 
handed, and the horizontal deviation approached 40 mm. Consequently, 
the machine was classified under a yellow risk warning level according 
to the platform ’ s risk warning mechanism. Guidance for adjusting the 
machine ’ s attitude was provided to the operator to make precise cor -
rections and promptly reduce trajectory deviation. This adjustment 
ensured that the pipe-jacking machine steadily shifted to a left-handed 
position with a negative yaw angle. After continued adjustments of 
the machine ’ s attitude, the risk level decreased to green as the jacking 
distance approached 30 m. Furthermore, the final horizontal deviation 
was   16 mm, significantly below the allowable deviation of ± 50 mm.
