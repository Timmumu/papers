Abstract
The unstructured construction site with a complex and changeable environment
brings challenges to human –machine interaction. In this study, the earthwork
digital twin (DT) is proposed to realize accurate and real ‐time perception, which
can support the teleoperation of an automated bulldozer. The overall framework
includes anterior ‐time DT, real ‐time DT, and posterior ‐time DT, which integrates
three ‐dimensional modeling, edge detection, and blade trajectory algorithms.
Multidimensional heterogeneous data in bulldozer teleoperation can be displayed
in real ‐time to ensure construction safety. Big data during teleoperation of the
construction process can be collected, stored, and analyzed. The DT proposed in
this study was successfully applied in a major construction project in China, and
testing results show its universality, robustness, and advanced performance. The
key technologies proposed in this study can be applied to solve the common
problems in the construction industry, which is promising for future intelligent
construction.
KEYWORDS
automated bulldozer, digital twin, earthwork, intelligent construction, teleoperation
1 | INTRODUCTION
Extreme and hazardous construct ion sites bring challenges to
workers' safety. Teleoperation can remotely control machinery or
robot, which is a promising way to avoid construction hazards and
has broad application prospects in mine drainage, water conserv-
ancy engineering, nuclear waste treatment, and other working
conditions (Lee et al., 2022 ;O k i s h i b ae ta l . , 2019 ;W a n g
et al., 2023 ). With the support of the digital twin (DT) system,
teleoperation can accurately per ceive the environment in real ‐
time for earthwork construction. The DT system is the interactive
integration of the physical world and the information world (Zhou
et al., 2021 ), which is the development direction to improve the
digitalization and intelligence o f the construction industry (Jiang
et al., 2022 ;S o n ge ta l . , 2023 ). DT has been applied to quality
control, safety management, site monitoring, and scheduling
during construction (Boje et al., 2020 ;S a v i c , 2022 ;T a o
et al., 2019 ;Z h o ue ta l . , 2022 ). The research on the combination
of teleoperation and the DT system has engineering significance
and scientific value (Li, Zheng, et al., 2022 ;W a n ge ta l . , 2021 ).

Unstructured construction sites need to deal with complex and
changeable environments (Vahdatikhaki et al., 2017), which chal-
lenges the human –machine interaction process between operators
and heavy equipment; thus, the support of DT is needed. The
difficulties of teleoperation include accurately perceiving heteroge-
neous data such as sound, light, and force during construction and
displaying them dynamically in real ‐time. The operator is an effective
guarantee to ensure safe and efficient construction. Affected by labor
shortage, the expert knowledge of experienced operators has
become a valuable digital asset (You, Ding, et al., 2022). Thus, the
collection and storage of construction process data is crucial (Ding
et al., 2022; You, Peng, et al., 2022). The research of DT systems for
earthwork construction requires expertise in the fields of civil
engineering, automation, and artificial intelligence, which is promising
for the interdisciplinary integration of intelligent construction.
Earthwork construction has the characteristics of large scope and
difficult scene perception. Especially for the working condition of
mine dumping, the height difference between different working
surfaces is huge. Constructing a digital three ‐dimensional (3D) model
of earthwork construction is an effective method for realizing high ‐
precision environment perception. Three ‐dimensional models based
on unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV) tilt photography have been applied
in geological disaster analysis (Wang et al., 2020), ecological
information management (Zhu et al., 2022), and monitoring and
detection of infrastructure structure (Atencio et al., 2022). The edge
detection of the 3D model can realize the accurate perception of
retaining walls, but the application in earthwork construction still
needs further research. In existing research, complete coverage path
planning for bulldozers has been identified as an effective method for
site leveling construction, leading to improved efficiency in site
operations (Li, Zhou, et al., 2022). However, it is important to note
that in the context of open ‐pit mines, the primary objective of
bulldozer construction is to facilitate the advancement of the
retaining wall during the soil discharge process, rather than achieving
complete coverage of the site's path (Oksanen & Visala, 2009; Pour
Arab et al., 2022). As a result, the applicability of existing research is
limited in this specific scenario.
The main challenges to overcome are as follows: (1) how to
construct the DT system of earthwork construction, (2) how to
realize real ‐time and accurate perception to support the teleopera-
tion of bulldozer under complex working conditions, and (3) how to
transfer and store multidimensional heterogeneous data in the
teleoperation for the construction process. To resolve these
problems, this study established earthwork DT for the teleoperation
of an automated bulldozer. Using the tilt photography technology of
UAV, we build the 3D model of a large range of earthwork
construction sites and combine deep learning to realize the edge
detection of retaining walls. On the basis of the expert knowledge of
experienced operators, this study constructs the construction path
generation algorithm of a bulldozer shovel. Through the real ‐time
feedback of sound, light, and force, the real scene of the cab is
restored in real‐time, which is conducive to the construction safety of
the site.
The structure of this study is as follows: Section 2 summarizes
the relevant research status, including the DT for the construction
industry, 3D modeling of the construction site, and teleoperation in
construction machinery. Section 3 introduces the architecture and
core algorithm of the DT system for earthwork construction.
Section 4 introduces the test and application of the DT system.
Section 5 analyzes the results of the DT system and discussed
research limitations and future work. Section 6 summarizes this
study.
2 | LITERATURE REVIEW
2.1 | DT for construction industry
The construction process requires the human –machine interaction,
and DT for construction industry has been applied in design,
manufacturing, operation, and maintenance (Lim et al., 2020; Moi
et al., 2020; Zhou et al., 2019). DT consists of physical models of the
real world and digital models of the virtual world (Zhou et al., 2022).
Data sources include the Internet of Things (Ding et al., 2022), the
Internet of vehicles, and multidimensional heterogeneous data of
different sensors (Rudrappa, 2019). Infrastructure maintenance and
operation is a successful example of DT application in the
construction industry (Gürdür Broo et al., 2022). A building
information modeling (BIM) model with semantic completion can
improve the influence of architectural DT in the design, construction,
and operation stages (Boje et al., 2020).
DT combined with artificial intelligence is significant for improv-
ing construction quality and ensuring management safety (Kamari &
Ham, 2022). Zhidchenko et al. ( 2018) simulated machine dynamics
based on DT to predict the fatigue life of construction machinery
engines. Jiang et al. ( 2022) built the DT system of tower cranes,
which can analyze the unsafe hoisting behavior of operators. DT can
be applied to strain analysis of construction machinery to realize
predictive maintenance based on sensor data and simulation results
(Moi et al., 2020).
The real ‐time interaction between the operator and the
construction machinery is required for the construction process.
Thus, the study of interpersonal interaction based on DT has practical
value (Zhang et al., 2018). On the basis of DT recording personnel
trajectories of construction sites, behavioral data analysis can be
carried out to ensure safety risk management (Jiang et al., 2021).
Combined with the integrated perception of sensor data, such as
machine vision, inertial measurement unit (IMU), and real ‐time
kinematics (RTK), the construction safety of unmanned bulldozers
and autonomous excavators can be ensured (You et al., 2021; Zhang
et al., 2021). Deryabina et al. ( 2020) combined with DT to realize
intelligent process control of mine construction, covering the
collaborative construction process of various construction machinery
(excavators, loaders, and mining cards). The research of DT in
earthwork construction can improve safety and is considered to be
promising.

2.2 | Three‐dimensional modeling of the
construction site
The 3D model is widely used in the construction industry (Wang &
Kim, 2019). Data sources for building 3D models include laser
scanning, images, and videos, and 3D point clouds are obtained based
on relative position information and coordinate transformation to
restore 3D geometric shapes of construction ‐related objects (Chen
et al., 2017; Lu & Lee, 2017; Tang et al., 2010; Yu & Wang, 2019).
The main applications of the 3D point cloud in the construction
industry include model reconstruction, quality detection, and
progress tracking in the construction process (Ma & Liu, 2018;X u
et al., 2018). Earthwork construction is characterized by a large area,
which brings difficulties in scene perception and challenges for
man–machine interaction between operators and construction
machinery. The 3D modeling of the construction site can obtain
the terrain and texture information of the whole construction site
(Mirzaei et al., 2022), which can improve the safety of teleoperation.
The difficulties in the application of the 3D model include the
acquisition of semantic information (Perez ‐Perez et al., 2021).
Czerniawski et al. ( 2016) realized the automatic location and
extraction of pipe spool in the point cloud based on curvature
estimation and clustering results of local data. However, this method
needs the support of the computer ‐aided design (CAD)/BIM model,
and its application is limited in the absence of the CAD/BIM model.
Wang et al. ( 2017) classify point cloud data by SVM to realize the
identification and location estimation of reinforcement in concrete
construction. Segmentation processing of point cloud data of building
structures is an effective way to classify objects (Kim et al., 2013).
With the development of artificial intelligence, point data processing
combined with deep learning has become a hot and challenging
research topic (You et al., 2023; Zhou et al., 2023).
2.3 | Teleoperation in construction machinery
Teleoperation of construction machinery can keep the operator from
a dangerous construction environment. The challenges of teleopera-
tion research include the acquisition, transmission, and presentation
of multidimensional heterogeneous data during construction. With
the application of 5G, low delay and stable transmission of high ‐
throughput data can be realized, which has become one of the key
technologies to support smooth teleoperation (Balkus et al., 2022;
Wang et al., 2019). On the basis of scene reconstruction and
recognition of object category and distance information, combined
with virtual reality, teleoperation can help the robot to carry out
remote control (Zhou et al., 2020). Augmented reality combined with
deep learning can improve the accuracy of device positioning and
provide effective digital information (Baek et al., 2019). These studies
can improve teleoperation stability and scene awareness.
Force feedback improves teleoperation task performance and
construction safety (Zhu et al., 2021). On the basis of the excavator's
attitude data and reaction force, Ito et al. realized the identification
and demonstration of machine instability to improve the safety of the
construction process. The force data analysis of excavators can break
through the limitation of visual perception (Ito et al., 2021). Fernando
and Marshall ( 2020) realize the identification of excavation materials
through a machine learning algorithm. The data involved in
teleoperation include force feedback, video image, and mechanical
attitude. How to display multidimensional heterogeneous data
graphically needs further research.
3 | DT FOR TELEOPERATION
3.1 | Over structure
The DT system constructed in this study includes anterior ‐time DT,
real‐time DT, and posterior ‐time DT. The overall framework is shown
in Figure 1. The anterior ‐time DT is a virtual world composed of 3D
models, which can be used for edge detection and path planning. The
real‐time DT includes a teleoperated platform and an automated
bulldozer. On the basis of the real ‐time interaction of multi-
dimensional heterogeneous data, the safe and efficient construction
of bulldozer teleoperation is realized. The posterior ‐time DT includes
data acquisition, storage, and analysis, which can realize the
DT‐based service system based on big data.
3.2 | Three‐dimensional modeling of the
construction site
UAV tilt photography is an effective method for large ‐scale 3D site
modeling. The tilt photography technique can be used to extract the
spatial position, color, and texture of ground objects from the image
and then quickly establish the real 3D data model of the construction
site according to the unified coordinate system. Considering the large
area of the mine dump site, the driver has difficulty obtaining the
overall situation of the construction site through ground observation
and realizing a globally optimal decision. The 3D real ‐scene model of
the construction site effectively breaks through the limitations of
ground observation.

3.3 | System design for DT
In the DT system constructed for this research, the data of
different components interact with each other to ensure
bulldozer's safe and efficient construction. The hardware and
software architecture of the DT system is shown in Figure 5.
Teleoperation contr ol cab can obtain real ‐time video images of
the bulldozer and can track the operator's attention based on an
eye tracker and display the naked eye 3D images. Force feedback
and surround sound improve the operator's perception of the
field environment. The operator realizes the bulldozer's move-
ment control, shovel control, and Scarifier attitude control
through the control lever on the seat. The reception and
transmission of data are realized through the data interaction
terminal and the 5G base station constructed on the site. The
remote ‐controlled bulldozer in the mine is fitted with various
sensors, including cameras, force sensors, and sound sensors,
which are coded and sent to the teleoperation control cab. The
bulldozer data ‐receiving module mainly realizes the decoding and
transmission of the control signal and sends it to the correspond-
ing actuator through the vehicle's electronic control unit. The
auxiliary construction system i s realized based on 3D modeling of
UAVs. The construction site 3D model can help operators obtain
the overall information of an extensive range of construction
sites.

4 | TESTING AND APPLICATION FOR
MINE DUMPING
4.1 | Introduction of the experimental site
The dump is an area where rock and topsoil are stripped and
deposited, whereas an open ‐pit coal mine is mining. The goal of
bulldozer construction is to backfill the open pit, which is of great
significance to ecological restoration. The experimental site selected
by this study is a typical open ‐pit dump, as shown in Figure 6, located
in Hulunbuir City, Inner Mongolia Autonomous Region, China. The
retaining wall of the dump is approximately 15 m ( H
m1) above the
bottom of the mine and 2 m ( Hm2) above the construction area. The
top of the retaining wall is approximately 1 m ( Wm1) wide. Mine
drainage construction is a project involving a lot of earthworks.
4.2 | Sensor configuration and installation
The bulldozer type selected in this study is the Shantui‐SD90 bulldozer, as
s h o w ni nF i g u r e7a. SD90 is the bulldozer with China's largest engine
power of 708 kW/2100 rpm. The machine's weight is 106,260 kg, the
overall dimension of the machine is 11,500 ∗ 5265∗ 4590 mm, and the
shovel's depth is 1010 mm.
In this study, the sensors installed on the SD90 bulldozer include an
environment sensing sensor (camer a, ultrasonic radar, and millimeter
wave radar), a position and pose acquisition sensor (RTK ‐GPS system,
IMU, and pressure sensor), and a communication sensor (5G base
station). Figure 7 shows the installation position of the sensor.

4.3.2 | Real‐time DT
Real‐time DT needs to display multidimensional heterogeneous data in
the bulldozer construction proce ss, including video images, obstacle
detection results, mechanical parameters, bulldozer 3D attitude, force
feedback, and field sound. These data are integrated and displayed on the
teleoperation platform, and the driver can control the dozer efficiently
and accurately based on the multid imensional heterogeneous data.
Figure 12 shows a real ‐time DT system in which the teleoperation
platform is supported by a hydrauliclever on a platform with six degrees
of freedom. The teleoperation platform has three degrees of freedom in
the X, Y,a n d Z linear directions and three rotational degrees of freedom
around the X, Y,a n d Z axes, respectively, as shown in Figure13.T h et i l t
angle of the platform is obtained by a sensor mounted on the bulldozer,
and the position of the platform is changed in real ‐time by a hydraulic
lever.
On the teleoperation platform, the manipulator controls the
bulldozer's movement and the shovel blade's attitude through the left
and right handles. The position of the left handle ① indicates that the
bulldozer rotates to the left, and the position ② indicates that the
bulldozer moves backward. The position ③ with the right handle
indicates that the spatula tilts to the right, and the position ④
indicates that the spatula is raised. Given the large size of the
bulldozer, the ultrasonic radar and millimeter wave radar installed on
the body of the bulldozer can cover the blind area of the driver's
vision and realize the detection of obstacles. The obstacle detection
results are shown in Figure 12b.
An eye ‐movement tracking instrument is installed on the tele-

walking surveyors to avoid safety issues (Hinze & Teizer, 2011;

6 | CONCLUSIONS
In this study, a DT system of earthwork construction is
constructed, which can help automated bulldozers to carry out
rock and coal seam stripping, dump construction, road repair, and
other work safely and efficiently. The pretwin system constructed
in this study is a virtual world composed of 3D models, which can
detect the edge of retaining walls and plan construction paths.
Real‐time DT through sound, light, and force feedback with rapid
perception ability contribute to site construction safety while
improving work efficiency. Post ‐DT includes data acquisition,
storage, and analysis, whic h can improve the scheduling
management.
On the basis of the DT system constructed in this study,
bulldozer operators do not have to work continuously in an open ‐pit
mine in a cold environment of minus 40°C in winter. The remote
operating platform can significantly improve the working environ-
ment for bulldozer operators. On the basis of the naked eye 3D
technology and light field constructed in this study, the real scene is
restored in front of the driver's eyes, transforming from manned
driving to unmanned driving, realizing the safety of no one. The
bulldozers could operate on the site in strict accordance with the
instructions. Retaining wall edge detection and real ‐time position
display effectively ensures construction safety.
Considering the construction site's complex and varied working
conditions, this study needs more practical tests to verify the
system's reliability. In the follow ‐up study, we will continue
optimizing the system, improving the algorithm's accuracy and
reliability, and boosting the construction of an intelligent mine.
