ABSTRACT 
High traffic flow in a confined tunnel makes fire safety a critical issue. This paper proposed a digital twin 
framework for tunnel fire safety management in real-time, driven by dynamic sensor data and AIoT technologies. 
A deep learning model trained by the Transformer network and simulation dataset is used to predict real-time 
fire location and size. Then, the AI model is integrated into a 3D digital twin platform developed by the game 
engine Unity 3D. The performance of the proposed digital twin framework is demonstrated using numerical 
experiments and large-scale tunnel fire tests. Results show that the established AI model achieved promising 
accuracy in predicting fire location and power for both numerical and experimental data. The digital twin 
platform can also visualize the 3D fire scene that supports evacuation, firefighting, and emergency rescue. This 
research demonstrates the feasibility of using a 3D environment and digital twin in real-time fire safety 
management. 
1. Introduction 
Driven by economic and urban development, a large number of 
tunnels and underground facilities have been built to facilitate trans -
portation. However, the tunnel has an enclosed environment. In a tunnel 
fire incident, it is difficult for people to evacuate from the narrow space 
with a single transferring direction ( Ingason et al., 2015 ). For example, a 
tunnel fire occurred in the Shanxi Yanhou tunnel in 2014, 40 people 
were killed, and 12 people were injured ( Zhang and Huang, 2022 ). Thus, 
fire safety management and emergency response for tunnels are 
essential. 
Tunnel fire safety can be improved through several aspects, such as 
enhancing the safety education of drivers to minimize traffic accidents, 
optimizing the fire safety design of tunnels, and improving emergency 
response strategies. However, from the principle of safety management, 
some accident like collision and fire in a tunnel is inevitable and un -
predictable. Thus, to achieve proper fire safety, we need to design a 
tunnel that can handle any possible fire scenarios ( Zhang et al., 2019 , 
2021a ) and improve emergency response to minimize fire casualties and 
damage through advanced technologies ( Ma and Wu, 2020 ; Wang et al., 
2015 ). Such a smart firefighting system can recognize and quantify fire 
timely and accurately using neural networks and provide more fire 
management functions and visualization tools by combining digital twin 
technologies ( Huang et al., 2022 ). 
To improve the fire safety of tunnel and underground space and 
handle tunnel fires caused by vehicle collisions, several studies have 
been done to investigate the application of the latest information tech -
nologies and computational methods in tunnel firefighting. For example, 
Wu et al. (2021) demonstrated the possibility of the Artificial Intelli -
gence (AI) method to detect and determine fire sources and hazards. An 
intelligent tunnel firefighting system based on IoT sensors and AI 
methods is further proposed to identify fire scenarios in a real-time 
manner and demonstrated in a reduced-scale tunnel model ( Wu et al., 
2022a ). Guo et al. (2022) investigated tunnel fire scenario reconstruc -
tion and key fire parameters prediction by integrating tunnel fire in -
formation and machine learning methods. Previous studies showed that 
it is very useful to deal with tunnel fire problems using smart algorithms

To enhance fire safety management and firefighting with greater 
intelligence and reliability, it is crucial to accurately predict fire devel -
opment and share real-time information about onsite situations and 
resources. Digital Twin offers a promising solution by providing an 
executable and dynamic virtual representation of real-world objects or 
activities, which reflects the geometrical or semantic properties and 
behavior patterns of objects using the latest data communication and 
modeling techniques ( Qi et al., 2021 ). A digital twin system consists of 
several key components, including physical entities, virtual models, 
services, fusion data, and connections. These components work together 
to enable real-time integration and interoperation among physical en -
tities, virtual models, and services through fusion data ( Glaessgen and 
Stargel, 2012 ). 
The framework of a digital twin system comprises five essential 
components. 
(1) Physical entities, representing objects with specific functions 
within the real-world environment, serve as the foundation for 
the digital representation created by the digital twin. 
(2) Virtual models, the digital equivalents of physical entities, 
replicate the geometrical or semantic properties and behavior 
patterns of their real-world counterparts. 
(3) Service layer, encompassing functions such as management, 
control, and optimization provided for stakeholders. This enable 
stakeholders to interact with and utilize the digital twin system. 
(4) Fusion data, acting as the core driver of the digital twin, inte -
grating information from various sources to provide a compre -
hensive and accurate representation of the real-world 
environment within the digital twin system. 
(5) Connection component, facilitating real-time integration and 
interoperation among physical entities, virtual models, and ser -
vices through fusion data. This ensures seamless operation of the 
digital twin system, providing timely and relevant information to 
stakeholders. 
To enhance the fire safety of buildings or infrastructures like tunnels, 
integrating digital twins with physical facilities or developing a digital 
twin system integrated with current facility management systems is a 
feasible approach. This integration enables real-time monitoring, accu -
rate prediction, and effective management of fire incidents, ultimately 
improving overall fire safety outcomes. 
However, several challenges persist in designing and developing a 
digital twin-enabled safety management system for smart tunnels. 
Firstly, the deployment of IoT devices necessary for real-time data 
collection in digital twin systems entails significant investment, leading 
to low cost-efficiency, especially concerning fire emergency manage -
ment alone. Addressing this challenge involves expanding the func -
tionalities of the tunnel digital twin system while enhancing its cost- 
efficiency. Secondly, ensuring adequate coverage of critical fire infor -
mation poses a challenge, particularly in long tunnels where sensors 
located far from the fire may not receive sufficient data. Prioritizing 
sensors closer to the fire source becomes essential to overcome this 
challenge effectively. Additionally, current deep learning models used in 
fire prediction may not promptly process the limited data collected 
during fire situations. Developing and applying new deep learning 
models to enhance fire information prediction based on the existing 
sensor deployment is another pressing issue. Thirdly, previous experi -
ments on fire prediction models have primarily relied on numerical 
experiments and scale-down demonstration simulations, lacking full- 
scale and realistic fire scenarios. Evaluating the prediction accuracy, 
system stability, and cost-efficiency of digital twin firefighting systems 
under such conditions remains a crucial yet unaddressed aspect. These 
challenges underscore the necessity for further research to enhance the 
scientific rigor and effectiveness of digital twin-enabled safety man -
agement systems for smart tunnels. 
This paper presents a novel artificial intelligence of things (AIoT)- 
enabled digital twin system designed for intelligent tunnel fire safety 
management. The system leverages advanced deep learning algorithms 
and numerical simulations to accurately identify and reconstruct fire 
scenarios in real-time, facilitating swift decision-making during emer -
gencies. The reliability and effectiveness of AI prediction model are 
rigorously validated through extensive large-scale tunnel fire tests, 
ensuring its practical applicability in real-world scenarios. This valida -
tion process underscores the capability of the digital twin system to 
provide precise and actionable insights into tunnel fire incidents,

2. Key concepts 
2.1. Digital twin 
The concept of the digital twin was introduced in 2003 by Grieves for 
product lifecycle management, comprising physical products, virtual 
products, and their connections ( Grieves, 2014 ). Revisited by NASA in 
2012, the digital twin represents the state of a corresponding entity 
based on historical data, real-time sensor data, and a physical model 
twin. Described by Gabor, a digital twin is a specialized simulation 
constructed with expert knowledge and real data, achieving more ac -
curate simulations across different scales of time and space ( Gabor et al., 
2016 ). According to Maurer, a digital twin is a representation that de -
picts production processes and product performance ( Maurer, 2017 ). 
While cyber – physical system (CPS) and digital twin share conceptual 
commonalities, their emphases diverge. While CPS and digital twins 
share conceptual commonalities, they diverge in emphasis. CPS centers 
on the 3C concept (computing, communication, and control), whereas 
digital twins prioritize data and models for real-time interoperation 
between physical entities and their virtual counterparts ( Tao et al., 
2019a ; Jiang et al., 2023a ). The concept of the digital twin comprises 
two facets: the physical twin and its corresponding digital counterpart. 
Physical entities, augmented with smart devices, update sensing data to 
cyberspace (P2C), which in turn provides control signals to optimize the 
behaviors of real-life objects (C2P). Over the past few years, digital twin 
has been successfully applied in many fields, such as agriculture, con -
struction, manufacturing, smart city, health care, and supply chain, as 
shown in Fig. 1 . 
Digital twins can reflect the multi-dimensional attributes and be -
haviors using models and data and further provide information services 
such as real-time monitoring, dynamic control, and information sharing 
( Tao et al., 2019b ; Tao and Zhang, 2017 ). It functions like Building In -
formation Modelling (BIM) to encompass procedures, technologies, and 
data schemas about the building ’ s physical and functional characteris -
tics. But digital twin integrates more technologies to achieve 
process-oriented and spatial-temporally dynamic mapping of resources 
and activities, forming a close-loop control system ( Deng et al., 2021 ). 
Digital twins can automatically update themselves following the 
continuous changes of physical twins to realize real-time cyber-physical 
interoperation and synchronization ( Jiang et al., 2022 ; Su et al., 2022 ). 
For civil and building engineering, Construction Digital Twin (CDT) 
is a proposed socio-technical mapping of building components that aids 
collaborative management ( Boje et al., 2020 ). Digital Twin Construction 
(DTC) is also introduced to utilize real-time monitoring data and AI to 
optimize construction ( Sacks et al., 2020 ). DTC integrates data clus -
tering and the PDCA cycle for lean planning. While CDT assists con -
struction execution, DTC offers a comprehensive model. Five layers 
include data acquisition, transmission, digital modeling, integration, 
and services. Various technologies are applied for data collection, 
fusion, and visualization ( Tuhaise et al., 2023 ). 
For building fire safety, Digital Twin is still a relatively new concept 
with much fewer publications ( Wu et al., 2022a ; Zhang et al., 2024 ). A 
Digital Twin-based framework is proposed to improve the capability of 
indoor safety management, which combines the Internet of Things (IoT), 
building information modeling (BIM), and machine learning methods 
( Liu et al., 2020 ). Zhang et al. (2022a) developed an 
Artificial-Intelligence Digital Fire (AID-Fire) system for real-time 
building fire identification, and such a digital twin system was demon -
strated with real-scale compartment fire demonstration. The trained AI 
model can automatically process the obtained sensor data, and real-time 
2D scene and critical fire information (e.g., fire power and location) can 
be displayed. Data communication between digital and physical is a key

2.2. Smart firefighting 
In building fire applications, AI, Virtual Reality (VR), IoT, and BIM 
are usually the key components to establishing a digital twin, and there 
has been an increasing number of related studies conducted in recent 
years. AI model trained with massive data (e.g., simulation, experi -
mental data, or historical data) can be used to predict real-time fire 
information (e.g., fire size or location) or forecast future events (e.g., 
flashover, backflow, or structure response) in advance. A data-driven 
method was adopted to predict the temperature and velocity profile 
inside the compartment ( Hodges et al., 2019 ). Real-time prediction of 
fire heat release rate (HRR) using deep learning algorithms and flame 
images has been investigated ( Wang et al., 2023 ), and transient fire HRR 
can be well recognized by the proposed method. 
Because of the challenges in conducting massive large-scale fire tests 
and form an experimental database, coupling ML and numerical fire 
simulations were more widely used to train the prediction model. A 
machine learning forecasting model P-Flash was developed to predict 
the risk of flashover in multiple-room compartment fire ( Wang et al., 
2021a ). Heat detector temperature data generated from one thousand 
numerical simulations were used to train the model. Smoke movement 
during tunnel fires (i.e., backflow occurrence and back-layering length) 
can also be predicted by machine learning methods in previous study 
( Hong et al., 2022 ). Their results showed that the ANN model has the 
best performance to estimate the back-layering length. A framework 
based on Finite Element and machine learning was developed to predict 
the real-time structural response under fire ( Ye et al., 2022a ). Temper -
ature data and structure displacement from the numerical simulation are 
used as input and output to train the ML mode. 
Building information modeling (BIM) and related technologies are 
usually used to generate and manage the digital representation of 
physical assets, which can be utilized to develop Digital Twins. Their 
applications in fire safety management have been widely investigated 
( Li et al., 2018 ). A BIM-based Intelligent Fire Prevention and Disaster 
Relief System was developed to support early fire detection and fire 
rescue ( Cheng et al., 2017 ). Bluetooth-based sensors were used to 
monitor the fire information and show the real-time fire information on 
mobile devices. BIM is also applied in emergency evacuation design, a 
fire evacuation model was developed to facilitate the two-way data 
exchange in fire evacuation design ( Yakhou et al., 2022 ). BIM-based 
indoor localization method was investigated to support the emergency 
response during fire events ( Li et al., 2014 ). The localization accuracy 
and robustness of the proposed algorithm were proved via serval 
simulation fire scenarios. 
BIM related technologies are also adopted to study the behavior 
decisions of building users during evacuation. A fire emergency man -
agement system with the function of intelligent fire monitoring and 
optimal evacuation route planning was developed to study user de -
cisions ( Ma and Wu, 2020 ). Results show that the evacuation effec -
tiveness of the proposed method is better than the evacuation process 
without auxiliary guidance measures. Another research found that the 
evacuees ’ decisions during the emergency will be affected by their 
neighbors ’ decisions ( Fu et al., 2021 ). Evacuees tend to follow others 
when making risky decisions. The evacuation during an emergency can 
be investigated or optimized by integrating BIM and VR ( Ronchi et al., 
2015 ; Arias et al., 2019 ). A VR model developed from BIM was applied 
to investigate the factors of evacuation decisions ( Lovreglio et al., 2022 ). 
They found that both unfamiliarity and the presence of smoke negatively 
affect the evacuation choice. BIM technology has also been applied in 
fire safety design ( Siddiqui et al., 2021 ; Tang et al., 2021 ) and fire risk 
assessment ( Wang et al., 2021b ; Hosseini and Maghrebi, 2021 ). 
To address the above challenges, this work presents a digital twin 
framework for the simulation and connection between real tunnel and 
virtual tunnel model, especially during tunnel fire events. The 
remainder of this paper is organized as follows. This work is going to 
present a framework for the simulation and connection between the real 
tunnel and virtual tunnel model, especially during tunnel fire events. 
The second chapter will introduce the overall framework and each 
component of the proposed system, and the third section will build an 
AIoT-enabled immersive digital twin for real-time fire recognition. 
Then, some demonstrations and discussions will be given in the fourth 
section. The final chapter will conclude the present work. 
3. Digital twin framework for tunnel fire safety management 
A digital twin is regarded as a virtual to reflect the physical object in 
Grieves ’ s whitepaper ( Grieves, 2014 ). It consists of three main parts, 
according to his whitepaper: (1) physical entities (or assets) in the real 
world, (2) virtual models in the digital world, and (3) real-time inte -
gration and interoperation between the physical world and the digital 
world.

example, the user can operate in the digital tunnel to control the smoke 
ventilation and fire suppression system in the physical tunnel. 
3.1. AIoT-enabled physical infrastructure 
In the proposed framework, the tunnel itself and related physical 
entities constitute the physical space. Three categories of entities are 
defined, including fixed entities (e.g., tunnel systems), movable entities 
(e.g., passing vehicles), and probabilistic entities (e.g., accidents and fire 
events). The tunnel itself usually does not change over time, except in 
some extreme situations, such as structural collapses due to severe fire or 
vehicle collisions. Tunnel structure resilience under fire is important for 
tunnel safety, and many previous studies have explored the behaviors of 
tunnel structures during tunnel fire (Ye et al., 2022b). Due to the 
extremely low probability of occurrence, the structural failure of the 
tunnel is not considered, and the tunnel itself is considered an immu -
table object and the container of the digital twin in the proposed 
framework. 
The facility management system is used to maintain the daily oper -
ations of the road tunnel, such as the power supply, lighting and way -
marking systems, or used to control or mitigate various risks, such as 
drainage, firefighting piping, and smoke ventilation system. In the cur -
rent study, the proposed digital twin system aims to manage fire risk and 
guide firefighting activities. Therefore, only the facility management 
systems related to fire safety are included in the physical layer. The 
details of the facility management systems are listed in Table 1. These 
conventional physical systems could update the real-time information 
(e.g., location, ID, status, data flow, and) to their corresponding digital 
twins or be automatically controlled or through visual input. 
The passing vehicles or passengers belong to the movable entities of 
the system, which may control or be affected by fixed entities. During 
normal operation of the tunnel, vehicles only pass through the tunnel 
quickly. During the emergency, the vehicles may cause or be stopped by 
accidents or fires, and passengers may need to be evacuated from the 
tunnel. Then passengers or vehicles may interact with the fixed entities. 
For example, the vehicles exited the tunnel according to the prompt of 
the lane control light, and the trapped people evacuated from the tunnel 
followed the evacuation sign. Unlike industrial or construction situa -
tions, vehicles or passengers cannot be traced using installed or wear -
able sensors. With the recent advancement of deep learning techniques 
and computational capabilities, humans and vehicles can be identified 
and tracked, and the speed can be estimated using computer vision 
methods and surveillance videos (Hua et al., 2018). However, the 
investigation of object tracking under fire and smoke environments is 
still limited. The integration and application of millimeter wave, LIDAR, 
Ultrasound, and Infrared cameras may help to identify the object under 
low-visibility environments (Starr and Lattimer, 2014). 
The fire hazard can be quantified by the probability of occurrence 
before the fire and the severity after the fire. Before the fire, the vehicle 
type can be identified, and the motion can be tracked using computer 
vision methods. Each vehicle can be marked with different fire risk 
levels according to its vehicle type, size, and speed. The overall tunnel 
fire risk can be derived by considering the risk of each vehicle, traffic 
flow, and the tunnel environment (e.g., lighting, tunnel geometry, 
weather, etc.). The severity of tunnel fires can be evaluated by their size 
(or power), location, visibility, distribution of temperature and toxic 
gases, spread speed, etc. The fixed entities in tunnels can be used to 
detect, monitor, and distinguish the fire, and guide the trapped people to 
evacuate. For example, the fire can be detected by sensors and be 
monitored by surveillance cameras, and distinguished by sprinkler sys -
tem. Then, people can evacuate to safe places by following the dynamic 
evaluation or warning signs. 
3.2. Real-time cyber-physical interoperation 
The fire safety digital twin system in the current work has the 
capability to evolve alongside the dynamic tunnel environment by uti -
lizing collected data from sensors or cameras. This comprehensive tun -
nel fire digital twin encompasses data management processes including 
acquisition, processing, and visualization, as demonstrated in Fig. 3. 
In the proposed tunnel safety digital twin system, the data could be 
collected from the IoT sensors, cameras, and other facilities inside the 
tunnel, or data generated by simulation or AI. Sensor data mainly 
include environmental parameters such as temperature, humidity, and 
gas concentration. These parameters are important for identifying fire 
occurrence and predicting fire development. The camera data is useful 
for monitoring the fire risk and detecting fire with the help of computer 
vision technology. However, the camera will be quickly blocked by high- 
density smoke after a fire occurs. Therefore, the combination of sensor 
and camera data is more reliable. The tunnel facilities, such as the 
ventilation system, fire protection system, lighting system, and evacu -
ation facilities, are helpful in responding to fires or accidents. Therefore, 
it is necessary to monitor the operation statuses of these facilities. 
The collected data could be transmitted either through wired or 
wireless means. To ensure the reliability of the system, wired data 
transmission is better, as the data transfer speed and capacity are suf -
ficient, but the wired sensor may cost more money and be difficult to 
update after the tunnel is built. Wireless sensor networks are more and 
more popular due to their high flexibility and convenience. In fire sce -
nario, both wired and wireless sensors may be quickly destroyed by fire, 
resulting in the system being unable to complete subsequent fire 
monitoring and prediction functions. Therefore, it is important to 
enhance the system’s reliability. Some approached can be adopted to 
address the realiablity issues of sensors, including sensor redundancy, 
fire-resistant materials, advanced transmission technologies, and regu -
lar facility mantance and testing. For long tunnels, a large number of 
gateways may be needed for a short-range network like Wi-Fi and 
Bluetooth. For the data transmission protocols, MQTT, HTTP, and COAP

After the data is collected, it needs to be well stored before further 
processing and management. Database techniques are the basis for data 
storage. Although some new data storage methods, such as NoSQL and 
cloud storage, have been developed recently to handle the massive 
amount of data, the traditional relational database is still very popular. 
Structured Query Language (SQL) and relational database management 
systems (DBMS) like MySQL are widely used to manage data in different 
applications and platforms. In the present work, the MySQL database is 
used to store the sensor and simulation data. 
Data processing aims to provide valuable information and support 
critical decision-making using IoT data. In the proposed system, the raw 
data mainly include environmental sensor data, visualization data, and 
system operation data. The first step is to preprocess the data, like data 
cleaning, data compression, and reduction. Then, data analyzing 
methods, including statistical methods and neural network methods, can 
be utilized to further process the preprocessed data. This work analyzes 
the temperature data collected from sensor networks by the deep neural 
network to predict fire information like fire size and location. The sys -
tem operation data can be processed using some simple statistical 
methods to monitor the real-time object status. Data fusion with mul -
tiple features or data sources, such as CCTV cameras, and smoke and 
temperature sensors, can be adopted by mixing the data in mathematical 
models to better understand the real fire situation. 
After processing the data and obtaining the desired results, visuali -
zation technology is required to display the results. Visual analytics aims 
not only to view the data from different perspectives through data 
mining, statistics, and visualization tools but also to enable human 
interaction ( Protopsaltis et al., 2020 ). Currently, different types of 
charts, 2D/3D rendered graphics, VR/AR/MR, and Digital Twin are the 
most widely used visualization methods. In the proposed system, an 
interactive user interface, including 3D geometry and real-time charts, is 
provided to display intuitive onsite fire information and warnings of 
critical fire events. The tunnel facilities related to fire safety can also be 
controlled through the user interface by integrating the Digital Twin 
system and the facility control systems. 
3.3. Functional digital twin for tunnel firefighting 
The proposed digital twin system is designed for managing tunnel 
fire safety for its whole lifecycle and different situations. The functions 
of the system focus on four aspects, monitoring and prediction of tunnel 
fire risk, control and management of tunnel facilities, fire identification 
and forecasting, and emergency evacuation guidance, which cover 
normal situations, fire situations, and emergency evacuation situations, 
respectively. The detailed descriptions of these functions are illustrated 
as follows and are shown in Fig. 4 . 
(1) Daily tunnel transportation and fire risk monitoring. The first 
function aims to monitor the vehicles and eliminate the fire risk 
prior to the fire. In view of safety science, the actions taken before 
the accidents are most useful, although these measures are often 
overlooked. From the perspective of fire safety management, 
there are many rules or guidelines to identify and eliminate fire 
risk, but these procedures are highly dependent on the operations 
of related personnel, and human failure may occur even if they 
are well trained. As a comparison, a machine with advanced al -
gorithms and techniques can avoid human errors. It is more 
achievable and reliable to monitor and reduce fire risk using 
technology. In the proposed system, the heat or temperature 
sensors as well as the CCTV cameras, are used to monitor the fire 
risk, as they have already been installed in the most important 
transportation tunnels. 
(2) Tunnel facilities management and control. Facilities related to 
fire safety inside the tunnel should be well-managed. During the 
daily operation, the facilities ’ statuses should be monitored, 
inspected, or maintained regularly, and these facility manage -
ment data shall be updated in time and shared with the fire safety 
digital twin system. During a fire, these fire safety facilities shall 
be activated to extinguish the fire or to mitigate the fire risk 
before the fire. For example, the corresponding ventilation and 
fire protection facilities near the fire shall be automatically acti -
vated based on the detected fire information to exhaust toxic 
smoke or put out the fire. These systems can also be manually 
controlled, which is very useful during emergencies or under a 
system error. All mentioned information can be integrated into 
the proposed digital twin system to enable better tunnel fire 
safety management. 
(3) Fire identification and prediction. The digital twin system 
should provide helpful fire information to help tunnel managers 
or the fire department make better decisions. In case of a tunnel 
fire, a proper response during the fire can minimize casualties and

(4) Evacuation and rescue guidance. The system will also provide

the trapped occupants are at great risk and need to evacuate as 
soon as possible. The only information that can be easily obtained 
by evacuees to help with their evacuations are the instructions 
from the technical installations of the tunnel, such as the infor -
mation signs, lighting, and alarms. These installations have been 
proven to significantly affect the evacuation performance of 
evacuees during tunnel fires ( Zhang et al., 2021b ). The proposed 
digital twin system aims to deliver useful onsite information to 
guide the evacuation process using technical installations or fa -
cilities. These facilities can be integrated with the digital twin 
system, and the dynamic information may be given based on 
real-time fire information and other conditions. For example, 
dynamic evacuation signs based on real-time fire information 
may have a higher probability of helping evacuees survive than 
static signs. 
4. AIoT-enabled fire cognitive system 
Among these functions proposed above, real-time fire recognition 
and rendering are the most crucial during a fire, such as the fire size and 
fire location. Therefore we will focus on the development of a fire 
recognition model and real-time fire rendering platform. The fire 
recognition model is developed based on a deep learning algorithm and 
a large amount of data. The data used in the present work is generated by 
numerical simulation due to insufficient fire data from real fire or ex -
periments. This section will describe how the training dataset is gener -
ated, how the AI architecture is built, and how the platform is 
developed. 
4.1. Dataset generation from numerical simulations 
The aim of the proposed fire recognition model is to identify the 
tunnel fire scenario in real-time, including tunnel fire location and HRR 
(or fire power), which requires a comprehensive tunnel fire dataset. To 
generate enough data for training, numerical simulation is the most cost- 
effective way. Many studies have conducted numerical simulations to 
study the tunnel fire behavior or key parameters, such as the smoke 
movement ( Zhang et al., 2021a ; Hu et al., 2007 ), ventilation ( Verda 
et al., 2021 ), and the tunnel ceiling temperature distribution ( Ji et al., 
2015 ; Huang et al., 2018 ). Compared with the limited experimental 
data, numerical simulation can obtain more detailed results (e.g., tem -
perature and velocity contour), and these detailed and comprehensive 
data can be used to get conclusions that cannot be found from the 
experimental studies. Using Computational Fluid Dynamics (CFD) sim -
ulations to generate training datasets for fire prediction model training 
has also been widely demonstrated ( Hodges et al., 2019 ; Wu et al., 
2022b ). 
In the present work, the Fire Dynamics Simulator (FDS) version 6.7 
released by NIST is used to simulate different tunnel fire scenarios and 
generate training datasets. A 3D full-scale tunnel model is established 
according to the laboratory tunnel in Sichuan Fire Research Institute 
(SCFRI), which has a dimension of 140 m, 8 m, and 6 m in length, width, 
and height, respectively. Ventilation is not considered in the simulation. 
Both portals of the tunnel are set to “ Open ” and connected to the 
ambient. A square “ Burner ” with n-heptane fuel is adopted as the fire 
source. The fire is located at the center of the transverse direction but at 
different longitudinal locations in different scenarios. The constant 
HRRPUA value of 1000 kW/m
2 
is adopted, so the area of the fire source 
controls its HRR or power.

4.2. A deep learning algorithm for fire recognition 
For tunnel fire safety, deep learning methods have been used to 
identify the fire source ( Wu et al., 2021 ), predict the critical ventilation 
velocity ( Zhang et al., 2021a ) and the real-time temperature distribution 
( Wu et al., 2022b ), and forecast the transient fire scenarios ( Zhang et al., 
2022b ). In the proposed digital twin system, sensor data is input into the 
system, and fire scenarios (e.g., severity and location) could be gener -
ated. Due to the correlation between input and output is hard to acquire 
through statistical methods or empirical equations, the AI model is used 
to conduct the transformation between input and output. When a fire 
occurs, both sensors and cameras can collect the information, but 
cameras will soon be obscured by high-density smoke and then cannot 
get any useful information. By contrast, sensor data becomes more 
valuable in such a situation. Therefore, sensor data is more reliable and 
preferable for fire recognition. In the present work, a Transformer-based 
deep learning architecture is used to identify the real-time tunnel fire 
scenarios using temperature data ( Li et al., 2023 ). 
Transformer is designed to process sequential data and has been 
widely adopted in language translation ( Devlin et al., 2018 ) and com -
puter vision (CV) ( Dosovitskiy et al., 2020 ). It is a deep learning archi -
tecture based on the self-attention mechanism ( Vaswani et al., 2017 ). 
Compared with recurrent neural networks (RNN), the Transformer 
model operates by processing the entire input sequence, and the weights 
and correlations of each input data are generated using the attention 
mechanism so that less training time is required. The sensors near the 
fire source can capture more important information than other sensors, 
so greater weighs should be assigned. Therefore, the self-attention-based 
Transformer model might perform better with distributed sensor data. 
Fig. 6 demonstrates the architecture of the proposed model. The fire 
information is predicted with input sensor data. Four encoder-decoder 
layers are adopted to understand and extract information from input 
data and generate results. In the encoder-decoder layer, multi-head 
attention structure and dense layers are included. Normalization and 
inverse normalization functions are added to improve the model per -
formance and boost training speed.

4.3. Immersive fire monitoring digital twins 
This work aims to build an immersive tunnel fire digital twin based 
on the information of SCFRI tunnel, so the tunnel model has the same 
dimensions of 140 m × 8 m × 6 m, as compared in Fig. 8 . The mentioned 
tunnel has installed a ventilation system to exhaust the polluted air and 
smoke generated by fire. Inside the tunnel, there are train platform and 
an abandoned train carriage. The wall is well protected using fireproof 
materials. 
Based on the proposed framework and trained deep learning fire 
recognition model, an immersive tunnel fire monitoring digital twin is 
developed to facilitate fire risk management and real-time firefighting 
guidance. The digital twin system is built for the SCFRI tunnel with the 
same dimension and many detailed features, as demonstrated in Fig. 8 . 
The model of a 3D road tunnel and different types of vehicles are con -
structed in 3D design software SketchUp on the same scale, some non- 
essential components have been simplified to facilitate modeling and 
real-time rendering. Particle system module in the game engine Unity 3D 
is adopted to create the realistic effects of tunnel fire and smoke 
movement, which is the state-of-the-art technique to render real-time 
fire and smoke. The status of flame and smoke can be adjusted based 
on the predicted fire source. Although the physical properties of the fire 
and smoke cannot be perfectly simulated in the proposed system, the 
rendering effect looks extremely realistic, as shown in Fig. 8 (e). The 
functions and logic of the digital twin are developed in Unity 3D and 
converted into a web-based application using Web Graphics Library 
(WebGL) API to facilitate execution on different platforms. 
LAMP stack is one of the most popular open-source software stacks 
for web development and is used to establish the web-based digital twin 
platform. LAMP is the acronym for four necessary components to 
establish a fully functional web development environment: Linux, 
Apache, MySQL, and PHP. Linux is a free and open-source operating 
system that serves as an integrated development environment. Apache 
HTTP Server is used as the webserver to host the WebGL application. 
MySQL is an open-source database management system to store data.

As the physical space of the digital twin system, the real-scale tunnel 
deployed Wireless Sensor Networks (WSN) to sense and collect the 
environmental information inside the tunnel. The adopted wireless 
temperature sensor consists of four main components: ESP8266 
NodeMCU, Type K thermocouple, MAX6675 converter module, and 
Lithium-ion battery, as shown in Fig. 9 . The ESP8266 can be pro -
grammed to connect to the Wi-Fi network using Arduino code in 
Arduino Integrated Development Environment (IDE). Multiple sensors 
distributed along the tunnel can communicate with the Base Station 
directly to form a single-hop wireless network. Because the sensors can 
be destroyed under fire environments, each sensor should work inde -
pendently. To improve the battery life, the WSN is awaked at fixed in -
tervals when there is no fire, and it will continuously work when a fire is 
detected. 
Message Queuing Telemetry Transport (MQTT) protocol, with 
publish-subscribe architecture, is adapted to transmit data between 
WSN and the MQTT server. MQTT is a lightweight protocol that runs 
over TCP/IP and can achieve bidirectional data interoperation in soft 
real-time. The real-time temperature data array can be published to the 
MQTT server by the WSN, and a Python script can be used as the client to 
subscribe to the specific MQTT topics. The MQTT temperature data can 
be stored in a Database through Python script or MQTT Rule Engine. 
MQTT data can also be used to control fire facilities like ventilation and 
alarm systems by publishing control orders to the devices automatically 
or manually. After the sensor data is stored in the database, the AI model 
can read the data from the database and give predictions. The prediction 
results can also be stored in the database. The WebGL platform can 
communicate with the database to render the fire and display important 
information. 
The proposed tunnel fire digital twin is to provide an immersive 
virtual environment for different firefighting purposes, so fire rendering 
is important. According to Section 3.2 , fire location and fire size can be 
predicted by the trained AI model in real time. The fire size and fire 
location can be mapped to the digital space (WebGL) using C# language, 
and corresponding fire scenes can be rendered (e.g., fire dimension, 
temperature distribution, smoke color, and smoke density). Due to the 
digital model being designed on a 1:1 scale, the location of the fire size 
can be easily determined. The fire size or HRR can be represented by the 
dimension of the fire. The dimensions of the fire, including diameter and 
height, can be calculated using empirical equations.

5. Implementation of digital twin framework 
In this section, numerical experiments and two full-scale demon -
strations are conducted to evaluate the performance of the proposed 
Digital Twin system in real-time fire recognition. The numerical exper -
iments are conducted based on the CFD simulation data with different 
fire scenarios. Then, full-scale demonstrations are conducted based on 
real-scale tunnel fire test data. The experimental conditions and the fire 
scenarios will be introduced in the following sections. 
5.1. Numerical experiments 
Generally, it is difficult and expensive to conduct large-scale fire 
experiments. Therefore, the established digital twin platform should be 
first verified using numerical experiments. As an alternative to experi -
mental data, simulation data is input into the system to test and 
demonstrate the functions and capabilities of the system. Two sets of 
numerical tests are conducted with changing fire locations and fire sizes, 
which have not been included in the training dataset of the proposed fire 
recognition model. In real tunnel fire scenarios, the fire may grow or 
spread over time. It is significant to predict the transient tunnel fire 
scenarios. 
Firstly, two different tunnel fire scenarios are simulated using the 
FDS code, as illustrated in the previous section. The tunnel geometry, 
boundary conditions, and fuel type are kept constant in section 3.1 . The 
fire size (HRR) varies with time in the first fire scenario, which is used to 
verify the prediction capability of the model and the Digital Twin sys -
tem. The profile of the simulated fire size is shown in Fig. 10 (a). The fire 
size will grow from zero to the peak value (10 MW), remain relatively 
stable, and then decrease to zero. The second simulation case aims to 
demonstrate the ability of real-time fire location prediction, and the 
curve of fire location versus time can be seen in Fig. 10 (b). The fire 
spread is modeled by a series of striped fire vents. The “ CTRL ” function 
in FDS is adopted to sequentially control the state of numerous fire vents 
( Zhang et al., 2022b ). Both scenarios are simulated for 900 s to ensure 
the development of the fire. During the simulation, ceiling temperature 
sensor data, fire size, or fire location data are recorded per second. 
Then the temperature data are fed into the trained Transformer 
model to predict the fire size or fire location. The predicted transient fire 
size or location is displayed in Fig. 9 and compared with the simulated 
HRR or fire location. For the HRR prediction case, the model can 
accurately predict fire size changes, demonstrating the trained AI 
model ’ s capacity to predict transient tunnel fires, as shown in Fig. 10 (a). 
The AI model can also give relatively accurate results for fire spread 
prediction cases but reflect greater fluctuations, as illustrated in Fig. 10 
(b). Such results can be attributed to the simulation settings. It should be 
noted that the prediction results are poor in the beginning stage for both 
cases. It is because the simulations have not yet reached a steady state in 
the beginning, so the obtained temperature data are inaccurate. It is 
worth noting that the ventilation in tunnel fires is significant, including
