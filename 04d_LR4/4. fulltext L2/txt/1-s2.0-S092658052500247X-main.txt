ABSTRACT
Training workers in safety protocols is crucial for mitigating job site hazards, yet traditional methods often fall 
short. This paper explores integrating virtual reality (VR) and large language models (LLMs) into iSafeTrainer, an 
AI-powered safety training system. The system allows trainees to engage with trade-specific content tailored to 
their expertise level in a third-person perspective in a non-immersive desktop virtual environment, eliminating 
the need for head-mounted displays. An experimental study evaluated the system through qualitative, survey- 
based assessments, focusing on user satisfaction, experience, engagement, guidance, and confidence. Results 
showed high satisfaction rates ( > 85 %) among novice users, with improved safety knowledge. Expert users 
suggested advanced scenarios, highlighting the system's potential for expansion. The modular architecture 
supports customization across various construction settings, ensuring adaptability for future improvements.
1. Introduction
Construction sites pose considerable safety risks due to their dynamic 
nature and diverse workforce expertise levels [ 1 ]. These risks are 
particularly acute for novice workers, whose limited safety knowledge 
and experience necessitate comprehensive training programs [ 2 ]. While 
training constitutes a crucial component of workplace safety protocols, 
other environmental factors and critical interventions, such as personal 
protective equipment (PPE), administrative controls, and engineering 
controls, play a vital role in mitigating job-site hazards. While these 
measures provide essential protection, educating workers about safety 
hazards and their mitigation methods remains a fundamental require -
ment. Despite the importance of such comprehensive approaches, 
limited budgets often restrict access to robust safety training programs, 
leading companies to rely on standardized methods, such as lectures or 
video-based instruction, which fail to address individual learning needs. 
These constraints are especially problematic for high-risk trades, where 
targeted, hands-on safety training is essential [ 3 ]. Consequently, un -
skilled labor, driven by low profit margins, can cause safety incidents, 
emphasizing the need for proper safety training [ 3 , 4 ]. Traditional 
lecture-based safety training, while widely used, has proven insufficient 
for addressing on-site challenges, as it primarily relies on static materials 
like text, images, and videos that lack adaptability to real-world, high- 
risk scenarios [ 5 – 8 ].
Emerging VR and AI technologies enable immersive, personalized 
learning experiences [ 9 – 12 ], [ 12 ], [ 13 , 14 ], with Non-Playing Charac -
ters (NPCs) providing tailored instruction based on individual field de -
mands [ 15 – 18 ]. Further integrating VR with AI offers a cutting-edge 
approach to personalized training, showing promise in effectiveness 
[ 19 , 20 ]. The immersive nature of VR offers immediate feedback, making 
it an effective alternative to real-world training in hazardous scenarios. 
However, traditional computer inputs may limit the effectiveness of 
virtual reality for less skilled or literate workers [ 21 ]. AI, particularly 
Large Language Models (LLMs) like GPT-4, can address this limitation by 
creating conversational agents that enhance engagement and under -
standing while reducing cognitive load [ 19 , 22 , 23 ].
This study introduces iSafeTrainer, an LLM-integrated VR system 
that overcomes the limitations of pre-scripted scenarios through im -
mediate, conversational, and adaptive training. The system employs a 
third-person perspective in a non-immersive desktop virtual environ -
ment, viewed via a standard monitor or desktop to simulate construction 
safety training environments, reducing the risk of motion sickness and 
dizziness often associated with head-mounted displays (HMDs) [ 24 ] 
while maintaining engagement through interactive, trade-specific sce -
narios. Unlike conventional methods that require manual updates for 
new scenarios, the proposed system dynamically personalizes training

2. Literature Review
2.1. Construction safety training
Construction Safety Training: The inherent hazards in construction 
necessitate robust safety training protocols [ 1 ]. As documented by 
Mohammed et al. and Boateng et al., conventional training approaches 
utilize lectures, toolbox talks, demonstrations, and various printed ma -
terials like manuals, posters, and videos [ 5 , 8 ], [ 32 , 33 ]. While B ę ´s et al. 
point out that these methods are familiar and easy to implement, they 
can be time-consuming and may disrupt workflow [ 34 ]. Furthermore, 
these materials might not address specific job hazards or individual 
learning preferences, limiting their effectiveness. A critique by Rafin -
dadi et al. emphasizes a major drawback of traditional methods, their 
limited ability to engage learners actively and accurately reflects the 
unpredictable nature of construction sites [ 35 ]. This gap between 
theoretical knowledge and practical application can leave workers, 
particularly new ones, unprepared for real-world scenarios, as Åsgård 
et al. observed [ 36 ]. Liu et al. also noted that construction workers often 
struggle to grasp standardized safety concepts in classroom settings due 
to limited experience, which compounds the challenge of applying 
theoretical knowledge in practical situations where quick decision- 
making is crucial [ 37 ]. Kaplan et al. and Hussain et al. further high -
light this issue [ 38 , 39 ].
2.2. Advanced technologies for enhancing construction safety
Recent advancements in visualization technologies, such as VR, AR, 
and BIM, have improved construction safety training by creating 
immersive hazard simulations [ 38 , 40 ]. Akinlolu and Haupt, along with 
Afzal et al., highlight how these technologies bridge the gap between 
theoretical learning and hands-on practice, improving safety outcomes 
[ 41 , 42 ]. Zhu et al. further emphasized their role in risk-free hazard 
simulation, allowing workers to experience dangerous scenarios in 
controlled settings [ 43 ]. However, Li et al. and Wen et al. noted that 
current VR-based training often relies on pre-scripted tasks and one-size- 
fits-all content, which fail to accommodate diverse learning preferences 
and real-world variability [ 14 , 44 ]. Furthermore, these approaches lack 
adaptability to different trade specializations and fail to provide im -
mediate feedback tailored to users' expertise levels. Studies such as those 
by Xu et al. and Lee et al. have emphasized the need for dynamic, 
scenario-based training models that can personalize learning experi -
ences based on a participant's trade, skill level, and progress [ 45 , 46 ]. To 
address these challenges, researchers have attempted to integrate BIM- 
based visualization and simulation software to create more realistic 
training scenarios [ 41 , 47 ]. However, even these advanced visualization 
techniques remain constrained by pre-planned tasks that do not offer 
dynamic adaptability or user-specific adjustments [ 14 ]. Therefore, it is 
crucial to develop personalized and adaptive training programs with 
instantaneous guidance and guidance, which should be capable of 
dynamically adjusting to the progress and requirements of individual 
learners, thereby enhancing the effectiveness of safety training. In 
response, researchers have integrated LLMs into VR for adaptive 
learning [ 48 – 50 ]. Several survey studies explore the use of LLM- 
integrated agents in training environments and highlight the associ -
ated limitations [ 51 – 54 ]. AI-driven non-player characters (NPCs) in VR 
dynamically adjust instructional content and feedback, enhancing 
interactivity and training personalization [ 48 , 55 , 56 ].
Immersive learning, when combined with personalized training, 
enhances engagement, comprehension, and retention. Studies by Xu 
et al. and Lee et al. suggest that tailoring VR training to individual user 
needs creates highly effective learning experiences by reinforcing crit -
ical safety skills through repeated exposure to hazardous situations in a 
risk-free environment [ 45 , 46 ]. Unlike static VR simulations, which rely 
on fixed training modules, iSafeTrainer introduces a fully adaptive AI- 
driven NPC that adjusts training complexity in real time, based on the 
user's trade, experience level, and performance. Prior research has 
highlighted the need for greater user engagement and adaptability in 
immersive training environments. However, fully immersive systems 
that rely on head-mounted displays (HMDs) introduce discomfort, such 
as motion sickness or dizziness, particularly during extended training 
sessions [ 24 , 50 , 51 , 57 ]. To overcome these challenges, the proposed 
research employs a third-person perspective in a non-immersive desktop 
setup, allowing for trade-specific, interactive learning while maintaining 
user comfort and engagement.
Such advancements indicate a shift towards tailored, 
communication-focused safety training that aligns with real-world

3. Research methodology
This section outlines the research methodology for the proposed VR 
safety training system. Fig. 2 provides a detailed visualization of the 
iSafeTrainer system architecture, outlining the workflow and in -
teractions among its core components. After the system development, 
the user training begins with trade and knowledge selection, followed by 
prompt engineering for content generation. The AI-driven system 
dynamically adapts content based on user input, enhancing the train -
ing's relevance. Finally, practical implementations and content delivery 
are evaluated through case studies, ensuring the system's reliability and 
applicability to various scenarios. The modular and layered design of the 
system ensures scalability and adaptability.
3.1. System architecture and development
The study employed an experimental approach based on construc -
tivist learning theory to address the personalized safety training needs of 
students and workers. The system applies constructivist learning, 
encouraging active participation, guided discovery, and reflection. Users 
interact directly with the Virtual environment, making safety decisions 
rather than passively receiving information. The AI NPC supports guided 
discovery by responding dynamically to user queries and providing real- 
time hazard identification guidance. Immediate AI-driven reflective 
learning allows users to correct errors and adjust their approach, rein -
forcing knowledge retention and critical thinking. To align with this, the 
iSafeTrainer, an LLM-integrated virtual instructor, was integrated into a 
Virtual environment, providing tailored learning experience. Fig. 3
shows how participants engage with the Virtual environment, per -
forming tasks and interacting with iSafeTrainer to enhance their 
learning.
3.1.1. iSafeTrainer development
The system was developed on Unity 3D (Windows 11, Intel Core i9 
10th-gen 3.70 GHz, 16GB RAM). Unity Editor version 2021.3.16f1 was 
used to develop the iSafeTrainer system. The system featured a 3D mesh 
from a real construction site, created with Gaussian Splatting and sup -
plemented with a Sketchfab mesh, and custom virtual objects for various 
construction hazards. These 3D objects, including buildings and equip -
ment, were sourced from the Unity Asset Store or created in Blender 3.5. 
[ 66 , 67 ]. Realistic sounds of construction machinery and site noises were 
also simulated, replicating the auditory atmosphere of a real construc -
tion site.
The interface of the training system features a main menu where 
users selects their trade (Electrical, Scaffolding, or Welding) and 
knowledge level (Novice, Beginner, Expert), this selection was self- 
identified, as shown in Fig. 4 . Users can start the training by selecting 
‘Start ’ or exit using the ‘Quit ’ button. Based on their selections, they are 
guided to specific environments where hazards are marked with yellow 
arrows. iSafeTrainer is available for interaction, and hazards are high -
lighted to ensure that users can identify and discuss them. If any hazards 
are missing, the AI NPC prompts users to revisit the site and identify the 
remaining hazards, thereby assessing their knowledge and active 
learning.
The proposed research study developed VR-based training environ -
ments for electrical, welding, and scaffolding sites, providing realistic 
settings where users can identify various hazards. The third-person 
perspective in a non-immersive VR desktop setup encourages

3.1.2. LLM-integrated iSafeTrainer avatar creation
The primary objective of the proposed VR system is to incorporate an 
LLM-integrated virtual instructor, known as iSafeTrainer, to enhance the 
training experience for students and workers for interactive and 
personalized communication to deliver training content in a way that 
aligns with constructivist learning theory, which emphasizes learning 
through experience and interaction with the environment [ 64 ]. The AI 
NPC in iSafeTrainer is developed using the “ Ready Player Me ” platform, 
a comprehensive library of customizable virtual characters, to create an 
instructor role tailored to meet the specific communication needs of 
students and workers which enables more effective acquisition of safety 
knowledge [ 68 ]. The instructor is animated within the Unity gaming 
environment, utilizing humanoid animations such as idle and talk mo -
tions from the publicly accessible Mixamo library [ 69 ]. To create a more 
realistic human-like interaction, an animator component is integrated 
into iSafeTrainer, allowing for synchronized hand and leg movements 
during communication. Additional scripts control eye and head move -
ments, enabling the avatar to blink and direct its gaze towards the stu -
dent or worker (player) during dialogue.
OVR (Oculus VR (Virtual Reality)) lip synchronization from Oculus is 
used to simulate realistic mouth movements when the AI trainer speaks 
[ 70 ]. The overall process of creating the AI NPC avatar and adding 
human-like characteristics through animation is illustrated in Fig. 7 .
3.2. Practical implementations
Existing construction safety VR training programs primarily deliver 
pre-defined courses and tasks using generalized AI NPCs for 
communication-based training aimed at students and workers. Howev -
er, a more proactive approach is personalized safety training, which 
better prepares individuals for real-world construction environments 
[ 14 ]. The proposed system dynamically adjusts training complexity, 
pacing, and terminology through AI-driven NPC interactions. The sys -
tem continuously refines its responses based on real-time user input, 
allowing for a context-aware and adaptive learning experience that 
mimics human instruction. By following a linear progression of pre-set 
scenarios, iSafeTrainer leverages LLMs to interpret user queries, video 
content recommendation and generate customized training responses in 
real time.

3.2.2. AI tools integration for personalized safety training
The iSafeTrainer integrates advanced AI tools, Text-to-Speech (TTS), 
Whisper, and ChatGPT, to create an interactive, human-centered 
training system that enhances engagement and accessibility by con -
verting text into realistic audio, making it particularly valuable for 
educational and interactive applications [ 75 ]. Whisper, another AI tool 
by OpenAI, transcribes and translates voice input by tokenizing audio 
into a spectrogram and processing it through an encoder-decoder 
transformer, generating a text caption with special tokens for tasks 
like transcription and translation. [ 76 ]. When a user submits a voice 
input through the microphone, the whisper initiates a complex tokeni -
zation process, breaking down the audio into discrete units called to -
kens. The whisper architecture, implemented as an encoder-decoder 
transformer, converts the input audio into a spectrogram, which is then 
passed into an encoder. A decoder trained within the system predicts the 
corresponding text caption, enriched with special tokens that guide the 
single model to perform various tasks, including language identification, 
multilingual speech transcription, and speech translation to English or 
other languages.
First, Whisper is trained to detect speech absence and then assigns 
task-specific tokens for transcription or translation [ 74 ]. Its zero-shot 
performance across varied datasets and ability to minimize errors 
make it ideal for the transcription and translation functions of the sys -
tem. After transcription, the text is routed to ChatGPT, which generates 
responses using advanced natural language processing. However, 
ChatGPT may produce irrelevant responses, so the VR system employs 
prompt engineering with contextualization techniques to refine an -
swers. Contextualization incorporates details relevant to the construc -
tion domain, ensuring responses are tailored to participants' needs.

refined text-based response undergoes processing for text-to-speech 
conversion. The system integrates Whisper, ChatGPT, and TTS to

4. Case study and experiments
This section includes a case study example to provide users with a 
clear understanding of how to use the iSafeTrainer system. The example 
offers a brief overview of hazard interaction, mitigation strategies, and 
resource allocation within an experimental setup. 
a. Learning Setup
Upon entering the virtual environment, participants are prompted to 
select their trade i.e., Electrical, Scaffolding, or Welding and their 
knowledge level: novice, beginner, or expert. They self-identified their 
trade and expertise level based on their academic background, course -
work, or prior job-site experience. Students determined their category 
based on their involvement in relevant academic projects or coursework, 
while workers classified themselves according to their hands-on con -
struction experience. This self-assessment ensured that the training 
content aligned with each participant's actual skill level and professional 
background. The iSafeTrainer system dynamically adapts training sce -
narios based on the participant's selection. For novices, the AI NPC 
provides step-by-step guidance, interactive prompts, and visual aids to 
enhance learning. For experienced participants, the system emphasizes 
advanced problem-solving tasks and hazard mitigation strategies, 
encouraging critical thinking and real-world application of safety con -
cepts. This personalization enhances engagement and knowledge 
retention by ensuring that training remains both relevant and 
appropriately challenging.
For instance, a user selecting “ Scaffolding Safety Training ” at the 
beginner level would begin by pressing the “ Start ” button, entering a 
virtual environment populated with various hazards. These hazards are 
indicated by pop-up arrows, which serve to engage beginners and aid 
them in recognizing potential dangers. For learners unfamiliar with risk 
detection, this visual aid is crucial in fostering an intuitive learning 
experience. As a case in point, a beginner may only identify three out of 
four hazards present in the environment. Before asking about the haz -
ards the AI trainer will ask the user “ Are you wearing appropriate pro -
tective equipment such as helmets, gloves, and safety boots? ” If user 
indicates non-compliance, the AI NPC provides corrective response and 
emphasizes the importance of PPE in reducing workplace hazards. After 
that AI NPC intervenes by asking whether all hazards have been found or 
not and when the user realizes one hazard remains unidentified, the AI 
trainer offers feedback and encourages them to search for the over -
looked hazard. This active learning loop is vital for real-time engage -
ment, allowing users to reflect on their hazard detection process. If a 
user struggles to locate all the hazards, AI NPC recommends supple -
mentary video-based content to reinforce learning, it not only aids in 
safety hazard identification but also sharpens the user's mitigation skills.

b. Hazard Identification and Mitigation
In this phase, the user's interaction with specific hazards is detailed. 
For instance, Fig. 12 (a) highlights how a user identifies the “ missing 
planks ” hazard, which poses a serious fall risk. In Fig. 12 (b), the player 
observes a stack of planks nearby on the rooftop of the construction site 
and picks them up one by one to address the hazard. Finally, in Fig. 12
(c), the player successfully places the planks over the scaffold, elimi -
nating the risk of falls and mitigating the danger. Through this process, 
users enhance their critical thinking and problem-solving abilities by 
actively engaging with the hazard within the Virtual environment which 
exemplifies how users can practically apply safety knowledge and 
collaborate with the system to resolve dangerous scenarios. By 
completing these tasks, users not only learn to identify hazards but also 
gain experience in real-world mitigation strategies, promoting a deeper 
understanding of workplace safety.

6. Discussion, limitations and future work
Discussion: The integration of VR and conversational AI (LLMs) ad -
vances construction safety training methodology. This approach ad -
dresses existing limitations through adaptive training that responds to 
individual trade expertise and knowledge levels to enhance their 
learning outcomes compared to traditional one-size-fits-all methods. 
The system leverages advanced AI technologies, including Whisper for 
speech recognition and ChatGPT for interactive dialogue, creating an 
immersive and impactful learning experience. Evaluation results 
showed high levels of user satisfaction, particularly among novices, 
aligning with findings by Admi et al. (2023) that demonstrated 
improved performance in VR-based training compared to traditional 
methods. [ 84 ]. Experienced users suggested more advanced scenarios to 
enhance training depth. It is important to note that the system prototype 
was validated using unsafe scenarios specific to building projects. 
However, the system's modular architecture allows for adaptation to 
various construction contexts, such as bridges, highways, or industrial 
facilities. The Virtual environment can be customized to include rele -
vant hazards, and the AI NPC can be updated through prompts to expand 
its knowledge and applicability to other types of projects.
Limitations: Despite the system's contributions to personalized safety 
training, several limitations warrant discussion. System performance 
depends significantly on VR and AI technological capabilities. 
Challenges such as hardware performance, software stability, and the 
accuracy of natural language processing have the potential to hinder the 
overall user experience and training outcomes. Virtual environment uses 
a third-person perspective controller to avoid motion sickness but may 
limit the immersive experience compared to fully immersive systems 
like head-mounted displays. Evaluation methods in this study were 
primarily survey-based and focused on usability and satisfaction, with 
no quantitative metrics like task completion rates or retention scores 
included. These metrics are planned for future research to provide a 
more rigorous validation of the system's effectiveness. Additionally, 
external factors such as environmental conditions, participants' prior 
experience, and organizational safety culture were not accounted for, 
which may influence training outcomes. The participant pool consisted 
primarily of individuals with similar educational backgrounds in con -
struction and architecture and from a single geographic region, poten -
tially limiting the generalizability of the findings to broader populations 
or industries. Finally, while LLMs can be trained on extensive datasets, 
there may still be limitations in terms of their depth and breadth of 
knowledge in the specific domain of construction safety. Ensuring 
comprehensive and up-to-date safety expertise across all relevant do -
mains may require extensive training and curation of the language 
model. Effective safety training often requires empathy, encouragement, 
and the ability to gauge the emotional state of the user. Integrating these 
soft skills into the conversational abilities of iSafeTrainer may be chal -
lenging, as current LLMs still have limitations in emotional intelligence 
and social awareness.
Future Work: Currently, this study focuses on the initial development 
and feasibility testing of the iSafeTrainer system. While descriptive 
analysis of survey responses provides valuable insights, future studies 
will incorporate comparative and statistical analyses and additional 
evaluation metrics such as task completion rates, hazard recognition 
scores, and learning retention with advanced visualization to rigorously 
validate the system's effectiveness in diverse scenarios and across 
different user groups. Future enhancements will focus on developing 
LLM-integrated virtual instructors capable of demonstrating safety 
tasks. These intelligent agents can simulate real-world safety scenarios 
at the request of the trainees, offering interactive and informative 
demonstrations to enhance trainees' comprehension and retention of 
safety protocols. This integration not only represents a significant step 
towards enhancing safety training initiatives in the construction, 
empowering trainees with practical knowledge and skills essential for 
on-site safety but also opens new possibilities for the advancement of 
safety training methodologies, ultimately contributing to improved 
safety standards within the construction sector. Likewise, enabling the 
LLM-integrated instructor to autonomously recommend video-based 
safety training content from a web database could significantly 
enhance the customization and effectiveness of safety programs which 
align well with the trend towards automated, AI-driven solutions in 
occupational safety and health training, ensuring programs remain up to 
date with the latest safety standards and practices.
7. Conclusion
This paper introduced iSafeTrainer, a personalized construction 
safety training system integrating LLMs with a third-person perspective 
in a non-immersive desktop virtual environment. This system was 
designed to address the heightened workplace risks faced by construc -
tion workers, providing personalized, interactive, and trade-specific 
safety education. The evaluation of iSafeTrainer demonstrated its 
feasibility and user satisfaction in delivering personalized safety 
training. The system's ability to dynamically adapt to individual exper -
tise and trade requirements showcased its potential to overcome the 
limitations of traditional, static training methods. The results from 
questionnaire-based surveys provided valuable insights into usability 
and user satisfaction.
