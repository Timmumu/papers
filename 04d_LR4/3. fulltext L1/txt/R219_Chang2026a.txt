ABSTRACT
Accurately predicting ground settlement and tunnel lining deformation is essential for ensuring the safety and 
efficiency of shield tunnel projects, especially in urban areas affected by adjacent excavations. Rapid and reliable 
forecasts of tunnel behavior support proactive design decisions and risk mitigation. However, most existing 
surrogate models offer limited predictive resolution and accuracy, as they predict responses only at sparse 
monitoring points and ignore spatial information, which restricts their applicability in building comprehensive 
digital twin (DT) systems for full-field simulation. To address these limitations, this study proposes a DT 
framework for predicting ground settlement and lining deformation in shield tunnels affected by adjacent 
excavation. A hybrid surrogate model, called the Dual-Fusion Swin-Table Transformer Network (DF-STTN), is 
developed by combining Swin Transformer and TabTransformer to handle spatial and non-spatial inputs. To 
enhance multimodal interaction, a dual-stage fusion strategy is applied. The model is trained on high-fidelity 
finite element data, then updated and validated using results from laboratory-scale tunnel excavation tests. 
Results demonstrate that the inclusion of spatial information improves prediction accuracy, with notable im -
provements achieved when three measurement points of the tunnel are used. DF-STTN outperforms conventional 
convolution-based and single-fusion models in both ground settlement and lining deformation tasks. The pro -
posed framework achieves high predictive accuracy and generalization capability for early-stage tunnel design 
and excavation risk assessment.
1. Introduction
Urban development has led to a significant increase in underground 
infrastructure projects, particularly in densely populated cities where 
space is limited. One common engineering challenge in the design phase 
is planning new tunnel construction near existing operational tunnels, 
where the impact of excavation-induced ground movements must be 
carefully assessed in advance. These interactions often lead to ground 
settlement and structural deformation of the existing tunnel lining, 
which may compromise structural integrity and operational safety 
[ 25 , 42 ]. Moreover, due to geological variability, complex soil-structure 
interaction, and evolving excavation stages, conventional deterministic 
models and static empirical charts often fail to capture the full range of 
possible deformation behaviors during adjacent tunneling[ 31 ]. Reliable 
prediction of such responses during the early design stage is therefore 
critical to avoid costly remediation, ensure compliance with safety 
standards, and support decision-making in the planning and design.
Recent advancements in computational modeling and sensing tech -
nologies have introduced the concept of a digital twin (DT) 
[ 10 , 18 , 19 , 36 ]. A DT is a virtual replica of a physical system, capable of 
dynamically utilizing various data sources to simulate structural 
behavior under different scenarios[ 2 , 6 ]. In tunneling applications, a DT 
can be used during the design stage to evaluate the potential impact of 
sequential excavation on nearby structures, allowing engineers to pro -
actively assess risk and optimize design strategies. This approach en -
hances the ability to test multiple design alternatives virtually, reducing 
reliance on conservative assumptions and overdesign. Despite its po -
tential, the application of DTs in the design of underground infrastruc -
ture presents several challenges. High-fidelity numerical simulations, 
such as finite element (FE) models, are commonly used to represent 
soil – structure interaction but are computationally intensive and not 
easily adaptable when new data becomes available[ 3 , 4 , 7 , 34 ].

Additionally, the behavior of tunnels under adjacent excavation is 
highly nonlinear and dependent on spatial–temporal factors, making it 
difficult for traditional models to generalize across different construc -
tion scenarios[ 5 ]. This gap between simulation and reality highlights the 
need for lightweight and effective models that can combine simulation 
accuracy with speed to support rapid exploration of design options.
Data-driven models, particularly surrogate models, have gained 
attention in this regard. These models approximate the behavior of more 
complex simulations by learning from precomputed results or experi -
mental data, enabling faster prediction with minimal loss of accuracy 
[ 8 , 9 , 43 , 44 ]. In tunnel engineering, surrogate modeling approximates 
the nonlinear mapping between excavation/tunnel parameters and 
deformation responses, thereby reducing reliance on costly FE simula -
tions. This approach follows the fundamental principle of function 
approximation, where machine learning models serve as universal 
approximators of complex soil–structure interaction functions that are 
otherwise captured through high-fidelity but computationally expensive 
FE simulations. There are various surrogate modeling approaches 
available, such as response surface methodology [ 24 ], Kriging [ 32 ], 
regression methods [ 1 , 16 , 46 ], polynomial expansions [ 15 ], and proper 
orthogonal decomposition [ 13 , 41 ]. In general, these surrogate modeling 
methods perform effectively when the prediction task involves only a 
few output variables, such as surface settlements at selected locations or 
deformations at key points around the tunnel lining [ 22 ]. However, the 
key challenge in the context of DTs for adjacent tunneling is that reliable 
design requires capturing full-field responses across many spatial posi -
tions, excavation stages, and even different types of outputs (e.g., 
ground settlement, tunnel lining deformation). Unlike point-based pre -
dictions, which only estimate behavior at selected monitoring locations, 
full-field prediction demands a model that can represent the continuous 
deformation field of the ground and lining. In this case, traditional 
surrogate modeling approaches reveal clear shortcomings. First, they 
typically scale poorly because their formulations (e.g., polynomial ex -
pansions, response surfaces, or regression-based methods) become un -
stable or computationally prohibitive as the dimensionality of the 
problem increases. Second, they often rely on simplified or limited input 
features, which restricts their ability to incorporate diverse information 
such as soil heterogeneity, construction parameters, and spatial geom -
etry. Third, they struggle to generalize beyond the conditions repre -
sented in the training data, making them sensitive to changes in geology, 
tunnel geometry, or construction sequence [ 2 , 3 , 4 , 6 , 22 ]. To address 
these limitations, more advanced surrogate modeling techniques are 
needed. Such models would enable the development of comprehensive 
and adaptive DT frameworks capable of delivering full-field predictions 
under varying scenarios.
Multimodal data fusion has emerged as a promising approach for 
enhancing surrogate modeling in complex engineering tasks, as it en -
ables the integration of diverse data types, such as spatial coordinates, 
tabular features, and sequential information, into a unified learning 
framework [ 11 ]. By combining different modalities, models can better 
capture the intricate relationships between excavation parameters, 
geological conditions, and system responses, ultimately improving pre -
dictive performance and generalization. For example, Luo et al. [ 26 ] 
proposed a visual navigation framework combining Multimodal Graph 
Learning and Action Boost Memory to help agents navigate using only 
visual input. By dynamically modeling spatial, semantic, and historical 
information, the method significantly improves navigation success and 
efficiency in unseen environments. Yang et al. [ 38 , 39 ] proposes a 
Multimodal Knowledge-Guided Model (MKGM) for the joint recognition 
of bridge defects and structural information by integrating visual fea -
tures, knowledge graphs, and label co-occurrence through a dual-graph 
convolutional network and MambaVision. By capturing hierarchical 
semantic associations among defects, components, and structural re -
gions, the model achieves superior performance. These examples 
collectively highlight the growing potential of multimodal data fusion to 
improve model robustness, accuracy, and applicability in a wide range 
of engineering contexts.
In this paper, the DT framework is designed to provide a lightweight 
yet accurate virtual replica of adjacent tunneling processes, with the 
specific goal of predicting full-field deformation of an existing tunnel 
during new excavation. The Dual-Fusion Swin-Table Transformer 
Network (DF-STTN) serves as the methodological core of the proposed 
DT framework. This capability is realized through a hybrid Transformer- 
based surrogate modeling architecture that integrates both spatial and 
tabular modalities [ 20 , 33 ]. Transformers, originally developed for nat -
ural language processing, are chosen in this work because of their 
theoretical strength in capturing long-range dependencies through self- 
attention mechanisms. This property is particularly relevant for 
tunneling problems, where localized excavation can induce deformation 
at distant parts of the tunnel and ground surface. In contrast to 
convolution-based networks that emphasize local features, attention- 
based models dynamically assign importance weights across the entire 
input domain, making them well-suited for geotechnical systems char -
acterized by both local variability and global interactions[ 21 , 35 , 45 ]. 
Spatio-temporal models (e.g., recurrent networks, ConvLSTMs) are 
effective at learning sequential dynamics but often face challenges in 
scalability and capturing long-range spatial dependencies, which are 
essential in adjacent tunneling where excavation at one location can 
influence deformation far away [ 40 ]. Graph-based models can represent 
irregular geometries and local connectivity, yet they typically require 
complex graph construction and incur high computational costs when 
extended to dense, full-field predictions [ 14 ]. Moreover, adjacent 
tunneling problems involve two distinct types of data: (1) non-spatial/ 
tabular data, such as soil stiffness, tunnel burial depth, and lining 
thickness, which describe global system properties, and (2) spatial data, 
such as settlement and deformation fields, which capture location- 
dependent responses. By explicitly designing a dual-fusion Trans -
former architecture, this study ensures that both modalities are pro -
cessed appropriately: TabTransformer for tabular features and Swin 
Transformer for spatial grids, before being integrated in a complemen -
tary way. This design reflects the principle that predictive accuracy 
improves when models account for both global system descriptors and 
local spatial patterns. This makes them especially well-suited for ap -
plications involving high-dimensional, structured, or sequential data. 
This architecture forms the core of the proposed DT framework, sup -
porting high-resolution, multimodal prediction of tunnel behavior.
In summary, this study proposes a DT framework tailored for the 
design phase of tunnel projects involving adjacent excavation. The 
proposed approach integrates deep learning-based surrogate modeling, 
feature encoding, and model updating to construct a lightweight and 
adaptive DT capable of simulating the deformation behavior of an 
existing tunnel. The proposed framework provides practical guidance 
for adjacent tunnel design by enabling efficient full-field prediction of 
deformation and supporting risk-informed engineering decision- 
making. Key innovations of the proposed framework include: 
(1) Spatial and non-spatial information are obtained from a para -
metric FE model, where simulation outputs are converted into 
structured inputs for the surrogate model. Including spatial fea -
tures, compared to counterparts using only non-spatial data, en -
hances the ability to capture spatial positional relationships and 
improves prediction accuracy.
(2) A surrogate model, i.e., Dual-Fusion Swin-Table Transformer 
Network (DF-STTN), is developed to integrate spatial and tabular 
modalities. It employs a two-stage structure to separately predict 
ground settlement and lining deformation, improving interpret -
ability and accuracy. Spatial and non-spatial features are 
extracted using a Swin Transformer and TabTransformer, then 
fused via dual-stage fusion (early and late) for effective multi -
modal interaction.
(3) A data-driven model updating method is used to iteratively best 
estimate soil parameters by minimizing prediction errors against

(4) A laboratory-scale test, emulating real tunnel projects, is con -

the new surrogate model, ensuring the modeled system accu -

2. Methodology
2.1. Overview of the method
This study proposes a DT framework for tunnel behavior prediction 
and evaluation. The DT framework is structured in four layers (see 
Fig. 1 ): (1) Physical system layer, where a scaled tunnel test system 
represents the real-world twin; (2) Data acquisition layer, which collects 
measurements such as settlement, lining deformation, and acceleration; 
(3) Digital modeling and data fusion layers, where numerical simula -
tions and multimodal features are used to train the surrogate model (DF- 
STTN) and iteratively update soil parameters; and (4) Service and 
interaction layer, which enables prediction, assessment, and test setup 
adjustments.
Firstly, the physical twin is established using a laboratory-scale 
testbed designed to replicate tunnel excavation scenarios. The system 
includes a physical tunnel model, a data acquisition system, and a sensor 
network that captures tunnel responses including settlement, lining 
deformation, strain, soil pressure, and acceleration. These experimental 
measurements form the test dataset, which is used both for model 
validation and for updating the virtual model.
It should be clarified that, due to the data availability and setup 
flexibilities, a laboratory-scale setup is adopted in this study to establish 
the DT framework. In particular, compared to the real tunnel physical 
twin, the designed system offers more flexibilities of setup configura -
tions, e.g., tunnel diameters and inter-tunnel distances, and provides 
more comprehensive data collection schemes in the lab. Furthermore, 
the proposed methodology is not limited to such lab test configuration, 
which can be further extended and applicable to real tunnels in full-field 
applications, if available.
Secondly, a high-fidelity FE Model is constructed to simulate tunnel 
responses under varying design and soil conditions. This includes 
parametric modeling where multiple input variables (e.g., soil proper -
ties, tunnel geometry) are systematically varied, verification of simula -
tion results, and generation of a comprehensive dataset comprising over 
1000 samples. Key outputs from the simulations, such as ground set -
tlement and lining deformation, are used to build the training dataset for 
the surrogate model.
Thirdly, a deep learning-based surrogate model is developed to 
replace the computationally intensive FE model simulations. This model 
includes both non-spatial (design parameters and soil properties) and 
spatial (a distance matrix representing spatial relationships between 
tunnel segments and soil) input features. To maintain accuracy under 
real-world conditions, a model updating module is integrated. It uses

seamlessly combining physical testing, numerical simulation, and deep

2.2. Data feature extraction
The input data used for surrogate model training includes both non- 
spatial and spatial information extracted from the FE simulation results. 
Two distinct types of spatial information, ground settlement and lining 
deformation, are extracted separately to construct two surrogate 
models, each tailored to its respective output type. Two types of feature 
information are illustrated as follows.
Non-spatial information : Non-spatial features consist of scalar 
input parameters that describe the tunnel design and soil conditions. 
These include soil mechanical properties (e.g., stiffness, friction angle, 
cohesion), tunnel geometric parameters, and construction-related inputs 
such as burial depth or cover thickness. These parameters are directly 
extracted from the FE model input.
Spatial information : Spatial information captures the geometric 
and positional context of deformation responses in the tunnel system. It 
is essential for learning location-dependent patterns in both ground and 
lining behavior. Fig. 2 illustrates the procedures of spatial information 
extraction.
Table 1 summarizes input and output variables for the surrogate 
model. Two distinct types of spatial data are extracted from the FE 
model outputs: ground settlement features and lining deformation fea -
tures. For each case, the input is structured as a 224 × 224 × 3 grid, 
where each spatial point includes three components representing rela -
tive distances between the surface (or lining) grid points and predefined 
tunnel measurement points. The 224 × 224 grid resolution was chosen 
as a balance between spatial detail and computational efficiency. 
Correspondingly, each case has a single-channel 224 × 224 target 
output, which is the interpolated deformation field (ground settlement 
or lining deformation) derived from the FE simulation. This output 
serves as the prediction target for the surrogate model.
(a) Ground settlement spatial information
Ground settlement features represent the distance features on the 
surface above the tunnel, influenced by tunnel geometry and location. 
These features are organized on a uniform grid to reflect spatial varia -
tions in surface location. To prepare this data for model input, the

spatial information: 
Step 1: Grid extraction: The ground surface information from the FE 
model is extracted and projected onto a surface grid. Due to sym -
metry, only half the domain is considered, and the data is mirrored as 
necessary.

interpolated onto a uniform grid of size 224 × 224 to ensure con -

2.3. Surrogate modeling method
To approximate the outputs of computationally expensive FE model 
simulations, a surrogate model that combines spatial and tabular mo -
dalities through the DF-STTN is developed (see Fig. 4 ). The proposed DF- 
STTN is grounded in the fundamental principle of function approxima -
tion, where deep neural networks serve as universal approximators that 
can map complex nonlinear relationships between input parameters (e. 
g., soil stiffness, tunnel geometry, depth) and output deformation re -
sponses. Its domain-specific superiority lies in its alignment with phys -
ical deformation behavior. In the context of adjacent tunneling, these 
nonlinear mappings represent the physical relationship between 
excavation-induced stress redistribution and resulting ground and lining 
deformation. The model ’ s dual-modal fusion mechanism enables 
simultaneous learning of global structural effects, captured through 
features like tunnel geometry, depth, and soil properties, and localized 
spatial interactions, represented by 2D spatial grids that reflect how 
deformation propagates around tunnels and interfaces. Additionally, the 
cross-modal fusion module captures correlations between input pa -
rameters and localized deformation responses, consistent with estab -
lished geotechnical principles where stiffer soils or deeper tunnels 
constrain settlement.

Due to the limited availability and high cost of obtaining compre -

reducing the reliance on extensive physical testing. Although the lab-

scale effects and simplified boundary conditions. To mitigate these is -
sues, measures such as careful material selection, similarity criteria, and 
cross-verification with FE outputs are implemented to enhance the

3.2.2. Model updating and analysis
To enhance the reliability of the baseline surrogate model, a model 
updating framework was established based on back analysis. In this 
study, soil parameters were chosen as the focus of the update process 
because they have a significant impact on ground deformation and are 
often highly uncertain in practice. Due to the limited availability of test 
data, the back analysis was conducted on a subset of specific monitoring 
points, as shown in Fig. 6 .
GA was employed as the optimization method due to its global search 
capabilities and robustness against local minima. Other parameters, 
such as design variables (e.g., tunnel geometry and tunnel location) and 
spatial information, were considered known and kept constant during 
the optimization process to isolate the influence of soil properties. The 
GA settings used in this study included a population size of 50, a 
crossover rate of 0.8, a mutation rate of 0.1, and a maximum of 100 
generations. These parameters were selected based on empirical testing 
to balance convergence speed and global search performance. The 
fitness function was defined as MSE between the predicted and observed 
settlement at selected monitoring points. As shown in Fig. 12 , both the 
best and mean fitness values rapidly decreased over generations, 
demonstrating effective convergence of the GA. The best fitness value 
stabilized after approximately 20 generations, indicating that the model 
successfully identified a set of soil parameters yielding high predictive 
accuracy. The resulting predictions and corresponding true values are 
summarized in Fig. 12 . The best individual identified by the GA corre -
sponds to an MSE of 0.9319. The predicted values closely match the 
measured settlements at all selected positions, validating the effective -
ness of the model updating procedure. This approach allows for the 
refinement of soil parameters in data-scarce conditions, thereby 
improving the fidelity of numerical simulations based on the trained DL 
model.
