ABSTRACT
Fatigue cracks are prone to initiate in rib-to-deck (RTD) weld joints of orthotropic steel decks (OSD) under traffic 
loading, posing a serious threat to functionality and safety of bridge structures. To achieve accurate fatigue crack 
life prediction of RTD weld joints of OSD, a digital twin-based multiscale fatigue crack life prediction framework 
from microcrack initiation and propagation to macrocrack propagation is proposed. Eccentric tensile fatigue tests 
as a physical entity are first conducted to obtain test data on fatigue crack propagation. A multiscale crack 
propagation model coupling crystal plasticity finite element model with fracture mechanics is then established as 
a virtual entity to describe microcrack initiation and propagation as well as macrocrack propagation. A dynamic 
Bayesian network integrated with a particle filter algorithm is finally introduced to fuse the test data from the 
physical entity with the numerical results from the virtual entity, enabling real-time updating of key model 
parameters and crack propagation states as well as quantitative uncertainty assessment and digital twin con -
struction. The results from this study demonstrate that the proposed digital twin effectively suppresses model 
uncertainty and significantly improves the prediction accuracy of crack propagation process and remaining fa -
tigue life.
1. Introduction
Orthotropic steel decks (OSD) have been widely adopted in long- 
span bridges owing to their favorable mechanical performance. Never -
theless, under long-term traffic loading, rib-to-deck (RTD) weld joints of 
OSD are highly susceptible to fatigue cracks and compromise the func -
tionality and safety of bridge structures [1] . Therefore, an accurate fa -
tigue life prediction of RTD weld joints of OSD is of great significance, as 
it will facilitate the early identification of potential fatigue damage and 
provide a scientific basis for condition assessment and maintenance 
strategies during the bridge operation.
The fatigue life of RTD weld joints can generally be divided into the 
stages of microcrack initiation, microcrack propagation, and macro -
crack propagation. Among these stages, the microcrack initiation and 
propagation usually account for a dominant proportion of the total fa -
tigue life. The microcrack initiation and propagation are primarily 
determined by the microstructural characteristics of the material. The 
welded joints are heterogeneous, consisting of weld metal, heat-affected 
zone (HAZ), and base metal. Previous studies have shown that both weld 
metal and base metal can exhibit significant cyclic plasticity under fa -
tigue loading conditions [ 2 ]. The HAZ has been widely reported as a 
preferential region for plastic strain concentration [3] . Even when the 
nominal stress is below the macroscopic yield strength of the material, 
fatigue crack initiation is primarily governed by localized cyclic plastic 
deformation at stress concentration regions rather than uniform plastic 
deformation, as local stress-strain incompatibilities at geometric dis -
continuities or stress concentration regions can induce such localized 
deformation, promoting the formation of slip bands and initiating fa -
tigue microcracks [4] . Under cyclic loading, the welded joints exhibit 
typical mixed-mode crack characteristics, in which both normal and 
shear stress components act simultaneously at the crack tip. The tran -
sition from microcrack initiation and propagation to macrocrack prop -
agation is associated with the evolution of the local crack tip stress field 
and the progressive accumulation of damage [5] . During cyclic loading, 
microcracks propagate and coalesce. Once the local stress intensity 
factor or energy release rate reaches critical threshold, crack propaga -
tion proceeds in accordance with fracture mechanics principles [6] .

[7,8] . At the microscale, the material in the weld zone is significantly 
affected by welding heat input, resulting in complex grain size, 
morphology, and orientation distributions which play a critical role in 
the microcrack initiation and propagation [9] . Zhou et al. [10] per -
formed numerical simulation of fatigue microcrack initiation and 
propagation in weld zone based on crystal plasticity finite element 
model (CPFEM) implemented in ABAQUS. CPFEM explicitly accounts 
for grain randomness, thereby enabling effective simulation of the 
microscale mechanical response of materials under cyclic loading and 
capturing localized stress-strain states. At the macroscale, the S-N curve 
method is a commonly used approach for fatigue life assessment but 
cannot be used to simulate the crack propagation process. The fracture 
mechanics-based methods, and damage mechanics-based methods are 
often used to estimate macrocrack propagation [11] . Among these, 
fracture mechanics-based methods can describe crack propagation 
behavior with relatively higher efficiency and accuracy than other two 
methods. Luo et al. [12] combined ABAQUS and Franc3D to establish a 
macrocrack propagation model for steel bridges based on fracture me -
chanics theory, through which the propagation behavior of macrocrack 
in RTD weld joints were systematically investigated.
However, the application of CPFEM in numerical simulations re -
quires solving complex nonlinear constitutive relations at each inte -
gration point, which leads to a high computational cost. Consequently, 
this method is generally applicable only to the stages of microcrack 
initiation and early propagation, and it is difficult to simulate crack 
propagation at macroscale. In contrast, the fracture mechanics-based 
methods have difficulty in accurately characterizing the mechanical 
behavior of materials subjected to pronounced plastic deformation. The 
predictions are highly sensitive to the assumed initial crack size, thereby 
limiting their predictive capability during the microcrack propagation 
stage. Despite these limitations, fracture mechanics-based methods can 
simplify complex crack propagation phenomena into relationships 
determined by a limited number of key model parameters, thereby 
enabling the computationally efficient modeling of macrocrack propa -
gation. Therefore, it is necessary to establish a multiscale numerical 
model to achieve accurate prediction of the entire fatigue crack propa -
gation process from microcrack initiation and propagation to macro -
crack propagation while maintaining computational efficiency.
It is also noted that in the prediction of fatigue crack propagation 
process using either CPFEM or fracture-mechanics-based methods, the 
results always exhibit a strong dependency on key model parameters. On 
the other hand, adopting a fixed set of key model parameters without 
dynamic updating often fails to fully capture the inherent randomness 
and uncertainties of crack propagation observed during fatigue tests. As 
a key emerging technology, digital twin (DT) is capable of efficiently 
fusing multi-source test data with high-fidelity numerical simulation 
models, characterized by continuous evolution and adaptive updating 
capabilities. Owing to its advantages in real-time perception, online 
parameter updating, and life-cycle management, DT provides a novel 
technical pathway for the intelligent operation and maintenance of 
bridge structures [13] . Specifically, Zeng et al. [14] proposed a 
sequential DT framework that integrates corrosion information and load 
data of concrete beams to construct multi-level DTs for parameter 
optimization, achieving high prediction accuracy. Lao et al. [15] utilized 
DT to fuse microcrack characterization data with CPFEM to dynamically 
eliminate model uncertainties, thereby realizing the fatigue life pre -
diction of weld microcracks. Cheok et al. [16] established a DT based on 
strain interfaces, combining Bayesian inference and Gaussian process 
regression to achieve real-time diagnosis of weld crack propagation and 
remaining life prediction. Peng et al. [17] developed a data-driven DT 
method using dynamic Bayesian networks(DBN) for online updating of 
crack propagation parameters, which, coupled with the weight function 
method, enabled high-accuracy fatigue life prediction.
This study therefore proposes a digital-twin based multiscale nu -
merical model to achieve accurate prediction of the entire fatigue crack 
propagation process from microcrack initiation and propagation to 
macrocrack propagation of RTD weld joints while maintaining compu -
tational efficiency. The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. 
Section 2 presents the overall framework of the digital twin-based fa -
tigue crack propagation and life prediction approach. Section 3 briefly 
introduces the design of eccentric tensile specimens and describes the 
corresponding fatigue test program and the obtained test data. Section 4
details the development of the virtual entity, which is established based 
on a multiscale crack propagation model combining microcrack and 
macrocrack propagation models. Section 5 elaborates on the construc -
tion of the DT using a DBN, where test data and multiscale numerical 
simulation results are fused to update key model parameters. Section 6
presents the prediction results of crack propagation process and 
remaining fatigue life of the test specimens based on the established DT. 
Finally, Section 7 summarizes the main conclusions of this study.
2. A framework for digital twin-based approach
A DT normally consists of four interrelated components [18] : (a) a 
physical entity and its test data acquisition; (b) a virtual entity and its 
simulation data; (c) the DT construction through data fusion; and (d) the 
application and prediction of the DT. Specifically, the DT proposed in 
this study integrates fatigue test data and multiscale numerical simula -
tion results within a DBN framework, by which fatigue crack states are 
inferred, estimated, and updated online and thereby the real-time 
updating of the entire fatigue crack propagation and the probabilistic 
prediction of remaining fatigue life are realized. The overall framework 
of the proposed DT is illustrated in Fig. 1 , and its key components are 
described as follows:
(a) Physical entity and test data acquisition: A novel eccentric tensile 
specimen is designed and fabricated to simulate the realistic stress state 
of RTD weld joints. Systematic fatigue tests are conducted with 
continuous monitoring of fatigue crack propagation process. Crack 
length is obtained and serves as reliable observation information for step 
(c).
(b) Virtual entity and simulation data: Based on the fatigue tests of 
the eccentric tensile specimens, a multiscale numerical model for crack 
propagation is established as a virtual entity. The virtual entity simulates 
the crack initiation and propagation process and provides simulation 
results, which include crack propagation paths, propagation rates, and 
stress–strain fields and serve as effective prior state information for step 
(c).
(c) DT construction and data fusion: Within the DBN framework, a 
particle filter (PF) algorithm is introduced to perform system inference 
and updating. Test data is fused with multiscale simulation results to 
achieve online estimation of state variables and key model parameters 
associated with fatigue crack initiation and propagation. Information 
exchange between the physical and virtual entities is realized by 
continuously updating the prior distributions of the parameters, 
enabling DT construction.
(d) Application and prediction: The established DT is employed to 
predict the microcrack initiation and propagation and macrocrack 
propagation processes and obtain the probabilistic distributions of 
remaining fatigue life of the RTD weld joints.

3.2. Fatigue tests
Two loading conditions were considered in this study with a nominal 
loading stress amplitude of 50 MPa and 60 MPa, respectively, and three 
fatigue test specimens were used for each loading condition. The fatigue 
tests were performed using a WANCE HDT255B electro-hydraulic servo 
control fatigue testing machine. Eccentric tensile loading was applied 
through rigidly connected rectangle steel plates at both ends of the 
specimen, thereby subjecting the RTD weld joint to a combined tensile- 
bending stress state. The test setup is shown in Fig. 5 . To accurately 
measure crack length, the beach marking technique was employed to 
record the fatigue crack propagation path during the fatigue tests [23] .

was set to 0.1, while that of the beach marking loading R was set to 0.5), 
different energy inputs are introduced during the loading stages [24] . 
This results in variations in crack propagation rates, producing distinct 
propagation fronts along the crack path. These fronts appear as alter -
nating bright and dark bands on the fracture surface (i.e., beach marks) 
[25] , which visually represent the crack propagation trajectory. Prior to 
the formal tests, preliminary tests were conducted to determine suitable

3.3. Test results
During the fatigue tests, surface cracks on the specimens were 
monitored using an optical microscope. After fatigue fracture, the 
fracture surface was imaged using an optical microscope, and the typical 
fracture surface morphology is shown in Fig. 7 . The fracture surface can 
be clearly divided into three characteristic zones: the prefabricated 
crack zone, the stable crack propagation zone, and the final instanta -
neous brittle fracture zone. For all specimens, fatigue cracks were 
initiated from the prefabricated circular hole defect, with one micro -
crack forming on each side of the hole. Owing to factors such as 
machining tolerances of the prefabricated defect and deviations from 
ideal loading, variations were observed in the crack initiation positions, 
propagation paths, and final crack morphologies. The cracks propagated 
along the plate thickness direction, and an evident crack-front coales -
cence phenomenon was observed, eventually resulting in an approxi -
mately semi-elliptical crack shape [28] . When the crack depth reached 
approximately 10 mm, the specimen entered the stage of final instan -
taneous brittle fracture, during which the remaining fatigue life was 
significantly reduced, ultimately leading to brittle fracture.
The test results are summarized in Fig. 8 . The crack length prior to 
crack coalescence is defined as the lengths of the two independent 
cracks, excluding the diameter of the prefabricated circular hole defect. 
After crack coalescence, the crack length is defined as the overall length 
of the merged crack formed after coalescence, which includes the 
diameter of the prefabricated circular hole defect. The crack coalescence 
refers to the process in which initially independent microcracks nucle -
ated on both sides of the prefabricated hole propagate under cyclic 
loading. As the cracks propagate, their stress fields gradually overlap 
and interact, eventually merging into a larger main crack. This phe -
nomenon typically occurs when adjacent cracks become sufficiently 
close so that the material ligament between them is eliminated, resulting 
in the formation of a unified crack front. It can be observed that both 
crack length and crack depth exhibit nonlinear propagation with 
increasing loading cycles. Under the nominal stress amplitude of 60 
MPa, the crack propagation rate is significantly higher than that under 
50 MPa. Meanwhile, a certain degree of scatter is observed in the crack 
propagation behavior among different specimens, which is mainly 
attributed to material microstructural heterogeneity, geometric toler -
ances of the prefabricated circular hole, and measurement uncertainties.

4.2. Microcrack initiation and propagation stage
4.2.1. Fatigue life of microcrack initiation and propagation stage
For RTD weld joints subjected to cyclic loading, transgranular frac -
ture is prone to occur within persistent slip bands inside individual 
grain. The activated slip planes gradually evolve into critical planes, 
leading to damage within the polycrystalline microstructure and ulti -
mately triggering the microcrack initiation. It is often assumed that 
microcrack initiation occurs in the grain exhibiting the highest level of 
damage. With continued cyclic loading, the crack propagates along 
grain boundaries or active slip planes into adjacent grains, preferentially 
activating crystallographic slip systems in grains adjacent grain, and 
subsequently entering next grain until the crack penetrates the entire 
polycrystal part.
Based on the physical description of microcrack initiation and 
propagation, the corresponding crack propagation criteria and numeri -
cal simulation model can be established to quantitatively characterize 
the initiation and propagation behavior of microcracks within poly -
crystalline materials. The microcrack initiation and propagation in 
polycrystalline materials require appropriate damage criteria for effec -
tive control. Fatigue indicator parameter (FIP) has been widely 
employed in fatigue analysis of metallic materials [32 – 34] . In CPFEM- 
based fatigue life prediction, FIPs are commonly introduced using the 
plastic stress amplitude and plastic strain amplitude calculated on 
individual slip systems within grains, serving as criteria for microcrack 
initiation and propagation. The Gibbs free energy criterion proposed by 
Fine and Bhat [35] provides an effective framework for multiscale 
characterization of fatigue behavior in materials. At the microstructural 
scale, the accumulated distortion energy between grains is regarded as 
the dominant driving force controlling microcrack initiation and prop -
agation. Under cyclic loading, persistent dislocation slip occurs along 
slip bands within crystals, while grain boundaries hinder dislocation 
motion, leading to gradual dislocation accumulation inside grains and 
the formation of localized energy concentrations. When the accumu -
lated energy reaches a critical threshold comparable to the variation in 
Gibbs free energy, fatigue microcracks begin to initiate.

4.2.2. Numerical simulation based on CPFEM
The modeling type of CPFEM generally includes 2D model and 3D 
representative volume element (RVE) model. Although 2D model offers

performed on isolated 3D RVEs are insufficient to capture the stress- 
strain states of specimens under realistic boundary conditions. Never -

macroscale specimen model enables a more accurate representation of

tained. Subsequently, a MATLAB script is employed to integrate the “ . 
stess ” file with the initial “ .inp ” file generated in Step (1), assign random 
grain orientations to each grain, and define uniform crystal plasticity 
material parameters. The crystal plasticity material parameters are 
adopted from the authors' previous study, in which they were identified 
using a DT framework coupled with a particle swarm optimization al -

μ m using C3D8 elements, and the CPFEM-based microcrack propagation 
model is generated.
(3) Model coupling and computational preparation: The generated 
polycrystal part is imported into ABAQUS and coupled to the initial

“ crystal-left ” and “ crystal-right, ” to facilitate automated crack path

simulations.
After the model is established, the numerical simulations of micro -
crack initiation and propagation are conducted following the flowchart 
shown in Fig. 11 , which consists of crack initiation analysis, single-step 
propagation update, and cyclic iterative computation. The detailed 
computational procedures are described as follows:
(1) Crack initiation analysis: After the first CPFEM simulation, post- 
processing is performed using a Python script developed via ABAQUS 
secondary development to extract the stress-strain status fields in the 
weld zone and compute the PSED for each grain. Automatic identifica -
tion is performed for both “ crystal-left ” and “ crystal-right, ” and the grain 
exhibiting the maximum PSED value is selected as the starting point for 
crack propagation. The two grains are recorded using the sets “ crack- 
left-01 ” and “ crack-right-01 ” , respectively. Their crack length, depth, 
and corresponding PSED values are extracted as initial conditions for 
subsequent crack propagation analysis.
(2) Single-step propagation update: To represent the state in which 
the crack has already propagated and to avoid numerical non -
convergence caused by element deletion, the equivalent stiffness of the 
cracked grain set “ crack-i ” is reduced to 1% of its original value. 
Meanwhile, the stiffness of the adjacent grain set “ crack-i + 1 ” , located 
ahead of the crack front grain set “ crack-i ” , is reduced to 10% of its 
original value to reflect the initial damage state near the crack tip and 
improve computational convergence. This stiffness degradation method 
provides an equivalent representation of local load-carrying capacity 
reduction induced by crack propagation, rather than explicitly modeling 
geometric cracks. After stiffness degradation, the next CPFEM simula -
tion is performed, and the updated stress-strain fields are extracted, 
while the grain information in “ crack-i + 1 ” as well as the crack ge -
ometry and corresponding PSED values are automatically recorded.
(3) Cyclic iterative computation: Step (2) was repeated iteratively 
until the crack length reached the micro-macro transition size defined in 
Section 4.3 , yielding the complete PSED evolution under a given random 
grain distribution.

4.3. Micro-macro transition
In fatigue crack propagation simulation, the transition size between 
microcrack and macrocrack is a key parameter that significantly in -
fluences the accuracy of fatigue life prediction. This transition size not 
only marks the end of the microcrack propagation stage but also rep -
resents the beginning of stable macrocrack propagation. Therefore, its 
determination plays a critical role in the predictive performance of the 
crack propagation model. It should be noted that the physical signifi -
cance of this transition size is not merely determined by the artificially 
prescribed crack length, but it is closely associated with the micro -
structural characteristics of polycrystalline materials. Owing to the 
pronounced randomness in grain size and spatial distribution, the 
microcrack typically needs to traverse multiple grains before forming a 
macrocrack. The cumulative length of cracked grain sizes cannot be 
strictly associated with a fixed deterministic value. Consequently, sim -
ply defining the micro-macro transition size as a constant may fail to 
correspond to the actual accumulated grain sizes and may not 
adequately capture the actual transition from microcrack to macrocrack 
behavior under different microstructural, thereby potentially intro -
ducing systematic errors in fatigue life prediction.
With the continuous improvement in the numerical accuracy and 
stability of CPFEM-based crack propagation simulations, together with 
advances in computational resources, the microscale simulation of 
relatively long crack propagation processes has become increasingly 
feasible. Based on these considerations, the micro-macro transition size 
is treated as a random variable that varies with the underlying micro -
structure in this study, with its specific value determined by the cumu -
lative size of several adjacent grains along the crack propagation path. 
Although the transition size corresponding to different random poly -
crystalline structures is not identical, its statistical distribution consis -
tently fluctuates around a characteristic length of approximately 4 mm. 
The transition size of 4 mm is defined as a representative micro-macro 
transition length based on a comprehensive consideration of both 
computational efficiency and physical rationality [40] . In fatigue test, a 
circular hole defect with a diameter of 2 mm is introduced, from which

By parameterizing the micro-macro transition size as a random

generalizability of fatigue life prediction results while maintaining 
physical consistency. As shown in Fig. 13 , these are 1000 randomly

4.4. Macrocrack propagation stage
Once microcrack propagation penetrates the polycrystal part, the 
crack propagation behavior is no longer primarily determined by indi -
vidual grain orientations or microscale slip mechanisms. Instead, it 
gradually transitions into a macroscale fracture process controlled by 
the global geometry of the component, loading conditions, and the SIF at 
the crack tip. At this stage, the crack can be regarded as a macroscale 
defect embedded in a continuous medium, and its propagation behavior 
can be described using LEFM.

The uncertainties associated with physical entity mainly originate 
from two aspects. One aspect is that the manufacturing process itself is 
inherently stochastic. During the specimen fabrication process, toler -
ances inherent in the cutting procedure and the drilling of a pre -
fabricated circular hole led to a dispersed distribution of initial crack

monitoring capabilities also constitute a significant source of uncer -
tainty. Owing to environmental noise and sensor accuracy constraints, 
measurement errors are inevitably embedded in the monitoring data. In 
contrast, the uncertainties of the virtual entity primarily come from 
model systematic deviations. Numerical simulation models are essen -
tially idealized and simplified representations of real physical processes, 
and various approximations and assumptions are inevitably introduced 
during model construction. These include stochastic deviations in 
microstructure generation algorithms, idealized assumptions in bound -
ary condition, and simplified treatments of key parameters such as the 
SIF during numerical simulation calculation. Collectively, these factors 
lead to discrepancies between the virtual entity responses and the actual 
physical entity behavior.
The superposition of these multi-source uncertainties can signifi -
cantly compromise the accuracy of crack propagation simulations. 
Therefore, it is necessary to employ data fusion techniques to compre -
hensively integrate information from different sources and at different 
levels and to achieve effective coupling between physical entity data and 
virtual entity data through appropriate algorithms, thereby alleviating 
the influence of uncertainties on the DT establishment process [44] . 
Through data fusion, the DT can more accurately reflect the actual state 
of the physical entity during its dynamic evolution, enhancing both the 
accuracy and credibility of its predictive results and providing a more 
reliable basis for engineering assessment and decision-making. 
Accordingly, this study introduces a DBN framework in combination 
with a PF algorithm to perform dynamic updating of the crack propa -
gation process across its two distinct propagation stages.
5.1. Dynamic Bayesian networks
DBN is a class of probabilistic graphical models designed to charac -
terize stochastic processes evolving over time. Owing to its strong 
capability in handling nonlinear and non-Gaussian uncertainties, as well 
as its support for online updating of both model parameters and system 
states, DBN has been widely applied in DT framework [45] . Within the 
DBN framework, heterogeneous information derived from fatigue tests 
and numerical simulations can be effectively fused. This fusion enables 
real-time correction of both model parameters and state estimates as 
new observational data become available. Consequently, the crack 
propagation states can be continuously updated and predicted, thereby 
significantly improving the accuracy of fatigue life prediction [46] .
Essentially, DBN is constructed by unfolding a static Bayesian 
network along the temporal dimension to form a sequential probabilistic 
model. Its fundamental assumptions include first-order Markov property 
and temporal stationarity, meaning that the system state at the current 
time depends only on the state at the previous time step and is inde -
pendent of earlier historical states. Within the DBN framework, the 
crack propagation process can be abstracted as a stochastic dynamic 
system in which the hidden state evolves over time. Specifically, the 
crack state variables are defined as latent states to describe the evolution 
behavior of cracks under cyclic loading, while the corresponding test 
measurements are treated as observation variables that provide con -
straints for the estimation and correction of the latent states. By estab -
lishing a state transition model and an observation model, the DBN can 
systematically describe the temporal dependency between hidden crack 
states and observational information during the crack propagation 
process. The overall schematic structure of the DBN employed in this 
study is illustrated in Fig. 17 .

rather than strict physical representations of fatigue crack propagation.

behavior, the current crack state is considered to implicitly contain 
the cumulative effects of prior loading history, making the first-order 
Markov assumption a practical simplification. The temporal statio -
narity assumption is mainly applicable to conditions involving piece -
wise constant loading or slowly varying statistical characteristics. In 
addition, the observation-based sequential updating mechanism incor -
porated in the model allows parameters to be continuously updated as 
new data become available, which partially accounts for historical ef -
fects and improves the adaptability of the model.
5.2. PF algorithm
Since the hidden states in DBN evolve dynamically over time and are 
updated in a recursive manner, their posterior distributions can only be 
progressively refined based on the currently available observations. 
Consequently, during the iterative processes of state estimation and 
parameter updating, it is necessary to introduce efficient filtering algo -
rithms to achieve online fusion of historical information, thereby 
avoiding explicit enumeration of the complete state sequence and 
effectively reducing computational complexity while improving infer -
ence efficiency. Among various filtering algorithms, the PF algorithm 
has been widely adopted within the DBN framework for state inference 
and updating problems [47] . This is primarily attributed to its strong 
capability to approximate
