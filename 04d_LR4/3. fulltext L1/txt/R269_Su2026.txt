Abstract
This study addresses the challenges of real-time response and closed-loop decision-
making in smart construction. We propose a dual-loop collaborative framework 
integrating Building Information Modeling (BIM), the Internet of Things (IoT), and 
LSTM-Attention networks, validated via a 261-sensor deployment in the Taipei Bridge 
and Tunnel Joint Project. The architecture achieves sub-second synchronization and 
enables multi-objective optimization using NSGA-II. Experimental results demonstrate 
statistically significant improvements ( p < 0.001): a 46.2% reduction in system latency, 
a 41.4% decrease in safety incidents, an 18.5% reduction in direct costs, and an 
11.1% decline in CO₂ emissions, while maintaining a 99.1% safety compliance rate. 
The framework successfully bridges the gap between digital models and physical 
realization, providing a scalable solution for adaptive and sustainable construction 
management.
Keywords Smart construction, Digital twin, BIM-IoT, LSTM-attention, Multi-objective 
optimization
 1 Introduction
Intelligent construction is undergoing a key transformation from “endpoint perception” 
to “overall collaboration” [ 1]. This transformation is critically important for the con -
struction industry, which not only contributes about 40% of the global carbon emissions 
[2], but also faces severe challenges such as high energy consumption, low efficiency and 
frequent safety accidents [ 3, 4]. Although the deep integration of building information 
modeling (BIM), Internet of Things and artificial intelligence is regarded as the core path 
to improve the transparency of construction and the agility of decision-making [ 5], and 
promotes the evolution from “one-way monitoring” to “digital twin collaboration” [ 1], 
practical problems such as sensor noise, model heterogeneity and decision-making delay 
remain prominent.
Taking the Taipei Bridge and Tunnel Joint Project in 2024 as an example, although 
more than 260 sensors were deployed on site, the average delay of BIM updating and 
real-time decision-making exceeded 13 s [ 3]. Moreover, the manual recheck during the 
peak hours of crane operation will increase the delay by 5 to 7  s, which will seriously 
endanger the “golden window period” of safety intervention [6], highlighting the defects

of one-way data flow in fast-paced construction [ 1, 7]. Existing research has several core 
limitations, including a lack of real-time capability, limited attention to end-to-end delay, 
packet loss compensation, and prediction algorithms [ 3, 8]. At the same time, there 
are issues such as insufficient closed-loop integrity, with control strategies mostly rely -
ing on manual scheduling or static rules, and a lack of adaptive “prediction-execution-
feedback” mechanisms [5, 9]. In addition, cost, schedule and safety objectives are often 
optimized in isolation, lacking unified indicators and dynamic trade-off analysis, which 
makes it difficult to achieve multi-objective coordination [ 10, 11]. The disconnection 
between the digital model and the physical realization seriously restricts the sub-second 
response ability required by the high-density construction environment.
Based on the above findings, a new dual-loop collaborative framework is designed and 
verified in this study. The architecture realizes real-time synchronization through the 
data loop of sensor → gateway → MQTT → BIM, and realizes closed-loop control with 
the help of time series prediction [ 12]. This mechanism not only shortens the round-
trip time of information between physical domain and digital domain, but also uses deep 
attention network to predict the construction progress, equipment health and environ -
mental safety in real time, thus realizing multi-objective parallel optimization [ 13, 14]. It 
is worth noting that the dual-loop architecture we designed is scalable, allowing sensor 
nodes to be added and enabling additional business rules to be integrated into decision-
making processes [15, 16].
This study aims to clarify the semantic interoperability mechanism of dual-loop archi -
tecture, and define the field mapping and timestamp synchronization strategy between 
edge gateway, MQTT proxy and IFC entity. Secondly, the collaborative advantages of 
the framework in delay convergence, risk identification and economic benefits are veri -
fied by experimental data. Finally, the quantitative relationship between interface delay, 
packet loss rate and security events can be summarized, which provides practical guid -
ance for the implementation of large-scale intelligent construction sites [17, 18].
2 Literature review
2.1 Technological foundations and persistent gaps in enabling components
The intelligent transformation of the construction industry is driven by the integration 
of dual carbon targets and core digital technologies. The integration of BIM and Internet 
of Things (IoT) is the key to create digital twins, with research progressing from seman -
tic mapping [ 19] to practical synchronization protocol [ 5] and spatial integration [ 3]. 
At the same time, deep learning models (such as LSTM network to enhance attention 
mechanism) have also made breakthroughs in equipment and process risk prediction 
[20, 21], and multi-objective optimization algorithms (such as NSGA-II and MOEA) 
have evolved to include sustainable development goals such as carbon emissions [ 22, 
23].
However, there are still significant gaps in these components. BIM-IOT integration 
usually lacks real-time, and the reporting delay exceeds the critical threshold [ 3], pre-
venting the full incorporation of environmental data. Predictive models usually analyze 
a single risk factor independently, ignoring the coupling between equipment operation 
and environmental conditions. Optimization algorithms mainly rely on static data, and 
rarely achieve integrated dynamic optimization of safety, cost, schedule and environ -
mental impact within a unified framework.

2.2 Systemic architectural challenges: from cloud-edge deployment to closed-loop 
integration
In addition to individual technology, agile computing architecture is needed to realize 
real-time closed-loop system. Edge computing provides a low-latency basis for respon -
sive decision-making [ 15, 16]. However, the current mainstream system architecture 
usually limits the function of edge devices to data preprocessing. This not only fails 
to fully tap the potential of localized intelligent processing but also fails to establish a 
robust and fault-tolerant cloud-edge collaborative mechanism to achieve uninterrupted 
control. This reveals a key systemic gap: although various component technologies (such 
as BIM, Internet of Things, and edge computing) are constantly improving, the overall 
architecture framework that can seamlessly integrate and operate together is still lack -
ing. Specifically, the lack of real-time semantic synchronization, the lack of dynamic 
closed-loop control, and the limitations of extensible cloud-side cooperation mechanism 
all constitute the bottleneck of the core architecture layer. These shortcomings hinder 
the fundamental transformation of intelligent building systems from isolated techni -
cal applications to collaborative network physical systems that can coherently complete 
“perception-analysis-decision-execution” .
2.3 Evolution of multi-objective optimization research: from economic scheduling to 
sustainability synergy
Facing the challenge of multi-objective decision-making in construction management, 
multi-objective optimization algorithm provides an effective solution tool. Many stud -
ies, such as Nusen et al. [ 17], combine genetic algorithm with BIM to schedule renova -
tion projects, while Alothaimeen et al. use non-dominated sorting genetic algorithm II 
(NSGA-II) to optimize the cost and sustainability scores of green building projects [ 23]. 
Besides the classic NSGA-II, advanced optimization algorithms such as multi-objective 
ant colony optimizer (MOALO) and multi-objective equilibrium optimizer (MOEO) 
show excellent convergence and coverage in solving complex engineering problems. 
These provide additional algorithm options for dealing with nonlinear multi-modal opti-
mization objectives in buildings [ 11, 24]. In addition, recent research attempts to com -
bine more advanced algorithms such as NSGA-III with BIM to solve the optimization 
problem of sustainable design involving more objectives [ 25], showing the potential of 
algorithm evolution in solving complex collaborative challenges. Recently, the research 
scope has been expanded to include the environmental aspect. For example, Weigert et 
al. established a method to calculate carbon emissions during construction [ 2], while 
Zhao et al. simultaneously optimized the project duration, cost and carbon emissions 
in highway construction progress [22]. These studies have proved the potential of multi-
objective optimization in promoting low-carbon construction and sustainable resource 
allocation [ 26– 28]. This change marks a change from pure economic efficiency to 
broader value pursuit including environmental responsibility. However, a common limi -
tation still exists: most optimization models rely on static or historical data and cannot 
be deeply integrated with real-time BIM Internet of Things data stream to respond to 
dynamic changes. In addition, safety objectives are often dealt with separately or sim -
ply combined, lacking systematic trade-offs within the same optimization framework 
as cost, schedule and carbon emissions. With regard to scheduling methods, besides 
genetic algorithm, new meta-heuristic algorithms, such as Sine-Cosine Algorithm

(SCA), are explored, and the construction sequence is generated by BIM, which shows 
the potential to meet the space-time constraints [ 29]. This research directly solves this 
challenge. We built a five-dimensional evaluation system at the decision-making rules 
level, including direct cost, safety indicators, project duration, carbon emissions and 
return on investment (ROI). The system adopts NSGA-II algorithm for real-time multi-
objective optimization, and automatically identifies the Pareto optimal balance among 
economic efficiency, safety and environmental performance in dynamic construction 
environment. This represents a leap from single-objective or double-objective optimiza -
tion to multi-dimensional collaborative decision-making.
2.4 Synthesis and positioning of the proposed framework
In short, the literature reveals an obvious disconnect: although enabling technologies 
have made progress, integrating them into a unified, adaptable, versatile, and real-time 
operating system remains an unsolved challenge. These limitations are not only technical 
but also architectural. Clearly, an overall system design that can seamlessly connect data, 
models, and decisions is needed. In this study, a dual-loop (data-control) collaborative 
architecture is proposed to solve this systematic gap identified above. The framework 
aims to fill the gaps in real-time synchronization, closed-loop optimization, semantic 
interoperability and elastic cloud-edge execution, and is positioned as a comprehensive 
solution for the next generation of intelligent construction sites. Accordingly, to further 
clarify the academic positioning of the proposed dual-loop collaborative framework, we 
systematically articulate its novelty by comparing it with existing BIM-IoT and digital 
twin architectures, with a particular focus on the fundamental innovations at the archi -
tectural level that distinguish it from traditional designs.
2.5 Novelty of the proposed dual-loop architecture
To clarify the framework’s novelty and state-of-the-art position, this subsection com -
pares it with typical BIM-IoT/digital twin architectures and elaborates on core innova -
tions (Table 1). Most existing architectures use single-loop synchronization or separate 
modules, leading to real-time/adaptability trade-offs. In contrast, the framework has 
three key innovations: data-control loop decoupling/dynamic coupling (real-time syn -
chronization + intelligent decision-making), elastic cloud-edge collaboration with fault-
tolerant rollback (distributed computing), and real-time ifcXML/SensorML semantic 
alignment (resolving inconsistency), distinguishing it from traditional architectures and 
offering unique academic and technical value.
3 Materials and methods
3.1 Dual-loop collaborative framework
In the dynamic and high-noise environment of construction site, data undergoes multi-
layer protocol conversion and semantic mapping in the round-trip process from sensors 
to decision nodes. Any delay may amplify the security risk. To overcome this bottleneck, 
we present the design of a parallel architecture that integrates a Data Loop and a Con -
trol Loop. The design is driven by the principle of separation of concerns, where the 
Data Loop ensures reliable state synchronization and the Control Loop handles intel -
ligent analysis. As shown in Fig.  1, Data_Loop establishes millisecond-level flow chan -
nel through edge gateway and MQTT Broker. BIM server creates a one-to-one mapping

On the control end, the analysis engine uses LSTM attention network to process time 
characteristics. The decision rule triggers the actuator according to the prediction and 
feeds back the state to the engine for secondary calibration. The double loop is coupled 
through two channels: synchronization and update. The former ensures the seman -
tic consistency between the model and the physical state, while the latter dynamically 
adjusts the weight of the algorithm in real time to realize the adaptive evolution ability.

fine-tuning and solidify the weight during off-peak hours at night. At the same time, the

beam displacement and steel bar strain; Equipment monitoring tracking crane load and 
vibration spectrum; Environmental monitoring tracks PM2.5, temperature/humidity 
and wind speed. All raw data are time-stamped with SHA-256 signatures before entering

3.5 Experimental design and control conditions
To rigorously assess the improvements brought by the dual-loop framework, the base -
line system corresponds to the traditional BIM-IoT management deployed on the same 
project during January–March 2024. Key characteristics of the baseline include identical 
sensor deployment (261 nodes, 28 categories), same sampling frequencies, data clean -
ing procedures (3σ rule and two-scale normalization), and static threshold-based alerts 
without real-time prediction or multi-objective optimization.
Strict control conditions were applied to minimize confounding factors between the 
baseline and experimental periods (April–June 2024), including:
1. Matching construction processes, personnel, and equipment, with quantitative 
constraints ensuring personnel turnover rate < 5% and equipment parameter 
fluctuation < 3%.
2. Ensuring sensor node operation stability with a failure rate < 1%.
3. Aligning environmental conditions (PM2.5, wind speed, temperature, humidity) and 
construction stage (concrete pouring and crane operation peaks), including matched 
extreme weather days.
4. Maintaining identical occupational safety regulations and cost accounting standards.
3.6 Statistical validation methods
In order to strictly verify the performance improvement of the proposed dual-cycle 
framework, a comprehensive statistical verification workflow is established, and the 
pre-specified significance level of all tests is α = 0.05, ensuring consistency with standard 
engineering research practices. The normality of all quantitative indicators was evalu -
ated by Shapiro–wilk test, and the data were processed to remove abnormal values to 
ensure the consistency between the experimental period and the baseline period.
3.6.1 Hypotheses for key metrics
For the core claims of latency reduction and safety improvement, a targeted two-dimen -
sional validation protocol was pre-defined, consistent with the overall statistical frame -
work above. The following statistical hypotheses were established prior to analysis:
1. Null hypothesis (H₀): No significant difference exists in system latency, safety incident 
frequency, or safety compliance rate between the proposed dual-loop framework 
(experimental group, April–June 2024, n = 90 days) and the conventional BIM-IoT 
Page 10 of 19

system (baseline group, January–March 2024, n = 90 days), meaning the relative 
improvement rate of all indicators resulting from the dual-loop framework is 0.
2. Alternative hypothesis (H₁): The experimental group has statistically significant 
advantages over the baseline group in the above core performance indicators, i.e., the 
system latency and safety incident frequency of the experimental group are significantly 
lower, the safety compliance rate is significantly higher, and the relative improvement 
rate of all indicators is greater than 0.
3.6.2 Hypothesis testing methods
Indicator-specific hypothesis testing was conducted according to the type and dis -
tribution of the data. Continuous system latency measurements were analyzed using 
independent samples t-tests when normally distributed, or Mann-Whitney U tests 
for non-normal distributions. Paired count-type safety incidents were evaluated with 
the Wilcoxon signed-rank test, while the safety compliance rate, a binary variable, was 
assessed using two-proportion Z-tests or logistic regression where appropriate. Effect 
sizes were calculated for each comparison to quantify practical significance, using 
Cohen’s d for t-tests, r values for non-parametric tests, and odds ratios or risk ratios for 
count and binary data.
3.6.3 Confidence intervals and robustness
In order to ensure the robustness of the reported improvement, 95% confidence inter -
vals of all indicators are calculated. The improvement in system delay and safety inci -
dents is evaluated by the bootstrap percentile method, with 1000 resampling iterations 
to account for potential time-series autocorrelation. At the same time, Wilson scoring 
interval method is used to calculate the security compliance difference. The 95% con -
fidence interval with a lower limit greater than zero is considered as evidence of stable 
and non-random improvement. For the indexes evaluated at the same time, Bonferroni 
correction is applied to control the family error rate to avoid exaggerating the signifi -
cance level.
The repeatability of the results was confirmed by three independent experiments last -
ing for 4 weeks. The intra-class correlation coefficient (ICC > 0.8) of all key indicators is 
calculated, which proves that the retest reliability is high. This comprehensive workflow 
integrates normality evaluation, index specific hypothesis test, effect size quantification, 
confidence interval estimation with autocorrelation adjustment and multiple compari -
son correction. In a word, these programs ensure that all the observed improvements 
(system delay reduction, safety accidents reduction and compliance improvement) are 
statistically significant, repeatable and robust, and provide a strict and transparent basis 
for evaluating the performance of the dual-loop framework.

In order to further test the robustness of the framework, the team conducted a stress 
injection experiment during the suspension of operation: 1000 msg/s background flow 
was manually injected into MQTT agent to simulate the simultaneous activation of 
cranes. The results show that when the depth of the proxy queue reaches 4000 messages, 
the delay from the edge to BIM is kept below 180 milliseconds. When the packet loss

from 0.75 to 0.68 to improve the risk sensitivity. Economic analysis shows that each 
remote degradation mode trigger can prevent an average of 7 min of downtime, which is 
equivalent to a loss of NT 23,000, saving about NT 580,000 per year.

4.3 Risk heatmap analysis and threshold adaptive optimization
After the dual-loop system runs stably, the team makes a spatio-temporal prediction on 
the 48-h continuous sampling data of the 5G main chain, and generates a four-layer risk

The comparative data of 3 weeks shows that the coverage of ultra-high risk grid cells 
has decreased by 43%, and the coverage of medium and low risk grid cells has increased 
to 79%. The number of accident reports decreased from 5.8 to 3.6 every 100 working

5 Discussion
5.1 Key findings and innovations
Taipei project field verification confirms that the designed dual-loop collaborative 
framework effectively resolves the poor coordination between real-time response and 
closed-loop decision-making in smart construction, achieving a 46.2% reduction in sys -
tem latency, 41.4% drop in safety incidents, 92% high-risk state recognition accuracy and 
99.1% safety compliance rate. It realizes sub-second perception-execution closed loop, 
driving construction management from manual lagged passive response to data-driven 
active intervention, and overcoming delay and data fragmentation in complex construc -
tion environments via the separation and dynamic coupling of data synchronization and 
intelligent control.
The core innovation lies in the systematic architecture-algorithm collaborative design, 
which resolves inherent contradictions in BIM-IoT-AI integration. An elastic cloud-
edge collaboration mechanism with fault-tolerant rollback is adopted for the real-
time-reliability conflict: lightweight edge models seamlessly take over prediction tasks 
when network deteriorates, ensuring monitoring continuity and system robustness. For 
the balance between safety control and economic efficiency, the framework integrates 
dynamic risk perception and five-dimensional multi-objective optimization (cost, safety, 
duration, carbon emissions, ROI), with safety as a hard constraint. It adaptively adjusts 
risk thresholds based on real-time environmental data and embeds the LSTM-Attention 
prediction engine into the NSGA-II algorithm, realizing synergistic optimization of mul-
tiple objectives—while raising safety compliance to 99.1%, it cuts direct costs by 18.5% 
and carbon emissions by 11.1%, verifying the feasibility of coordinated optimization of 
safety, economic and environmental goals through integrated system design.
5.2 Theoretical contributions
When placed in a wider academic context, this study provides a reproducible system par-
adigm and quantitative evidence, and push the intelligent building site from “conceptual 
framework” to “closed-loop operation” . Unlike most projects reviewed by Skibniewski 
[1], which are still limited to one-way monitoring, this paper achieves a complete closed 
Page 16 of 19
Su Discover Applied Sciences (2026) 8:769 
loop of “perception-prediction-decision-execution-feedback” . Fundamentally, as elabo-
rated in the “Novelty of the Proposed Dual-Loop Architecture” subsection, the innova -
tions of our framework at the architectural level lie in the decoupling-coupling design 
of the data-control dual-loop, which breaks through the limitations of traditional one-
way monitoring architectures and enriches the theoretical system of BIM-IoT integra -
tion in intelligent construction. This integrated architectural design not only realizes the 
real-time synchronization between physical construction sites and digital twins but also 
supports the closed-loop intelligent control of construction safety and multi-objective 
optimization, advancing the development of intelligent construction from fragmented 
monitoring to integrated management.
The framework of Hong and Guo [ 5] realizes sub-second data synchronization, but 
ultimately depends on manual decision-making. Unlike this, this study adopts an algo -
rithm model to automatically generate control commands, which significantly simplifies 
the decision-making chain. In addition, unlike previous studies on single-dimensional 
optimization (such as schedule, cost or indoor environment), this paper establishes 
the first unified quantitative framework of construction collaborative management. It 
dynamically balances economic, safety and environmental performance in the shared 
optimization space, and provides a new method for comprehensive efficiency evaluation 
of intelligent building sites. In fact, the research results show that when the decision-
making process of the system is transparent and the feedback is timely, the acceptance 
and satisfaction of front-line personnel (such as foremen) to automatic scheduling 
instructions are significantly improved. This highlights an important insight: the success 
of intelligent construction site systems depends not only on the technical maturity, but 
also on their ability to build transparent and credible man-machine cooperation inter -
faces, which enhance rather than weaken workers’ sense of control over the site.
5.3 Research limitations and reflections
However, this study clearly recognizes its boundaries and limitations, and points out 
the direction for future exploration. First of all, the current design validation focuses on 
viaducts, mainly involving the pouring and hoisting process. The generalizability of the 
architectural model across different project types and universality of the frame requires 
further cross-project deployment and design adaptation. Moreover, the scalability of the 
proposed framework needs to be further explored; the current system is tailored to the 
specific regulatory context of the research area, and potential integration barriers may 
arise when applied to different international construction contexts with diverse regula -
tory requirements, which has not been fully verified in this study. Second, the autono -
mous evolution ability of the edge model is constrained. The current lightweight edge 
model relies on periodic knowledge extraction and weight update of the cloud-based 
main model. Under the extreme conditions of prolonged network isolation, this method 
has the risk of model performance degradation. Future research should explore more 
powerful marginal autonomous learning and federal renewal mechanisms. Third, data 
privacy governance has not been thoroughly addressed; although personal location 
data is anonymized during storage, the study lacks a systematic privacy governance 
framework to adapt to different regional data protection regulations (e.g., GDPR, local 
data security laws), which may limit the application of the system in cross-border con -
struction projects. Finally, the dimension of multi-objective optimization model has

expansion space. In addition to the direct cost and carbon emissions currently consid -
ered, the increasingly important indirect cost factors in high-risk large-scale projects, 
such as dynamic insurance premium, social reputation gains/losses and the far-reaching 
social impact of security incidents, have not been included in the model. Solving these 
problems is very important for achieving wider “sustainable” cooperation.
5.4 Future research directions
Looking forward to the future, the path pioneered by this study can be deepened in 
many directions. Technically, exploring the weight tracking mechanism of blockchain 
integration model can enhance the trust-based cooperation between distributed nodes. 
Developing a scene playback and simulation platform integrating high-fidelity digi -
tal twinning and VR/AR technology will be helpful for solution pre-test and personnel 
training. In terms of optimization algorithm, future research can explore the integra -
tion of recently improved multi-objective evolutionary strategies (for example, Zhang 
et al. and Liu et al.) [ 34, 35], in order to improve the convergence efficiency and solution 
set distribution when solving high-dimensional nonlinear structural coordination prob -
lems. At the application level, it is urgent to carry out reproducible construction and 
comparative research across various typical engineering scenes (such as underground 
projects and coastal high humidity areas) to verify and improve the universality of the 
framework. Theoretically, the future multi-objective collaborative model should con -
sider a wider range of socio-economic factors and establish a more inclusive “engineer -
ing value” evaluation system. This will eventually promote the intelligent building site 
from a tool to improve the efficiency of a single project to an infrastructure to promote 
the overall green and intelligent transformation of the construction industry.
6 Conclusion
This study proposes and empirically investigates a novel dual-loop (data-control) archi -
tectural framework for smart construction sites. The framework is designed to bridge 
the systemic disconnects between data, models, and decisions by establishing a sub-sec -
ond closed loop from real-time sensing to automated execution. The empirical results 
indicate a potential for co-optimization, with the framework associated with reductions 
in system delay (46.2%), safety incidents (41.4%), direct cost (18.5%), and carbon emis -
sions (11.1%), while maintaining a high safety compliance rate (99.1%). These findings 
lend support to the possibility that safety, economic, and environmental objectives may 
be synergistically addressed through integrated system design. Beyond a specific project 
application, this work offers a potentially scalable architectural blueprint and a quantifi -
able decision-making model. It thereby contributes to advancing the field of intelligent 
construction from fragmented monitoring toward more adaptive, collaborative, and sus-
tainable project management practices.
