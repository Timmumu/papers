A B S T R A C T
Risk monitoring is a critical task in numerous industrial fields, including construction engineering. Existing
approaches primarily focus on identifying the immediate incidents of unsafe events, often neglecting the
latent risks arising from the high uncertainty of agents’ motions. To bridge this gap and lay the basis for
preemptive risk monitoring, this paper proposes a novel and effective digital twin-based motion forecasting
framework that leverages cloud-edge collaboration to predict the future trajectories of workers on construction
sites, while ensuring data privacy and reducing computational burden. The edge-end acquires multi-view
images about the physical scene and extracts multi-scale features with a deep learning-based backbone. In
the cloud-end, where the digital world is hosted, a Transformer-based trajectory tracking model is developed
to construct a behavioral digital replica comprising of multiple workers’ historical trajectories from the bird’s-
eye view perspective. Subsequently, a LSTM-based model, combined with the social pooling mechanism, is
deployed to obtain spatiotemporal interactive features from the historical trajectories and predict workers’
future motions. Experiments are conducted on a dataset (WHUT-MF) collected from real-world constructions
sites. Our framework achieves 0.528 m in ADE and 0.84 m in FDE on entire WHUT-MF, which demonstrate
that the proposed digital twin-based motion forecasting model accurately predicts the future trajectories of
workers in various scenarios with potential risks.
1. Introduction
Risk monitoring is a crucial and challenging issue in complex and
highly dynamic scenes such as construction sites. These sites consist
of numerous heterogeneous entities, also referred to as agents, such
as building materials, heavy machinery and workers, which inter-
connect with each other and affect the security of construction sites
collectively. Existing risk monitoring approaches typically focus on
identifying the current safety state of construction sites through the
recognition of workers’ behaviors and machinery operational states [1,
2]. However, in complex scenes involving heterogeneous and dynamic
agents, evaluating the current state alone is insufficient. It is essential
to assess the latent risks arising from motion uncertainties and the

methods to inference a probabilistic model of human motion trajec-
tories based on historical movements. This means they predominantly
consider motion information but lack a detailed understanding of the

and complex interactions between agents. On construction sites, agents’
movements are influenced by a multitude of diverse environmental fac-
tors, making it challenging for mechanism-based approaches to forecast
human motion accurately. To improve the accuracy and robustness of
motion forecasting in dynamic scenes, data-driven learning methods
have been extensively explored in domains such as computer vision [5,
6], intelligent transportation [7,8], etc. In comparison, there is still
limited research in the context of construction sites. To predict the
future trajectories of workers using data-driven methods in construc-
tion site scenarios, acquiring a massive amount of historical motion
data is necessary. Furthermore, it is necessary to establish refined
representations of large-scale scenes, enabling the encoding of complex
scene dynamics and interactive relations to realize accurate motion
prediction.
However, the aforementioned work faces significant challenges due
to the following reasons: (1) The most commonly adopted sensors on
construction sites are cameras typically deployed independent of one
another, making it difficult to obtain a comprehensive fine-grained
representation of large-scale scenes. (2) The limited computational
power of onsite computing platforms hinders real-time execution of
deep learning-based motion prediction model. Moreover, the platform’s
ability to support the upstream tasks of motion prediction, namely
object detection and tracking, is even more demanding. The prevalent
approach adopted in recent years has been to transmit the collected
data from construction sites to the cloud for processing. Nevertheless,
this practice is unfavorable for privacy protection and data security,
while also imposing significant pressure on data transmission.
To address the aforementioned issues, our work proposes leveraging
the digital twin technology, which has been widely utilized to simu-
late and model physical counterparts, capturing their physical details
and visual features. For example, numerous researches [9–11] adopt
digital Twin for the purpose of building information modeling (BIM)
to optimize the management of the production process. However, for
certain tasks, such as motion prediction, what is required is not limited
to observable information of the physical counterpart, but also implicit
and intricate features.
In this paper, we propose an edge-cloud collaboration framework
to implement digital twin-based motion forecasting for risk monitoring
on construction sites. At the edge-end, a pre-trained ResNet is utilized
as the backbone network to extract multi-view feature maps at each of
the three edge-computing platforms we deployed in the construction
sites, for the purpose of privacy protection, data security and lower
transmission burden. The cloud-end hosts the digital twin in which
a multi-object tracking and a motion forecasting model is deployed.
Specifically, inspired by previous works [12–15], a Transformer-based
multi-object tracker is devised to exploit the cross-attention mecha-
nism, so as to capture the expressive motion characteristics of multiple
agents across multi-view features transmitted from the edge-end. His-
torical trajectories are then obtained by decoding the learned query
and utilized to form a fine-grained task-oriented behavioral digital
replication of the scene. Subsequently, a social LSTM-based [16] motion
forecasting model is deployed to obtain spatiotemporal interactive
features among the multi-agents, which are then decoded to produce
the predicted future trajectories of agents.
The main contributions of our work can be summarized as follows.
1. This study proposes a cloud–edge collaborative framework for
constructing a digital twin aiming to perform motion forecasting of
workers. At the edge-end, multi-scale features are extracted from the
acquired multi-view images for privacy protection and data security.
The cloud-end hosts the digital realm in which a Transformer-based
multi-object tracking network and a social LSTM-based motion forecast-
ing model are deployed to obtain historical trajectories and furthermore
predict future trajectories of multiple workers.
2. To create a task-centric digital replication of the physical scene,
a query-based tracking model is developed to obtain historical trajec-
tories of multiple objects across multiple views. Building upon this,
we employ social LSTM to capture the spatiotemporal interaction fea-
tures among the agents, ultimately predicting high-precision future
trajectories for each and every worker.
3. The experimental results, conducted on a dataset collected from
real-world construction sites, demonstrate that the proposed digital
twin-based motion forecasting model can accurately predict the future
trajectories of workers in various scenarios with latent risks.
2. Related work
2.1. Risk monitoring on construction sites
Prompted by the rapid development of Artificial Intelligence (AI)
algorithms and their successes in computer vision, vision-based ap-
proaches for risk monitoring have become a research focus in construc-
tion engineering [17–19]. These approaches typically focus on detect-
ing the risks stemming from unsafe behaviors of workers [20–22] such
as not wearing helmets [23–25], falling from heights [26–28], and col-
lisions with safety-related objects [29,30], etc. However, evaluating the
immediate safety status of the scene through detection-based methods
is inadequate for handling fast-evolving and stochastic potential risks,
promoting the development of proactive prediction-based approaches.
Several studies have approached risk prediction through motion fore-
casting, primarily utilizing model-based or mechanism-based methods:
Rashid [31] developed a physics-based human trajectory prediction
model for evaluating collision risks around hazard areas, Amir [32]
studies the feasibility of the proactive safety framework by utilizing two
mechanism-based trajectory prediction models via field experiments.
A significant limitation lies in the difficulty of rule-based approaches
to adapt to complex environments. For scene-adaptive and robust
motion forecasting, we propose a deep learning-based framework, en-
abled by the digital twin technology, in the service of preemptive risk
monitoring.
2.2. Motion forecasting methods
Motion forecasting serves as the foundation for preemptive risk
monitoring by predicting the motion trends of dynamic agents, which
are inherently influenced by both the environmental conditions and in-
dividual behavioral intentions. Several motion forecasting studies [33–
36] employ recurrent neural networks (RNNs) to iteratively capture
the temporal evolution patterns of agents’ motion for prediction. To
model the complex and diverse interactive relationships among agents,
other research works [16,37,38] employ convolutional neural networks
(CNNs) to capture spatial interactions based on the encoded histori-
cal motions with RNNs. Recent studies [39,40] propose to integrate
the spatiotemporal encoding of dynamic and static elements in the
environment, utilizing cross-attention mechanisms to learn intricate
interactions between the agents and scene context. Furthermore, gen-
erative models such as Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) [41]
and Gaussian Mixture Models (GMMs) [42] have also been introduced
for motion forecasting.
Some studies have focused on motion forecasting of workers on
construction sites, where explicit geometric and topological structures
may be absent. Therefore, they emphasize the use of historical motion
and the modeling of interactions between dynamic agents via Gener-
ative Adversarial Networks [43] and recurrent neural network based
methods [44,45].

2.3. Digital twin-based applications in construction engineering
The essence of a digital twin is to establish a task-oriented replica-
tion of the physical world as the digital realm [ 46–49]. As a universal
technical framework, it has been incorporated into the whole life
cycle of various infrastructural entities [ 50–52] such as machinery
and buildings. Utilizing the digital twin technology, the construction
community proposes numerous approaches to construct fine-grained 3D
digital replications of infrastructures to implement effective and effi-
cient construction progress management, thereby empowering digital
fabrication [ 53–58]. In these studies, the construction of the digital
twins focus primarily on replicating explicit and visible 3D details about
the physical counterparts. Our study extends the concept of digital twin
to replicate implicit motion and interaction features of dynamic agents,
constructing a digital replica comprising of historical trajectories to lay
the basis for robust and accurate motion forecasting.
3. Approach
3.1. Digital twin-based motion forecasting framework leveraging cloud-edge
collaboration
In this paper, the digital twin technology is adopted to develop a
motion forecasting model that enables risk monitoring and prediction
on construction sites. The architecture of the proposed framework is
illustrated in Fig. 1 , comprising two main components: the physical
world and the digital world. Besides, the edge-cloud framework is
utilized to bridge the physical world and the digital world. It combines
cloud computing and edge computing to enable efficient behavioral
digital twin construction and accurate motion forecasting. In this frame-
work, edge computing platforms provide real-time and low-latency
processing for handling multi-view raw images captured in the physical
world. The cloud computing platform receives the feature queues trans-
mitted from the edge computing platforms, extracts motion information
from the constructed digital description of surrounding context in BEV
space, and perform motion forecasting exploiting its robust computing
and storage capabilities.
In the physical world, multiple cameras are deployed to capture
multi-view videos of the scene, which are processed by deep CNN-based
backbones. In the digital world, a Transformer-based tracking model
is devised to exploit the cross-attention mechanism, implementing a
query-based approach to extract the motion features of workers in a
unified global bird’s-eye-view (BEV) space, and subsequently obtains
historical trajectories to construct a fine-grained task-oriented behav-
ioral digital replication of the physical world. Subsequently, a social
LSTM-based model is designed to encode interactive features in both
spatial and temporal dimensions, and predict the future trajectories of
workers. The overall architecture of digital replica construction and
motion forecasting is presented in Fig. 2 .
3.2. Construction of the behavioral digital replica
The digital replica we construct in this study is a behavioral replica-
tion of workers’ motions, effectively represented as historical trajecto-
ries. To accurately detect and track multiple workers based on features
collected from multiple perspectives, we propose an instance-oriented
query that encodes cross-view motion features by leveraging the cross-
attention mechanism of Transformer, building upon the baseline model
proposed by Zhang et al. [ 15]. The obtained query is then decoded
into a 10-dimensional 3D bounding box, which captures the dynamic
motion characteristics of the worker at the current frame in the BEV
space. Moreover, the 3D bounding boxes of the same query across
continuous frames within the observed time span naturally form a
tracked trajectory, serving as the basis of the digital replication.
In order to represent workers’ motion features in a global-refined
3D scene space for robust digital replica construction, we integrate
the information from multi-view camera in the birds’ eye view (BEV)
space. First of all, we take the area covered by multiple cameras as
the entire BEV space and divide it into grids of the same size. Each
grid represents an area in the real world corresponding to an image
region captured by multi-view cameras. Second, the center point of
BEV space is set as the origin, and we perform joint calibration on
all cameras to obtain the transformation matrix from pixel space to
BEV space, including rotation matrix and translation matrix. Finally, we
initialize some 3D reference points in BEV space to roughly represent
the locations of target instances, which will be projected to multi-view
image and the corresponding query aggregates temporal-spatial motion
features via the cross-attention mechanism. Note that there are no
explicit grids in the constructed BEV space. Instead, we just exploit the
sparse reference point in BEV space and the corresponding query-based
feature to represent the location and context-related motion feature of
instance in construction scenarios.

3.3. Digital twin-based motion forecasting
Based on the digital replica constructed, a motion forecasting model
is developed to encode both spatial and temporal features shaping
the motions of multiple agents in the scene. Due to the absence of
geometric and topological structures in construction site scenes, graph-
based scene representation is not suitable. In the previous section, we
generated the historical trajectories of the agents in the BEV space.
Therefore, we also convert the entire construction site scene into a
rasterized BEV grid. Building upon this, inspired by the social LSTM
proposed by Alahi et al. [16], we first utilize LSTM to extract temporal
motion features from the target’s historical trajectories. Subsequently,
the social pooling mechanism is employed to model the spatial interac-
tive relations among multiple agents, ultimately deriving a social tensor
that characterizes the spatiotemporal interaction among them. This
tensor is then decoded to generate predictions of future trajectories.

4. Experiments and results
Our proposed digital twin-based motion forecasting model is trained
utilizing two public dataset, ETH and UCY, which are widely used
to train and test human motion forecasting approaches. We test the
proposed model on a dataset collected from real-world construction
sites in Wuhan, China. Both quantitative and qualitative analysis are
conducted, and the results clearly demonstrate the effectiveness of our
proposal in forecasting workers’ motion robustly and accurately in
various scenes on construction sites.
4.1. Dataset
The self-created test dataset WHUT-MF comprises 165 sequences,
each containing 20 images captured by multiple cameras over a span
of 5 s. The label for each sequence is represented as [pedestrian ID,
𝑥 coordinate, 𝑦 coordinate, 𝑧 coordinate, predicted timestep], where
the predicted timestep depends on both the prediction horizon and
frequency. The accurate 3D positions (𝑥, 𝑦, 𝑧) of multiple workers in
the predicted time domain are measured using LiDAR and portable
positioning sensors, with a sampling frequency matching that of the
cameras.
For the purpose of validation, we specifically focus on challenging
scenarios that might harbor hidden risks and pose difficulties for work-
ers’ motion forecasting. Examples include the vicinity of ascending or
descending commodities suspended from tower cranes, the surround-
ing areas of slow-moving trucks, and the proximity to rotating crane
counterweights.
4.2. Evaluation metrics
To evaluate the effectiveness of our proposed digital twin-based
motion forecasting model, we utilize the following two commonly used
metrics to measure the accuracy of motion forecasting: (1) Average
displacement error (ADE): the average Euclidean distance between the
real value of the target’s position and its predicted value on all of
the prediction steps. (2) Final displacement error (FDE): the Euclidean
distance between the real value and predicted value of the target’s
position only on the final prediction step. Furthermore, we propose a
new metrics named weighted displacement error (WDE), which is the
weighted average of ADE and FDE. Given the nature of most accidents
resulting from sudden latent risks, we intuitively prioritize FDE in
assessing whether workers can timely avoid hazards. Specifically, we
empirically assign a weight of 0.3 to ADE and 0.7 to FDE.
4.3. Implementation details
The proposed framework for edge-cloud collaboration is supported
by the ROS operating system, ensuring well-organized and efficient
communication between edges and the cloud. 3 cameras are securely
mounted on a stationary platform in three perspectives: front, left, and
right. To obtain 3D accurate positions of workers as ground truth labels,

Edge computing platforms are equipped with cameras deployed on
construction sites. The central computing platform is equipped with
4 high-performance Nvidia 3090 GPU. To facilitate real-time analysis,
our system employs the MQTT (Message Queuing Telemetry Transport)
protocol to transmit data streams generated by the edge computing
platforms to the cloud.
For training of the motion forecasting model, the Adam optimizer
with an initial learning rate of 0.001 is adopted. We use the same set
of key hyperparameters as in [16], and set the training epoch to 30.
The training platform is Intel(R) Xeon(R) Silver 4210 CPU @ 2.20 GHz,
Nvidia GTX 3090 GPU with 32G RAM. It takes roughly four hours to
train the model.

In Table 1, we show the performance of our proposed motion fore-
casting model on public datasets ETH and UCY, which are merged into
ETH/UCY comprising five scenes (ETH, Hotel, Univ, Zara1, Zara2) . Re-
sults reveal that our approach achieves performance on par with SOTA
(State of the Art) when predicting the movements of human-beings in

Furthermore, to demonstrate the ability of motion forecasting of our
proposed model on specific construction sites, we have conducted ex-
periments on the collected WHUT-MF. The results in Table 2 reveal that
our approach can predict motions of workers accurately across various
scenes in general. It is also suggested that the model performs the best
in scene B. We attribute this discrepancy to the lack of inconspicuous
motion characteristics among workers in Scenes A and C. In these
particular scenes, workers tend to remain stationary or exhibit minimal
movement for extended duration. Consequently, it becomes challenging
to extract rich and expressive features from historical motion, resulting
in a lower prediction accuracy compared to Scene B.

precision of the constructed digital replica. Since the digital replica is
in fact composed of tracked historical trajectories of multiple objects,
we adopt the metrics including average multi-object tracking accuracy
(AMOTA) and average multi-object tracking precision (AMOTP). Ad-
ditionally, we also aim to investigate the optimal time span of the
historical trajectories to be utilized, which would not only enhance
the accuracy of motion prediction but also lower the computational
overhead associated with constructing the digital replica.

5. Conclusion
In this paper, a digital twin-based motion forecasting framework
is developed and validated on real-world construction sites, laying
a solid foundation for preemptive motion risk monitoring in service
of intelligent safety management and provides a prototype for large-
scale applications. In the physical world, cross-view images acquired
by multiple cameras are processed with deep CNNs deployed on the
edge-computing platform to obtain multi-scale and de-identification
features about the scene. In the digital world hosted on the cloud, a
fine-grained behavioral digital replication consisting of tracked trajec-
tories of workers is constructed using a Transformer-based multi-object
tracking model. Afterward, a social LSTM-based model is developed to
capture the spatiotemporal interactive features, which are then decoded
to generate future trajectories of workers. A series of experiments is
designed and conducted to evaluate the effectiveness of the proposal.
The results show that the proposed framework performs accurate and
real-time motion prediction for multiple workers on construction sites.
Our work extends the concept of digital twin by incorporating
human motion features into the digital replica and introduces a novel
approach to monitor latent risks on construction sites. Future studies
will focus on further exploiting digital twin technology to refine the
construction of the digital replication with more detailed and nuanced
information, such as worker’s body movements, so as to integrate the
recognition of workers’ behaviors with motion forecasting to further
enhance the accuracy of motion prediction. Additionally, multi-modal
data instead of single-modal images could be used to further improve
the model’s robustness.
