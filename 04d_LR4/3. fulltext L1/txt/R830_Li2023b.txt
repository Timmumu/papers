ABSTRACT 
An accurate and timely design of individual processes in drill and blast tunneling is essential for robust and low- 
risk construction of rock tunnels. Conventional dynamic design method of rock tunnel focuses on the modifi -
cation of preliminary design schemes using exposed on-site data. A novel conceptual model, just-in-time (JIT) 
design of rock tunnel, is proposed in this study to denote the intrinsic requirement that tunnel support param -
eters should be determined in a JIT way when the rock mass are excavated. Specifically, implementation of JIT 
design of rock tunnel can be divided into three steps, i.e., acquisition of on-site raw data, interpretation of on-site 
raw data, and aggregation of the interpreted results to obtain a revised design scheme. Time consumption, ac -
curacy and degree of automation are selected as three main indicators to measure the performance of such 
conceptual model. Moreover, to further improve the efficiency and accuracy, digital twin (DT) is integrated into 
the implementation of JIT design of rock tunnel, where Scene-Entity-Relationship-Incident-Control (SERIC) 
modelling method is proposed as a new DT application mode for share and reuse across the domain. The pro -
posed DT-enabled JIT design of rock tunnel is applied into a practical engineering project, where several 
advanced data acquisition, interpretation and aggregation approaches are integrated using DT. Results show that 
the design efficiency and accuracy have been largely improved and the proposed method could facilitate in -
formation sharing between end-users and stakeholders. 
1. Introduction 
An accurate design of individual processes in drill and blast (D & B) 
tunneling is essential for robust and low-risk construction of rock tun -
nels. Due to the uncertainty and variability of geological conditions, 
field engineers need to revise the preliminary design of the tunnel using 
the newly-exposed information, and the two-stage design process is also 
called the dynamic design of the rock tunnel ( Feng et al., 2016 ). Hence, 
in D & B tunneling, the efficiency and accuracy of the obtained newly- 
exposed on-site information greatly influence the dynamic design of 
the rock tunnel. 
The key to successful implementation of dynamic design is quick and 
accurate acquisition of geological information and structural response 
during the construction, as well as timely feedback of design changes to 
construction. Conventionally, field engineers tend to use equipment 
such as geological compass and roughness profile gauge to record on-site 
information ( Reid and Harrison, 2000 ). However, such practice is 
plagued with the problem of being error-prone and laborious as the 
efficiency of information acquisition mainly lies on the engineer expe -
rience. With the advancement of information technology (IT), multiple 
research efforts have been allocated to apply IT to individual processes 
in rock tunnel design to improve efficiency and accuracy. For instance, 
techniques such as digital photogrammetry ( Li et al., 2019; Zhang et al., 
2020 ), terrestrial laser scanning ( Cacciari and Futai, 2016; Li et al., 
2016 ) and machine learning ( Liu et al., 2020; Wang et al., 2021 ) have all 
been implemented into rock tunnel design. Though these implementa -
tions have improved dynamic optimization efficiency of the design work 
to a certain level, the disconnection between individual processes still 
leads to poor coupling between design and construction as the existing 
studies mainly focus on one individual process (e.g., data acquisition, 
interpretation or analysis) in the design. From a holistic design and 
construction perspective, a novel conceptual model, just-in-time (JIT) 
design of rock tunnel, is proposed in this study to denote the intrinsic 
requirement of the rock tunnel support that support parameters need to 
be revised and determined when the rock mass are exposed during 
construction in a JIT way. JIT design of rock tunnel aims to use advanced

IT technologies to assist the acquisition-interpretation-aggregation 
workflow and incorporate the design work into construction to form 
close integration. However, such conceptual framework has not been 
implemented in the practical engineering project. Hence, the first 
research question this study aims to answer is how to conduct the JIT 
design of rock tunnel using existing IT technologies. 
The key for JIT design of rock tunnel is to improve efficiency. 
Recently, DT has been increasingly used in the architecture, engineering 
and construction (AEC) industry for its anticipated benefits such as 
improved productivity and efficiency ( Bosch-Sijtsema et al., 2021 ). DT is 
the connection loop between physical system and virtual system which 
can synchronize for monitoring, simulating and optimizing purposes in a 
timely manner ( Almatared et al., 2022; Boje et al., 2020 ). Actually, the 
core of DT is consistent with the goal of JIT design, which is to improve 
efficiency. Hence, it comes naturally that DT paradigm can be incor -
porated into tunnel design to improve design efficiency and accuracy. 
This has been verified by Shen et al. (2022) who used virtual world 
simulation and real world validation to design the luminaires inside the 
tunnel and found DT paradigm could contribute to design work. How -
ever, existing studies rarely apply DT to the whole design and con -
struction process to assist rock tunnel design. As for the application 
mode of DT, some researches have been carried out trying to propose a 
unified framework for DT application ( Bamunuarachchi et al., 2021; 
Zheng et al., 2019 ). In AEC industry, in most cases (such as Ding et al. 
(2018); Ritto and Rochinha, 2021; Yuan et al., 2016 ), a DT-oriented 
framework was also firstly put forward to show how to build DT of 
the target domain and then the physical- and digital- twin was set up 
based on the framework. However, this kind of method cannot be shared 
or reused as the established architecture or framework was different. In 
the study carried out by Grieves and Vickers (2017) , DT was divided into 
three types: the DT Prototype, the DT Instance and the DT Aggregate. 
Using this type of modeling method, McClellan et al. (2022) described 
the construction of a two-level DT Instance for autonomous unmanned 
aerial vehicle landing. However, the modeling method of DT is too 
general to be used in rock tunnel design. Actually, a detailed and 
structured modeling method of DT can have important implications to 
not only rock tunnel design but also other domains in AEC industry as it 
shapes a reusable and easy-to-share paradigm for DT application. 
Motivated by such need, the second research question this study aims to 
investigate is how to build the DT of the rock tunnel during construction 
in an easy-to-share and – reuse way to better conduct the JIT design 
work. 
Focusing on the two research questions, this study proposes SERIC 
modeling method for DT application and applies the proposed SERIC 
modeling method to a practical rock tunnel project, where DT is inte -
grated into the JIT design of the tunnel. The remaining sections of this 
paper are organized as follows: Section 2 introduces JIT design of rock 
tunnel, as well as SERIC modeling method of DT and explains how to 
integrate DT into JIT design of rock tunnel. Section 3 is a case study 
which implements the proposed framework based on a rock tunnel 
under construction in Yunnan Province, China. Finally, Section 4 and 5 
present limitations and conclusions. 
2. Conceptual framework development: JIT design of rock 
tunnel 
In this section, the above-mentioned conceptual framework of JIT 
design of rock tunnel is discussed in detail, including framework over -
view, relationships with widely-known dynamic design of rock tunnel 
and how DT can be incorporated into JIT design of rock tunnel to further 
increase design efficiency. 
2.1. Framework overview 
According to previous study on tunnel support design, time con -
sumption and accuracy, as well as degree of automation, are main

factors that influence the performance of tunnel support design ( Ling 
et al., 2022 ). Hence, the starting point of JIT design of rock tunnel is to 
reduce time consumption and improve design accuracy of tunnel sup -
port. The term “ just-in-time ” denotes that the support parameters need 
to be determined as soon as the hidden volume of the rock mass is 
exposed during tunnel construction. 
As mentioned above, the design of rock tunnel support can be 
divided into three steps, i.e. data acquisition, interpretation and aggre -
gation. Data acquisition refers to acquisition of raw data on site such as 
images of tunnel face and waves of geological prospecting to record 
newly-exposed geological conditions, data interpretation refers to pro -
cessing of on-site raw data to extract useful information for support 
design, and data aggregation means using processed data to give the 
revised support schemes. Previous studies have shown that many 
different technologies have been applied in the design of rock tunnel 
support. Considering that different technologies in the data “ acquisition- 
interpretation-aggregation ” workflow have pros and cons in terms of 
time consumption and accuracy, in the JIT design of rock tunnel, tech -
nologies with acceptable time consumption and accuracy are adopted. 
Fig. 1 presents the overall workflow of JIT design of rock tunnel. 
Technologies such as digital photogrammetry (DP) ( Garcia-Luna et al., 
2019 ), terrestrial laser scanning (TLS) ( Monsalve et al., 2019 ), ground 
penetrating radar (GPR) and transient electromagnetic method (TEM) 
( Ling et al., 2022 ) are incorporated in the workflow as these technolo -
gies can acquire on-site data quickly and accurately (in most cases, 
< 1h). After acquiring raw data, data interpretation follows up to extract 
useful information (e.g., discontinuity, water inflow and profile infor -
mation) by using corresponding interpretation methods such as edge 
detection ( Azarafza et al., 2019; Leu and Chang, 2005 ), clustering 
method ( Chen et al., 2016 ), deep learning ( Chen et al., 2021a; Chen 
et al., 2021b ), which have proven to be intelligent approaches with 
small time consumption but high accuracy. Then, the extracted infor -
mation is used for aggregation to give final revised support schemes. In 
this step, numerical analysis and artificial intelligence (AI)-support ap -
proaches are adopted for their superiority of providing results in a short 
period of time. Specifically, artificial neural network (ANN) ( Nie et al., 
2018 ), genetic algorithm (GA) ( Alvarez-Fernandez et al., 2020; Liu et al., 
2019 ), particle swarm optimization (PSO) ( Li et al., 2021 ), expert 
system, and other AI-based approaches have proven to be effective in 
tunnel support design. Finally, revised support schemes are fed back to 
the site for construction. 
It should be noted that though the proposed conceptual framework 
of JIT design of rock tunnel support includes different methods and 
conceptions, its main aim is to improve the efficiency of each step in the 
“ acquisition-interpretation-aggregation ” workflow. The mentioned 
methods and conceptions in Fig. 1 are summarized from previous studies 
and proven to be effective for tunnel support design. Some conventional 
methods such as manual sketch of the exposed tunnel face are not 
included in the proposed framework as they are neither time-saving nor 
accurate. 
2.2. Comparison with dynamic design of rock tunnel support 
Fig. 2 presents the comparison between the proposed JIT design of 
rock tunnel support and widely-known dynamic design of rock tunnel 
support. It can be seen that the focus of the dynamic design of rock 
tunnel support lies in the modification of original support schemes using 
newly-exposed data during construction. In this practice, some time- 
consuming data acquisition methods such as manual sketch of tunnel 
face, which may take as long as 4 h ( Huang et al., 2019 ), and inaccurate 
data interpretation and aggregation methods such as empirical analysis 
are all included. 
By comparison, the focus of JIT design of rock tunnel support lies in 
reducing time consumption and increasing accuracy in the data 
“ acquisition-interpretation-aggregation ” workflow. It does not mean 
that JIT design of rock tunnel support is completely different from the 
dynamic design of rock tunnel support. Actually, JIT design of rock 
tunnel support is still within the scope of dynamic design of rock tunnel 
support, but it raises higher requirements for dynamic design of rock 
tunnel support that the revision of original support schemes should be 
made in time when geological conditions are newly exposed during 
construction, while the latter one focuses on the revision of the pre -
liminary design parameters using on-site data regardless of the speed or 
the efficiency. 
Hence, in the workflow of JIT design of rock tunnel support, intel -
ligent acquisition and interpretation methods are recommended such as

2.3. DT-enabled JIT design of rock tunnel support 
DT and JIT design of rock tunnel support share one common goal, 
which is to improve efficiency. Hence, in this study, DT is incorporated 
into JIT design of rock tunnel support to further improve the perfor -
mance of JIT design of rock tunnel support. The SERIC modeling method 
is proposed and integrated into JIT design of rock tunnel support to 
improve the design efficiency of rock tunnel and interconnect key 
technologies in JIT design of rock tunnel for better comprehension and 
application of DT in JIT design of rock tunnel, trying to provide a 
paradigm shift for JIT design of rock tunnel. 
2.3.1. SERIC modeling of DT 
After the concept of DT was introduced in a presentation to industry 
in University of Michigan, Prof.Grieves further expounded the definition 
of DT and stated that DT (DT Prototype and DT Instance) was operated 
in a DT Environment. On this basis, we subdivide DT into DT Scene 
(DTS), DT Entity (DTE), DT Relationship (DTR), DT Incident (DTI) and 
DT Controller (DTC) for better comprehension and application of DT in 
AEC industry. 
DT scene is similar to the concept the DT environment proposed by 
Grieves and Vickers (2017) . What really sets them apart is that one DT 
environment can incorporate many DT scenes. Here, DT scene refers to 
the specific application scene in AEC industry, and it is a specific 
description of the task and action within certain time and space in order 
to solve a practical engineering problem. Taking rock tunnel construc -
tion as an example, real-time acquisition of newly-exposed tunnel face 
data and subsequent rock classification based on the acquired data is a 
DT scene which aims to use DT technologies to assist tunnel design 
( Yang et al., 2021 ). In addition, intelligent control of jumbos on site is 
also a DT scene which aims to use DT technologies to assist tunnel 
construction ( Navarro et al., 2018 ). 
DT entity is the entities in the DT scene. According to the DT para -
digm, i.e. physical twin, virtual twin and data flow, equipment, stuff, 
structures and other physical entities (PE) that have a counterpart in 
virtual space and have data flows between them can all be considered as 
DT entity. DT entity contains geometry, properties, states, forms, 
mechanisms, connections, and behaviors of entities in DT. Taking the 
lighting design inside the tunnel as an example ( Shen et al., 2022 ), 
tunnel structure and luminaires are two DT entities as they both have 
counterparts in the virtual environment and data collected in the virtual 
environment are used to revise parameters of physical entities. 
DT relationship describes relationships between DT entities, 
including information correlation, spatial correlation, physical correla -
tion, aggregation correlation and so forth. Understanding how the DT 
entities are correlated is crucial as their relationships determine how the 
entire DT should be constructed. Specifically, spatial correlation, phys -
ical correlation and aggregation correlation determine how the model in 
virtual space is established, while information correlation and aggre -
gation correlation determine how data are transmitted, aggregated and 
analyzed. 
DT incident refers to events in a DT scene. Event-driven method uses 
real-time data collected by the production system to capture the 
disturbance events in the production process and explores the real-time 
changes of system performance under random disturbance scenario 
( Gou et al., 2021 ). Hence, in the modeling of DT, we use DT incident to 
denote events in the whole DT system which can trigger data exchange 
between digital- and physical-twin. For instance, in the study carried out 
by Ritto and Rochinha (2021) , damage detection is one DT incident and 
triggers data exchange between physical twin, discrete computational 
model and stochastic computational model. In the study conducted by 
Yuan et al. (2016) , structure monitoring is one DT incident and triggers 
data updates and remote control between virtual model and physical 
structure. It should be noted that one DT scene can exist different DT 
incidents as in some complex tasks there may be different data exchange 
aims. 
Last but not least, DT controller is the data and information pro -
cessing and analysis center in DT scene. DT controller is the core 
component in virtual space which incorporates algorithms, models, 
knowledge base and so forth. Data collected from the physical-twin are 
the input to DT controller, and orders and other feedbacks to the 
physical-twin are the output of DT controller. Up until fairly recently 
researchers have been allocating efforts to improve the performance of 
DT controller, such as introducing more computationally-power ap -
proaches ( Chong et al., 2014 ) and investigating better-performing 
models ( Liu et al., 2021 ). 
The proposed SERIC modeling method is consistent with DT para -
digm, as shown in Fig. 3 . The relationships between DT scene, DT 
incident, DT entity, DT relationship and DT controller are also demon -
strated in Fig. 3 . In a specific DT scene, DT incident is the trigger that 
causes interaction between physical space and virtual space. Data 
collected from PEs in the physical space are transmitted to DT entities 
and DT controller in the virtual space for processing and analysis. It 
should be noted that DT entities in the virtual space are the digital-twin 
of the PEs in the physical twin. The results or orders given by the DT 
controller are then fed back to PEs in the physical space.

3. Framework implementation: Case study of Laoying tunnel 
The proposed JIT design method of rock tunnel is applied and vali -
dated in Laoying Tunnel, which is a practical engineering project in 
Yunnan Province, China. As an extra-long tunnel with a length of more 
than 11 km, Laoying Tunnel passes through many different strata and 
the geological conditions are complex, which poses a challenge on the 
design of the tunnel. Based on the paradigm depicted in Fig. 1 , this 
section tackles the JIT design of Laoying Tunnel. Location of Laoying 
Tunnel, as well as its profile, is presented in Fig. 5 . 
Implementation of JIT design of rock tunnel can be divided into three 
steps, i.e., acquisition of on-site raw data, interpretation of on-site raw 
data and aggregation of the interpreted results to obtain revision of 
preliminary design schemes. In Laoying Tunnel, tunnel seismic predic -
tion (TSP) is selected as the data acquisition approach, based on which 
the -subsequent data interpretation and aggregation are conducted. 
Time consumption, accuracy and degree of automation are three main 
indicators influence the performance of JIT design of rock tunnel. Spe -
cifically, data acquisition using TSP and interpretation using geo -
statistical method can fulfill the requirements of time consumption, 
accuracy and degree of automation, as data acquisition time can be 
controlled within 30 min, geostatistical method can eliminate 
uncertainty in the data and improve prediction accuracy, and the whole 
interpretation and aggregation process can be automatic. 
3.1. Data acquisition 
TSP is a widespread non-destructive geophysical techniques of ahead 
geological forecast to detect water bodies, fissures and fractured rock 
ahead of the tunnel face ( Bu et al., 2019 ). The principle of TSP geological 
forecast is to receive artificial seismic waves formed by low-dose ex -
plosives near the tunnel face( Lin and Li, 2013 ). The reflected waves are 
usually associated with discontinuities of rock masses. Due to its long 
detection distance and high prediction accuracy, TSP has become one of 
the most commonly-used methods for tunnel ahead geological forecast 
( Fu et al., 2018 ). As for the acquisition time, use of TSP on site to acquire 
data can be as quick as 30 min, which can meet the demands of rock 
tunnel JIT design. 
In this study, the section from K12 + 100 to K 11 + 020 was selected 
as the study area. A total of 9 TSP has been carried out in this area. A 
portion of the acquired data is shown in Table 1 .

3.2.1. Calculation of RMR hard data 
In order to conform to the characteristics of dynamic acquisition of 
tunnel excavation information, four mileages, namely K 11 + 900, K 11 
+ 760, K 11 + 480 and K 11 + 360, were selected as the RMR prediction 
mileages, where the variogram was fitted with the measured RMR value 
after the tunnel face. That is to say, with the progress of tunnel con -
struction, the hard data in the study area increased gradually. For the 
hard data at the four selected mileages, experimental variation of the 
data was calculated first and then spherical variogram was used to fit the 
experimental variation. Spatial variability of RMR value at the four 
mileages is presented in Fig. 9 . 
According to the theoretical variogram obtained in Fig. 9 , the pa -
rameters calculated by the variogram at the four mileages are shown in 
Table 2 . 
From the fitting results, the range value increases with the increase of 
the measured data, which indicates that with the increase of the 
measured data for fitting variogram, the data range affecting the pre -
dicted mileage also increases, and more hard data will participate in the 
prediction process. At the same time, the increase of measured data 
leads to the gradual increase of the nugget, which shows that using a 
large amount of historical data for prediction cannot fully guarantee the 
improvement of prediction accuracy. In some areas, such as the frac -
tured section of a fault, the prediction error caused by the internal 
randomness of surrounding rock parameters will be more obvious. 
Therefore, it is necessary to use the soft data formed by TSP to improve 
the prediction accuracy.

3.3. Data aggregation 
With the updated RMR value using interpretation method in Sub -
section 3.2 , the support parameters can be obtained correspondingly. 
As can be seen from Table 5 , the one-to-one relationship between RMR 
value and support parameters can ensure the quick acquisition of sup -
port schemes. 
In addition to the empirical approach to assess obtained RMR data, 
BIM is also adopted to aggregate multi-source heterogeneous data dur -
ing construction such as design changes and construction progress, 
which are actually also crucial for accurate JIT design of rock tunnel. 
From one hand, BIM approach has undergone to a growing interest in 
tunnel engineering in the last few years for the evident benefit in terms 
of time and efficiency ( Fabozzi et al., 2021 ), and from the other hand, 
BIM allows to facilitate the exchange and interoperability of information 
in digital format ( Ryu et al., 2015 ), thus allowing stakeholders to rapidly 
and formally modify or evaluate design schemes. Hence, BIM approach 
is not only consistent with the aim of JIT design and DT, but also acts as 
an important part in DT paradigm as it facilitates data exchange and 
improve data interoperability. 
Specifically, an automatic parametric modeling method of rock 
tunnel is proposed based on Revit , which can realize automatic updating 
of the model during construction using the engineering project progress 
and construction data, to provide guidance for JIT design and realize 
construction organization management. Then, a digital BIM platform 
( Zhu et al., 2018 ) is established to integrate multi-source heterogeneous 
data (such as TSP data, geological model, and survey data), display BIM 
model, and analyze the empirical results of support changes. 
Based on the interpreted RMR value and the proposed paradigm of 
DT-enabled JIT design of rock tunnel, a BIM platform was established for 
Laoying Tunnel, where the DTE of geological conditions were depicted 
using GIS, and the establishment and updating of tunnel and support 
structure were implemented using parametric automatic modeling 
method. Spatial relationships between tunnel structure, support struc -
ture and geological model were established using DTRs mentioned in 
Fig. 4 . Moreover, the BIM environment also integrated multi-source data 
according to the DTR of information aggregation. The support and 
tunnel model is presented in Fig. 12 (a) , and the BIM platform inte -
grating GIS and multi-source data is presented in Fig. 12 (b) . 
The application of such DT-enabled JIT design of Laoying Tunnel 
appears effective, and both design efficiency and accuracy can be 
guaranteed. Based on the BIM platform, several functions have been 
implemented: 1) automatic support determination using updated RMR 
value based on TSP data and empirical method; 2) automatic updating of 
tunnel support model for visualization and further analysis; 3) quick 
inquiry of geological conditions of tunnel excavation face using GIS data 
and other preliminary investigation data, which can assist the JIT design 
of tunnel support parameters; 4) visualization of construction progress 
and design schemes for end-users and stakeholders. 
Compared with conventional design practice using manual sketch 
and empirical analysis, the proposed JIT design of Laoying Tunnel has 
several advantages. Firstly, the time has been greatly saved. According 
to experienced engineers on site, conventional design practice may need 
2 – 3 days to complete the final design as manual sketch of tunnel face can 
take as long as 4 h, and the sketch results need to be sent to and

4. Limitations and future work 
A conceptual framework of JIT design of rock tunnel and its imple -
mentation in a practical rock tunnel are presented in this paper, and 
results indicate that implementation of the proposed JIT design of rock 
tunnel outperforms conventional approaches in terms of time con -
sumption and design accuracy. However, admittedly, this study bears 
several limitations that are noteworthy. 
First of all, the proposed conceptual framework is based on previous 
studies on tunnel support design, and is validated using TSP data and 
intelligent algorithms to interpret and aggregate data. However,

design of rock tunnel is also presented.

further improve the performance of JIT design of rock tunnel 
support. The SERIC modeling method is proposed and integrated 
into JIT design of rock tunnel support to improve the design ef -

design of rock tunnel for better comprehension and application of 
DT in JIT design of rock tunnel. 
(3) The proposed DT-enabled JIT design method of rock tunnel has 
been successfully implemented in a practical engineering project, 
where TSP is used as data acquisition method, and corresponding 
interpretation and aggregation have been conducted. Compared 
with conventional design practice using manual sketch and 
empirical analysis, the proposed JIT design of Laoying Tunnel 
outperforms in terms of time consumption (2–3 days vs 4 h) and

and stakeholders.
