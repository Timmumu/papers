ABSTRACT
In view of the shortcomings of traditional slope safety monitoring methods, such as low calculation efciency and poor real-time
performance, this paper proposes a digital twin modelling method for slopes based on a surrogate model. Through a stacking
integrated learning model combined with a multidimensional error analysis mechanism and adaptive parameter optimisation
strategy, a high-precision surrogate model for slope displacement prediction was constructed. Compared with the real dis-
placement data obtained from laboratory experiments and digital image technology (DIC), the prediction error of the optimised
surrogate model is reduced from 20% to 5%. In this study, a digital twin system of the Donglingxin landslide was successfully
constructed, and the deformation process from 2010 to 2022 was dynamically displayed. The results show that the deformation of
the Donglingxin landslide clearly experienced acceleration�stabilisation, and the displacement rate frst increased from 12.5 mm/y
to 15.5 mm/y and then decreased to 11.8 mm/y, indicating that the landslide entered a relatively stable deformation stage.
1 | Introduction
Landslides are among the most common and destructive geo-
logical disasters worldwide. The global spatial distribution of
major landslide disasters is shown in Figure1 [1]. The geological
environment in China is complex, especially landslide disasters,
which have become among the main types of geological disasters.
According to the data of the frst national comprehensive risk
survey of natural disasters, 132,000 hidden landslide hazards
have been identifed and registered nationwide, which shows
that the distribution of landslide disasters in China is very ex-
tensive and that the demand for landslide monitoring and early
warning is urgent. Landslides are usually caused by the coupling
of a variety of complex factors, such as earthquakes, rainfall and
climate change [2], and their occurrence is very concealed and
sudden. In addition, their development law is complex, and
accurate determination of the early warning threshold is difcult,
which presents great challenges for slope stability monitoring
and risk prevention and control. Landslides and other geological
disasters not only severely damage the surrounding ecological
environment but also directly endanger the infrastructure and
safety of people’s lives and property. Therefore, it is highly im-
portant to carry out real-time safety monitoring of slopes and
comprehensively and dynamically determine their deformation
state and development trend to efectively reduce disaster losses
and improve the ability to prevent and mitigate disasters.
Traditional slope safety monitoring technology (such as manual
inspection, total station monitoring and displacement metre
monitoring) has difculty addressing the challenges associated
with landslide prevention and measurement. In recent years,
slope safety monitoring methods have increasingly tended to be
digital and intelligent, gradually forming a multidimensional
collaborative monitoring method of space (synthetic aperture
radar, optical remote sensing, etc.)–space (UAV tilt photography,
ground laser scanning, etc.)–Earth (global satellite navigation,

With the rapid development of digital technology, digital twin
technology is playing an increasingly important role in slope
safety monitoring [10,11]. The digital twin model of a slope
can continuously refect the actual state of the slope on the
basis of real-time monitoring data, which plays an important
role in comprehensively understanding the overall situation of
the landslide and determining the state of the slope. Dong
et al. [12] used displacement monitoring data to directly drive
the dynamic quantitative evaluation of slope safety, and the
error was controlled within 1%. Ju et al. [13] developed
a digital twin model that integrates rainfall data and disaster
consequences and performed real-time simulations based on
position movement. Shao et al. [14] proposed a method for
constructing a dam digital twin platform based on next gen-
eration modelling technology (NGM) that could visualise
detection results in real time. Wang et al. [15] proposed
a method to extract a one-dimensional geometric model from
point cloud data and successfully constructed a digital twin
model of a long-span structure. To ensure real-time
communication between the physical model and the virtual
model and to consider the accuracy and efciency of the
model, a surrogate model was introduced into this feld, and
signifcant progress has been made [16–18]. Zhou et al. [19]
established a digital twin model of the bridge temperature
feld based on a particle swarm optimisation (PSO) algorithm
and realised accurate prediction of the overall temperature
feld. In addition, the generation countermeasure network
(GAN) [20], physical information neural network (PINN) [21]
and a variety of stacking algorithms [22,23] are widely used in
the construction of surrogate models, which further promotes
development in this direction.
Although digital twin technology based on surrogate models has
been widely used in slope safety monitoring, the current research
still has the following gaps: (1) in slope safety monitoring, the
methods of numerical simulation analysis and monitoring data
analysis are relatively mature, but real-time monitoring data and
numerical simulations are not integrated for comprehensive
monitoring; (2) the prediction accuracy of common surrogate
models is often insufcient when dealing with highly nonlinear
and complex scenarios of slope deformation and (3) existing
models often lack adaptive optimisation mechanisms based on
real-time monitoring data. In view of these research gaps, this
study proposes a digital twin modelling method for slopes based
on a surrogate model and its adaptive optimisation ability, with
the aim being to replace expensive numerical simulations with
high-precision surrogate models and directly derive the full-feld
displacement of slopes from limited monitoring data. The main
contributions of this paper are as follows:
1. In terms of algorithm development, a novel adaptive sur-
rogate modelling framework is proposed. Unlike traditional
static models, this framework integrates a multidimen-
sional error-driven parameter optimisation strategy and
a noise-resilient weighting mechanism. These algorithmic
improvements enable the model to dynamically self-correct
against the “Sim-to-Real” gap, signifcantly improving
prediction accuracy in complex geological environments.

2 | Digital Twin-Driven Framework and Surrogate
Model Optimisation Method
2.1 | Digital Twin Collaborative Driving
Framework for Slopes
The digital twin model is a system that integrates physics, data
and services. The diferences between the digital twin model and
the numerical simulation model; the pure data-driven model are
shown in Table1. A numerical simulation model is a high-fdelity
model based on a physical mechanism that generates high-
quality data by solving partial diferential equations, but its
computational cost is high, and it has difculty meeting the
requirements of real-time analysis. The pure data-driven model
directly learns the relationship between the input and output
from the monitoring data without the constraint of a physical
mechanism. Although its calculation efciency is high, it
strongly depends on the quality and quantity of monitoring data,
which contradicts the fact that the number of slope monitoring
points is limited. In this study, a numerical simulation model is
used to construct and update the high-precision virtual slope, and
a pure data-driven model (i.e., a surrogate model) ensures the
real-time calculation of the digital twin model.
The framework of the slope digital twin monitoring system
proposed in this study is shown in Figure2. The framework is
composed of fve core parts: an actual slope, a virtual slope,
a slope surrogate model, an Internet of Things (IOT) data
acquisition system [24] and a visual display platform.
Through the dynamic interaction and efcient integrated
processing of multisource heterogeneous data, the system
achieves full-range real-time monitoring of the actual state of
the slope.
As the physical foundation of the digital twin system, the actual
slope contains real geological structure information, such as
information related to accurate stratigraphic division, fracture
occurrence and rock and soil mechanical parameters. The
digitisation of its geometric and physical properties can be
achieved by UAV tilt photography, geological section modelling,
laser scanning and other technologies. A variety of sensors are
arranged inside and on the surface of the slope, including in-
clinometers, earth pressure boxes, vibration accelerometers, rain
gauges and displacement metres, to continuously collect real-
time dynamic data such as surface deformation displacement,
internal earth pressure, pore water pressure and environmental
parameters and provide real-time data input for the construction
and updating of virtual slopes.
As the digital mapping of the actual slope, the virtual slope is
constructed on the basis of the mechanical parameters of the rock
and soil, geological structure characteristics and real-time
sensing data. It can dynamically refect the actual state of
a slope and predict and analyse its future behaviour. It is the core
of numerical simulation and state deduction.
As a lightweight calculation module in the framework, the slope
surrogate model extracts key features from the high-dimensional
simulation data generated by the virtual slope based on deep
learning technology and constructs a high-precision approximate
calculation model. The module has the characteristics of rapid
response and low demand for computing resources. It can
quickly output stability assessments and early warning in-
formation in scenarios where high-fdelity numerical simula-
tions are difcult to run in real time and signifcantly enhance
the real-time performance of the digital twin system.
The IOT system performs data transmission from the actual slope
to the digital twin platform and relies on wireless communication
technologies such as Lora, 5G, Nb-IOT and edge computing
methods to transmit multisource heterogeneous monitoring data
to the cloud processing centre in real time. In the process of data
integration, the system performs cleaning and fusion to ensure
the reliability and consistency of the data and provide stable data
support for the dynamic updating of the virtual slope and
surrogate model.
As the core interface of human‒computer interactions, the visual
monitoring platform integrates real-time monitoring data,
a three-dimensional virtual model and prediction analysis re-
sults. With the help of a three-dimensional rendering engine, the
slope state is visually presented in graphical form and supports
the visualisation of the risk level, historical data backtracking and

In stacking ensemble learning, the selection and combination of
basic learners have a key impact on the fnal prediction performance
of the model. In accordance with the nonlinear and high-
dimensional characteristics of slope deformation and displacement
data, fve machine learning algorithms, namely, the BP neural
network, random forest (RF), extreme gradient lifting (XGBoost),
support vector regression (SVR) and radial basis function (RBF)
neural network, are preliminarily selected in this study. The BP
neural network has strong nonlinear ftting and feature learning
ability and is thus suitable for capturing fne patterns in complex
data; the RF is insensitive to noise and outliers and has excellent
generalisation ability and good robustness; XGBoost can efectively
reduce the model deviation through the gradient lifting mechanism,
which can signifcantly improve the prediction accuracy; and on the
basis of the principle of structural risk minimisation, SVR can obtain
the global optimal and marginal maximum solution; the RBF neural
network is good at dealing with local approximation problems, and
its physical intuition is strong. It is efectively complementary to the
BP neural network in terms of the activation function and structure.
This heterogeneous combination of basic learners can ensure that
various models produce diverse prediction errors, integrate and
correct them through meta-learners, and fnally construct a more
powerful integrated model. In this study, LightGBM is selected as the
meta-learner because it achieves a good balance of prediction ac-
curacy, computational efciency and generalisation performance
and can efectively integrate the output of the basic model to further
improve the performance of the overall model.

on error analysis by comparing the diferences between their pre-
dicted values and the actual monitoring data at corresponding lo-
cations. This method includes three main components: an error
feature analysis mechanism, adaptive parameter adjustment, and
model updating and retraining.

3 | Verifcation and Materials
3.1 | Numerical Simulation
To expand the training samples to compensate for the limited
amount of actual monitoring data, in this study, a systematic
numerical simulation analysis is performed, and the node dis-

2.91 × 10
5 m 2; the landslide is characterised as an “armchair-like”

fgure shows the main structural components of the landslide
mass, including the sliding mass, sliding belt, sliding surface,
sliding bed and other elements. The sliding mass is composed
mainly of gravel and block stone, with obvious stratifcation
characteristics from top to bottom: the upper part is a silty clay

taining gravel, and the lower part is a silty clay layer containing

muddy sandy gravel rock samples or gravelly clay, with a maximum
burial depth of 145 m. The bedrock of the sliding bed is mainly thick

5.2 | Landslide Monitoring
The natural water level of the Donglingxin reach of the
Sanbanxi hydropower station is 425 m, and the water level in
the design is 475 m. The reservoir has been impounded since
January 2006. From April to July 2007, the reservoir water
level in this reachrose rapidly from 430 to 470 m, an increase of
40 m in three months, with an average daily rate of increase of
approximately 0.5 m. In late July 2007, cracks appeared on the
surface at the rear edge of the reservoir bank slope, accompanied by
signs of geological disasters such as wall dislocation and house tilt.
Since 2008, a systematic geological survey has been carried out on
the slope of the reservoir bank, and deformation monitoring began
in 2009. The layout of the monitoring points is shown in Figure23. A
total of 13 monitoring points are established, of which DLXG04 is
located outside the landslide, and the remaining 12 points are lo-

5.3 | Landslide Monitoring Results
On the basis of the digital twin model and real-time monitoring
data of the Donglingxin landslide, a displacement nephogram of
the landslide is generated, as shown in Figure27. The slope
displacement nephogram in 2010 is shown in Figure27(a), with
a maximum displacement of approximately 54 mm, refecting the
initial deformation characteristics of the landslide; Figure27(b)
shows the cloud map for 2014, with a maximum displacement of
approximately 103 mm and a signifcant increase in displace-
ment within four years, with a growth rate of approximately
12.25 mm/y; Figure27(c)shows the 2016 cloud map. The
maximum displacement increased to 134 mm, indicating that the
deformation rate further increased, reaching 15.5 mm/y, and the
landslide entered the accelerated development stage; Figure27(d)
shows the cloud map for 2021, with the maximum displacement
reaching 193 mm, revealing that the landslide displacement con-
tinued to increase, but the growth rate slightly decreased to
11.8 mm/y. This series of cloud maps clearly shows the deformation
law of the Donglingxin landslide over more than 10 years: the
displacement increases annually, and the rate of increase frst in-
creases but then decreases with time. The results show that the
Donglingxin landslide experienced “acceleration–stabilisation” and
entered a relatively stable deformation stage.
6 | Conclusion
In this paper, a digital twin modelling method based on an
optimised surrogate model is proposed for slope monitoring; it
achieves accurate real-time mapping and visualisation of the
slope state driven by data from limited monitoring points and
efectively considers the calculation efciency and response
accuracy of the digital twin system. The main conclusions are as
follows:
1. To address the problems of high computational complexity
and poor real-time performance of digital twin models of
slopes, a surrogate model based on stacking-based
ensemble learning is constructed. On the training set, the
goodness of ftR

and generalisability. Furthermore, a dynamic optimisation
mechanism that integrates multidimensional error analy-
sis, adaptive parameter adjustment and error sample
weighting is proposed to achieve continuous performance
improvement of the surrogate model in practical
applications.

which verifes the efectiveness of the proposed optimisa-

the model.
