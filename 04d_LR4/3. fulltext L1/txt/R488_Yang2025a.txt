Abstract
This study presents an innovative performance prediction and optimization method for prefabricated bridge structures, 
which integrates the strengths of Physical Information Neural Network (PINN) and Building Information Modeling (BIM). 
By combining the computational power of PINN for solving structural mechanics problems with BIM’s data management 
and modeling capabilities in construction engineering, a multi-layer, multi-dimensional intelligent framework for predic-
tion and optimization is developed. BIM models provide crucial data, including geometric details, material properties, 
and construction process information, which are used to create a digital twin of the bridge. Leveraging PINN’s robust 
fitting ability, physical laws such as stress, strain, and equilibrium equations are incorporated into the neural network to 
predict the bridge’s performance under various operational conditions. The integration of physical constraints during 
PINN training enhances both the accuracy and reliability of the predictions. Subsequently, optimization algorithms refine 
the bridge design based on these predictions, ensuring improvements in performance while meeting key criteria such 
as safety, cost-effectiveness, and environmental impact. This method offers a scientifically rigorous and highly accurate 
approach to supporting the design and construction of prefabricated bridges, advancing intelligent construction and 
sustainable building practices. Experimental results demonstrate that the integrated PINN-BIM model outperforms tradi-
tional methods, exhibiting superior accuracy, generalization capability, and offering a novel solution for efficient bridge 
engineering construction and performance optimization.

2 Related work
2.1 Development and application of physics‑informed neural networks (PINN)
Physical Information Neural Network (PINN) is an innovative method that integrates physical laws and deep learning 
techniques [6]. It embeds physical equations directly into the training process of neural networks (NNs), enabling the 
model to not only learn patterns from data but also automatically satisfy physical constraints during the solving pro -
cess. This approach allows for a more accurate and complex representation of practical engineering problems. In this 
context, physical laws refer to the fundamental equations that describe physical phenomena, and deep learning refers 
to advanced algorithms that simulate the human brain’s neural structure for pattern recognition and problem-solving. 
The architecture of the Physical Information Neural Network (PINN) is shown in Fig.  1.
In Fig. 1, “PDE” denotes the partial differential equation, “BC” denotes the boundary condition, and “IC” denotes the 
initial condition.
Unlike traditional deep learning methods, PINN does not rely on a large amount of annotated data, but can ensure 
the physical consistency of the model output by incorporating known physical laws (such as fluid mechanics equations, 
structural mechanics equations, etc.) into the loss function of the network. This method is particularly suitable for solving 
problems that are difficult to efficiently solve through traditional numerical methods, especially when dealing with com-
plex boundary conditions, nonlinear phenomena, or large-scale data. PINN exhibits unique advantages. In recent years, 
PINN has made significant progress in multiple engineering fields, especially in fluid mechanics, structural mechanics, 
and other fields, and has begun to be widely applied. In fluid mechanics, PINN is used to solve complex fluid dynamics 
problems such as Navier Stokes equations, achieving efficient flow simulation and prediction. In the field of structural 
mechanics, PINN also performs well, especially in bridge engineering, and has gradually been applied in structural health 
monitoring, stress analysis, material fatigue prediction, and other aspects. For example, researchers use PINN to moni-
tor the stress state of bridge structures in real time, which can effectively predict the deformation behavior of bridges 
under different load conditions, identify possible structural failures in advance, and provide scientific basis for bridge 
maintenance and management. In addition, PINN is also applied in the bridge design phase to help engineers optimize 
structural design, reduce material waste, and improve the safety and economy of the design [7 ].
In the future, with the improvement of computing power and the development of artificial intelligence technology, 
the application prospects of PINN in the engineering field will become increasingly broad. In the future, PINN will not 
only provide more accurate performance predictions, but also play an important role in optimizing design, construction 
management, and other aspects. Especially when combined with advanced technologies such as Building Information 
Modeling (BIM), it will further promote the development of intelligent construction and improve the overall performance 
and efficiency of bridge engineering.

2.2 Building information modeling (BIM) and bridge structure design
Building Information Modeling (BIM) is a technology-driven approach that integrates detailed data from the design, 
construction, and operation stages of buildings through digital and 3D modeling technologies. This integration greatly 
enhances the efficiency and accuracy of bridge structure design. In the traditional bridge design process, designers often 
rely on manually drawn drawings and their experience to carry out scheme designs. However, this method is prone to 
information asymmetry or deviation during construction and maintenance, leading to wasted project costs and time. 
The introduction of BIM facilitates collaborative management and real-time updates of all data, materials, construction 
methods, and post-operation processes related to bridges on a unified platform. This ensures the accuracy and trace -
ability of the design, construction, and operation processes.
In this context:
BIM stands for Building Information Modeling, a digital representation of physical and functional characteristics of a 
building or infrastructure.
3D Modeling Technologies refer to software tools and techniques that create three-dimensional digital models of 
structures.
Design, Construction, and Operation stages represent the entire lifecycle of a building or infrastructure project, from 
conceptualization to completion and ongoing maintenance.
Information Asymmetry refers to a situation where some stakeholders possess more or different information than 
others, often leading to errors or inefficiencies.
Collaborative Management involves multiple stakeholders working together, often in real-time, to manage the pro -
ject’s data and progress.
BIM Bridge 3D Modeling is shown in Fig. 2.
In bridge engineering, BIM can not only optimize structural design, but also simulate the construction process of 
different design schemes, predict possible risks and problems [8 ]. For example, in the design phase, BIM can help engi-
neers create precise 3D models, evaluate the safety, stability, and economy of different bridge design schemes, and 
determine the best design solution in the shortest possible time [9]. During the construction process, BIM can also serve 
as a guide for construction personnel, providing accurate construction paths, schedule planning, and material manage-
ment information, effectively reducing errors and rework, and ensuring the controllability of construction quality and 
progress [10]. BIM still plays an important role in the operation stage of bridges, by monitoring the structural health 
status of bridges in real time, warning possible faults in a timely manner, guiding bridge maintenance and repair work, 
thereby extending the service life of bridges and reducing maintenance costs [11]. Especially in the design of prefabri-
cated bridges, BIM plays a crucial role [12]. Prefabricated bridges rely on prefabricated components and modular design, 
which can significantly improve construction efficiency and reduce on-site construction uncertainty [13]. Through BIM 
technology, designers can accurately simulate the installation process of prefabricated components, optimize the size 
and combination of components, and ensure the accuracy of assembly and smooth construction [14]. In addition, BIM 
can effectively manage the material requirements and inventory of prefabricated bridges, avoid resource waste, and

2.3 Performance prediction and optimization methods for prefabricated bridge structures
The performance prediction and optimization methods of prefabricated bridges traditionally rely on finite element 
analysis (FEA) and optimization algorithms. These methods can predict the stress behavior and deformation of bridges 
to a certain extent through discretization of bridge structures, simplification of boundary conditions, and assumptions, 
but there are still some limitations [16].
Firstly, finite element analysis requires a large amount of computational resources when dealing with complex geo -
metric shapes and nonlinear material behavior, especially in bridge structure optimization, where the computational 
workload grows exponentially, resulting in significant computation time and resource consumption [17]. Secondly, 
traditional methods often rely on simplifying assumptions when dealing with complex boundary conditions or multiple 
influencing factors, which may lead to bias and low accuracy in the results, especially in high-level design stages [18]. 
In addition, the finite element method also faces certain challenges in multi-objective optimization or real-time sched-
uling, and cannot quickly respond to changes in engineering requirements [19]. In recent years, data-driven artificial 
intelligence technologies, especially deep learning and neural network (NN) methods, have been widely applied in 
the field of bridge structure analysis and optimization [20]. These technologies utilize a large amount of experimental 
data, on-site monitoring data, or simulation data to more accurately describe the structural performance of bridges 
and provide intelligent decision support for bridge design and optimization [21]. Neural networks can automatically 
extract potential patterns and patterns from large amounts of data by learning the mechanical properties and dynamic 
behavior of bridge structures, thus compensating for the shortcomings of traditional analysis methods [22]. On this basis, 
the combination of Physical Information Neural Network (PINN) and Building Information Modeling (BIM) has opened 
a new chapter in predicting and optimizing the performance of prefabricated bridge structures [23]. PINN can improve 
the accuracy and reliability of models while maintaining computational efficiency by embedding physical laws such as 
mechanics equations, heat conduction equations, etc. into neural networks [24]. It can predict structural performance 
in real-time through data-driven methods, combined with the actual physical characteristics of the bridge [25]. For 
example, PINN can quickly predict the stress and strain distribution of bridges under different working conditions and 
possible structural problems by inputting their geometric parameters, material properties, and load conditions, providing 
a basis for subsequent design optimization. BIM, as the core platform for prefabricated bridge design and construction, 
provides rich data support for the application of PINN. Through BIM models, engineers can accurately obtain geometric 
and material information of various parts of the bridge, as well as the interrelationships between components. These 
data can be directly input into the PINN model to achieve more accurate performance prediction. In addition, the BIM 
platform can integrate dynamic data such as bridge construction progress, material usage, and environmental changes, 
providing PINN with real-time updated data sources to further improve the accuracy of prediction and optimization.
In short, combining BIM and PINN methods can not only provide more accurate performance predictions during the 
design phase, but also monitor the structural status of bridges in real-time during the construction phase, and optimize 
the service life and safety of bridges through prediction and maintenance plans during the operation phase. Performance 
in this context includes factors such as stress states, deformation, and displacement, and these factors are evaluated 
across different stages: during the construction phase, where the structural integrity and adaptability to construction 
processes are critical; during the operability phase, focusing on the bridge’s performance under normal usage and load 
conditions; and in the case of extreme events, where the behavior of the structure under unusual or disaster condi-
tions is also assessed. This fusion method provides intelligent management support for the design, construction, and 
maintenance of prefabricated bridges throughout their entire lifecycle, making bridge design and construction more 
efficient, economical, and safe.

2.4 Main research content and innovative points of this article
This article proposes a method for predicting and optimizing the performance of prefabricated bridge structures by 
combining Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINN) with Building Information Modeling (BIM), aiming to enhance 
decision support capabilities during bridge design, construction, and maintenance phases. By integrating the high-
precision geometric, material, and construction information provided by BIM, as well as the advantages of PINN in 
handling physical laws and data-driven models, this paper proposes a new intelligent framework that can improve the 
performance prediction accuracy and optimization effect of prefabricated bridges while ensuring computational effi-
ciency. This method not only considers the static and dynamic behavior of the structure, but also dynamically adjusts 
prediction and optimization schemes to cope with complex working conditions and environmental changes, thereby 
achieving full lifecycle management of the bridge.
The innovation of this article is mainly reflected in the following three aspects:
1) Designed a framework that integrates PINN and BIM, fully utilizing the precise data provided by BIM models to guide 
the construction and training of PINN models, overcoming the shortcomings of traditional methods in geometric 
and material property accuracy;
2) A deep learning optimization method based on physical constraints is proposed, which embeds physical laws into 
the deep learning network, improves the accuracy of structural performance prediction, and avoids the overfitting 
problem that may occur in data-driven models;
3) The effectiveness of this method in optimizing the performance of bridge structures has been verified through 
experiments, demonstrating its feasibility and superiority in practical applications, providing new ideas and techni-
cal support for the intelligent design and construction of bridge engineering.
3 Bridge structural performance prediction method integrating PINN and BIM
3.1 Design of PINN and BIM Fusion framework
In order to achieve accurate prediction and optimization of prefabricated bridge structures, this paper designs a frame-
work that integrates Physical Information Neural Network (PINN) and Building Information Modeling (BIM). This frame -
work consists of two core steps: data integration and model training. It can effectively combine the high-precision 
geometric information and material properties provided by BIM, while utilizing the advantages of PINN in processing 
physical laws and data-driven models to improve the accuracy and efficiency of structural performance prediction.
In this context:
Geometric Information represents the precise measurements and shapes of the bridge structure, typically provided 
in 3D models.
Material Properties include the characteristics of the materials used in the construction of the bridge, such as strength, 
elasticity, and durability.
Physical Laws are fundamental equations that describe the natural behavior of materials and structures under differ-
ent conditions.
Data-driven Models refer to machine learning or artificial intelligence algorithms that extract patterns from data to 
predict outcomes without explicit physical formulas.
Model Training involves the process of using data to improve the performance of a predictive model by adjusting its 
parameters.
Data Integration is the process of combining different types of data (e.g., geometric, material properties) into a unified 
format that can be processed by the model.

Modeling (BIM) for predicting and optimizing the performance of prefabricated bridge structures, this paper designed a

the model can accurately predict the behavior of the bridge under different working conditions [52, 53].

and simpler joint connections. The medium bridge structure is designed for moderate spans and medium traffic loads, 
usually incorporating standard reinforced concrete and higher-grade steel bars. This bridge includes more robust precast 
concrete beams and girder sections and requires more complex joint designs to manage increased traffic and environ-
mental stresses. The heavy bridge structure, on the other hand, is designed for large spans and heavy-duty loads, such 
as highway and railway traffic. It incorporates high-performance concrete, advanced steel alloys, and heavily reinforced
