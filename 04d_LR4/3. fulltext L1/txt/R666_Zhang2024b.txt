ABSTRACT
The geometric digitization of cable dome structures using Point Cloud Datasets (PCD) holds significant impor -
tance as it serves as a foundational step in constructing the Digital Twin of such structures. However, accurately 
detecting the positions of structural joints for effective construction of a geometric digital twin from PCD remains 
a challenging task. To address this issue, we propose an enhanced algorithm and system solution for joint 
detection based on target recognition. The method leverages a novel shape estimation model to identify struc -
tural components by randomly sampling the minimum set of points from the point cloud. Subsequently, 
connection joints and their coordinates are determined according to the spatial relationships of the components. 
This enables the digital reconstruction of the structure based on the topological relationships of the joints, ul -
timately facilitating the establishment of a geometric digital twin. The efficacy and reliability of the proposed 
method are validated through a case study involving PCD from a scaled cable dome structure, demonstrating 
outstanding performance in joint detection and fitting with accuracy levels exceeding 99 %. Based on the results 
of joint detection, the feasibility of model topology reconstruction is corroborated, and the potential for accu -
rately assessing joint deviations is explored. These research findings serve to advance the progress of digital twins 
in large-span dome structures, facilitating enhanced analysis and inference regarding structural performance, 
safety, and stability within the digital twin framework.
1. Introduction
The cable dome structure, characterized by its efficient material 
usage, high load-bearing capacity, and ability to achieve large spans 
with elegance, finds extensive applications in numerous national land -
mark large-span public buildings. From the standpoint of engineering 
safety assessment, the substantial span of such structures renders joint 
deviations pivotal factors impacting prestress and structural stability [1, 
2] . The endeavor to construct a real-time analysis model of structural 
performance holds immense significance, with the crucial prerequisite 
being the establishment of accurate and efficient geometric Digital Twin 
(gDT) of the structures concerned. Digital twins (DTs) have demon -
strated their significance in various phases of the building life cycle, 
including construction, maintenance, and reconstruction [3 – 7] . The 
foundation of gDT modeling rests upon creating a precise digital 
three-dimensional representation rooted in the physical realm. In the 
context of cable dome structures, the precise location of key joints stands 
out as the most crucial and tangible factor for mirroring the physical 
entity. The objective of developing a gDT serves a dual purpose: firstly, 
to enable rapid detection of joint deviations and early warning of po -
tential security risks; secondly, the gDT serves as the fundamental model 
and primary initiative for mechanical DT projects. The construction of 
gDTs for tensioned cable dome structures rely on the PCD. This necessity 
arises from the presence of numerous joints within these structures, 
whose spatial coordinates cannot be swiftly measured by traditional 
methods, such as total stations. PCDs are characterized by its high pre -
cision, resolution, and dimensionality, offering rich geometric infor -
mation that holds significant potential for extracting the intricate 
features of dome structures.
Current commercial point cloud processing solutions lack the 
inherent capability to generate gDTs for dome structures. Typically, 
these solutions focus on specific functionalities such as component shape 
recognition and segmentation. Moreover, these processes often involve 
semi-automatic or manual procedures (e.g., CloudCompare). Manual 
selection of similar points for joint position representation hampers 
automatic modeling, and establishing joint connections through

Establishing the gDTs of cable dome structures relies on accurately 
detecting components spatial relationship and fitting the spatial posi -
tions of key joints. Analogous to the detection of connectivity among 
various functional units in building structures, topological information 
effectively facilitates the construction of the building ’ s gDT [4] . Recent 
research indicates that detecting and fitting joints in large-span steel 
structures yield various practical applications. For instance, determining 
the degree of structural components bending based on joint coordinates 
[8] , utilizing fitting results for joint segmentation and topological in -
formation in establishing BIM models for dome structures [9] , and 
deriving construction errors from joint position coordinates [10] . 
Consequently, the spatial location and topological relationships of joints 
constitute a pivotal step in the digitization of cable dome structures.
The process of structural digitization, particularly in the context of 
cable dome structures, necessitates the detection and segmentation of 
target objects, along with the establishment of digital spatial relation -
ships. Target detection and segmentation involve identifying the geo -
metric entity shape within the PCDs and segmenting the object based on 
the inter-entity relationships. In the case of dome structures, the target 
object encompasses all the constituent components forming the struc -
ture. While existing methods can effectively detect and classify certain 
types of geometric objects, they currently lack the capability to auto -
matically fit key joints based on the detection and segmentation results, 
or to automatically establish the connection relationships between 
joints.
Therefore, we propose the introduction of the concept of a gDT for 
cable dome structures. Central to this is the pivotal challenge of joint 
position detection, which essentially entails the precise fitting of spatial 
coordinates and positional relationships of all joints based on PCD. To 
address this challenge, we present a comprehensive process that spans 
from the extraction of low-level geometric information to the estab -
lishment of high-level spatial structural topology, as depicted in Fig. 1 . 
The extraction of geometric information encompasses target object 
detection, component segmentation, joint coordinate fitting. Mean -
while, spatial structural topology relies on the spatial topological re -
lationships among joints to establish gDTs. Notably, our proposed 
method integrates joint fitting and inter-joint relationship connection 
within the same solution, thereby significantly advancing the realization 
of digital automation. In summary, our contributions are outlined as 
follows:
• A proposal is outlined for constructing a gDT for cable dome struc -
tures, based on joint position detection. This solution integrates 
target detection, segmentation, joint fitting, and automatic topology 
reconstruction functionalities.
• The minimum set shape estimation model of point cloud is proposed 
innovatively. This model ensures computational accuracy and effi -
ciency while mitigating the impact of noise points.
• Innovatively introduced into the target recognition framework are 
the random sampling constraint mechanism and adaptive termina -
tion mechanism, enhancing efficiency and precision.
• We validate the effectiveness of the proposed method through a case 
study and explore its potential applications in joint deviations.
2. Background and related work
Digital twin technology offers an effective and dependable approach 
for the rapid and precise detection of structural joint positions. It facil -
itates the transformation of safety detection from labor-intensive pro -
cesses to digital automation, while also supporting the information 
management of large-span structures. However, challenges remain in 
creating DTs for cable dome structures and meeting the demands of joint 
detection. Traditional methods like total station measurements have not 
met the desired efficiency levels [11] . Joint detection using PCD is 
mainly manual or semi-automated [12] . There is an urgent requirement 
to develop algorithms that can meet the needs of digital processing and 
information management in structural domains.
For the joint detection in cable dome structures, the fundamental 
objective lies in detecting the target object accurately. Currently, 
methods for target object detection can be categorized into model- 
driven and data-driven approaches. The model-driven method ach -
ieves object recognition and segmentation in PCD by optimizing 
parameter input according to the mathematical representation of the 
target object. For instance, a predominant feature of cable dome struc -
tures comprises tubular components, effectively represented as cylinders 
within PCD. Automated techniques, including RANSAC [13,14] , Hough 
transform [15] , and region growth segmentation [16] , are employed for 
object segmentation by leveraging original shape detection methodol -
ogies. The connectivity or adjacency of distinct target objects is estab -
lished through joints, enabling the realization of joint fitting and 
coordinate extraction subsequent to object segmentation. Data-driven 
detection involves inferring patterns from extensive sets of labeled 
data to detect objects. Research indicates that machine learning can 
enable the digital processing of building structures and direct object 
detection from large-scale point clouds [17 – 19] . While this approach is 
not confined by specific environmental constraints, enhancing the 
recognition success rates of diverse objects necessitates the expansion of 
the training dataset.
The application of the conventional model-driven approach to point 
cloud target detection and segmentation has demonstrated favorable

essential information for deriving structural topology. Nevertheless, 
existing research predominantly centers on reverse modeling or defor -

thereby impeding the creation of a true gDT [24] . Simultaneously, the 
mentioned methods cannot be directly applied to establish the gDT of

the most widely employed robust estimation tools. Its widespread 
adoption stems from not only its simplicity but also its capacity to 
furnish reliable parameter estimation even in scenarios where more than 
half of the data comprises outliers. Nevertheless, while the basic RAN -
SAC algorithm is robust, further enhancements are imperative to elevate 
its accuracy, efficiency, and stability [25,26] .
The process of topology reconstruction, predicated on joint detection 
outcomes, represents an ongoing endeavor aimed at automatically 
interlinking an explicit three-dimensional geometric model through the 
topological associations within joint space to facilitate visualization. 
Effectively addressing this challenge hinges on establishing joint corre -
spondences and articulating them through mathematical frameworks. 
Prior research has employed direct array or sequential representations 
to express the planar distribution of joints, proving highly efficient and 
rapid in delineating an optimal structural geometric model [27] . 
Nevertheless, the geometric model constructed based on the detected 
joint coordinates cannot be directly resolved using the above method 
due to the inherent deviation of joints from their intended positions. 
Additionally, topology reconstruction can be applied by integrating 
model information directly into finite element software for efficient 3D 
geometric modeling, reducing manual effort and time needed for 
modeling tasks.
According to related work, the following research gaps have been 
summarized:
• Currently, there is no comprehensive and systematic fully automated 
method for detecting joint positions and establishing geometric 
digital twins of cable dome structures.
• Data-driven object detection struggles to achieve ideal performance 
with sparse datasets. Model-driven approaches, such as traditional 
RANSAC algorithms, fail to meet the accuracy and efficiency re -
quirements for detecting joints in cable dome structures.
• Implementing automated 3D topology reconstruction based on 
detected joint coordinates is also challenging. Deviations in actual 
joint positions prevent direct application of theoretical topology al -
gorithms for geometric modeling.
This paper aims to provide a systematic and universally applicable 
method for the geometric digital twin (gDT) of cable dome structures, 
while addressing the aforementioned gaps.

Considering the vast scale of initial point cloud, down-sampling is 
necessary to compress data and reduce computational costs. Currently, 
there are several classical point cloud down-sampling methods such as 
voxel grid down-sampling, uniform down-sampling, and octree down- 
sampling. Voxel grid down-sampling computes the centroids of point 
clouds within each grid cell. Consequently, after down-sampling, the 
positions of points in the point cloud may change, as they represent 
centroids or average positions of points within respective grid cells, 
rather than specific points identified by their original IDs in the initial 
point cloud (as shown in Fig. 4 a). While uniform down-sampling theo -
retically preserves original positions, it may exhibit non-uniform effects 
across different regions, as demonstrated in the experimental results of 
this study ( Fig. 4 b) demonstrate this phenomenon. Octree down- 
sampling recursively subdivides the point cloud into parent and child 
relationships, demonstrating superior down-sampling effectiveness 
compared to the above methods. Initially, the maximum scene size is 
determined, and the first cube is established. Unit elements are 
sequentially placed into cubes that can contain them without child 
nodes. If a cube exceeds the minimum voxel size, it is subdivided into 
eight equal parts, distributing all unit elements within it among its eight 
child cubes. Subdivision stops for a child cube containing a nonzero 
number of unit elements identical to its parent cube. All data points 
associated with octree cells are replaced by the cell ’ s center, effectively 
compressing these elements into a single octree cell. Fig. 5 illustrates the 
application of octree-based down-sampling. It should be noted that if 
there are a significant number of noise points in the PCD, noise point 
reduction can be performed using algorithms such as radius filtering, 
statistical filtering, and Gaussian filtering before down-sampling.

3.3. Object component segmentation
The RANSAC (Random Sample Consensus) paradigm extracts shape 
primitives by randomly selecting the minimum set of points from the 
point cloud data and constructing the corresponding shape primitives. 
The minimum set refers to the minimum number of points required to 
uniquely define a geometric primitive of a given type. Model equation 
parameters and error rates are computed using randomly selected points 
to determine whether the established model is optimal, and iterative 
calculations are performed until the best model is achieved. The geo -
metric feature model of interest in this study is the cylinder. The tradi -
tional cylinder fitting method involves using two points with normal 
lines and judging the number of inner points based on the distance 
threshold to obtain the best fitted cylinder [13] . However, this method is 
greatly influenced by the quality of the point cloud. In cases where 
sampling times are consistent, the data set of the cylindrical model

3.3.2. An adaptive termination
The termination criterion of the traditional RANSAC algorithm in -
corporates an adaptive termination (AT) criterion by updating the 
number of iterations N using the iterative interior point rate ε . When N 
exceeds a predetermined threshold, the process is terminated ( Eq. 9 ). 
The interior point ratio refers to the ratio of the number of interior points 
T to the total number of points M . The subset with the highest number of 
interior points is considered the best matching point, which leads to the 
generation of the best model. Since N depends on ε , the interior point 
rate directly influences the termination process. When the number of 
interior points is small, it increases the likelihood of selecting a local 
optimum. To improve efficiency and result accuracy, a double 
termination criterion is adopted. This criterion combines the traditional 
adaptive termination criterion with a similarity criterion and distance 
threshold. The similarity criterion considers the similarity of interior 
points between two consecutive iterations. If the similarity exceeds 
95 %, the algorithm terminates; otherwise, it continues until a certain 
threshold is met.

4. Experiment and results
In this section, we will assess the practical application and efficacy of 
the proposed methodology via a case study. The chosen case is a scaled 
model of a fully assembled cable dome structure, with pertinent struc -
tural design details available in the references [30,31] . The holographic 
point cloud data of the structure was acquired using a laser scanner 
( Fig. 12 ).
4.1. Point cloud processing
Utilizing the module segmentation principle and down-sampling 
method, the acquired PCD of the structure undergoes processing. 
Initially, the PCD is segmented into four modules (M1~M4), as depicted 
in Fig. 13 (a). To streamline the subsequent gDT establishment, all joints 
are systematically numbered following the methodology outlined in 
Section 3.4.2 prior to module subdivision. Subsequently, each module 
undergoes down-sampling using the Octree method. The down-sampling 
results are depicted in Fig. 13 (b), showcasing the quantity of point 
clouds before and after the down-sampling process for each module. Our 
approach leverages the Octree framework (OctFilter / addPoints -
FromInputCloud) within PCL, assigning a unique identifier to each 
voxel. Following extensive experimentation, a voxel size of 2 mm ×
2 mm × 2 mm is selected, and the Octree resolution is fixed at 128 for 
optimal results. An inherent advantage of this approach lies in the hi -
erarchical organization of PCD facilitated by the Octree data structure, 
enhancing down-sampling efficiency while mitigating shape distortion 
or information loss arising from random sampling. The results of module 
segmentation reveal the absence of duplicate joints across modules, 
indicating that module segmentation occurs solely at the component 
level rather than among adjacent joints within each module. With geo -
metric shape features remaining unaltered, down-sampling results in 
approximately a 98 % reduction in the number of points within each 
module ’ s point cloud. This reduction significantly optimizes memory 
utilization and enhances algorithmic efficiency in subsequent stages.

5.5. Limitations of DS-gDT
Although DS-gDT proposed in this paper can effectively solve the 
problem of constructing the geometric Digital Twin of cable dome 
structures, this method has certain limitations. The limitations of this 
method are mainly reflected in the following aspects.
(1) According to the Standard for Design of Steel Structures (GB 
50017 – 2017) [32] , if the eccentricity between the brace member and 
the chord member connection joint of the intersecting joint ( Fig. 23 ) 
does not exceed the limit specified in Eq. (28) , the bending moment 
caused by eccentricity can be disregarded. Therefore, even when the 
intersection point of the component axes is non-singular, the joint po -
sition can still be determined using the method proposed in this paper. 
However, if the eccentricity exceeds the limit defined by Eq. (28) , and 
when calculating the bearing capacity of the compressed chord member, 
the influence of eccentricity must be considered. In such cases, the ec -
centricity value is derived from design information. Automating the 
calculation of eccentricity remains a challenge that requires further 
resolution.

(2) The proposed method in this paper addresses the issue of 
detecting joint coordinates in cable dome structures. However, in

6. Conclusions and future work
In this paper, a method for establishing geometric digital twins (DS- 
gDT) for large-span cable dome structures is proposed, offering a 
comprehensive solution and technical pathway. The primary results are 
outlined as follows:
(1) DS-gDT demonstrates proficiency in automatically fitting joint 
coordinates while accurately identifying and segmenting pivotal 
components within PCD. The segmentation of components serves 
as the fundamental prerequisite for achieving precise joint 
localization. This segmentation process hinges on spatial posi -
tional relationships of components, with joints ’ positions deter -
mined by transitions between distinct components. In this study, 
the success rate of component segmentation is approximately 
51.5 % higher compared to traditional methods. DS-gDT achieves 
joints fitting accuracy of 99 %. Furthermore, the automated 
fitting of joint coordinates, guided by component segmentation 
outcomes, plays a crucial role in the establishment of gDT, 
reducing the time investment required for gDT construction.

ventional software and other gDT solutions available in the
