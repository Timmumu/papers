ABSTRACT 
The impossibility of visual inspection and the complexity of combined loads hamper the quantitative assessment, 
lifetime prediction and control of underground pipelines during their lifecycles. A methodology based on mobile 
augmented reality (MAR) and Brillouin fiber optic sensors (BFOSs) is presented to build a digital twin (DT) for 
underground pipelines. Field experiments were carried out to demonstrate that the proposed method can 
quantitatively assess and predict the structural safety of an underground pipeline from the DT. The results 
demonstrate that the distributed sensor networks can measure important but unpredictable deformations (i.e., 
longitudinal bending and axial thermal strain), the joint data-physics driven model can estimate the structural 
stress state more accurately than the common calculation model, and the MAR-based human – asset interaction 
interface enables more intuitive, efficient, automated operation and maintenance (O & M). In the future, in-line 
robotic systems and localized damage models should be further adopted for lifecycle O & M. 
1. Introduction 
Underground pipelines are widely used engineering assets, and their 
safety management is easily neglected because they are not visible and 
direct structural damage inspection is difficult [ 1 ]. However, with the 
rapid growth of underground infrastructure assets and the deterioration 
of existing structures over time, the increasing number of public emer -
gencies caused by underground pipelines have brought safety manage -
ment to the forefront [ 2 ]. Underground pipelines have a complex spatial 
distribution and are controlled by different departments. In addition, 
there are significant differences in the structural states of pipelines 
caused by specific operating conditions, such as foundation settlement 
and internal fluid pressure/temperature. Therefore, the digitalization 
and automation of safety management for underground pipelines is still 
a challenging issue. 
As a next-generation technology that can integrate a series of 
advanced technologies (e.g., sensors, modeling and visualization), 
emerging digital twin (DT) technology, which is considered to be an 
efficient intelligence solution for digitalization and automation, uses 
digital models to dynamically monitor, describe, simulate, predict and 
control physical counterparts in a timely manner [ 3 – 5 ]. Recently, DT 
technology has drawn much attention from both the academic and in -
dustrial communities [ 6 ]. Gartner predicted that 50% of large industrial 
corporations would use DT technology by 2021, corresponding to a 10% 
improvement in effectiveness [ 7 ]. Since the National Aeronautics and 
Space Administration (NASA) gave the first specific definition in 2012 
[ 8 ], the DT concept has gradually been extended from the field of in -
dustrial manufacturing to construction engineering and from theoretical 
research to application practice [ 9 – 13 ]. At the asset scale, Lin et al. [ 14 ] 
proposed a DT-based collapse fragility assessment method for long-span 
cable-stayed bridges during strong earthquakes. Mohammadi and Taylor 
[ 15 ] introduced a smart city DT paradigm that revolutionized human -
– infrastructure interactions and urban planning through the Internet of 
Things (IoT) and virtual reality (VR). Lu et al. [ 10 , 16 ] presented a sys -
tem architecture for DT technology that is specifically designed at the 
building and city levels and developed a DT demonstrator at the West 
Cambridge site of the University of Cambridge in the UK. Although each 
of these cases has its own unique definitions and requirements [ 5 ], a 
successful DT paradigm relies on (1) the mutual interaction between the 
physical asset and its DT, which is related to high-performance sensor 
technologies and high-fidelity data communication; (2) DT modeling 
approaches that should focus on both geometrical appearance and the 
ability to carry out asset-specific analysis, prediction, optimization and 
decision-making support; and (3) a human – asset interaction interface 
presented to the end users, which should improve the ability to under -
stand the unique characteristics of an existing physical asset and be

In pipeline engineering, Siemens proposed the Pipeline 4.0 concept 
[ 17 ]. Pipeline 4.0 utilizes DT technology to explore innovative ap -
proaches to further reduce the operating expenses of pipeline systems 
with no risk or cost. In the current phase, Pipeline 4.0 mainly focuses on 
developing DTs for pumping stations rather than pipelines. PetroChina 
has adopted digital recovery techniques, including aerial photogram -
metry, 3D laser scanning, 3D terrain construction, oblique photogram -
metry and digital 3D modeling, to build the DT of the China – Myanmar 
oil and gas pipeline [ 18 ]. Stennikov et al. [ 19 ] presented an object- 
oriented computer modeling technology to construct the DTs of pipe -
line systems for online hydraulic calculations. Lieberman et al. [ 20 ] 
discussed the potential benefits of using big data analytics and IoT 
principles to develop functional DTs of underground infrastructures and 
improve livability and resilience in modern cities. Carvalho [ 21 ] pre -
sented a DT framework combining building information modeling (BIM) 
and asset management (AM) techniques to support the maintenance and 
operation of water and wastewater utility assets. According to a litera -
ture survey, pipeline DT technology is still in its infancy, and there are 
few reports on using DTs for the structural safety assessment of under -
ground pipelines. 
The application of DTs to underground pipelines faces the following 
challenges. (1) The sensor network technique underpins the mutual in -
teractions between DTs and hidden buried assets. Although nonde -
structive test (NDT)-based in-line inspection (ILI) tools, including 
magnetic flux leakage (MFL), ultrasonic wall measurement (UT) and 
remote field eddy current (RFEC) tools, are commonly adopted in oil and 
gas pipelines, they are local inspection techniques rather than real-time 
sensing techniques. However, these methods cannot measure the stress 
states along a pipeline. The supervisory control and data acquisition 
(SCADA) system uses only sparse low-sampling transducers to monitor 
the internal pressure, flow rate and temperature parameters of pipelines; 
however, this system is unable to provide information regarding struc -
tural deterioration during a lifecycle. The comprehensive sensing of the 
structural state, including both stress/deformation and deterioration in 
the pipeline, is recognized as one of the most critical issues for pipeline 
DTs. (2) Human – asset interactions and visualization are vital for un -
derground pipelines since they are not visible in the buried environ -
ment. The state of the art is summarized in Table 1 . Augmented reality 
(AR) is considered to be a friendly human – asset interaction technique 
due to its fast communication, 3D visualization and beyond-reality 
interaction for underground pipelines. However, the current AR-based 
modeling and visualization methods mainly focus on the geometric 
attribute information (design and construction phases) rather than the 
dynamic evolution of structural states (operation and maintenance 
(O & M) phase). During the O & M phase, which covers > 80% of the 
lifecycle, pipelines suffer from corrosion and load or operating condition 
changes, resulting in the dynamic evolution and deterioration of the 
structural state over time. Therefore, the current studies lack a modeling 
and interaction approach for the dynamic evolution of the pipeline 
structural state to guide lifecycle safety management. 
To address these challenges, a framework for DTs is presented in this 
study for underground pipelines by using mobile augmented reality 
(MAR) and Brillouin fiber optic sensors (BFOSs). BFOSs, in addition to 
IoT-based pressure and temperature sensors, constitute distributed 
sensor networks, which are used to comprehensively sense the structural 
stress states of underground pipelines. Then, a joint data-physics driven 
model is presented to achieve dynamic modeling of the evolution and 
deterioration of specific asset structures. A MAR-based human – asset 
interaction interface is proposed to visualize geometric attributes, real- 
time monitoring data and structural safety assessment results. The 
feasibility of the proposed methodology for automated safety assessment 
was validated by experimental investigations in a real pipe network. 
2. Methodology development 
Underground pipelines are not visible and are large-scale. These 
pipelines are subjected to complex combined loads and inevitably suffer 
from structural deterioration during long-term service. A framework of a 
DT system based on MAR and BFOSs is proposed for quantitative safety 
assessment and O & M decision-making support. As shown in Fig. 1 , the 
DT of an underground pipeline has three main components: distributed 
sensor networks oriented toward the physical pipeline, a joint data -
– physics driven model oriented toward the DT, and a human – asset 
interaction interface based on MAR oriented toward safety inspectors. 
Based on this framework, the methodology is presented in detail as 
follows. It should be noted that only continuous metallic pipelines are 
investigated in this study, and jointed pipelines (e.g., bell-and-spigot 
cast ‑ iron and concrete pipes) are beyond the scope of the proposed 
approach.

and deterioration mechanism. In general, a combination of circum -
stances leads to pipeline failure. Pipeline breakage is likely to occur 
when operational and environmental loads are imposed on deteriorated 
pipes with corrosion, degradation and distress. According to O'Day et al.

[ 32 ] and Rajani and Klenier [ 33 ], the main failure modes of pipelines 
can be categorized into circumferential and longitudinal breaks. 
Circumferential breaks are mainly caused by longitudinal stress due to 
thermal contraction, soil settlement or voids, and third-party interfer -
ence. Longitudinal breaks are the result of excessive hoop stress caused 
by internal pressure, soil cover, frost load, structural deterioration, etc. 
Current design codes clearly present a calculation model for hoop stress 
from external loads (soil overburden and traffic load) and internal 
pressure [ 34 , 35 ]. The thermal axial stress in a continuous pipeline is also 
considered in the model. Rajani et al. [ 36 ] developed a pipe – soil inter -
action analytical model for the longitudinal stress of jointed pipelines. 
This model accounts for temperature differentials and explains the 
frequently observed occurrence of circumferential breaks in pipelines. 
Notably, the longitudinal bending stress due to foundation defects 
(settlements and voids) or third-party interference is not considered in 
the model because these events cannot be predicted in advance. During 
long-term service, the longitudinal bending actions induced by foun -
dation defects and third-party interference may occur with unforeseen 
magnitudes and at arbitrary times and locations during the lifecycle. 
However, third-party interference and foundation defects are the second 
and third greatest contributions to pipeline failures [ 37 – 39 ], 
respectively. 
As indicated earlier, longitudinal bending stress may occur arbi -
trarily along pipelines. The commonly used discrete point sensors, i.e., 
strain gauges and fiber Bragg grating (FBG) sensors, can only measure 
the structural strain/stress in limited specific locations. This contradicts 
the fact that the locations of the longitudinal actions on a pipeline 
cannot be determined in advance. As a cutting-edge sensing technique, 
Brillouin backscattering-based fiber optic sensors can provide real 
distributed measurements for strain as well as temperature with a 
sensing range of up to a few tens of kilometers [ 40 – 43 ]. BFOSs are ideal 
candidates for monitoring pipelines due to their large spatial range and 
relatively small spatial resolution [ 44 ]. Inaudi and Glisic [ 45 ] reported 
several significant field application examples using BFOSs, including 
leakage detection for brine and gas pipelines and strain and deformation 
monitoring for gas pipelines. Based on strain and temperature moni -
toring data from BFOSs and structural analysis of buried pipelines, Feng 
et al. [ 46 ] proposed a methodology for pipeline structural performance 
assessment and applied it to practical cases. The capability of distributed 
sensors to accurately measure the strain/stress state relies on their po -
sitions on the pipeline. To monitor the longitudinal strain, mounting a 
single Brillouin fiber optic strain sensor on the pipe crown was proposed, 
as shown in Fig. 1 . Ni et al. [ 47 ] and Lan et al. [ 48 ] showed that the 
flexural response (i.e., bending deformation) is dominant in the longi -
tudinal strain caused by surface faulting. With the exception of geo -
hazards and foundation defects, according to Li and Feng [ 49 ], the 
failure of oil and gas pipelines and heating pipelines can be caused by 
excessive thermal stress. The thermal exchange process between the 
internal hot fluid and the pipe commonly lasts only a few hours before 
reaching a steady state; thus, we assume that thermal loading does not 
occur simultaneously with geohazards and foundation defects. If no 
thermal action is identified from the monitoring data, the measurements 
of a single BFOS will only reflect the bending responses in the vertical 
plane. The proposed single-sensor configuration has the advantage of 
easy installation. At the same time, a distributed fiber optic temperature 
sensor is placed in parallel along the strain sensor on the pipe crown. 
This sensor independently measures the temperature around the pipe -
line, so it can provide leakage detection [ 50 ] as well as thermal 
compensation for the strain sensor. The proposed sensing scheme pro -
vides distributed real-time monitoring of the longitudinal strain/stress 
for a pipeline DT. 
Hoop stress in a pipeline is created by the soil cover, traffic load, and 
internal pressure. Even with BFOSs, distributed monitoring of hoop 
stress is not cost-effective or practical for underground pipelines. In 
previous research [ 34 , 36 , 46 , 51 – 53 ], theoretical models were provided 
for hoop stress in underground metallic pipelines with diameters < 500 
mm. These theoretical models are related to critical loads, such as in -
ternal pressure, overburden loads and traffic loads. According to 
Ahammed and Melcher [ 34 ] and Mahmoodian and Li [ 51 ], the varia -
tions in surface loads (soil overburden and traffic load) have low con -
tributions to failure with increased exposure period. Attention should be 
given to abnormal events related to surface loads by routine inspection 
instead of real-time monitoring. The internal pressure, temperature 
differential, and continuous corrosion play significant roles in pipeline 
failures [ 34 , 54 ]. Thus, we propose a sensing scheme for the real-time 
monitoring of internal pressure and temperature. The IoT-based digi -
tal temperature sensor and pressure transmitter are placed at a certain 
distance along the pipeline. By using the measured internal pressure and 
temperature data, the hoop stress can be accurately estimated by the 
well-developed analytical model [ 34 ]. The effect of corrosion was also 
considered in the stress analysis by introducing the corrosion model 
[ 51 ]. With increasing elapsed service time, well-planned inspections 
should be conducted periodically, and the data from ILI tools can be 
utilized to calibrate and update the corrosion model. In conjunction with 
BFOSs, digital pressure and temperature gauges are used in real-time 
sensor networks for the pipeline DT.

2.2. Digital modeling 
A DT is a computational model or a set of coupled computation 
models that evolves over time to persistently represent the structure, 
behavior and context of a unique physical asset [ 55 , 56 ]. To fulfill this 
goal, different models or model types can be adopted for different pur -
poses in DTs. In the construction sector, BIM or geographic information 
system (GIS)-based modeling approaches are adopted to establish visual 
models at the building and city levels [ 57 , 58 ]. In addition to geometry 
visualization, data-driven modeling approaches have mostly been used 
to map physical assets into a DT [ 16 ]. Smart data analysis using machine 
learning (ML) and artificial intelligence (AI) play a major role in the 
data-driven model [ 59 ]. However, the data-driven model has some 
downsides. The most serious limitation is the lack of interpretability. In 
particular, for safety-critical applications, black-box approaches are 
unacceptable. Physics-based modeling is the classic approach to 
analyzing, simulating and predicting the physical world. The major 
advantage of physics-based approaches is that they are generally less 
biased than data-driven models because they are mathematical de -
scriptions that characterize physical phenomena [ 60 ]. With the rapid 
development of high-performance simulators, physics-based modeling 
approaches have been used to establish DT technology. The disadvan -
tage of physics-based models is that they rely on parametric assump -
tions. Therefore, they are mainly limited to the design phase of 
engineering systems. 
The overarching goal of this study is to develop digital models for 
data analysis, safety assessment, and health prediction of pipelines. As 
presented in Section 2.1, the actual longitudinal bending behavior in a 
pipeline cannot be determined by physics-based models in an a priori 
manner. On the other hand, hoop stress should be estimated by using a 
structural analysis model with monitoring data (internal pressure and 
temperature). Neither pure physics-based nor pure data-driven ap -
proaches can individually model the stress state of pipelines. To close 
this gap, a joint data-physics-driven model is proposed for data analysis 
and structural state assessment in this study. This novel approach is 
intended to overcome the limitations of pure physics-based and pure 
data-driven modeling methods. This model introduces monitoring data, 
i.e., longitudinal strain, internal pressure, and temperature differential 
data, into a well-understood physics-based model to accurately obtain 
structural stress in a real-time manner. By utilizing real data, the new 
modeling approach eliminates the shortfalls of parametric assumptions 
and simultaneously improves the interpretability, accuracy and 
robustness of the model. Then, a reliability-based approach incorpo -
rating the joint data-physics-based structural analysis model is proposed 
for online structural safety assessment. The presented modeling meth -
odology allows DTs to dynamically update computational models inte -
grated with observational data so that they reflect the changing 
structural state of physical pipelines.

foremost issue for the digital model is to determine the occurrence and 
range of the thermal stress in the pipeline.

2.2.2. Reliability-based structural safety assessment 
The proposed digital models involve a physics-based model and 
monitoring data simultaneously. Several parameters in the models are 
derived from design values rather than actual values. The structural 
deterioration process (i.e., corrosion) is associated with a stochastic 
phenomenon. As a result, uncertainty inevitably exists in material and 
geometrical properties, the physical models of structural stress analysis, 
and the monitoring sensors. Aleatory and epistemic uncertainties should 
be effectively reduced for structural safety assessment and O & M 
decision-making. In this section, a digital model for pipeline structural 
assessment and service life prediction based on time-dependent reli -
ability is proposed to quantify the uncertainty during lifecycle service. 
The failure condition of pipelines needs to be defined in DTs. Both 
the Von Mises and Tresca failure criteria are extensively adopted to 
assess the structural failure of ductile metal pipelines [ 34 ], and fracture 
toughness-based limit states have been proposed to compute the reli -
ability of pipelines with localized defects such as corrosion pits [ 51 ]. For 
urban pipeline networks, strength theory-based approaches (i.e., Von 
Mises and Tresca) are more appropriate because they seldom perform 
inner inspections. Without loss of generality, the Tresca failure criterion 
(i.e., maximum shear stress criterion) is adopted to formulate the time- 
dependent reliability-based structural safety and prediction model.

2.3. Human – asset interaction interface 
DTs enable the real-time sensing, analysis, assessment, and predic -
tion of the structural states of underground pipelines. Undoubtedly, a 
more efficient human – asset interaction interface is required for DTs. The 
major obstacle in interactions between the O & M operator and under -
ground pipeline is that the buried asset is not visible, which makes it 
difficult to effectively find hidden defects and respond quickly to 
emergencies (i.e., pipe leakages or breaks). The human – asset interaction 
interface needs to have location-based accessibility and intuitive visu -
alization. AR can allow an operator to have real-time interactions with 
hidden assets during O & M activities. Extensive investigations have 
demonstrated that AR is more specifically appropriate for in situ appli -
cations (references [ 72 – 77 ]). The implementation of AR as a human -
– asset interaction interface can add to the long-term value of DTs. In this 
study, a MAR-based approach is proposed to manage and visualize the 
data, analysis results and prediction outcomes, which allows a high 
degree of interaction and immersive visualization with underground 
pipelines and DTs. 
2.3.1. Visualization of geometric and physical attributes

assets. Geometric and physical attributes include the geometry, location, 
and structural parameters of pipelines. Since pipelines are large-scale, 
both precision and weight need to be considered for attribute 
modeling. Highly detailed models can fundamentally ensure precise 
descriptions, while coarse models can improve modeling efficiency and 
prevent a single monolithic model from being used for a whole regional 
pipeline system [ 78 ]. Hence, a dynamic hierarchical method based on 
the Unity3d game engine and Mapbox software development kits (SDKs)

2.3.3. Mobile augmented reality visualization 
AR is considered to be an effective and fast communication and 
interaction tool that can visualize virtual objects [ 79 , 80 ]. However, 
current AR alignment methods, such as quick response (QR) markers 
[ 81 ], simultaneous localization and mapping (SLAM) [ 82 , 83 ] and global 
positioning systems (GPSs) [ 28 ], have two main limitations: (1) the 
positioning accuracy of currently commercially available SLAM systems 
and GPSs is far from sufficient for practical engineering requirements, 
and (2) the application of QR markers is limited to the tabletop level, far 
from the city level. In addition, the computer vision (CV)-based method 
[ 84 ] is affected by the environment and is mainly used for the appear -
ance of buildings rather than for hidden pipelines. With the rapid 
development of AR-enabled software (e.g., ARCore, ARKit) and micro -
global navigation satellite system (GNSS) surveying receivers with 
centimeter-level location accuracy (e.g., Lite RTK from Qianxun Spatial 
Intelligence, Fig. 5 (b)), AR-GIS technology can be used to achieve pre -
cise mapping of spatial locations between DTs and physical assets in the 
field ( Fig. 5 ). This solution features lightweight equipment, good pre -
cision, environmental robustness and large-scale applicability. 
As shown in Fig. 5 , GNSSs (e.g., GPS or BeiDou) are used to send 
signals for navigation and location. Ground-based augmentation sys -
tems are responsible for collecting and processing satellite data and 
providing calibration data to the GNSS receiver in real time. GNSS 
receivers are used to receive high-precision positioning coordinates 
using the global positioning system fix data (GGA) format [ 85 ]. The 
mobile app obtains precise positioning coordinates from GNSS receivers 
in real time via a Bluetooth connection so that the Mapbox map layers 
are perfectly aligned with the physical world. In addition, ARCore/ 
ARKit's plane anchoring capability (the so-called ARPlaneAnchor object 
[ 28 ]) can be used to “ seamlessly ” overlay DTs with physical assets. This 
system continuously searches for feature points to identify the plane of 
the surroundings and creates ARPlaneAnchor objects to anchor the 
Mapbox map layers. Alignment of the orientation angle is carried out 
using a combination of the electronic compass of a mobile device and 
manual rotation of the map layers [ 24 ]. Location-based human – asset 
interaction is achieved by the above approach.

3.1.1. Sensor networks and instrumentation 
The modeling parameters of the pipeline networks were collected 
from the design documents and as-built surveys. The digital models were 
then built at the system level. As the pilot demonstration at the unit level 
for structural safety assessment, GasPipe_6 and HeatingPipe_2 (as shown 
in Fig. 6 and Table 2 ) were selected to have distributed sensor networks 
installed for real-time monitoring of the structural states. These pipeline 
segments are located below roads with high pedestrian and vehicular 
traffic. BFOSs were used to monitor the longitudinal strain along the 
pipeline segments. The commercially available NZS-DSS-C02 and NZS- 
DTS-C05 were mounted on the pipeline to measure the strain and tem -
perature based on the sensor topology presented in Section 2.1. The 
sensing fiber core of these sensors is a common SMF-28 single-mode 
optical fiber with a sensing fiber. Commercially available fiber optic 
sensors were used in this study. The strain and temperature coefficients 
were provided by the vender. These coefficients were calibrated by 
laboratory tests before field application. During sensor installation, the 
pipe surface was cleaned to remove dirt and grease. Then, high- 
performance epoxy resin was applied on the top and two sides of the 
strain sensor to ensure that it was firmly bonded to the surface of the 
pipe [ 49 ]. During this process, the strain sensor was slightly preten -
sioned to keep it straight along the longitudinal axis of the pipe. Thus, 
good strain transfer was achieved, and the readings of the strain sensor 
represented the longitudinal strain of the pipeline. Neubrex NBX-6050A 
was adopted for real-time data acquisition from the BFOSs. The spatial 
resolution and sampling interval were set to 10 cm and 5 cm, 
respectively, and the measurement accuracy was ± 10 με . These BFOSs 
were arranged along HeatingPipe_2 from the fixed support to the 
compensator, with a total measurement length of 57.7 m. The mea -
surement length of GasPipe_6 was 61.3 m, and the BFOSs were arranged 
from the south fixed support to the north fixed support ( Fig. 6 ). Thus, 
distributed measurements could be obtained along the sensor path. To 
monitor the internal pressure and temperature, SITRANS P DS III digital 
pressure transmitters and DS18B20 digital temperature sensors were 
placed on the pipeline. The SITRANS P DS III digital pressure trans -
mitters were connected to a personal computer (PC) through an analog- 
to-digital (A/D) converter, while the DS18B20 digital temperature 
sensors were connected to the PC via an RS-422 data interface. The 
sampling frequency was 1 Hz. All monitoring data were transmitted to 
the cloud platform.
