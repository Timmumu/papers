1 | Introduction
The construction industry is transitioning from fragmented,
craft-based methodologies to integrated cyberphysical pro-

(RL) for control—trained on large, curated, and annotated

Governed by a considerable number of interdependent design,

2.3 | 3DCP as a Complex CPPS
TheArchitecture,Engineering,andConstruction(AEC)industry
is increasingly embracing digital fabrication technologies, with
3DCP representing an advanced manifestation of this trend.
3DCPofersunprecedentedgeometricdesignfreedom,enabling
the creation of complex, optimised structures that were pre-
viously economically or technically unfeasible [36]. This capa-
bility is intrinsically linked to signifcant gains in material
efciency,astheadditive,layer-wisedepositionprocessenables
material placement only where structurally needed, thereby
reducing waste and promoting sustainable construction prac-
tices. However, this promise is counterbalanced by the tech-
nology’s fundamental nature as a complex CPPS, in which
performanceisnotdeterminedbyasinglematerialpropertybut
by the intricate interplay of multiple parameters. The inherent
complexity of 3DCP arises from the interdependencies among
the fresh-state rheology of the cementitious material, environ-
mental conditions during printing and curing, and the precise
control of the robotic deposition process [37]. Moreover, the
rheological properties, which must simultaneously ensure ex-
trudability, shape retention and interlayer adhesion, are highly
sensitive to ambient factors such as temperature and humidity.
Concurrently, the robotic system’s parameters—including
printing speed, nozzle geometry and layer deposition strat-
egy—directly infuence the structural layered morphology and
the resulting mechanical properties. Even the curing environ-
ment and conditions can afect the fnal grade of 3DPC. This
interconnectedness of parameters leads directly to anisotropic
behaviour and performance variability in the hardened state,
a critical challenge for structural reliability. The mechanical
performance,particularlyinthedirectionparalleltothelayers,is
governed by the strength of the interlayer bond, or ‘cold joint’,
which becomes a plane of weakness [38]. Consequently, the
ultimate properties of a 3DPC element are not isotropic or
perfectlypredictableasincast-in-placeconcretebutareadirect
function of the specifc printing process and environmental
conditions.
Thisinherentvariabilitypresentsafundamentalstandardisation
problem, as current prescriptive-basedcodes are nottailored to
the performance-based, process-dependent nature of 3DCP as
aCPPS.Recentresearchisnowaddressingthisgapbyproposing
novel frameworks for standardisation, moving towards decen-
tralised workfows that can reliably link specifc printing pa-
rameters to fnal mechanical performance and material grades,
which is a critical step for the broader industrialisation of the
technology [18,19].

2.7 | Prompt Engineering Techniques
Prompt engineering has emerged as a pivotal discipline for ef-
fectively harnessing LLM capabilities in complex, data-driven
domains such as structural health monitoring and adaptive
control systems. Rather than retraining model parameters,
prompt engineering focuses on the strategic design of input
queries to guide pretrained models towards domain-specifc
reasoning and task execution.
Foundational techniques include zero-shot and few-shot in-
context learning (ICL), where the LLM is guided towards the
expected outputs by providing one or a few examples of
input–outputcorrelationwithintheprompt,therebyadaptingits
vast knowledge to niche applications without preliminary fne-
tuning[42].Thisiscomplementedbythechain-of-thought(CoT)
mechanisms [46], which allow the model to articulate its rea-
soning step-by-step (a feature that has recently become more
evident with the ‘deep think’ option in several GPTs), thereby
providing more accurate, logically coherent and verifable re-
sponses.Theapplicationofsuchstructuredreasoningiscritical
in domains requiring precise analysis, such as real-time defect
detection in additive manufacturing, where understanding the
causal chain of a failure is paramount [17]. In multimodal
contexts involving visual data from sensors or cameras, image
segmentation (IS) is a crucial preprocessing step that enables
mLLMs to systematically analyse images by dividing them into

3.5 | Uncertainty Propagation via Monte Carlo
Simulation
A Monte Carlo Simulation is used to simulate thousands of
experiments, extracting the mean performance and the 5th
percentile (5%ile). Considering the 5%ile means designing for
safety [63–65], and applying existing reliability frameworks to
3DPC [66]. The choice to use the 5%ile is as follows:
• Codifed in international standards (Eurocode, ISO, fb);
• Rooted in structural reliability theory;
