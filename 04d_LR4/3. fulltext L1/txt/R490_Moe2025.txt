 A B S T R A C T
The construction industry remains one of the most hazardous sectors, requiring innovative solutions to 
safeguard workers, especially in complex, dynamic outdoor environments. Digital Twin (DT) technology offers 
promising capabilities for real-time safety monitoring through virtual replicas of physical systems. However, 
existing DT frameworks rarely integrate comprehensive safety monitoring via a centralized model consolidation 
mechanism, such as Federated Learning (FL), which is explicitly tailored for multi-worker scenarios. Addressing 
this challenge, this paper proposes a FL-based Safety Monitoring Digital Twin (SMDT) framework designed to 
enhance multi-worker safety in resource-constrained settings. This enables real-time safety monitoring and 
control by representing on-site workers as virtual objects within a synchronized DT environment. A dynamic 
node selection mechanism based on client performance is employed to optimize global model convergence in 
FL. To validate the proposed approach, an edge computing-based experimental testbed using actual Raspberry 
Pi devices was implemented, using real-world construction safety data including worker status, weather 
conditions, and building structural parameters. Experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of the 
proposed framework in significantly improving safety predictions and real-time monitoring efficiency. This 
research establishes a foundational work towards safer construction sites through intelligent, synchronized 
safety monitoring systems.
1. Introduction
The construction industry is one of the most hazardous sectors, 
characterized by dynamic and transient work environments where tasks 
and hazards constantly evolve. Non-fatal injury rates in construction 
surpass those in other industries by 71%, underscoring the critical need 
for innovative and effective safety solutions (Li et al., 2019). This sector 
presents a unique opportunity for technology-driven interventions due 
to its reliance on diverse processes, personnel, and equipment, coupled 
with the increasing availability of sensor-generated data. By leveraging 
data-driven management of physical systems, significant advancements 
in operational efficiency and safety can be achieved. DT technology, 
first introduced by Michael Grieves at a 2002 conference in Troy, 
Michigan Grieves (2019), has emerged as a transformative concept for 
creating digital replicas of physical systems, referred to as physical

To bridge these gaps, this study introduces a novel framework 
that integrates FL with DT to enhance safety monitoring in multi-
worker outdoor construction environments. FL offers a decentralized 
machine learning approach, enabling devices to collaboratively train 
models while preserving the privacy of locally stored data (McMahan 
et al., 2017). Unlike centralized learning, where data is aggregated at a 
central server, FL transmits only model updates from individual clients, 
significantly reducing communication overhead and addressing data 
privacy concerns (Li et al., 2021). This is particularly advantageous for 
resource-constrained IoT devices that generate continuous streams of 
time-series data, where constant data transmission to a central entity 
would be impractical (Wang et al., 2022).
In this study, a decentralized learning paradigm, FL, is employed 
to consolidate knowledge and form a global model through the collab-
orative training of multiple clients, which represent the multi-worker 
scenario, especially in outdoor occupational settings. Without needing 
to share the raw data, FL enables the construction of an optimal 
model that is used to monitor the safety of the workers. Moreover, a 
performance-oriented device selection method is employed to ensure 
the optimal performance of the learning model aggregation in every 
round. To do this, DT serves as a virtualization platform, where multi-
ple devices can be dynamically registered and created as virtual entities 
to mirror the characteristics of their physical counterparts (Gao et al., 
2023). This virtualization ensures synchronization between physical 
and virtual objects, enabling efficient participation in the FL process 
and facilitating real-time monitoring for worker safety. Fig. 1 illustrates 
the DT architecture, where the first layer comprises virtual twins of 
physical devices to establish a synchronized connection. The DT envi-
ronment facilitates visualization of FL training processes, safety metrics, 
and performance predictions through an interactive web interface. 
Furthermore, the integration of DT enables monitoring and control to 
be conducted through HTTP, while model updates are transmitted via 
TCP (Pinyoanuntapong et al., 2022). To operationalize the framework, 
an edge computing system based on prior studies (Xu et al., 2022) is 
employed to communicate with sensor-based IoT devices and stream-
line data collection. A DNN (Liu et al., 2017) is collaboratively trained 
across FL clients and then deployed for inference.
The paper makes the following key contributions:
1. Proposes a novel federated learning-based safety monitoring 
digital twin (SMDT) framework that addresses current gaps in 
integrated safety monitoring systems and improves the safety 
of multiple workers in dynamic, outdoor construction environ-
ments.
2. Develops an integrated system that enables real-time safety mon-
itoring and control while maintaining synchronization between 
physical and virtual devices, representing resource-constrained 
AIoT devices as virtual objects within a DT environment.
3. Introduces a dynamic node selection mechanism based on nor-
mal distribution to improve the convergence efficiency of global 
federated learning models, ensuring optimal client participation 
in each collaborative learning round.
4. Implements and experimentally validates the proposed frame-
work on a physical testbed, using real Raspberry Pi edge de-
vices and IoT sensors to collect and manage live construction 
safety data, demonstrating practical feasibility and performance 
in realistic settings.

2. Related work and background
The following subsections review the broad applications of DT 
technology, its utilization in construction safety, the integration of FL 
with DTs, and the use of advanced machine learning-based monitoring 
in industrial systems, which together form the conceptual foundation 
of our proposed SMDT framework.
2.1. General applications of digital twin technology
Digital Twin technologies have gained increasing attention across 
various industrial domains due to their ability to provide real-time 
synchronization between physical and digital entities. In structural 
engineering, DTs have been employed for assessing the seismic collapse 
resistance of infrastructure such as long-span cable-stayed bridges, 
helping to identify vulnerable components (Lin et al., 2021). In man-
ufacturing, DT-driven integration frameworks have been developed to 
optimize performance evaluation in smart manufacturing enterprises, 
including the evaluation of green performance metrics (Li et al., 2022). 
Beyond engineering, DT concepts have expanded into the healthcare 
domain (Hassani et al., 2022), where human-centered digital twins 
are used to enhance personalized healthcare and wellness services. 
Recent research also explores Human Digital Twin (HDT) frameworks 
in Industry 5.0 environments, aiming to empower individuals and fos-
ter adaptive learning within cyber–physical–social ecosystems (Wang 
et al., 2024). Across all these sectors, the ability of DTs to integrate real-
time sensing, virtual modeling, and predictive analytics has opened new 
frontiers for intelligent monitoring and control. Within the construction 
industry, DT adoption is accelerating (Jiang et al., 2021), yet compre-
hensive frameworks for real-time safety management remain relatively 
underexplored.

2.2. Digital twin applications in construction worker health and safety
The construction sector, with its dynamic, hazardous work environ-
ments, presents a prime opportunity for applying DT-based solutions 
to improve worker health and safety. Several innovative systems have 
emerged in this space. For instance, IoT-integrated DT frameworks 
have been used for monitoring worker status and location in real-time 
using RFID and Bluetooth technologies (Zhao et al., 2021). Wearable 
sensor-based DT systems have also been designed to capture worker 
posture and motion through Inertial Measurement Units (IMUs), with 
real-time feedback enabled through AR (Akanmu et al., 2021). DT-
based behavioral simulations have been developed to model worker 
responses to various safety interventions (Fang et al., 2023), providing 
valuable insights into optimizing safety training and protocols. Closed-
loop DT systems for proactive safety control have also been demon-
strated, using machine learning and case-based reasoning to guide 
safety decisions in ongoing projects (Sacks et al., 2020). Additionally, 
DT-based risk prediction models have been applied to specific con-
struction tasks such as hoisting operations, where they help visualize 
and predict spatiotemporal safety risks in real-time (Liu et al., 2022). 
While these efforts demonstrate the potential of DTs for enhancing 
safety management, many current approaches are limited to single-
use cases or small-scale implementations, lacking broader integration 
across multi-worker, outdoor environments.
2.3. Federated learning and digital twin integration
Recent years have seen growing interest in combining Federated 
Learning (FL) with DT to support privacy-preserving, distributed in-
telligence across diverse systems. Pioneering frameworks have been 
proposed to enable collaborative FL among city-level DTs for public 
health applications such as pandemic response (Pang et al., 2021). 
In industrial IoT environments, adaptive DT architectures have been 
designed to enhance FL by enabling device-specific modeling while 
reducing communication costs (Sun et al., 2020). Drone-based DT-FL 
frameworks have been introduced for privacy-aware data aggregation 
and storage in blockchain networks (Islam and Shin, 2023). Health-
care applications have benefited from hierarchical FL-DT models for 
anomaly detection with improved resilience to data poisoning and 
communication delays (Gupta et al., 2021). Other studies integrate 
FL with DT and mobile edge computing (MEC) to overcome latency 
and scalability challenges in heterogeneous network environments (He 
et al., 2022; Alhartomi et al., 2023; Lu et al., 2020a; AbdulRahman 
et al., 2023). Despite these advancements, existing works often focus on 
static or semi-static environments (smart cities, healthcare, IIoT), with 
limited attention to highly dynamic, mobile, multi-agent environments 
such as outdoor construction sites. Furthermore, many studies remain 
at the simulation stage, without extensive validation using real-world 
edge hardware.
2.4. Advanced machine learning-based monitoring in industrial systems
Advanced machine learning-based monitoring has become a cen-
tral focus in the development of intelligent industrial systems, where 
real-time fault detection, anomaly recognition, and process optimiza-
tion are critical. For instance, wavelet-entropy aided ML approaches 
have improved multiscale monitoring accuracy in chemical process 
industries (Ali et al., 2023). Integrated ML frameworks for physico-
chemical process monitoring have shown strong performance in han-
dling dynamic system behavior (Ali et al., 2024a). Distributed Canon-
ical Correlation Analysis (CCA) with low-frequency control charts has 
enhanced statistical fault detection capabilities in complex industrial 
processes (Ali et al., 2024b). Likewise, dynamic ICA-distributed CCA 
methods (Ali et al., 2024c) and explainable fusion-based monitor-
ing techniques (Ali et al., 2025) have contributed to improved inter-
pretability and robustness in safety-critical applications. These trends 
highlight the increasing demand for intelligent, scalable, and inter-
pretable monitoring frameworks across industrial domains. Our pro-
posed SMDT framework draws on these concepts, applying federated 
learning, dynamic node selection, and DT-based explainable monitor-
ing to the multi-worker construction domain — an area where such 
integrated approaches remain underdeveloped.

3.2. FL-based safety monitoring digital twin system
We developed an integrated framework combining DT and FL mech-
anisms to enhance the safety of construction workers. Fig. 3 illustrates 
the architecture of the proposed DT-based worker safety prediction 
system. The process begins with leveraging the core capabilities of the 
DT framework, including virtualization, synchronization, and visualiza-
tion. Each AIoT device, representing an individual construction worker, 
is virtualized on the DT platform as a digital twin. This virtualization 
mirrors the physical device’s characteristics to enable real-time moni-
toring and safety assurance. For this integration, physical devices must 
register with the DT platform by providing their port number and IP 
address to establish a connection.
In our proposed system, each worker’s digital twin is constructed by 
combining multiple attributes captured in real time from edge devices. 
These include: (1) Worker Status — heart rate (biometric signal) and lo-
cation relative to danger zones (via GPS); (2) Environmental Conditions 
— wind velocity, temperature, and precipitation; and (3) Structural 
Conditions — load, strain, and inclination of structural elements. To-
gether, these attributes enable the dynamic representation of each 
worker’s physical state and environment within the DT, supporting 
individualized safety monitoring and predictive analysis.
The FL mechanism is incorporated into the framework to collabora-
tively construct a globally optimal model while preserving the privacy 
of sensitive worker data. The FL server, embedded within the DT 
platform, facilitates both the initialization and aggregation of models. 
The initial model, constructed and tested on the DT platform, serves as 
the baseline, while the final model is obtained through iterative global 
aggregation of locally trained models across multiple devices. A DNN 
model is employed as the primary predictive model for assimilating 
worker safety information in outdoor construction environments. To 
expedite model convergence and improve performance, a dynamic de-
vice selection mechanism (explained in SubSection 3.4.1) is integrated 
during the FL server’s aggregation process. This mechanism ensures 
efficient participation of devices in collaborative training.
The proposed framework utilizes communication protocols such 
as HTTP for real-time monitoring and control and TCP for secure 
model sharing. These protocols are implemented using lightweight 
web servers, with Flask managing monitoring commands and Django 
handling model update transfers. These technologies ensure efficient 
and scalable communication between the DT platform and the FL 
clients. Each client device is equipped with a DT assistance application, 
installed locally, which responds to control and monitoring commands 
from the DT platform. This application operates on the client’s local IP 
address and registered port, maintaining synchronization with the DT 
platform. Additionally, the data across clients is assumed to be both

Fig. 4 presents an integrated, four-tier architecture that underpins 
our SMDT, integrating with FL. At the base is the Construction Site 
Environment, where heterogeneous sensors capture real-time worker 
status (heart rate and GPS location), environmental conditions (wind, 
temperature, and precipitation), and structural parameters (load, strain, 
and inclination). These raw data feed upward into each AIoT Device, 
functioning as an edge node. Within each device, EdgeX microservices 
handle data acquisition and management, while control and monitoring 
services manage communication with the DT platform. The AI Module 
on each device adopts the global model architecture, fine-tunes it on the 
local data, and then extracts and sends updated model weights back to 
the federated server.
Above the AIoT layer, the Federated Server coordinates the FL cycle 
by preparing and broadcasting the initial model, receiving client up-
dates, and aggregating them based on performance before redeploying 
a refined global model. At the top, the Digital Twin Layer provides three 
core functional spaces — Model Management (metadata collection, 
model creation, and layer setup), Monitoring Space (device registra-
tion, status monitoring, and metadata retrieval), and Control Space 
(parameter setting, device activation/deactivation, model broadcast, 
and inference control) — all supported by a central repository that 
stores device metadata and model performance. Communication uses 
HTTP for control and monitoring and TCP with JSON for model syn-
chronization, enabling a seamless, secure, and transparent dashboard 
for visualization and system management.
3.3. Implementation of digital twin
The DT platform is implemented as a lightweight web-based in-
terface accessible through any modern web browser (e.g., Chrome,

inference results, and worker safety indices with an update rate of 
approximately 1 s. Visualization efficiency is ensured through asyn-
chronous REST APIs combined with WebSocket-based live updates, 
even when operating on resource-constrained devices such as Rasp-

options, allowing users to dynamically register devices, configure FL

3.3.1. Synchronization and management of data and model on DT
Fig. 5 depicts the conceptual diagram for data and model synchro-
nization and management between the FL-based SMDT and its AIoT de-
vices. These devices are created on the platform as virtual objects (VO) 
after registration. Model management system encompasses core func-
tionalities: Model Visualization, which offers a graphical inspection of 
model architectures or metrics; Model Synchronization, ensuring align-
ment of various model versions across devices; Model Creation, the ini-
tial phase of defining new models; Model Update, which deals with re-
fining existing models; and Model Deletion, eliminating obsolete mod-
els; Model Preparation, which indicates preliminary processes such as 
model initialization that takes user input to construct the model on DT.
The data in this study refer to the performance of the local update 
model in each round of collaborative training, as well as the features 
and data distribution of the sensitive local data. While local model per-
formance is used in node selection for aggregation, local data features 
and their data distribution are considered when constructing the initial 
global model. Model Aggregation, central to FL, involves merging 
model parameters from diverse sources, as denoted by the formula

The FedAVG is used for model aggregation at the server by assigning 
weights to the models based on their performance. Moreover, the 
performance of the received models is first analyzed; if the performance 
is below the threshold, the model will be excluded from the aggrega-
tion process for that specific iteration. In cases where the virtual and 
physical objects need to be synchronized, the synchronization property 
is crucial. Monitoring and controlling the FL training process is possible 
through DT, and the interactive web application of DT displays the 
prediction outcomes and performance metrics. TCP is mainly used for 
model synchronization, while HTTP is used for control and monitoring.
To facilitate the management, configuration, and monitoring of 
AIoT devices, the DT platform offers a web-based user interface (Fig. 6) 
that provides real-time visualization of safety indices, model training, 
and inference progress, while supporting device registration, model 
customization, and parameter tuning for various users and stakeholder 
needs. Each registered device is represented as an individual entity 
within the DT framework. The primary objective of this interface is 
to allow for the efficient monitoring and control of these devices 
in accordance with their designated functionalities. Users of the DT 
platform may initiate their experience by selecting the ‘‘Environment’’ 
option located at the top of the sidebar panel. Upon doing so, the system 
will present a list of registered devices along with their corresponding 
metadata, provided that the devices have already been registered. To 
configure the platform, DT users must first access the ‘‘Device Registra-
tion’’ section, which enables the registration of devices by entering their 
IP addresses and port numbers. Additionally, the ‘‘Model Management’’ 
feature plays a crucial role in configuring the learning model used in the 
FL process. The ‘‘Preparing Platform’’ function is designed to oversee 
registered devices that have successfully established a connection to the 
server following the initial registration phase.

3.4.2. FL-based collaborative training
Fig. 16 shows the collaborative learning process based on the FL 
mechanism. In our experiment, six devices (Raspberry Pi 4B) are em-
ployed to evaluate this approach. Additionally, two types of experi-
ments are conducted, such as full device participation and the dynamic 
node selection method. On each device, local data collected from the 
respective emulator is stored in CSV format. The following are the steps 
in each training round of the FL process.
1. Initialization: In the first round, the federated server distributes 
the global model weights as the starting point.
2. Local Training: For subsequent rounds, the federated server 
sends the current global model weights to the participant nodes. 
These nodes then perform local training using their own data, 
which has been preprocessed and transformed for compatibility 
with the learning model.
3. Parameter Aggregation: After local training, each node sends its 
updated model parameters to the server. Since each node uses 
different data, these parameters will vary.

Fig. 19 illustrates the inference process deployed on the edge nodes 
to perform real-time worker safety prediction. In this system, sensor 
data from the outdoor construction environment is collected every

deployed inference model on each edge device. The model outputs 
a Safety Index, representing the predicted safety level of the corre-
sponding worker in near real-time. This process enables continuous 
monitoring and prediction of worker safety with low latency at the 
edge, without requiring centralized computation, making it suitable for 
dynamic outdoor construction sites.

dynamic node selection: by adapting to client performance, it can help 
mitigate potential bottlenecks caused by slower or unbalanced nodes.
