ABSTRACT
The aerial building machine (ABM) provides an integrated solution for high-rise construction through its ability 
to hoist construction platforms, materials, and equipment to elevations of several hundred meters while main -
taining high operational adaptability. The jacking process of the ABM depends on coordinated extension and 
retraction of multiple cylinders. However, high-altitude wind loads and non-uniform stacking loads on the 
construction platform introduce significant uncertainties that may compromise structural safety. This paper 
presents the development of an intelligent synchronous control system based on a scaled experimental platform, 
employing a deep neural network for dynamic error compensation, together with a digital twin framework to 
improve operational efficiency and human – machine collaboration. Reinforcement learning tuned control pa -
rameters, and transfer learning migrated DNN-PID controller to the scale model. Jacking tests showed 0.407 mm 
synchronization error, a maximum error below 0.46 mm across ten conditions, and a 29% maximum synchro -
nization error reduction with fewer iterations.
1. Introduction
The number and average height of super tall buildings in the world 
have continued to increase steadily in recent years, showing a sustained 
growth trend. As of February 2025, China has a total of 3465 super high- 
rise buildings over 150 m in height and 122 super high-rise buildings 
over 300 m in height [1] , ranking first globally in both categories. Super 
tall buildings inherently have the characteristics of maximizing land 
resource utilization, and can meet the demands of functional use and 
urban aesthetics [2] . However, compared with low-rise buildings, the 
construction of high-rise buildings has the characteristics of high con -
struction difficulty, high safety risk, and complex processes [3] . Most 
notably, the deformation, collapse, and overturning of the scaffolding 
system have resulted in severe production safety accidents and sub -
stantial losses [4] . Additionally, the traditional scaffolding construction 
mode cannot efficiently integrate material transportation, concrete 
pouring, and steel reinforcement trapping. Construction equipment such 
as tower cranes and fabric machines is not able to quickly climb with the 
construction of the building core, leading to low construction efficiency 
and difficulties in cost control during the construction cycle of high-rise 
buildings. To solve the problems of high-altitude operations, the aerial 
building machine (ABM) was developed [5] .
ABM is a self-jacking scaffolding system with a steel platform, which 
has a complete sensing and control system. Its main subsystems include 
the power system, support system, and formwork system [6] , which is 
characterized by high load capacity, strong adaptability, easy operation, 
intelligent control, and comfortable environment [7 – 9] . In order to 
achieve its switching under various working conditions and complete 
self-climbing for the construction of high-rise buildings, its support and 
power systems need to smoothly complete the jacking of the steel plat -
form [10] . The ABM integrates multiple functions, and its complex 
structure and diverse working conditions impose stringent requirements 
on the stability of the power system and control equipment. Any 
instance of asynchronous jacking necessitates an immediate construc -
tion halt for troubleshooting and can lead to severe consequences such 
as structural damage [10,11] . Therefore, millimeter-level synchroniza -
tion accuracy is important in the control process, and high-performance 
control algorithms are required to drive the synchronous jacking process 
of multiple oil cylinders and effectively correct asynchronous climbing 
in real time, thereby ensuring the precise levelness of the steel platform.

These engineering challenges place strict requirements on the reliability 
and synchronization performance of the jacking control system, moti -
vating the development of more advanced synchronous control strate -
gies for ABM platforms [12] .
Traditional synchronous jacking control methods used in ABM sys -
tems still exhibit limitations such as insufficient control accuracy, slow 
response speed, and insufficient real-time monitoring feedback under 
complex working conditions, and in some cases still rely on manual 
observation of platform levelness for safety management. In current 
research and engineering practice, multi-cylinder synchronous control 
methods enhance their control performance mainly through improve -
ments in target tracking accuracy and advanced coupling strategies 
[13] . These synchronous jacking control methods are primarily based on 
parallel [14] or master-slave [15] approaches, which often exhibit poor 
anti-interference capability [16] . Coupling structures [17,18] have 
improved this aspect. In applications where operating conditions are 
relatively stable and system parameters can be accurately modeled, 
closed-loop control methods have evolved toward adaptive, robust, and 
intelligent approaches. Representative examples include fuzzy adaptive 
control [19] , sliding mode control [20] , neural-network-based control -
lers such as DNN-PID [21] , generalized predictive control (GPC) [22] , as 
well as synchronization strategies based on deviation-coupling struc -
tures [23] and FPGA-based digital synchronization [24] . Although 
existing methods have improved multi-cylinder synchronization accu -
racy, they typically rely on accurate system modeling and relatively 
stable operating conditions. In practical ABM jacking scenarios, varying 
loads and inconsistencies in actuator hydraulic circuits make accurate 
system parameterization difficult, which limits the applicability of 
model-based control approaches. Precisely because of these modeling 
challenges, engineering practice often continues to rely on traditional 
PID-based synchronization strategies, which may become insufficient 
under complex operating conditions [25] .
The application of the jacking control method in actual operations is 
the most effective way to validate its effectiveness. In practice, field 
observations provide valuable data for safety management and iterative 
improvement of control strategies. However, setting up multiple oper -
ating conditions and conducting comparative experiments with different 
algorithms is difficult to achieve in actual construction processes. Due to 
the high risks involved in high-rise building construction and the high 
costs of experiments, algorithms are difficult to directly deploy in ABM 
equipment, and control parameters and attitude feedback during asyn -
chronous jacking are also challenging to obtain. To address these issues, 
current mainstream solutions include combined simulation of hydraulic 
and control circuits [12] . Simulation systems generally struggle to 
accurately replicate real-world operational states due to factors such as 
the inability to account for random fluctuations in hardware and cir -
cuits, as well as numerous constraints. Multi-domain research has pro -
posed using scale models to simulate system motion states [26] . This 
paper employs a scale model to reconstruct the main structure and 
power system of the ABM and applies it to a jacking test execution 
platform. This provides a pathway for the research and experimental 
validation of synchronous control methods.
Although the methods mentioned can achieve acceptable perfor -
mance, they often involve high training or tuning costs and slow 
convergence, especially under varying operating conditions. These limit 
their practicality in real ABM applications. Moreover, closed-loop digital 
twin control frameworks validated through physical experimentation in 
ABM systems remain largely absent. To address this issue, transfer 
learning is introduced to bridge simulation-based optimization and 
physical deployment. By leveraging prior control experience from 
simulation, the controller can be initialized in the physical system with 
reduced manual tuning, improving convergence efficiency and robust -
ness under asymmetric and dynamic loads. To support systematic vali -
dation and management of control strategies, a digital twin framework 
is introduced as a system-level support layer. Through bidirectional 
interaction with the scaled model, it enables structured testing, real-time 
monitoring, and traceable evaluation. This design is intended to support 
future deployment and system integration, rather than implying direct 
applicability to full-scale ABM systems at this stage.
In summary, this paper addresses the following three key issues: (1) 
How to design the ABM scale model platform for synchronous jacking 
validation; (2) How to develop an online-learning adaptive controller 
capable of maintaining multi-cylinder synchronization under non- 
stationary load conditions; (3) How to drive the intelligent control and 
management of synchronous jacking control with digital twins, and 
provide a structured experimental basis for future investigation of con -
trol strategies in ABM systems. To solve the above problems, this paper 
proposes a synchronous control framework based on the scale model, 
transfer learning, and digital twins, which realizes adaptive intelligent 
synchronous control of multiple motors under uneven loads. The con -
tributions of this research lie in: (a) A scaled ABM experimental platform 
is developed to enable safe and repeatable validation of synchronous 
jacking control strategies under diverse loading conditions; (b) A 
transfer learning–based DNN-PID control strategy is proposed to bridge 
simulation and physical deployment, improving convergence efficiency 
and synchronization performance under non-stationary conditions; (c) A 
digital twin–supported framework is established to coordinate control 
execution, data logging, and experimental management, providing a 
structured basis for future investigation and potential deployment in 
real ABM systems.
The three main objectives of this paper are listed below: (1) Scaled- 
Model Design: A scaled-down experimental platform was designed and 
developed, integrating a sensor suite and an embedded control system to 
address the practical challenge of validating algorithmic feasibility 
under real-world conditions, where direct experimentation is often 
infeasible. (2) Intelligent Synchronization Control: A transfer lear -
ning–driven DNN-PID control strategy is proposed, leveraging prior 
knowledge acquired from simulation to achieve high-precision motor 
synchronization under asymmetric load conditions in physical envi -
ronments of scale model. (3) Digital Twin–Enabled Management and 
Control System: A full-lifecycle digital twin framework is established, 
enabling integrated closed-loop management and control through data 
mapping and visualization, thereby laying the foundation for the 
deployment of the proposed methods in real-world engineering systems. 
Together, these three components form a closed-loop research frame -
work. Transfer learning bridges simulation-based controller develop -
ment and physical experimentation on the scale model, while the digital 
twin enables system-level coordination and monitoring of the experi -
mental process, thereby linking algorithm adaptation, physical valida -
tion, and system-level management within a unified environment.
The remaining research contents of this study are organized as fol -
lows. Section 2 provides the related studies on synchronous control. 
Section 3 introduces the details of the intelligent control algorithm. 
Next, in Section 4 , the intelligent control algorithm is employed in a 
scale model of ABM to validate its effectiveness and practicability. 
Section 5 demonstrates the superiority and key links of intelligent al -
gorithms. In the last section, the conclusions and future works are 
presented.
2. Related studies on synchronous jacking control
This paper focuses on the synchronous control of the ABM jacking 
process under varying construction conditions. In this scenario, control 
performance is jointly influenced by the adaptability of the control al -
gorithm, the sim-to-real transferability of the control strategy, and the 
integration level and systemic completeness of the control framework. 
Accordingly, the integration of intelligent algorithms, simulation-to- 
scale transfer learning, and digital twin–driven control constitutes a 
comprehensive control framework that addresses complex challenges 
from complementary perspectives. Numerous studies have investigated 
the application of intelligent algorithms, transfer learning, and digital 
twin technology in engineering. The relevant research can be

summarized as follows: 
(1) Intelligent algorithms in synchronous control . Intelligent synchro -
nization control algorithms are providing robust solutions for 
networked systems under topology uncertainty, random pertur -
bations, and resource-constrained scenarios through adaptive, 
event-triggered, neural network, and hybrid strategies. Wang 
et al. [27] use the artificial bee colony (ABC) algorithm to opti -
mize the Relative coupling control (RCC) structure for a multi- 
motor synchronous control system and improve its coupling 
performance. Xie et al. [28] proposed a generalized model of a 
dual parallel motion system to solve the problem of large differ -
ences in control objectives and mechanical structures in parallel 
control systems. Shouran et al. [29] proposed a controller 
combining fuzzy PIDF with fractional order proportional differ -
entiation (FOPD) to minimize the ITAE objective function by 
particle swarm optimization (PSO) and sand cat swarm optimi -
zation (SCSO) algorithms to improve the disturbance resistance 
by 40%. Xu et al. [30] proposed a multilevel fuzzy-PID control 
strategy to solve the problems of synchronization speed deviation 
and positioning error caused by time-varying and nonlinearities 
in the jacking synchronization control system. Chen et al. [31]
proposed a recursive Chebyshev fuzzy neural network (RCFNN) – 
based current angle controller for synchronous reluctance mo -
tors, capable of searching for the maximum power factor point 
and improving efficiency across varying operating conditions. 
Their practical implementation relies on iterative offline debug -
ging and precise system modeling. In certain scenarios, they even 
sacrifice other performance metrics to optimize for specific task 
objectives. In ABM jacking applications, challenges persist in 
directly deploying such algorithms due to continuously varying 
load distributions, fluctuating actuator performance across con -
struction phases, and extremely limited on-site debugging 
opportunities.
(2) Transfer learning of control strategies. Transfer learning (TL) aims 
to enhance the performance of target learners in the target 
domain by transferring knowledge from different but related 
source domains, thereby addressing challenges such as data 
scarcity and distribution shifts in engineering applications [32] . 
Kuang et al. [33] proposed an unbalanced adversarial transfer 
learning network for industrial fault diagnosis, addressing data 
scarcity under fault conditions and extending the applicability of 
transfer learning. In additive manufacturing, Pashmforoush et al. 
[34] used TL to predict mechanical properties of selective-laser- 
melted materials by transferring simulated data to physical ex -
periments, slashing data needs by 50% with < 5% error, and to 
guide the optimization of control strategies for this process. Wang 
et al. [35] transferred the knowledge learned by the transient 
stability assessor (TSA) established by the graph convolutional 
neural network (GCNN) to the entropy-enhanced twin delay deep 
deterministic policy gradient (DRL) model to improve the 
learning efficiency and performance of transient stability pre -
ventive control (TSPC). Yin et al. [36] proposed a deep transfer 
reinforcement learning method based on QMIX that accelerates 
convergence through policy transfer and enables multi-UAV task 
allocation. Gadiraju et al. [37] used a method that utilizes the 
transferability of CNN networks to obtain pre-trained weights 
from a crack image dataset, thereby completing crack identifi -
cation and detection using drones and improving the perfor -
mance of crack identification and localization. Existing research 
on transfer learning primarily focuses on perception, diagnosis, 
or high-level decision-making tasks, while its functional role as a 
controller remains underdeveloped. Ideally, such methods should 
be integrated with system dynamics to serve as an integral part of 
the closed-loop control. Consequently, its applicability to real- 
time synchronous control of multi-actuator systems remains 
limited.
(3) Digital twin-driven control . Driven by data and models, digital twin 
(DT) promotes interaction and integration between the physical 
world and the information world [38] and can perform functions 
such as monitoring, simulation, prediction, and optimization 
[39] . Some scholars have pointed out that DT-enhanced parallel 
computing can accelerate the execution of real-time optimization 
algorithms in closed-loop control systems, guiding the applica -
tion of digital twin methods in the field of control [40] . DT ar -
chitecture has practical applications in the research of 
engineering equipment such as tunnel boring machines (TBM) 
and tower cranes. Jiang et al. [26] developed a scale mod -
el – based digital twin framework to simulate tower crane jacking 
behavior and analyze its dynamic effects, providing experimental 
insights for monitoring unsafe jacking operations. Zhang et al. 
[41] established a digital twin model of a TBM integrated with 
deep learning and online multi-objective optimization to deter -
mine optimal control parameters, and validated its effectiveness 
using real metro construction data. Existing digital twin appli -
cations play different roles across construction equipment. For 
tower cranes, control remains largely operator-driven, and digital 
twins mainly support monitoring and safety assistance rather 
than real-time control. ABM jacking, by contrast, requires a high 
degree of automation, where operators specify target states and 
synchronous control is executed by the control system itself. 
Although this makes ABM superficially similar to TBM systems, 
TBMs typically rely on dense sensory information for environ -
mental perception and decision support. ABM jacking must 
instead maintain reliable multi-actuator synchronization under 
uneven and time-varying loads with limited environmental 
sensing, and load information is rarely directly incorporated into 
the control loop. This difference limits the direct transferability of 
existing DT frameworks and highlights the need for a control- 
oriented digital twin tailored to ABM systems.
The research in the field of multi-cylinder synchronous control 
technology has developed a relatively mature technical route from 
theory to implementation, among which the PID control strategy opti -
mized by intelligent optimization algorithms and deep neural networks 
has shown strong performance and stability. However, most existing 
studies implicitly assume stable operating conditions, accurately mod -
elable system parameters, and high actuator consistency, which limits 
their direct applicability in complex construction scenarios. As a large- 
scale civil engineering equipment that has emerged in recent years, 
the control and safety management system of the ABM in engineering 
practice is still in the process of construction and improvement. The 
safety of the jacking process of some ABM used in super high-rise 
building construction projects still relies on ground observation and 
manual control by operators. At present, systematic research focusing on 
digital twin-driven jacking modes and safety risk mitigation for ABM 
systems remains limited. Furthermore, the data links in existing Digital 
Twin (DT) research remain insufficiently defined. Most studies are either 
based on simulations or rely on a unidirectional data flow from physical 
engineering equipment to DT systems. Under the constraints of real- 
world conditions, there is a lack of a physical entity capable of estab -
lishing bidirectional data links within the DT closed loop. Consequently, 
research using scaled models to fulfill this role and provide closed-loop 
feedback remains limited. By integrating transfer learning for control 
adaptation, scale-model-based validation, and digital twin – based 
system-level coordination, this paper aims to bridge the gap between 
algorithmic advances and practical ABM jacking control systems, 
thereby promoting the automation and intelligent development of large- 
scale construction equipment.

3. Methodology
This paper investigates synchronous jacking control of ABM using an 
experimental scale model. For the asynchronous jacking problems that 
may occur in the jacking process, a set of monitoring, analysis, control, 
and early warning programs is designed to reduce the safety risk and 
extend the service life of the equipment. Firstly, key structural compo -
nents are replicated according to similarity principles to complete the 
design, manufacture, and assembly of the scale model together with the 
corresponding digital model, and then the multi-objective optimization 
reinforcement learning algorithm is adopted to optimize the dynamic 
control parameters of the scaled model, together with a DNN-PID – based 
synchronous control strategy for the multi-motor system. Subsequently, 
the single-motor and multi-motor tracking simulation models are con -
structed in MATLAB/Simulink. The DNN is trained in the simulation and 
adapted to the physical environment by transfer learning. Finally, the 
synchronization performance is evaluated through jacking experiments 
using maximum steady-state error (MSSE), maximum synchronization 
error (MSE), average synchronization error (ASE), and other indexes, 
and a twin system integrating data interaction, safety warning, and 
remote control is constructed. The framework is shown in Fig. 1 .
3.1. System architecture and scale model
This section introduces the system architecture of the proposed 
experimental platform for synchronous jacking control of the ABM. The 
system integrates a physical jacking platform, an embedded control 
system, and a digital twin interface to support control execution and 
data interaction. The design of the platform is described from three as -
pects: the overall system architecture, the scaled ABM model and the 
modeling of the jacking scenario, and the embedded control system.
3.1.1. Physical system configuration
The study of the synchronous jacking control algorithm was con -
ducted on a self-developed scaled experimental platform. From the 
perspective of system architecture, the platform consists of three parts: a 
physical jacking system, an embedded hardware control system, and a 
data communication interface reserved for digital twin integration. The 
physical jacking system includes the structural components of the scaled 
model, electric linear actuators used to execute the jacking motion, and 
sensors for state acquisition. The actuators are responsible for realizing 
the lifting motion of the steel platform, while the sensors are used to 
obtain the operating state information of each actuator. The embedded 
control system is built on an STM32 series microcontroller. It is used to 
perform sensor signal acquisition, control computation, and actuator 
driving. Each actuator motor is equipped with an encoder to provide 
displacement and speed feedback signals, which are collected and pro -
cessed through the timer interfaces of the controller.
During each control cycle, the controller drives the motor driver 
boards through PWM signals, thereby regulating the motion state of the 
actuators and enabling coordinated jacking control among the four ac -
tuators. In addition, the system is configured with a serial communica -
tion interface to enable bidirectional data exchange between the 
embedded controller and the upper-level service system. Through this 
interface, the upper-level system can transmit control targets and com -
mands to the embedded controller, while the controller returns the 
motor operating states and displacement data in real time, providing 
support for system monitoring, log recording, and visualization in the 
digital twin platform. Through the coordinated operation of the physical 
system, the embedded control system, and the communication interface, 
an experimental platform architecture is established for synchronous 
jacking control experiments and data interaction. To clarify the physical 
process represented by the experimental platform, the jacking mecha -
nism of the ABM system is briefly introduced below. The scale model

used in this study reconstructs the key structural components and mo -

The supporting and power system of ABM consists primarily of steel 
columns, supporting equipment, cylinders, and supporting frames. 
Specifically, the steel columns are held in place with a steel platform 
above. The supporting frame is connected to the steel column by hy -
draulic cylinders. The steel platform is extended and retracted syn -
chronously by several hydraulic cylinders, thus enabling the 
construction of high-rise buildings. The workflow of the protrusion 
operation begins with the cylinders extending to raise the steel columns 
and the steel platform, which are then secured by the equipment sup -
porting the new position. Subsequently, the cylinder retracts and moves 
to a higher position on the support frame. This sequence of extension 
and retraction of the cylinder is repeated continuously throughout the 
jacking process. Under normal circumstances, the hydraulic cylinder is 
extended and retracted multiple times to raise the platform to a height 
corresponding to one floor of the building. The complete jacking process 
generally consists of five jacking stages of 700 mm, 700 mm, 700 mm, 
800 mm, and 800 mm. Fig. 2 illustrates the complete jacking process, 
from the first extension of the hydraulic cylinder to the completion of the 
climbing frame's retrieval. In this study, the climbing phase is omitted 
since it does not involve synchronization, automation, or structural 
safety concerns. The scale model is constructed based on the extension 
and retraction stages that constitute the core synchronous control task.
3.1.2. ABM scale model
The scale model represents the physical entity dimension of the DT 
five-dimensional model [42] and serves as the experimental platform for 
synchronous jacking control research, as well as for verifying the reli -
ability and robustness of the proposed control strategies. Similarity 
theory guides the development of the scale model in multiple aspects, 
such as size and shape structure [43] . In the ABM construction system, 
the components that most significantly influence the motion state and 
mechanical characteristics of the main structure are the support system, 
power system, and steel platform. Therefore, the establishment of the 
scale model focuses on these key structural components, with a simi -
larity coefficient of 1:25, which is reflected in the dimensional scaling of 
the steel platform and the support system. This paper focuses on the 
levelness of the structure during the jacking process. The steel platform 
mainly serves to bear the load, and its static characteristics have limited 
influence on jacking levelness, therefore, PLA material is used for its 
fabrication. The power system of the actual ABM consists of 12 hydraulic 
cylinders, which are simplified to 4 motor-driven actuators in the scale 
model to achieve the jacking of the steel platform. As shown in Fig. 3 (a), 
numerous components were constructed for assembling the scale model, 
in which modules ① - ③ are the connectors, module ④ is the bailey truss, 
module ⑤ is the supporting column, module ⑥ is the motor, and module 
⑦ is the steel platform. Fig. 3 (b) shows the ABM scale model used for the 
jacking test.
3.2. Control algorithm design and training
This section describes the design and training process of the pro -
posed synchronous jacking control method. The control framework is 
built on an improved deviation coupling structure to achieve coordi -
nated control among multiple actuators. In the virtual environment, the 
PPO algorithm is first employed to perform multi-objective parameter 
optimization, obtaining a set of control parameters used to initialize the 
DNN. Subsequently, the DNN-PID controller is trained in the simulation 
environment and then transferred to the physical scaled experimental 
platform for validation. Multiple synchronization performance metrics 
are used to evaluate the control performance in the physical 
experiments.
3.2.1. PID parameter optimization based on PPO
PID parameter tuning is a type of multi-objective optimization task 
that requires finding a balanced optimal solution among multiple in -
dicators. Reinforcement learning based on the PPO algorithm is used to

seek robust control schemes through the simulation of the ABM model's 
dynamic system. In each iteration of PPO, the agent interacts with the 
environment using the current policy to collect data. It assesses the di -
rection of policy improvement by calculating the probability ratio of 
actions generated by the new and old policies (importance sampling 
ratio), and strictly limits the magnitude of policy updates by using a 
clipped objective function to enhance smoothness. The core approach 
driving this process is to have the agent (representing the optimizer) 
make numerous attempts in the simulated system environment. Through 
repeated simulated interactions, PPO explores the PID parameter space 
and converges to gain values that achieve stable multi-motor synchro -
nization under specified conditions. These optimized parameters pro -
vide a high-quality initialization for the subsequent DNN-PID controller, 
reducing exploration cost and improving convergence efficiency. Unlike 
direct deployment on physical systems, offline PPO training enables the 
acquisition of prior knowledge by capturing fundamental control be -
haviors from simulation.
The following formulas summarize the primary content of the PPO 
algorithm: (1) is the ratio of the probability of taking action under the 
current strategy to that under the old strategy in a given state, referred to 
as the importance weight. (2) is the pruning strategy objective function, 
which uses the min( ⋅ ) operation to encourage beneficial updates while 
suppressing excessive updates that may cause training instability. (3) 
Value function loss, which minimizes the prediction error of the value 
network, improves the accuracy of state value estimation, and provides a 
stable baseline for policy updates. (4) PPO overall objective function, 
which balances policy loss and prevents local convergence. (5) Gener -
alized advantage estimation, which generates advantage estimates with 
balanced bias and variance, improves the stability of policy updates.

3.2.2. Multi-motor coordination based on deviation coupling
The jacking power system of ABM is a kind of multi-cylinder syn -
chronous control scenario that is highly sensitive to inter-actuator po -
sition consistency, and it also requires speed regulation to satisfy the set 
jacking velocity requirements. Conventional position-synchronization 
schemes typically employ a dual-loop PID architecture, wherein an 
outer position loop generates the control reference for an inner velocity 
loop. Although effective for stable tracking tasks, this structure focuses 
solely on position-tracking performance. Under synchronization tasks 
involving varying loads, the control reference for the velocity loop can 
exhibit significant fluctuations, which adversely affect the ABM steel 
platform, the equipment, and materials mounted on it. In this paper, 
deviation coupling combined with an improved incremental PID scheme 
is adopted to coordinate the motion of multiple power motors, as 
illustrated in Fig. 4 . In the deviation coupling mechanism, the 
displacement deviation between each actuator and the group mean is 
used as the compensation term, which is then fed into the speed regu -
lation loop through a coupling gain. In this process, the deviation 
compensation quantity is strictly limited to ensure that the speed fluc -
tuates within a certain range from the set value. The adjustment of the 
compensation gain parameter is to find a balance between the position 
synchronization control accuracy and the stability of the speed control. 
This parameter can be adjusted within a certain range according to the 
needs of equipment use in the actual application process, and the ra -
tionality of this adjustment range will be given through the multi- 
objective optimization of the control effect.

The primary functions of the proportional model control system, in 
accordance with its execution logic sequence, are as follows: 
(1) Timer reads the encoder pulses of the four actuator motors to 
obtain the speed and stroke information;
(2) Execute the DNN-PID controller operation module, input the

(3) Regulate the PWM duty cycle based on the control signal and

(4) Realize cyclic control by timing interrupt, complete the operation

(5) The communication module completes the data exchange be -
tween the STM32 chip and the host computer, utilizing serial 
communication to realize the control target setting, real-time 
feedback of the control effect, and the operation of virtual 
model driving.
In this process, the elements that have a large impact on the control 
effect include the gradient tailoring of the DNN and the limitation of the 
control output. In the initial stage of control, due to the difference in 
response performance between each power pusher, it is challenging to 
avoid the scenario of increasing error. The gradient explosion caused by 
the large error will ultimately lead to the DNN failing. The reasonable 
gradient trimming used in the initial stage reduces the sensitivity of the 
network so that it can continuously output the control parameters. In 
scenarios with large errors, DNN may experience gradient explosion. 
Additionally, the PI elements of PID controllers may output extremely 
large control signals under certain control conditions. Therefore, an 
output limiting strategy is employed to optimize control performance.
