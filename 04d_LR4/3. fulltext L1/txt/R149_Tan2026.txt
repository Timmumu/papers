ABSTRACT
Accurate parameter identification is essential for ensuring the structural safety and operational performance of 
long-span bridges. This paper presents a digital twin-based multi-objective optimization framework for identi -
fying structural parameters of a cable-stayed bridge by matching its frequencies, mode shapes, and deflections. 
The framework integrates three techniques: (1) adaptive reference point relocation to maintain solution diversity 
across irregular Pareto fronts; (2) sensitivity-informed variation operators that concentrate exploration on pa -
rameters with significant influence; and (3) hybrid local search incorporating quasi-Newton refinement for 
accelerated convergence. The framework was evaluated through implementation on a case study of a 580-meter- 
long cable-stayed bridge. Experimental results demonstrate up to a 52% reduction in parameter identification 
error and 25 – 35% reductions in total runtime compared with the Non-dominated Sorting Genetic Algorithm II 
(NSGA-II) and standard NSGA-III. Robustness analysis confirms the framework ’ s reliability with errors consis -
tently below 3%. This research contributes to advancing bridge condition assessment capabilities, thereby 
enhancing structural monitoring and optimizing maintenance strategies for critical infrastructure.
1. Introduction
Cable-stayed bridges constitute a prominent category of long-span 
structures acclaimed for their superior structural efficiency, architec -
tural aesthetics, and construction adaptability [1,2] . In practice, reliable 
bridge condition assessment is essential for ensuring operational safety 
and supporting risk-informed maintenance, because long-span cable- -
stayed systems are highly sensitive to localized deterioration and stiff -
ness/force redistribution in safety-critical components. Nevertheless, 
the inherent complexities of these structures, such as geometric non -
linearities and material property uncertainties, pose considerable chal -
lenges for accurate condition assessment. In long-term service, key 
components such as girders, stay cables, pylons, and anchorage systems 
inevitably deteriorate due to fatigue damage [3] , material aging, and 
environmental corrosion, which can reduce structural performance and 
trigger safety risks [4] . Since condition assessment fundamentally relies 
on comparing measured and predicted structural responses, establishing 
reliable Finite Element (FE) models and conducting effective parameter 
identification are critical but technically demanding tasks for integrity 
assurance and maintenance optimization [5 – 7] . Consequently, the fi -
delity of bridge FE models governs the precision of structural response 
analysis and prediction capabilities [8,9] .
The mechanical responses of cable-stayed bridges are governed by 
numerous interdependent parameters, such as material properties, 
stayed-cable pretension forces, tower stiffness, and bearing support 
conditions [7,10] . Conventional parameter identification methods have 
often relied on single-objective optimization frameworks [11] , wherein 
individual response metrics such as stayed-cable forces [12 – 14] or 
bridge deflections [15] serve as optimization targets. Although compu -
tationally tractable, these approaches exhibit a fundamental deficiency: 
optimization of individual response parameters fails to ensure consis -
tency across other critical structural behaviors, resulting in parameter 
estimates with limited adaptability and reliability.

transferability of identified parameters across different operational 
states. To overcome the above limitations, researchers have pursued 
multi-objective optimization methods to enable comprehensive param -
eter identification strategies. Huang et al. [16,17] developed a 
bi-objective optimization framework incorporating both cable preten -
sion forces and tower-deck deflections, whereas Peng et al. [18]
formulated multiple objective functions encompassing static displace -
ments and modal frequencies. These multi-objective approaches have 
demonstrated enhanced reliability through simultaneous consideration 
of multiple influential parameters [19 – 21] . However, they inevitably 
confront the curse of dimensionality, wherein exponentially increasing 
computational demands and conflicting objective functions significantly 
impede convergence efficiency and parameter estimation robustness.
In response to these computational bottlenecks, data-driven methods 
have been introduced as practical alternatives. Wu et al. [22] developed 
FE-based Kriging surrogate models for parameter identification, Qiu 
et al. [23] proposed two-phase model updating strategies integrating 
multi-objective optimization with elimination and choice expressing 
reality III (ELECTRE-III) decision-making frameworks, and Xu et al. [24]
implemented enhanced particle swarm optimization algorithms for vi -
bration control objectives. Despite these advances, computational effi -
ciency improvements come at the cost of vulnerability to measurement 
noises and uncertainties that are ubiquitous in real-world bridge moni -
toring systems [25 – 27] .
Collectively, these evolutionary developments reveal three persistent 
fundamental challenges: (1) high-dimensional parameter spaces (often 
exceeding 100 parameters) creating computational inefficiency and 
overfitting risks; (2) irregular, non-convex Pareto fronts from simulta -
neous multi-objective matching requirements that conventional algo -
rithms struggle to navigate; and (3) measurement uncertainties in field 
monitoring data that significantly compromise identification accuracy 
under realistic conditions.
Addressing these challenges requires innovative optimization 
frameworks that transcend conventional limitations. Recent research 
reveals promising pathways via integrating reference-point based 
mechanisms [28,29] for maintaining solution diversity across irregular 
Pareto fronts, sensitivity analysis techniques [30] for efficient 
high-dimensional exploration, and hybrid optimization approaches [31, 
32] for enhanced robustness. While conventional algorithms such as the 
Non-dominated Sorting Genetic Algorithm II (NSGA-II) [33,34] and 
Multi-Objective Evolutionary Algorithm based on Decomposition 
(MOEA/D) [35] exhibit limitations in high-dimensional scenarios [36] , 
the emergence of NSGA-III [37] with its reference-point mechanism 
represents a significant advancement for multi-objective optimization 
tasks. This transformative potential is evidenced by successful applica -
tions across diverse engineering domains: Hernandez et al. [31] ach -
ieved superior power transformer optimization through hybrid methods; 
Zhang et al. [32] overcame complex hydraulic parameter constraints; 
Sun et al. [30] integrated sensitivity analysis for high-dimensional motor 
optimization; and Li et al. [38] demonstrated advantages in structural 
bridge optimization compared with conventional algorithms [39] .
The synergistic integration of these advanced techniques offers un -
precedented capabilities: reference-point mechanisms employed in 
NSGA-III [28,29,37] ensure uniform exploration of non-convex Pareto 
fronts; sensitivity-informed approaches [30] conquer dimensionality 
curses by focusing on influential parameters while maintaining system 
fidelity; hybrid local-global strategies [31,32] provide robustness under 
realistic noise conditions through combined exploration and refinement. 
However, integrating these techniques into a cohesive framework spe -
cifically tailored for cable-stayed bridge parameter identification re -
mains an open challenge.
As discussed above, this paper presents a digital twin-enabled multi- 
objective optimization framework that integrates a four-stage pipeline 
to tackle both computational complexity and measurement 
uncertainties in bridge parameter identification. As shown in Fig. 1 , the 
framework consists of: (1) problem definition and setup with physics- 
constrained digital twin modelling; (2) sensitivity analysis for dimen -
sionality reduction; (3) an enhanced NSGA-III algorithm with adaptive 
reference-point management and sensitivity-informed variation opera -
tors; and (4) multi-objective optimization followed by robustness eval -
uation under measurement noise. This work represents the first 
integrated solution that systematically addresses both complexity and 
uncertainty challenges in large-scale bridge parameter identification, 
providing a robust and computationally efficient approach essential for 
practical engineering applications and advancing the state-of-the-art in 
structural health monitoring and digital twin technologies.
2. Methods
This section presents the methodological framework for digital twin- 
enabled multi-objective optimization in bridge parameter identification. 
The methods comprise problem definition, multi-objective optimization 
formulation with constraint systems and sensitivity analysis, and an 
improved NSGA-III algorithm featuring adaptive mechanisms for 
enhanced convergence and robustness. Each component addresses 
computational complexity and measurement uncertainty in large-scale 
structural parameter identification.

2.3. Improved NSGA ‑ III algorithm

identification in large-span cable-stayed bridges, several enhancements 
are implemented to the standard NSGA-III framework [43] . The key 
innovations are: An adaptive reference ‑ point relocation strategy to 
maintain diversity in irregular Pareto fronts; Sensitivity ‑ informed vari -
ation operators that bias exploration toward the most influential pa -
rameters; A hybrid local ‑ search module that accelerates fine ‑ scale 
convergence; A brief convergence discussion under realistic modeling 
assumptions.

4.2. Robustness assessment under measurement noise
To evaluate algorithm robustness under realistic field conditions, 
comprehensive noise sensitivity analysis was conducted across three 
progressively challenging scenarios: S1 (low noise, 1% modal frequency

were performed to assess: (1) average normalized objective at termi -
nation; (2) mean relative parameter identification error across 16 
reduced parameters; (3) generations required for convergence (defined 
as no improvement in objective for 20 consecutive generations); and (4) 
hypervolume of the final Pareto front.
Table 5 demonstrates exceptional algorithm robustness across noise 
scenarios. Under low noise (S1), the improved NSGA-III achieves overall 
average error of 0.9%, representing a 55% reduction compared to 
standard NSGA-III (2.0%). At baseline noise (S2), average error in -
creases moderately to 1.3% (52% improvement), while under high noise 
(S3) it remains at 2.8% (39% improvement). Parameter category anal -
ysis under S2 shows consistent performance: cable tensions (1.4% error), 
deck properties (1.4%), and tower parameters (1.1%). Convergence 
acceleration of 25 – 29% and hypervolume improvements of 18 – 24% are 
maintained across scenarios, while computational time reductions of 
30 – 37% offset the 9.5% per-generation overhead. The noise sensitivity 
coefficient of 0.75 (vs. 0.85 for standard NSGA-III, 0.90 for NSGA-II) 
confirms graceful performance degradation, validating practical appli -
cability for real-world structural health monitoring.

5. Conclusions
This paper presents an enhanced NSGA-III framework for multi- 
objective parameter identification in cable-stayed bridge digital twins, 
addressing critical challenges in solution diversity maintenance, 
convergence efficiency, and measurement noise robustness. The main 
findings are summarized as follows: 
• A unified multi-objective formulation balancing modal frequency 
matching, mode shape correlation, and static deflection error is 
established under physics-based constraints. Sobol global sensitivity 
analysis reduces the parameter space from 156 to 16 variables 
(89.7% reduction) while retaining 99% of system sensitivity, effec -
tively addressing the curse of dimensionality.
• Three coordinated innovations improve standard NSGA-III: adaptive 
reference point relocation (every 20 generations) preserves diversity 
across non-convex Pareto regions; sensitivity-informed variation 
operators emphasize high-impact parameters; and intermittent 
quasi-Newton local search accelerates convergence by 25 – 30% while 
eliminating Pareto front gaps.
• On a 580 m-span finite element model, the enhanced algorithm re -
duces average identification error from 2.7% (standard NSGA-III) to

• Under realistic noise levels (1 – 5% frequency error, 0.5 – 2 mm

Current validation relies on synthetic data with controlled noise 
profiles; real-world measurements exhibit non-Gaussian noise, inter -
mittent data loss, and unmodeled effects (soil-structure interaction, 
temperature gradients, traffic-induced vibrations) that may challenge
