Abstract
The complexity of a building’s indoor environment exacerbates its ﬁre safety issues, making them particularly problematic.
Traditional ﬁre protection systems only detect ﬁres when their characteristics are already visible, and ﬁre recognition delays
have become a signiﬁcant problem. This paper presents a digital twin framework for managing building ﬁre safety in real time.
Sensor data and a Bert_C model are used to analyze the transition of a ﬁre from smoldering to ﬂaming combustion. The results
show that the model achieved 96.1% accuracy and 96.2% F1 score, and the framework signiﬁcantly improves the timeliness
and accuracy of ﬁre warnings and visualizes 3D ﬁre scenarios, providing powerful technical support for ﬁreﬁghting and
emergency rescue operations. This study conﬁrms the feasibility of real-time ﬁre safety management using 3D environments
and digital twins, providing an innovative solution for ﬁre safety management in the built environment.

2 Related work
2.1 Digital twin
The concept of the digital twin was ﬁrst introduced by Dr.
Grieves in 2003 under the term "Mirrored Space Model"
[13]. The initial framework of the digital twin was identi-
ﬁed as comprising physical products, virtual products, and
their interconnections [ 14]. Gabor et al. [ 15] described the
digital twin as an advanced simulation created using domain
expertise and real-world data, capable of achieving accu-
rate simulations across varying temporal and spatial scales.
Tao et al. [16] emphasized that digital twin modeling serves
as the core of representing physical entities and, driven by
data and models, enables monitoring, simulation, and pre-
diction. Subsequently, Tao and Zhang [ 17] proposed that
a comprehensive digital twin comprises ﬁve key compo-
nents: physical entities, virtual entities, interconnections,
data, and services, with data being fundamental for gener-
ating new knowledge. These elements collectively form the
core structure of the digital twin [ 18]. A digital twin is not
merely a digital representation of a physical entity; it also
facilitates real-time acquisition and analysis of operational,
surrounding, and status-related information, thereby support-
ing decision-making processes. With advancements in the
Internet of Things (IoT), big data, artiﬁcial intelligence, and
high-performance computing, the concept of the digital twin
has evolved and found applications beyond manufacturing.
Its adoption has expanded into diverse ﬁelds, including smart
cities, healthcare, aerospace, and transportation.
Digital twin technology represents a relatively novel
approach in the domain of building ﬁre safety. To address
the increasingly stringent requirements for ﬁre safety in
complex and dynamic building environments, a digital twin
framework integrating IoT, building information modeling
(BIM), and advanced machine learning techniques has been
developed. This framework enables real-time monitoring
and dynamic simulation of critical elements, including the
internal environment, ﬁre protection systems, and personnel
distribution within a building, by constructing a digital repre-
sentation of the physical structure. These ﬁndings underscore
the framework’s potential to provide robust technical support
and a reliable decision-making foundation for intelligent ﬁre-
ﬁghting applications.

2.2.1 BIM application
The application of BIM in ﬁre safety management has been
extensively studied [ 22], highlighting its signiﬁcant poten-
tial in enhancing ﬁre prevention and response systems. BIM
not only facilitates the digital creation and management of
building models but also serves as a foundational tool for
constructing digital twins. For instance, Cheng et al. [ 23]
developed a BIM-based intelligent ﬁre rescue system that
integrates sensor technologies, such as Bluetooth sensors, to
enable real-time monitoring of ﬁre-related information. This
system efﬁciently displays ﬁre data on mobile devices, signif-
icantly improving early ﬁre detection and expediting rescue
operations. Moreover, BIM has been applied to emergency
evacuation planning.
The application of building information modeling (BIM)
technology in building ﬁre protection is primarily demon-
strated in its role in intelligent decision-making. For instance,
Cheng et al. [ 23] developed a BIM-based intelligent ﬁre
prevention and disaster relief system, which implements
personal localization, evacuation/rescue route optimization
using Bluetooth technology, and mobile guidance. This sys-
tem also enables 3D visualization to support ﬁre safety
assessment and planning.
Although building information modeling (BIM) has been
widely adopted in the ﬁelds of architecture and construc-
tion, its current applications remain largely conﬁned to
static representations of physical assets. This inherent limi-
tation restricts BIM’s capacity to dynamically integrate with
real-time data streams generated by ﬁre safety sensors and
Internet of Things (IoT) devices. Conventional BIM models
typically serve as static digital twins of the built environ-
ment and lack the interoperability required to ingest and
process time-sensitive environmental data—such as temper-
ature variations or smoke detection alerts—produced by IoT
infrastructures like LoRaW AN or NB-IoT. Consequently,
this disconnect hinders the establishment of real-time interac-
tions between BIM and ﬁre safety systems, thereby reducing
the operational effectiveness of emergency response mecha-
nisms in smart building contexts.
Teizer et al. [ 24] emphasize the potential of integrating
BIM with IoT sensor networks to address this shortcom-
ing. By aligning the spatial representation capabilities of
BIM with the temporal resolution of IoT-derived data,
such integration facilitates proactive facility management
through continuous monitoring of key ﬁre safety indicators.
For instance, sensor-triggered events—such as abnormal
increases in temperature—can be visualized in real time
within the BIM environment. This enables facility managers
to pinpoint high-risk zones, optimize evacuation planning,
and coordinate emergency interventions with enhanced spa-
tial accuracy and operational precision.
2.2.2 Bert model
Deep learning-based text categorization models cover a wide
range of architectures such as CNN models, RNN models,
and Bert models. In the early days of the natural language
ﬁeld, the TextCNN text classiﬁcation model came to the fore-
front, which simpliﬁed the convolutional layers based on the
original network structure and improved the training speed,
but the model interpretability was lacking. Lai et al. [ 25]
proposed a recurrent convolutional neural network for text
classiﬁcation without human-designed features. To solve the
short-term memory of RNN, LSTM and GRU were proposed
one after another, and then the Bert model was proposed as
a pre-training model, which does not need a large amount
of corpus for training in a speciﬁc scenario, and it has the
advantages of strong generalization ability and simplicity of
use. Chen et al. [26] proposed a Bert-based local feature con-
volutional network (LFCN) model to enhance the accuracy
of long-text classiﬁcation in Chinese news.
Shi el al. [ 27] proposed a BERT-CNN network model
based on the combination of pre-trained BERT network and
convolutional neural. The BERT structure is used to express
sentence semantics as text vectors, and then the local features
of the sentences are extracted by convolutional neural net-
work, and the experiments on the labeled dataset of reviews
of a cell phone in Jingdong show that the method has good
performance in this ﬁeld. Therefore, this paper proposes to

2.2.3 Sensor data
Due to the inherent challenges associated with conducting
large-scale ﬁre tests and generating extensive experimen-
tal datasets, the integration of machine learning (ML) with
numerical ﬁre simulations has been proposed as a viable
approach for training predictive models. For instance, Wang
et al. [ 28] developed a prediction model known as P-Flash,
which effectively forecasted the risk of ﬂashover in multi-
room compartment ﬁres. This model was trained using heat
detector temperature data obtained from numerical simula-
tions. Furthermore, machine learning techniques have also
been employed to predict smoke movement during tunnel
ﬁres.
Data extracted from ﬁre scenes can be used to predict ﬁre
occurrence and enhance the effectiveness of ﬁre monitor-
ing. To address the challenges in ﬁre detection, Li et al. [ 29]
developed an integrated dual-parameter ﬁre detector based
on mid-infrared technology, which simultaneously measures
carbon monoxide (CO) and carbon dioxide (CO
2) concen-
trations. This innovation was further complemented by a
mobile ﬁre detection method. Similarly, Solórzano et al.
[30] proposed utilizing an array of gas sensors to monitor
CO concentrations at ﬁre scenes, enabling the generation of
early warning information. Schmitt et al. [ 31] employed gas
detection techniques for CO, O
2, and other gases to facilitate
ﬁre detection and identiﬁcation, further enhancing the accu-
racy of ﬁre warning systems. Additionally, Deng et al. [ 32]
demonstrated that the light scattering intensity of ﬁre smoke
is correlated with the volume and surface area of smoke par-
ticles.
To address the challenges associated with intelligent
decision-making, and sensor data, this study proposes a
digital twin framework that integrates sensor data with an
enhanced Bert model. The framework is designed to simu-
late and establish dynamic connections between real-world
and virtual buildings.

(3) the interaction between physical entities and their digital

3.2 Sensor data
In the event of a building ﬁre, CCTV camera systems can
monitor the ﬁre scene; however, their coverage is often
limited due to cost constraints and privacy protection con-
cerns. Furthermore, in actual ﬁre scenarios, the presence of
smoke frequently obstructs the camera’s ﬁeld of view, ren-
dering continuous monitoring ineffective. To address these
challenges more effectively, ﬁre events can be identiﬁed by
analyzing gas concentrations. This study employs a sen-
sor system that integrates smoke, temperature, and humidity
detection to accurately quantify smoke concentration and sur-
rounding environmental conditions. Additionally, a portable
smoke analyzer equipped with built-in CO and O
2 sensors
is utilized to monitor changes in CO and O 2 concentrations
during ﬁre incidents.
Pﬁster et al. [ 33], during a series of experiments aimed
at monitoring and recording the progression of several stan-
dardized ﬁre scenarios, observed that the concentration of
carbon monoxide (CO) increased notably after a certain
period of ﬁre development, reaching levels signiﬁcantly
higher than the ambient CO concentration in non-ﬁre con-
ditions. This observation underscores the potential of CO
concentration changes as a critical indicator of ﬁre onset,
thereby offering substantial value for early ﬁre detection and
warning systems. Similarly, Du et al. [ 34] demonstrated that
considerable amounts of carbon monoxide (CO) and car-
bon dioxide (CO
2) are generated during the initial phase
of combustion. These ﬁndings reinforce the notion that the
variations in CO and CO 2 concentrations are particularly
prominent in the early stages of a ﬁre, making them reliable
indicators for timely detection. Moreover, as evidenced by
the ﬁndings of Study Cleary et al. [35] integrating smoke con-
centration measurements with CO concentration monitoring
reduces the false alarm rate by approximately 40% compared

In this study, AUTBUS bus technology is utilized for
both sensor data acquisition and device control. The system
supports a bandwidth of up to 100 Mbps and allows the inte-
gration of up to 254 nodes, thereby fulﬁlling the real-time
requirements for multi-sensor data collection. Moreover,
AUTBUS supports a communication distance of up to 500 m,
making it suitable for long-range transmission in large or
complex environments. A bus-type network topology is
adopted, in which all relevant components—including sen-
sors, sprinkler heads, and ﬁre alarms—are connected to the
AUTBUS network. This conﬁguration establishes a uni-
ﬁed and scalable communication framework. Within this
architecture, AUTBUS facilitates not only the real-time
transmission of environmental parameters, such as smoke
concentration, temperature, and gas levels, to the central
processing unit, but also the dispatch of control signals to
corresponding actuators. For instance, it enables the activa-
tion of sprinklers or alarm modules upon the detection of
ﬁre-related risks.
In order to ensure the accuracy of the sensor data, this
paper transmits the data with the system through the RS485
communication protocol, which has been certiﬁed by the IEC
61158–2 standard. This protocol has a signaling delay of
about 5 ns/m, which provides high timeliness and reliability
in ﬁre-monitoring applications.
3.3 Digital twins
The digital twin system presented in this study is speciﬁ-
cally designed to enhance indoor ﬁre safety management by
providing efﬁcient ﬁre facility management and control, ﬁre
information visualization, ﬁre risk assessment, and evacua-
tion and rescue guidance capabilities. By integrating multiple
advanced technologies, the system establishes a real-time,
data-driven platform that seamlessly fuses virtual and physi-
cal environments, offering intelligent management solutions
for indoor ﬁre safety.
Regarding ﬁre facility management and control, the sys-
tem enables real-time monitoring of the operational status of
ﬁre safety equipment through the digital twin model, includ-
ing systems such as water sprinklers, smoke alarms, and
other related devices. Furthermore, the system incorporates a
remote-control function, allowing managers to directly oper-
ate the equipment via the interface, such as activating the
sprinkler system or deactivating air conditioning and other
devices, thereby ensuring prompt response in case of an
emergency.
Regarding ﬁre information visualization, the system inte-
grates various data collected by sensors, such as smoke
concentration and carbon monoxide levels, and presents the
indoor ﬁre situation in real time through dynamic curves and
3D modeling. Additionally, by incorporating camera data, the
system enables visualization of the ﬁre scene, offering man-
agers a comprehensive basis for assessing the situation and
ensuring the accuracy and timeliness of decision-making.
Regarding ﬁre risk assessment, the system employs deep
learning models to analyze and predict the real-time data
collected by the sensors. By identifying whether a ﬁre has
reached the critical point of transitioning from smoldering
to ﬂaming combustion, the system can implement suppres-
sion measures in advance, thereby effectively preventing the
further spread of the ﬁre and mitigating the risk of property
damage and casualties.
Regarding evacuation and rescue guidance, the system
offers intelligent support for evacuation and rescue oper-
ations during emergencies, such as ﬁres. The system’s
evacuation indicators (e.g., escape route lights and ﬁre
alarm devices) provide escape guidance for individuals
who are trapped. Additionally, the system supplies rescuers
with detailed disaster data and surroundings information,
including the site of the ﬁre origin and the distribution of
high-temperature zones, thereby ensuring the efﬁciency and
safety of rescue operations.

4.2 Improved bert structure
To address the issue of early-stage classiﬁcation of both
smoldering and ﬂaming combustion scenarios, this paper
introduces the Bert_C model, the architecture of which is
depicted in Fig. 5. The Bert model has demonstrated strong
performance in short-term time series classiﬁcation tasks,
while convolutional neural networks (CNN) are particularly
effective at capturing local features. To enable the model to
capture both local and global features, this paper integrates
the Bert and CNN models. Initially, the bidirectional trans-
former feature extractor of the Bert model is used to extract
contextual features from the time series data. Subsequently,
123

the CNN is responsible for identifying local features and
pattern changes within a speciﬁed time window. After the
text vectors reach the Bert layer, an attention mechanism is
utilized to extract global features. In the CNN layer, convolu-
tional operations are employed to extract locally signiﬁcant
features, such as key phrases within the text. A max-pooling
layer is also employed, which automatically identiﬁes the
words that play a critical role in text classiﬁcation, thereby
capturing the key components within the text. The feature
vectors generated from these two layers are then fused, and
the predictions are converted to probability values as the
results using the softmax function.
In this study, data on smoke concentration, carbon monox-
ide (CO), carbon dioxide (CO
2), and temperature, obtained
from multiple types of sensors, are utilized as input features
for an enhanced BERT-based model. These inputs are ﬁrst
encoded into vector representations and subsequently passed
through a 12-layer transformer encoder. Notably, only the
outputs from layers 9 through 12 are employed in this work,
as each encoder layer incorporates a multi-head self-attention
mechanism, residual connections, and layer normalization,
enabling the model to capture complex dependencies within
the data.
After the encoding phase, the extracted features are fur-
ther reﬁned using convolutional operations and max pooling,
with Dropout applied to reduce the risk of overﬁtting. The
ﬁnal classiﬁcation is performed via a linear transformation
followed by a softmax layer, which maps the reﬁned fea-
tures to binary probabilities corresponding to either “ﬁre” or
“non-ﬁre.”
By integrating the global representation capabilities of the
transformer with the local feature extraction strengths of con-
volutional operations, the proposed approach is particularly
well-suited for classiﬁcation tasks involving multi-sensor ﬁre
detection data.
For model training, the binary cross-entropy loss function
is employed as the objective function, while the AdamW opti-
mizer is selected, resulting in a model accuracy of 96.1%.
The experimental results, presented in Fig. 6, demonstrate
a steady decrease in the loss value on the validation set as
the number of training iterations increases, with the model’s
accuracy gradually improving. Upon reaching 100 training
cycles, the model’s loss stabilizes, and the validation set accu-
racy achieves a peak value of 96.1%, indicating that the model
has converged.

5 Conclusions
This paper proposes a digital twin-based framework for
intelligent building ﬁre safety management. The framework,
which integrates the Internet of Things, is demonstrated with
123
Digital twin-enabled deep learning for real-time ﬁre situation … 11867
a real-time ﬁre recognition model that utilizes deep learning
algorithms to enable ﬁre detection and monitoring. A ﬁre-
monitoring digital twin platform was developed within the
Unity 3D environment, which facilitates human–computer
interaction. The accuracy of the developed predictive model
and the overall system capabilities were subsequently val-
idated through numerical experiments. The results indicate
that both the proposed framework and the predictive model
are effective for simulating and managing full-scale building
ﬁre scenarios. The platform supports routine ﬁre safety man-
agement and emergency response, with the system capable
of rapidly detecting ﬁres and triggering the alarm mechanism
automatically.
The proposed digital twin-based ﬁre protection system
breaks through the limitation that the traditional system is
triggered only when the ﬁre characteristics are obvious;
integrates multi-source sensor data to build a real-time 3D
ﬁre model; visualizes the development of the ﬁre dynam-
ics (including smoke diffusion, temperature distribution,
etc.); solves the defects of the traditional system of a single
alarm and the lack of spatial information; and integrates the
Internet of Things (IoT), artiﬁcial intelligence and 3D visu-
alization technologies; and builds a closed-loop “sensing-
analysis-warning-disposal” system. It integrates the Internet
of Things, artiﬁcial intelligence, and three-dimensional visu-
alization technology, and builds a closed-loop “perception-
analysis-warning-disposal” system.
The system is highly scalable and adaptable, and can be
ﬂexibly conﬁgured to ﬁt the speciﬁc characteristics of various
building types, including ofﬁce buildings, shopping centers,
and residential structures. By ﬁne-tuning the threshold and
weight parameters of the ﬁre recognition algorithm, the sys-
tem can be effectively adapted to different spatial topologies,
such as large-span atriums or densely partitioned interior
spaces. In addition, the system supports deep integration with
structured data imported from building information modeling
(BIM), which facilitates a more comprehensive understand-
ing of the spatial and functional layout of a building.
In addition, the system is capable of incorporating a wide
range of ﬁre protection equipment, such as gas-based sup-
pression systems and smoke extraction devices. Tailored
emergency response plans can be developed based on the
speciﬁc functional requirements and risk proﬁles of differ-
ent building types. Through these capabilities, the system
enables precise and efﬁcient ﬁre prevention and control
across a broad spectrum of architectural environments.
However, several limitations exist in this study, includ-
ing the absence of real-world data and the lack of realistic
considerations within the proposed framework. Additionally,
the evaluation of the sensor network and predictive model is
insufﬁcient, and the adaptability of the framework to var-
ious scenarios remains untested. These challenges can be
addressed by collecting more comprehensive, high-quality
data from real ﬁre incidents, adopting advanced modeling
techniques, and conducting extensive testing and validation.
