ABSTRACT
Digital twin-enabled safety monitoring of concrete arch dams requires deformation prediction models that are 
continuously updateable, physically informed, and uncertainty-aware. Nevertheless, existing approaches often 
struggle to effectively integrate simulation knowledge with monitoring data, especially under the dual con -
straints of limited response time and previously unseen real operating conditions. To overcome these challenges, 
this study proposes a physics-guided multi-fidelity Bayesian neural network (PGMF-BNN) framework for digital 
twin-oriented probabilistic deformation prediction. Low-fidelity data are generated from a three-dimensional 
finite element model to represent the dominant deformation behavior under hydrostatic loading initially. A 
multi-input multi-output (MIMO) architecture is constructed to capture spatial dependencies among monitoring 
points and enable collaborative prediction. The model is subsequently pre-trained using low-fidelity data to learn 
a mechanically consistent response skeleton, followed by a two-stage transfer learning process using prototype 
monitoring data to achieve virtual – physical alignment. Finally, Bayesian inference is incorporated to quantify 
epistemic and aleatoric uncertainties and to produce calibrated prediction intervals. The effectiveness of the 
proposed framework is demonstrated using a real concrete arch dam case. The results indicate that the proposed 
method achieves superior predictive performance, with coefficients of determination exceeding 0.90 at five of 
the six representative monitoring points (reaching 0.975 at PLA2) and mean absolute percentage errors below 
7%. The model successfully reconstructs the spatial correlation structure of multi-point deformation and dem -
onstrates robust performance under limited-data conditions and moderate noise levels (0% – 10%). These findings 
confirm that the proposed framework delivers accurate, physically consistent, and uncertainty-aware deforma -
tion predictions under both normal and disturbed operating conditions, providing a reliable and extensible so -
lution for digital twin-based dam safety monitoring and risk early warning.
1. Introduction
Concrete arch dams are critical hydraulic infrastructures character -
ized by high structural efficiency, strong adaptability to complex 
geological conditions, and long service lifetimes [1 – 4] . Ensuring their 
operational safety requires accurate characterization and prediction of 
structural responses under continuously evolving environmental and 
loading conditions [5 – 8] . In recent years, the emergence of digital twin 
technologies has fundamentally transformed dam safety management 
from conventional offline assessment to real-time and model-integrated 
monitoring paradigms [9 – 12] . Under this condition, deformation pre -
diction is no longer limited to retrospective fitting, but is expected to 
support continuous real-time state awareness, trend inference, and early 
warning under nonstationary operating conditions [13,14] .
Among the various monitoring indicators, deformation provides a 
direct and sensitive measure of the structural response to external loads. 
However, the deformation behavior of concrete arch dams is governed 
by the coupled effects of hydrostatic pressure, temperature variation, 
and time-dependent material properties, resulting in highly nonlinear, 
path-dependent, and temporally evolving characteristics [15,16] . These 
intrinsic complexities pose significant challenges to the development of 
predictive models that are simultaneously accurate, physically consis -
tent, and robust under diverse operating scenarios.

computational efficiency and interpretable parameterization of engi -
neering factors. For example, Mata et al. [17] proposed a water lev -
el – temperature – time (HTT) statistical model based on principal 
component analysis (PCA), designed to replace the seasonal sine func -
tion in the traditional water level – season – time (HST) model with 
measured temperature data from within the dam body, thereby more 
accurately capturing the influence of thermal effects on the deformation 
of arch dams. Studies have shown that this model significantly out -
performs traditional models in terms of displacement prediction accu -
racy and reduction of residual standard deviation. Sigtryggsd ´ottir et al. 
[18] developed an HST multi-source linear statistical model for pre -
dicting in-service settlement of concrete-faced rock-fill dams (CFRDs), 
correlating each statistical component with a viscoelastic-plastic 
constitutive model. The model innovatively accounts for unloa -
ding – reloading behavior caused by water level fluctuations and the 
creep effects of rock fill. These studies demonstrate how empirical 
monitoring knowledge can be formalized through physically meaningful 
statistical components. Nevertheless, their reliance on predefined 
functional forms and linear assumptions limits their capability in rep -
resenting complex nonlinear interactions among influencing factors
[19] . To address this limitation, some researchers have opted to employ 
deterministic numerical simulation models, such as the finite element 
method, which offers an alternative formalized approach to engineering 
knowledge by directly embedding structural mechanics and boundary 
conditions into the prediction process. For example, Xiong et al. [20]
built upon traditional deterministic displacement models by introducing 
a residual sequence correction approach. They first decomposed the 
residuals using CEEMDAN and multiscale permutation entropy; the 
high-frequency components were denoised using wavelets and then 
predicted by a GRU-LSTM, while the low-frequency components were 
predicted by an ARIMA model. Finally, these results were combined 
with those from the deterministic model to form a hybrid prediction 
model. Results demonstrate that this model better captures the chaotic 
and uncertain information within the residuals, thereby improving the 
prediction accuracy of arch dam displacement. Yang et al. [21]
addressed the shortcoming of traditional HST models in adequately 
characterizing the combined effects of “ structure-topography-geology- 
high water pressure ” in high-arch dams. They proposed a hybrid HST 
model that couples water pressure-induced deformation obtained from 
elastic three-dimensional finite element simulations with seasonal and 
temporal terms. They performed full-parameter optimization using an 
improved PSO algorithm that incorporates stochastic inertia weights 
and dynamic learning factors. Case studies demonstrate that this model 
achieves higher fitting and prediction accuracy compared to traditional 
models. These studies indicate that numerical simulations can 
strengthen the mechanical interpretability of dam response prediction. 
However, their practical application in real-time prediction is con -
strained by high computational cost, model simplifications, and un -
certainties in material parameters and boundary conditions, which often 
lead to systematic discrepancies between simulated and measured re -
sponses [22,23] .
To further enhance the characterization ability of complex nonlinear 
responses of dams and improve the shortcomings of traditional models 
in real-time performance, data-driven models represented by deep 
learning have gradually become an important research direction for dam 
safety prediction. This type of model, with its excellent feature extrac -
tion ability, can efficiently and accurately capture the nonlinear features 
of structural deformation. For example, Jia et al. [24] used a PSO- 
optimized XGBoost model and SHAP values to analyze the contribu -
tions of water pressure, temperature, and time-effect factors, thereby 
achieving mechanistic clustering of monitoring points. Based on this, 
they employed a PSO-optimized iTransformer to construct a panel data 
prediction model capable of capturing spatiotemporal correlations 
across multiple monitoring points. This method overcomes the 
shortcomings of traditional clustering that ignores factor contribution 
and single point prediction that cannot reflect overall deformation. Yang 
et al. [25] utilized an improved cloud model to extract digital features 
from displacement sequences and employed Agglomerative Hierarchical 
Clustering (AHC) to partition monitoring points with similar deforma -
tion patterns; subsequently, based on the partitioning results, they 
constructed a deep learning multi-input multi-output (MIMO) predic -
tion framework that integrates CNN and BiLSTM to simultaneously 
predict the displacements of multiple monitoring points within each 
partition, while further enhancing model performance through batch 
normalization, dropout, and early stopping mechanisms, providing an 
efficient and practical new method for dam structural health moni -
toring. These studies show that deep learning can transform monitoring 
data into high-dimensional feature representations and capture spatially 
coordinated deformation patterns. However, purely data-driven ap -
proaches still suffer from several critical limitations in engineering 
practice. In particular, the scarcity and noise of monitoring data, the lack 
of extrapolation capability under unseen operating conditions, and the 
absence of physical constraints may result in predictions that violate 
fundamental mechanical principles, thereby undermining their reli -
ability in safety – critical applications [26,27] .
In response to these challenges, multi-fidelity surrogate modeling 
has emerged as a hot research topic in recent years. Multi-fidelity 
modeling seeks to balance physical laws and prediction accuracy by 
integrating low-fidelity numerical simulation data with high-fidelity 
experimental data. Its core concept involves using simulation data to 
construct a fundamental mechanical framework and then applying 
nonlinear corrections based on experimental data sequences [28] . For 
example, Guo et al. [29] used spatial mapping techniques to integrate 
numerical simulations from low-fidelity extended finite element models 
(XFEM) with high-fidelity in-situ monitoring data. By employing multi- 
fidelity surrogate models to establish complex mappings between 
damage parameters and structural responses in their predictions, they 
significantly improved identification accuracy and computational effi -
ciency while avoiding repetitive grid division. Multi-fidelity approaches 
provide an effective pathway toward balancing physical interpretability 
and predictive accuracy [30] . However, when applied to complex dam 
systems, several key challenges remain unresolved. First, the discrep -
ancy between virtual simulation models and real structural responses is 
difficult to eliminate, particularly under time-varying operational con -
ditions. Second, most existing studies focus on single-point prediction 
and fail to explicitly model the spatial coupling and collaborative 
behavior of deformation across the dam body. Third, uncertainty 
quantification is often treated in a simplified manner, without dis -
tinguishing between epistemic uncertainty arising from model structure 
and parameter limitations, and aleatoric uncertainty induced by envi -
ronmental variability and measurement noise. These limitations hinder 
the construction of reliable probabilistic prediction boundaries and 
restrict the applicability of existing methods in digital twin-based real- 
time monitoring and risk assessment.
To address the above challenges, this study proposes a physics- 
guided multi-fidelity Bayesian neural network (PGMF-BNN) frame -
work for probabilistic deformation prediction of concrete arch dams in a 
digital twin-based monitoring. The proposed framework integrates 
physical simulation, monitoring data, and probabilistic learning within 
a unified modeling architecture. Specifically, low-fidelity data gener -
ated from a three-dimensional finite element model are utilized to guide 
the pre-training of the neural network, enabling it to learn the funda -
mental mechanical response patterns of the dam. A two-stage transfer 
learning strategy is then introduced to systematically reduce the 
discrepancy between simulated and measured responses under opera -
tional conditions. Furthermore, a multi-input multi-output (MIMO) ar -
chitecture is constructed to explicitly capture the spatial coupling 
characteristics among multiple monitoring points. To enhance predic -
tion reliability, Bayesian inference combined with MC Dropout is 
employed to jointly quantify epistemic and aleatoric uncertainties,

It should be pointed out that the novelty of this study does not lie in 
the separate use of multi-fidelity learning, transfer learning, MIMO 
modeling, or Bayesian inference. On the contrary, its main contribution 
lies in integrating these technologies into a digital twin framework 
guided by dam mechanisms. In this framework, low-fidelity finite 
element simulations are used to understand mechanical response skel -
eton dominated by hydrostatic pressure, high-fidelity monitoring data 
are used to correct residual responses related to temperature and time- 
effect, MIMO modeling is used to represent spatially coordinated de -
formations, and Bayesian inference is used to construct probabilistic 
monitoring boundaries.
The main contributions of this study are summarized as follows: 
• A physics-guided multi-fidelity learning framework is developed to 
integrate finite element simulation data with monitoring data, 
enabling consistent representation of dam deformation under 
evolving operating conditions.
• A two-stage transfer learning strategy is proposed to bridge the gap 
between virtual simulation models and real structural responses, 
improving model adaptability and accuracy.
• A Bayesian neural network with MC Dropout is introduced to achieve 
unified quantification of epistemic and aleatoric uncertainties, 
enhancing the credibility of probabilistic prediction.
• A MIMO-based collaborative prediction architecture is established to 
capture spatial coupling relationships among monitoring points and 
improve multi-point prediction performance.
Unlike purely data-driven approaches, the proposed framework le -
verages multi-fidelity modeling to construct a mechanically consistent 
response skeleton with full operating-condition coverage, which is 
subsequently refined using monitoring data to enable real-time adaptive 
prediction under evolving and previously unseen operating conditions.

2. Research framework
To address the challenges of virtual – physical inconsistency, adaptive 
monitoring under evolving operating conditions, and the lack of unified 
representation of multi-point structural responses in digital twin appli -
cations of concrete arch dams, a physics-guided multi-fidelity Bayesian 
neural network (PGMF-BNN) framework is developed for online state 
monitoring. As illustrated in Fig. 1 , the proposed framework integrates 
physical simulation, monitoring data, and probabilistic learning within 
a unified digital twin paradigm. It follows a closed-loop modeling 
mechanism that combines multi-source data fusion, physics-informed 
initialization, data-driven correction, uncertainty-aware prediction, 
and spatial collaborative perception. Through this integration, the 
framework enables continuous alignment between virtual models and 
real structural responses, while dynamically updating monitoring 
benchmarks under changing operational conditions.
Specifically, the framework consists of five key components: 
(1) Multi-source heterogeneous data integration
Low-fidelity simulation data generated from finite element models 
and high-fidelity field monitoring data are integrated within a unified 
data representation space. This integration establishes a transferable 
mapping between virtual simulations and real structural observations, 
providing the foundation for multi-fidelity learning and digital twin 
updating. 
(2) Physics-guided initialization of the virtual mechanical 
representation
A physics-guided pre-training strategy is employed using low-fidelity 
simulation data to encode the dominant mechanical behavior of the 
dam, particularly the deformation response driven by hydrostatic 
loading. This process embeds structural mechanics knowledge into the 
model initialization, forming a physically consistent baseline represen -
tation for subsequent data-driven refinement. 
(3) Virtual – physical discrepancy correction via residual learning
To bridge the gap between simulated and observed responses, high- 
fidelity monitoring data are incorporated through a residual learning 
mechanism. This component captures complex effects that are difficult 
to model explicitly, such as temperature variation and time-dependent 
behavior, enabling the digital twin model to evolve from a static simu -
lation surrogate to a dynamically updated representation of the real

(4) Probabilistic boundary construction and reliability quantification
To enhance prediction reliability, the framework extends determin -

inference. By modeling network parameters as random variables, both 
epistemic and aleatoric uncertainties are jointly quantified. The result -
ing probabilistic prediction intervals serve as dynamic health envelopes 
of the structure, providing a quantitative basis for anomaly detection 
and risk-aware decision-making. 
(5) Multi-point collaborative state perception
Considering the strong spatial coupling characteristics of arch dam 
deformation, a multi-input multi-output (MIMO) architecture is adopted 
to jointly model multiple monitoring points. Through shared latent 
representations, the framework captures spatial correlation and collab -
orative response patterns across different dam regions, enabling system- 
level state perception beyond isolated single-point predictions.
Overall, the proposed framework establishes a scalable methodo -
logical foundation for intelligent monitoring and risk early warning of 
large-scale hydraulic structures.
3. Methodology
This section presents the methodology of the proposed physics- 
guided multi-fidelity digital twin framework for dam deformation pre -
diction. A physically interpretable decomposition of deformation 
mechanisms is first introduced to establish the basis for model devel -
opment. Building on this foundation, a physics-guided multi-fidelity 
framework is constructed, in which two-stage transfer learning is used 
for virtual – physical alignment and MIMO-based modeling is adopted to 
characterize spatial collaborative responses. The deterministic formu -
lation is further extended to probabilistic prediction and uncertainty 
quantification by incorporating Bayesian modeling and MC Dropout. 
Finally, the implementation details are provided.

3.2. Physics-guided multi-fidelity MIMO framework of deformation 
prediction for concrete arch dams
3.2.1. Overall framework of the physics-guided multi-fidelity model
Conventional deterministic finite element models provide a well- 
established mechanical basis for describing dam behavior. However, 
within the context of dam safety monitoring and digital twin modeling, 
they are more appropriately treated as low-fidelity data sources. Here, 
the term “ low-fidelity ” (LF) does not imply that the numerical solutions 
themselves are inaccurate. Rather, it indicates that a non-negligible 
discrepancy may exist between simulation-based responses and the 
actual structural behavior, owing to uncertainties in material parameter 
identification, simplifications of boundary conditions, approximations 
in time-dependent constitutive modeling, and the omission of complex 
environmental disturbances [33] . Therefore, finite element models are 
more suitable for providing mechanistic prior knowledge and virtual 
prototypes within a digital twin framework. While, on-site monitoring 
data directly reflect the actual operating state of the dam and therefore 
constitute high-fidelity (HF) observations. Nevertheless, such data are 
often constrained by limited sample size, measurement noise, and 
incomplete coverage of extreme operating conditions. Moreover, purely 
data-driven models trained only on monitoring data may generate pre -
dictions that lack physical plausibility when extrapolated beyond the 
historical data domain.
To simultaneously ensure physical consistency and engineering ac -
curacy, this study develops a physics-guided multi-fidelity Bayesian 
neural network (PGMF-BNN) framework, in which low-fidelity data are 
first used to establish a fundamental mechanical response skeleton, and 
high-fidelity data are subsequently introduced to correct the discrep -
ancy between simulation and reality. On this basis, Bayesian inference is 
incorporated to generate probabilistic predictions for adaptive moni -
toring benchmarks.

3.2.2. Physics-guided two-stage transfer learning for virtual – physical 
alignment
To address the discrepancy between simulation-based representa -
tions and real structural responses, a physics-guided two-stage transfer 
learning strategy is developed for virtual – physical alignment. Based on 
the additive digital twin formulation in Section 3.2 , the proposed 
approach separates the learning process into two sequential stages with 
clearly differentiated yet complementary roles. In Stage I, low-fidelity 
simulation data are used to establish a mechanically consistent 
response skeleton, providing a physics-informed initialization of the 
model. In Stage II, high-fidelity monitoring data are introduced to cor -
rect the virtual – physical discrepancy through residual learning, 
enabling adaptive refinement toward real operating conditions. This 
staged learning mechanism ensures that physical consistency is pre -
served while allowing data-driven correction under evolving conditions.
The detailed formulations of the two stages are presented in Sections 
3.2.2.1 and 3.2.2.2 , respectively.

3.3. Probabilistic prediction and uncertainty quantification
3.3.1. Bayesian neural network for deformation prediction
In practical dam operation, deformation prediction is affected not 
only by the uncertainty of model parameters and network structure, but 
also by measurement noise and environmental disturbances. Conse -
quently, deterministic models that provide only point estimates cannot 
fully satisfy the requirements of digital twin-based safety monitoring, 
where both predictive accuracy and reliability assessment are essential
[37 – 39] . To address this issue, the proposed framework adopts a

variables, the BNN provides a rigorous mathematical framework for 
capturing both epistemic uncertainties associated with model parame -
ters and aleatoric uncertainty arising from observation noise, thereby 
enabling the construction of reliable prediction intervals for dam 
deformation monitoring.

3.4. Implementation and deployment of the digital twin prediction 
framework
To illustrate the implementation procedure of the proposed frame -
work, the technical route for digital twin-based deformation prediction 
of concrete arch dams is summarized in Fig. 3 .
The procedure consists of seven interconnected stages. First, low- 
fidelity simulation data and high-fidelity monitoring data are con -
structed to form a multi-source heterogeneous dataset. Second, a dual- 
branch additive mapping model is established, in which the physical 
branch captures the dominant mechanical response and the residual 
branch corrects the virtual – physical discrepancy. Third, the physical 
branch is pre-trained using low-fidelity data to establish a mechanically 
meaningful response skeleton. Fourth, high-fidelity monitoring data are 
introduced for transfer learning, where the residual branch is activated 
and the virtual model is aligned with actual operating conditions 
through differential learning rates and physical regularization. Fifth, a 
MIMO-based collaborative prediction mechanism is constructed to 
capture the spatial coupling among multiple monitoring points. Sixth, 
Bayesian inference with MC Dropout is incorporated to achieve proba -
bilistic prediction and uncertainty quantification. Finally, dynamic 
monitoring benchmarks are generated from the predictive mean and 
uncertainty intervals to support online trend inference, anomaly 
detection, and risk early warning. Overall, the proposed framework 
provides an integrated technical route for physically interpretable, 
adaptively updated, and uncertainty-aware digital twin prediction of 
arch dams.
To further improve the reproducibility of the proposed framework, 
the implementation details and key hyperparameters used in model 
training and probabilistic inference are summarized in Table 1 . The 
hyperparameters include the network architecture, optimizer, loss 
function, learning rates in the two-stage training process, dropout 
setting, batch size, and the number of MC Dropout samples. These set -
tings were adopted consistently in the following case study and model 
comparison experiments.
Full-batch training was adopted in both stages because the sample 
size of the low-fidelity simulation data and high-fidelity monitoring data 
was relatively limited. In Stage I, all low-fidelity samples were used in 
each epoch to learn the mechanically consistent response skeleton. In 
Stage II, all high-fidelity training samples were used in each epoch for 
virtual – physical alignment and residual correction.

the upstream and downstream directions, respectively. The finite 
element model was discretized primarily using hexahedral and tetra -
hedral elements, with a total of 151,200 mesh cells. Since the dam uti -
