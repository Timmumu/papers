ABSTRACT 
The safety and effectiveness of marine piling construction are hindered by the display of imprecise spatial in -
formation and the potential for collisions due to the complexity of pile positions and attitudes. Digital twin (DT) 
can solve the above problems in piling construction because of their inherent three-dimensionality and real-time 
feedback. The present study proposes a system that utilizes the DT framework to construct a collision early 
warning system intended for marine piling. A five-dimensional model is used to introduce the overall archi -
tecture. The main twin objects are piles. To maximize the effectiveness of DT, the five-dimensional model is 
composed of four independently maintainable development modules. Consequently, a prototype of the system is 
created. The core of the system comprises an algorithm for pile positioning and an algorithm for collision early 
warning, especially for complex pile groups. Two distinct implementations are identified: one involves con -
trolling the virtual with the real, while the other involves controlling the real with the virtual. Field experiments 
are conducted with the aim of establishing a benchmark for collision early warning during the construction 
process. The prototype of the system is capable of producing a simulated environment through a dual-layer 
mapping approach, which involves pile-boat mapping and early-warning feedback mapping. The presented 
system in a practical wharf project showcases both the capacity for three-dimensional visualization of collision 
warning and the ability to provide feedback for boat control. The research enables more intuitive and effective 
pile positioning. 
1. Introduction 
Pile foundations have emerged as a crucial component in the 
development of viaducts, high-rise buildings, offshore ports, and water 
transport wharves. Based on the characteristics of the construction 
environment, piling can be divided into two categories: land piling and 
marine piling. The conventional approach for piling involves the utili -
zation of a total station, or theodolite, which is measured by construc -
tion personnel [1] . In contrast to piling on land, marine piling is 
vulnerable to external influences, including the swinging of the piling 
boat, fluctuations in the water surface, and the effects of wind [2] . The 
efficiency of manual construction positioning is low, and its precision is 
limited [3] . 
Global navigation satellite systems (GNSS) and real-time kinematic 
(RTK) positioning are getting more mature, and their costs are steadily 
decreasing. These tools find extensive application in the domain of civil 
engineering, including but not limited to construction machinery con -
trol [4,5] , high-rise buildings [6] , bridges [7] , and dam structural health 
monitoring [8] . The level of accuracy in positioning has reached a res -
olution of millimeters. The initial implementation of the GNSS in piling 
was documented by Seward et al. in their publication [1] . The automatic 
land drilling positioning was achieved through the utilization of the 
advanced PC1O4 processor. The positioning of piles on land was 
investigated by Takeshi et al. [2,9,10] through the utilization of a line 
laser range finder. Yao et al. [3] and Tserng et al. [11] have presented 
models for marine piling that address the challenge of computing the 
coordinates of the pile center in real time. The proposed model utilizes a 
combination of multiple GNSS receivers, inclinometers, and range 
finders to develop diverse algorithms for pile positioning calculations. 
The algorithms use three types of coordinate systems, namely geodetic 
coordinate system, engineering coordinate system, and ship-fixed co -
ordinate system. The geodetic coordinate system is used to measure the

it achieves the positioning of marine piling without the involvement of a 
total station or theodolite. 
The algorithm for pile positioning utilizes a combination of GNSS 
receivers, inclinometers, and rangefinders to facilitate accurate posi -
tioning across a variety of working conditions. Although positioning 
technology has been improved, the two-dimensional thinking that 
originated from manual drawings still continues. The pile is represented 
as an ellipse at the point where the engineering design elevation plane 
intersects with the axis of the pile. This replacement of the pile results in 
the loss of information regarding the three-dimensional staggered 
arrangement of the piles in space. Practical engineering experience in 
marine piling construction tells us that the layout of the pile body is a 
staggered arrangement of straight piles and inclined piles. Considering 
that the objects below the water surface cannot be foreseen during 
construction, the positioning challenge is further increased. Also, it is 
challenging for construction personnel to acquire precise spatial posi -
tion data for complex construction conditions. They must rely on their 
experience to steer the boat. In addition, unexpected pile positions and 
attitudes may occur during construction. There are two reasons for this 
unexpectedness. On the one hand, while the design phase has success -
fully addressed the potential for pile collision, the limitations imposed 
by the vessel’s configuration and the load-bearing capacity of the pile 
framework during construction have not been taken into account. On 
the other hand, the physical act of construction can lead to a minor 
deviation in both the torsion and inclination angles of the pile. The angle 
deviation is determined by the inclination of the pile, the frame of the 
boat, and the weight of the pile. Small changes in angle can cause large 
changes in the position and attitude of the pile in three-dimensional 
space. Therefore, the current two-dimensional marine piling posi -
tioning software is unable to intuitively and in real-time map the actual 
construction conditions for construction operation feedback. The actual 
construction pile position does not match the one specified in the design. 
The difficulty of construction and the risk of collision increased. This 
collision refers to the collision between two pile bodies. The collision 
between piles under construction and piles that have been completed is 
something that should be avoided during construction and is the main 
purpose for establishing the digital twin (DT). 
Therefore, based on the current pile positioning technology, marine 
piling urgently needs intuitive and precise technology to reduce the 
challenges and risks of pile sinking construction. The DT has natural 
three-dimensional properties. It is a real-time twin of reality. Grieves 
first proposed the concept of the DT as a “product life cycle management 
model” [16]. Despite the fact that many research results have surfaced 
after two decades of development and implementation, 96% of them 
have emerged since 2016 [17], with 50% of these results pertaining to 
manufacturing and production [18]. Only 5% are associated with the 
built environment [19]. The absence of comprehensive theoretical 
frameworks and construction techniques for unified DT models is 
evident, even within the context of manufacturing and production. Tao 
et al. described in detail the essential technologies, enabling technolo -
gies [20,21], and instruments for establishing DT models [22]. Jia et al. 
[23,24] constructed a DT model with multiple scales and scenes in two 
sections. In recent years, hardware and algorithm iterations have 
resulted in the emergence of new technologies like 5G [26], the Internet 
of things [27], and mixed reality [28], which have been integrated with 
DT to adapt to modern Industry 4.0 development with the concept of 
“combining the virtual and the real, controlling the real with the vir -
tual.” The implementation of this technology is progressively being 
observed in various sectors such as manufacturing and production 
[29,30], construction, energy, and medical domains [31]. The civil en -
gineering is currently in the early stages of conducting research on DTs, 
which means implementing a DT is extremely challenging [25]. In 
addition to the complexity of DTs mentioned above, there exists some 
confusion and its potential overlap with similar technological research, 
including building information modeling and cyber-physical systems 
[25]. 
DTs can provide real-time mapping of the real physical world. This is 
important for marine piling construction. Because it requires intuitive 
and precise technology to solve three problems: (1) two-dimensional 
representation that lacks spatial information, for example, ellipse 
replacement for inclined piles; (2) the position of piles under the water 
surface is not visible; (3) actual construction machinery limitations and 
the physical act of construction cause it to deviate from design and in -
crease the risk of collisions. The utilization of intuitive three- 
dimensional displays and real-world control over virtual counterparts 
has proven to be an effective solution for piling. At this stage, marine 
piling construction applies GNSS receivers, inclinometers, and range -
finders. An increasing volume of data has been produced, encompassing 
both structured data, such as time series that incorporate positional 
data, and unstructured data, such as audio and video. These data are 
important and necessary conditions for building a DT system. Real-time 
and rich data can establish a practical DT-enabled collision early 
warning system for marine piling. The construction of a DT and its 
paradigm consists of at least three essential components: the entity, the 
twin virtual body, and the twin data [21]. This study represents the 
inaugural application of the DT in the context of collision early warning 
for marine piling. 
The aforementioned issues have prompted the investigation and 
development of algorithms for pile positioning and collision early 
warning in challenging pile construction. One algorithm offers precise 
positioning, while the other algorithm supplies collision warning data 
during the initial piling phase. Subsequently, a virtual model that 
accurately corresponds with the entity in real-time is generated to 
simulate the process of piling. Piles serve as main DT objects. At the 
actual construction site, the DT-enabled collision early warning system 
was used. The utilization of the system has the potential to facilitate the 
management of the boat and pile frame through a simulated model that 
provides timely feedback. The construction process is systematically 
monitored and achieved a collision early warning between piles under 
construction and completed piles. At the same time, the intuitiveness 
and accuracy of pile positioning are enhanced.

2. Framework of the system 
2.1. Basic process and collision probability of piling 
The marine pile positioning is achieved by first striking the pile body 
with the boat and then driving it with a pile hammer. The primary hull, 
cockpit, and pile frame make up the main part of the boat. The cockpit 
regulates the movement of the hull, pile hanging, and pile hammering. 
The holder and the pile hammer make up the pile frame. The hammering 
and hanging are handled by the pile frame. There are four significant 
steps in the piling process: 
(1) Hanging. The pile frame leans slightly forward to lift the pile 
body. As the holder is stabilized, gradually move the hull closer to 
its intended position. The pile is then locked by the holder to 
reduce shaking. 
(2) Adjusting angles. The angles are determined by the designated 
torsion, slope ratio, the actual weight, and the pile frame bearing 
capacity. During this time, the pile positioning algorithm adjusts 
the pile ’ s position in accordance with the design. 
(3) Fine positioning. The pile is gradually fine-tuned to the design 
position. At the same time, move the boat so that it is at a spec -
ified safe angle from the cable. Then, sink the pile into the water 
until it reaches the bottom and stabilizes the hull for a period of 
time. 
(4) Start piling. The diesel hammer on the pile frame is placed at the 
proper height so that the force of the pile is even. Evacuate 
workers after installation and plumbing. The cockpit gives in -
structions to complete the construction via pile driving. 
Fig. 1 shows the four important steps of the piling process, where 
changing the angles and fine-tuning the position in the middle photo -
graph can easily lead to a collision. It is also the fundamental reason for 
establishing a DT-based collision early warning system for marine piling. 
The construction difficulty increases when piles are relatively dense and 
arranged in a staggered manner. Suppose the bearing capacity of the pile 
frame is insufficient to elevate the pile to the design slope ratio. In this 
case, there will be a non-negligible deviation between the constructed 
and designed pile positions. 
2.2. Challenges of collision 
The main function of the DT-enabled collision early warning system 
is to indicate possible collisions and then avoid them. The collision here 
refers to the collision between piles under construction and completed 
piles. In marine piling construction, especially in wharves, the 
arrangement of piles is relatively complicated. Straight piles and in -
clined piles are arranged crosswise to meet the bearing capacity re -
quirements. In the design stage, collision has been avoided, but the hull, 
construction personnel, and construction environment have not been 
considered. They are factors that determine whether the final project 
conforms to the design. Based on actual construction experience, our 
designs may be subject to change due to insufficient geological survey. 
For example, increase pile density or increase the inclination angle of 
inclined piles to improve the overall bearing capacity of a wharf. 
Embedding the piles to be constructed around the piles that have already 
been constructed increases the challenges of construction and the pos -
sibility of collisions. It is rare when designs change. What is more 
common is that construction personnel relax the implementation of 
design requirements due to the constraints of the hull shape and con -
struction environment. For example, because the angle of the inclined 
pile is too large, the weight of the pile body exceeds the bearing capacity 
of the pile frame of the boat, making it impossible to lift the pile body 
during piling. In this situation, the angle of the inclined pile must be 
reduced. Therefore, construction deviations occur. The inclination and 
position that will not cause collision are no longer the design values, 
when the construction of the next pile is carried out. Therefore, both 
inclination and displacement cannot be warned before collision takes 
place. The closer the piles are, the more challenging it is to avoid 
collisions. 
However, two-dimensional drawings of pile bodies and hulls are 
used to guide piling construction. Planar images inevitably lose spatial 
information, so the possibility of detecting collisions is very small. 
Almost all solutions rely on the experience of construction personnel. 
Any carelessness may cause a collision. Therefore, collisions are difficult 
to avoid relying on construction personnel. Avoiding collisions is 
challenging.

2.4. Digital twin-enabled collision early warning system for marine piling 
This system combines three fundamental elements, namely a DT 
entity, a twin virtual body, and twin data, with the requirement for 
collision early warning in pile positioning under complex pile positions. 
The main purpose of the DT is to prompt and avoid collisions between 
pile bodies, so the main object of the twin is piles. The proposed model 
shown in Fig. 3 is a five-dimensional model of marine piling, comprising 
a physical environment, a virtual environment, a feedback service, a 
connection, and twin data. 
(1) Physical environment 
The five-dimensional model is based on the physical environment 
and includes GNSS receivers, inclinometers, rangefinders, terrain, water 
surface, and physical entities of pile and boat. Our analysis involves the 
examination of torsion and slope ratio, as well as the amplitude of water 
surface fluctuation. The accuracy of pile positioning is directly influ -
enced by factors such as the degree of terrain undulation, tide level, pile 
attitude, and boat position. Piles refer to both piles that are currently 
under construction and those that have already been constructed. In the 
design phase, particular emphasis is placed on spatial information such 
as torsion, slope ratio, and spatial location within the design elevation 
plane. For the boat, the focus is on swing and spatial position, especially 
the shape of the hull.

The basis of a five-dimensional model is twin data, which primarily

Business services: (a) Provide various monitoring methods for pile 
positioning, such as status display, information prompts, and operation 
feedback; (b) Provide real-time data for functional services; (c) Provide 
decision-making rules to construction personnel through human -
– computer interaction. 
(4) Virtual environment 
The virtual environment is characterized by its ability to replicate the 
geometric properties of the physical environment, including its shape, 
size, position, and other relevant parameters, through the use of three- 
dimensional modeling techniques. As such, it bears a strong resem -
blance to its physical counterpart. The utilization of model rendering 
technology enhances geometric modeling, while the incorporation of 
essential lighting, materials, and special effects imbues the virtual 
environment with a natural texture. Simultaneously, the ability to 
navigate through scenes is achieved through the development of a 
human – computer interaction interface and corresponding external 
input event response mechanisms.

3. Procedures for pile positioning with collision early warning 
A methodology has been devised to assess the suitability of the five- 
dimensional model, which aligns with the concept of controlling the 
virtual with the real. It comprises four distinct modules, namely the data 
management module, feedback module, motion drive module, and 
system management module. Each module may be developed and 
maintained independently. 
The interconnection among the modules of the aforementioned five- 
dimensional model is shown in Fig. 4 . The physical environment of the 
method is accessed through the data management module, which pri -
marily relates to different forms of instrument interfaces that represent 
physical reality, such as GNSS receivers, inclinometers, and range -
finders. Three kinds of instruments are used to convert piles into DTs. 
Additionally, it creates and stores twin data, such as dynamic process 
data and attribute data for physical elements. The motion-driven mod -
ule is a virtual environment implementation that incorporates twin data 
real-time mapping, including real-time sensor data display, two- 
dimensional and three-dimensional graphic rendering, human -
– computer interaction, and other related functionalities. The feed back 
module that responsibles for command feedback is associated with the 
feedback service, which facilitates the implementation of the virtual 
control function. This function encompasses the pile positioning algo -
rithm and collision early warning algorithm, both of which are designed 
to operate effectively in complex pile positions. The system management 
module coordinates the connection of all modules pertaining to the five- 
dimensional model. 
3.1. Data management module 
This module is responsible for governing the entirety of the system ’ s 
data, including the refreshing of external data, driving model move -
ment, and conducting data analysis. It comprises two distinct modules, 
namely a data transmission module and a data visualization module, as 
shown in Fig. 5 . 
The primary function of the data transmission module is to oversee 
the management of information obtained by the data portal while 
simultaneously addressing data requests from each module. The data is 
generated by real-time measurements of the instrument. GNSS receivers 
receive latitude and longitude coordinates, inclinometers measure the 
angle of the pile, and rangefinders measure the distance between the pile 
and the hull. These data are sent to the computer via RS232 or RS485 
protocol. DT-enabled collision early warning systems can receive these 
data by setting the same protocol parameters. The software interface 
structure is defined by the Serial Port class in .NET. The Serial Port class 
contains many core methods for controlling hardware devices. It is the 
main way to realize twin data and data flow from physical to virtual. The 
information is transmitted to the program in the form of dictionary data 
pairs. After the above data is received, it is processed by other modules 
to form twin data. The Viewport3D class in .NET is utilized by the data 
visualization module to offer a display scene that effectively presents 
comprehensive details regarding the functioning of the construction site 
through an intuitive visualization. It depicts a hierarchical structure of 
terrain, water, piles, and vessel objects, which are organized and labeled 
for content display management. 
3.2. Motion drive module 
The motion driving module is responsible for controlling the virtual 
environment, including both dynamic and static data driving. Dynamic 
data drive pertains to the fluctuation of torsion angle, slope ratio, water 
surface, and spatial boat position. This ensures conformity with reality 
by promptly updating the data based on the twin data, thereby enabling 
control of the virtual and the real. The static data includes terrain, pile 
size, hull shape, and other three-dimensional model information related 
to a given scene. 
The motion driven module is accountable for the construction of 
scenes in the program. The flow of this module is illustrated in Fig. 6 and 
primarily encompasses geometric modeling such as piles, boats, water, 
terrain, etc. In order to effectively execute collision warnings, it is 
imperative to utilize high-fidelity models. The high-fidelity model rep -
resents an enhanced version of the geometric model, while the realistic 
texture incorporates the lighting, materials, and special effects, as per 
the twin data-driven approach. The field of human – computer

3.3. Feedback module 
The contents of the feedback service, which are essential for 
achieving the concept of “ controlling the virtual with the real ” , are 
encompassed within the feedback module. The algorithm for pile posi -
tioning is utilized to compute the boat and pile attitude. This informa -
tion is then employed to provide advanced warning regarding potential 
collisions between the under-construction pile and the piles that have 
been constructed. The feedback is relayed to the construction personnel, 
who can then adjust the pile attitude or move the boat accordingly. This 
process enables the control of virtual elements in reality. The procedure 
incorporates crucial components such as the pile positioning algorithm 
and the collision early warning algorithm, particularly in situations 
where pile positions are intricate. 
3.3.1. Pile positioning algorithm 
GNSS receivers are a crucial tool for acquiring precise location data 
during the process of pile driving in marine environments. It is designed 
to receive latitude and longitude coordinates, which can subsequently 
be transformed into specific engineering coordinates using seven pa -
rameters [14] . These parameters provide guidance to the boat, enabling 
it to navigate with precision within the construction area. Global posi -
tioning and navigation technology is now very mature. GPS, GLONASS, 
BeiDou, and Galileo are the four major GNSS in the world [34] . The 
GNSS receiver we use can receive all GNSS satellite signals and uses 
mature RTK technology. Therefore, in terms of positioning theory and 
hardware, its positioning accuracy can reach the millimeter level. In 
Sections 4.2 and Section 4.4 , we list in detail the results of positioning 
calculations and collision calculations involved in actual piling projects. 
Ultimately, it is shown that global position data provide the required 
accuracies for the purpose of application. The use of a rangefinder or 
inclinometer can establish the appropriate orientation for the pile. The 
collection of measurement data in the physical environment is con -
ducted in real-time, and the formation of twin data is achieved through 
the data management module. The motion drive module is responsible 
for achieving the rendering of piles. 
As a result of the unique nature of pile construction, a hydraulic or 
diesel hammer is utilized to strike the pile body. After construction is 
completed, most of the piles are embedded in the soil. Consequently, the 
installation of a positioning device at any stage of the pile construction 
process is challenging due to cost considerations. The soil will squeeze or 
damage the device attached to the pile.

3.3.2. Collision early warning algorithm 
The DT-enable early collision warning system provides alerts for 
potential collisions between piles that are under construction and those 
that have already been constructed. Several collision detection methods 
exist. The advancement of computer graphics and building information 
modeling (BIM) technology in recent years has led to significant prog -
ress in the field of collision detection [32] . The utilization of BIM has 
been extensively employed in various situations, including metro and 
underground pipelines [33] . In ArcGIS and AutoCAD software, collision 
detection necessitates a pre-existing three-dimensional model. 
Currently, the correct positioning of piles claims a greater emphasis on 
collision avoidance, whereby there is no spatial overlap between the 
two. In the event that the distance between the two entities falls below 
the established safety threshold, a prompt notification is generated, 
furnishing details regarding the closest proximity point and offering 
corrective measures for distance regulation. 
The algorithms for detecting collisions in spatial geometry exhibit a 
greater degree of complexity and diversity. The process of collision 
detection involves assessing the positional relationship between the 
center lines of two cylindrical objects, specifically determining whether 
they are co-linear, parallel, intersecting, or dissimilar. Additionally, the 
position of the standard plumb line and the center lines of the two pipes 
are taken into consideration. In cases where the central axis of a pipe 
deviates and the two pendants of its standard plumb line do not fall 
within the line segment concurrently, the algorithm may yield inaccu -
rate results, indicating imprecision in the judgment condition. The 
precise characterization of the bus bar and the criteria for the junction of 
its ends are inadequate. Furthermore, there is a tendency to overlook 
computations or scenarios that have not been taken into account. 
Most of these methods involve calculating cylinder-shaped spatial 
distances. Assuming diverse pile categories, such as square piles, are 
being contemplated within the simulated setting. Under such circum -
stances, the level of complexity is likely to escalate swiftly, rendering the 
task of devising a consistent and precise computational methodology 
arduous. Marine pile positioning has a vast spatial extent, and the 
number of piles designed is typically in the hundreds. An increase in the 
number of piles results in a significant rise in computational demands, 
thereby exerting a considerable processing burden on the data man -
agement and motion drive modules of the system. The operational ef -
ficiency of the DT system may be diminished as a result of this. Ensuring 
worldwide expeditiousness and optimal levels of exactitude and preci -
sion in collision detection is imperative. Consequently, the system in -
corporates a collision early warning algorithm that exhibits high 
efficiency. The procedural steps are as follows:

3.4. System management module 
Data transmission, interface calling, signal transmission, etc. are all 
processed between modules via the system management module. It 
mainly has three functions, namely real-time data flow update, file and 
log management, and inter-module coordination. Fig. 11 shows these 
three functions and the specific contents they contain. 
For real-time data flow updates, the data management module sends 
requests to the data transmission module, and the system management 
module provides a response based on the data management module ’ s 
mapped data list to obtain data in real time. At last, the data is updated 
in the request-response cycle, allowing the data visualization module to 
update the appropriate graphical user interface. 
For file and log management, first, implement the hardware device 
data archiving of the data management module. These data are original 
monitoring data during construction. They are first-hand information on 
the construction site directly measured by various monitoring in -
struments. Then, the virtual environment data archiving of the motion 
drive module is implemented. The evolution process of the virtual 
environment is recorded to facilitate subsequent analysis and tracing. 
Finally, the two algorithm calculation processes and result archiving in 
the feedback module are implemented. This is the purpose and core of 
the entire piling construction, and it is also the most valuable data in the 
DT system proposed in this study. 
For inter-module coordination, in order to achieve the normal 
operation of various functions, coordination between modules is 
necessary. In particular, the DT-enabled collision eraly warning system 
uses a method that encompasses four modules and sets up relatively 
independent modules based on the topological relationship of modular 
subsystems, allowing for rapid updates to the iterative module subsys -
tem based on the needs of field operations and access to the real-time 
data trigger procedure. The functionality of other modules will not be 
adversely affected by maintenance on any one module ’ s subsystem. For 
instance, it is possible to iterate the data visualization module ’ s function 
while maintaining reliable motion driving and data transfer. Therefore, 
the system management module must undertake the coordination work 
between modules.
