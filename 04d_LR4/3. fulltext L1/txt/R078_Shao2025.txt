ABSTRACT
For decades, research on dam safety monitoring systems has received considerable critical attention due to their 
significance in ensuring public safety and social economic development. In view of the rapid development of 
computational technologies, it ’ s becoming increasingly difficult to ignore the urgent need to accelerate the 
intelligent development of dam safety monitoring systems. The emphasis of the prior studies usually lay in the 
dam safety monitoring data and structural health analysis. However, they neglect the importance of the real-time 
interactions of the systems, which makes the presented work not suitable for today ’ s digital age. As such, based 
on digital twin platform, this paper aims to propose a visualization system for dam safety monitoring. Primarily, 
the next-generation and real-time rendering technologies are introduced to construct digital twin platform. The 
digital twin platform serves as a digital sand table to accurately reflect the dam current state and real-time 
monitoring information. Subsequently, artificial intelligence algorithms and numerical simulation are applied 
in monitoring data analysis, prediction model construction, structural safety evaluation, and risk early warning. 
Based on the windows platform, all of the above system functions are implemented in C ++ , Python languages, 
and OpenGL. Eventually, this presented real-time digital twin system was applied in a real dam in China. The 
direct outcome shows its superiority and effectiveness in a continuous and accurate dam safety evaluation.
1. Introduction
In today ’ s society, dams have a significant impact on water supply, 
irrigation, flood control, and power generation. During their servicing 
period, a large number of dams are facing the challenges of unusual 
weather, aging, and increasing external load. Therefore, dam damage 
and failures sometimes may occur, which will lead to a number of ca -
sualties and enormous loss of property. To prevent the collapse of dams, 
developing a more scientific and intelligent dam safety monitoring 
system is a research hotspot in the field of dam disaster management.
During the past decades, the pioneer work can be traced to some 
dam-safety-related information systems overseas ( Gorini et al., 2010 ). 
But these works have only considered the basic databases or materials, 
and ignored the automation and diverse functions of the systems ( Man 
et al., 2018 ). The advent of computer technologies and new dam health 
diagnose theories accelerate the development of dam safety monitoring 
system. In the late 1980s, to analyse dam safety, a function to evaluate 
the flood hazard was applied in the system DAMSAFE ( Bocchiola & 
Rosso, 2014 ), which helps to approximate but comprehensive assess -
ment of flood hazard and dam risk. For the purpose of ensuring dam 
consistent safety, the Korea Water Resource Corporation (Kwater) has 
developed a dam safety management system, which is called KDSMS in 
2009 ( Jeon et al., 2009 ). Although a lot of effort has been spent on 
promoting KDSMS to serve as a multi-functional enterprise management 
system, including dealing with safety management of each field, 
research centre, headquarters office and their correlation, it can only

storage, pre-processing, and analysis of monitoring data ( Li & Wang,

readability and maintainability of SQL, the development efficiency of 
the system has been significantly improved.
To date, artificial intelligence (AI) technologies are changing the 
world, especially affecting the engineering management. In the domain 
of dam safety monitoring, the advanced technologies have been widely 
used in monitoring outlier detection ( Zheng et al., 2022; Chen et al., 
2024; Meng et al., 2024 ), safety monitoring model construction ( Zheng 
et al., 2024; Li et al., 2021; Hu et al., 2020 ) and risk early warning ( Chen 
et al., 2021 ). Furthermore, the concept of Digital Twins (DTs) was 
investigated quite intensively in recent years. A digital twin can be 
regarded as a virtual substitute of a physical system that enables real- 
time monitoring, analysis, and optimization of its performance ( Juarez 
et al., 2021 ). The use of digital twins is attracting lots of attention in 
various fields, such as, manufacturing, energy, healthcare, trans -
portation, and construction. The technology effectively reduces addi -
tional costs of physical experiments for testing different conditions in a 
real environment. There has been a turning point in developing dam 
safety monitoring system in accordance with the advent of DTs. The 
initiative aim of DTs is to digitise and informatize physical character -
istics of a real dam, which provides a new solution to simulate and 
analyse complicated behaviours of dams ( Kaewunruen et al., 2021; Lu

The main contributions of this paper are as follows. Firstly, the 
specific framework of the visualization system for dam safety moni -
toring with application of dam digital twin platform is proposed. Sec -
ondly, we clarify the approach of construct visualization model of the 
dam by applying next-generation modelling and real-time rendering 
technologies, which serve as a digital twin platform. C ++ and Python 
programming languages, database technology and intelligent algo -
rithms are used to process and analyse the real-time monitoring data, so 
as to reflect the real-time structural behaviours of the dam in the visu -
alization window of the system. Thirdly, we apply this proposed system 
to a real super-high arch dam in China to test the suitability of the 
system. The practical adoption indicates that the system is able to 
exhibit the current safety state of the dam, at the same time, to realize 
real-time warning of the dam risk. The structure of the paper is orga -
nized as follows. Section 2 proposes the overall framework of the pro -
posed system for dam safety monitoring. Section 3 presents the 
development methods of digital twin platform, automatic data man -
agement module, dam monitoring field construction module, and safety 
monitoring and early warning module. Section 4 shows the practical 
application of an arch dam in China. Section 5 concludes the whole 
paper and points out the future research directions.
2. The overall frame work of the proposed system
The overall framework of the proposed system is presented in Fig. 1 . 
As depicted in Fig. 1 , based on the establishment of the 3D model of the 
dam project, this model serves as a digital sandbox for displaying scene 
information. By seamlessly integrating the model with automatic data 
management, monitoring field construction, and safety monitoring and 
warning modules, monitoring data and deformation field results are 
directly displayed within the digital twin platform. This system enables 
intelligent preprocessing, storage, and updating of monitoring data, 
providing real-time visualization of the overall dam structural charac -
teristics. Moreover, it also has functions of predicting the dam opera -
tional behaviour and risk warning information visualization. The 
proposed system collects and gets engineering information and moni -
toring data from sensors automatically. Finally, the system will be 
deployed on the servers or web platforms, enabling users to perform 
real-time dam risk analysis and daily management in time.
It can be noticed that two spaces existed simultaneously, the physical 
space and the digital space. The physical space primarily encompasses 
the actual dam structure, the surrounding environment, and the asso -
ciated safety monitoring facilities. The digital space seems like a digital 
representation of the actual project. The digital twin of the dam is 
created by mapping the physical characteristics and operational data of 
the physical dam onto a digital model in real-time, resulting in a virtual 
“ twin ” of the dam. This process involves several key steps: Firstly, 
continuous monitoring of real-time data from the physical dam. Sec -
ondly, transmitting the collected data to the digital twin platform, where 
it is processed and analyzed. Thirdly, simulation and prediction, utiliz -
ing both real-time and historical data to accurately reflect and forecast 
the current state of the physical dam. Fourthly, decision support, 
enabling risk warnings for the dam and providing engineers with sci -
entific insights for dam management, thereby optimizing operational 
and maintenance strategies to enhance safety and efficiency.
3. Design of the proposed system for dam safety monitoring
3.1. Digital twin platform construction based on NGM and real-time 
rendering technology
The digital twin platform serves as digital sand table for displaying 
the engineering scene effectively, including the dam body, dam foun -
dation, reservoir basin, and surrounding mountainous environment. The 
platform incorporates information such as monitoring point locations, 
monitoring data, site information, and model information, enabling 
users to easily acquire the operational status of the dam. Therefore, the 
emphasis of developing the platform lies in constructing the visualiza -
tion model with sufficient realism, and further simulating the actual 
engineering scenario through model rendering.
A common strategy used to establish the basic model is to collect the 
engineering data and drawings. This kind of model is characterized by 
its simplicity, low polygon count, and high operability, making it suit -
able for real-time rendering. However, the basic model suffers from a 
lack of detail and low precision, resulting in obvious discrepancies

The specific steps of constructing next-generation model are as fol -
lows. After constructing the basic model based on engineering data and 
drawings, the next-generation model is created by using ZBrush, a 
commercial modeling software. The modelling process, as shown in 
Fig. 3 , involves several steps, most of which are conducted within 
ZBrush (indicated by the gray boxes). In Fig. 3 , “ U ” and “ V ” refer to the 
coordinates of texture maps that define the position of each point on the 
texture map. By unfolding the UV map of the basic model, the points on 
the texture can be linked to the 3D model, allowing for accurate 
placement of textures. Once the high-fidelity model is established, 
several common texture maps can be baked from it, such as, texture 
maps, normal maps, and ambient occlusion (AO) maps. Texture map 
primarily records the surface color of the model, representing the 
appearance. Normal map captures the direction of surface normal at 
each point on the surface of an object. By encoding these directions in 
the RGB color channels, normal maps are able to simulate the details of 
surface bumps and depressions. When lighting is applied, this map al -
lows surfaces with fewer polygons to exhibit high-detail lighting and 
reflection effects. AO map enhances the realism of the model by simu -
lating how ambient light is occluded or blocked in crevices or where 
objects intersect. It helps reduce light leaks and inaccurate shadowing, 
enhancing the realism of the model, particularly in shadowed areas.
Fig. 4 illustrates a high-fidelity model of the arch dam, alongside its 
corresponding texture map, normal map, and AO map baked from the 
high-fidelity model.
On the basis of the next-generation model, real-time rendering 
technique is employed to set up the environment, lighting, materials, 
and rendering parameters for the construction of the digital twin plat -
form. Real-time rendering refers to the process of calculating and 
outputting graphical data in real time. In this study, the programmable 
rendering pipeline of OpenGL is utilized to achieve real-time rendering 
of the model. The programmable pipeline streamlines the rendering 
process by simplifying its logic, thus enhancing rendering efficiency. 
Moreover, it allows developers to implement specific algorithms and 
logic to render effects that are difficult to achieve with a fixed pipeline. 
The flowchart of the programmable rendering pipeline is depicted in 
Fig. 5 , and the programmable stages are indicated by gray boxes. Uti -
lizing this programmable pipeline, various rendering effects can be 
achieved, including the simulation of different weathers, seasons, and 
times of day in engineering scenarios. This helps make it possible to 
realize real-time visualization of flood discharge, accurately reflecting 
the actual engineering scene.

Rendering (PBR) and High Dynamic Range (HDR) rendering techniques 
are important to increase the realism of the digital twin platform. PBR 
aims to simulate light in a manner consistent with physical laws. It in -
volves defining surface materials based on physical parameters, which 
ensures that the appearance of the materials remains realistic under 
various lighting conditions. PBR (Physically Based Rendering) technol -
ogy is primarily grounded in three theoretical foundations: the micro -
facet theory, the principle of energy conservation, and the Bidirectional 
Reflectance Distribution Function (BRDF). Based on these mathematical 
principles, the technique controls the response of specific surface points 
to light on a per-fragment basis. By configuring or adjusting commonly 
used maps in the PBR rendering pipeline at the texel level, material 
properties are defined to reflect the physical characteristics of real-world 
surfaces. The maps utilized include normal maps, metallic maps, 
roughness maps, ambient occlusion (AO) maps, and albedo (texture) 
maps, among others. Fig. 7 exhibits the PBR-rendered model of an arch 
dam. In Fig. 7 (a), it is evident that the concrete surface of the dam re -
flects its real-world texture accurately after applying the PBR rendering 
technique. Fig. 7 (b) illustrates the reflective effect on certain parts of the 
dam body caused by water stains, which appear after the floodgates are 
opened.
HDR is known as High Dynamic Range imaging, involves combining 
low dynamic range images captured with different exposure times into a 
single high dynamic range image. HDR (High Dynamic Range) 
rendering involves two primary steps. The first step is exposure control, 
which maps the high dynamic range image to the displayable range of 
(0,1) on the screen. The second step is to enhance particularly bright 
areas by introducing a bloom effect, creating the appearance of a radiant 
glow. This technique synthesizes the best details from each exposure to 
produce an image with a greater dynamic range and more image detail, 
thereby achieving more realistic visual effects that closely mimic the 
real-world environment. Fig. 8 presents the HDR rendered image of an 
arch dam. The rendering clearly enhances the contrast between light and 
dark areas, resulting in more pronounced depth and a heightened sense 
of the digital twin platform.

3.3. Development of dam monitoring field construction module
The construction, information storage, and display of the dam 
monitoring field are primarily realized by the dam monitoring field 
construction module. This module utilizes a controller to import data 
from the valid database of the automatic data management module into 
the finite element analysis module. The output files are then automati -
cally imported into the field information database for the dam moni -
toring field construction. Efforts have been made to link this model with 
the digital twin platform by integrating an interface for the dam moni -
toring field visualization. Fig. 10 illustrates the workflow of this module.
For the purpose of establishing an effective dam monitoring field, it 
is of great importance to develop a finite element analysis module 
capable of performing finite element numerical simulations. Fig. 11
presents the workflow of FEM analysis module. In Fig. 11 , CFEM means 
Conventional Finite Element Method, SFEM means Stochastic Finite 
Element Method. They are two types of approaches used in the finite 
element analysis (FEM) primarily in engineering simulations. CFEM 
involves determining various physical quantities like stress, strain, 
temperature of the dam. SFEM is an extension of the conventional FEM 
that incorporates uncertainty into the model. SFEM aims to provide 
more realistic simulations in the presence of randomness and uncer -
tainty. The input file generated by the dam monitoring field construction 
module is imported into the FEM analysis module for solving. This 
process generates an output file, along with a result file that is stored in 
the result database. The result file can be processed by post-processing 
software, allowing users to view the numerical simulation results 
directly. We have introduced the FEM analysis method proposed ( Shao 
et al., 2023 ), and referred to the current open source software programs 
( Zaheer et al., 2020 ). The solver in the FEM analysis module is written in 
Python, and its source code is fully open, enabling facilitating devel -
opment, maintenance, and learning.
Fig. 12 describes the display interface for the numerical simulation 
results. This interface allows users to load models manually and select 
specific calculation results. According to the selected results, users can 
adjust the visualization results of the finite element calculation model 
and the contour map of dam monitoring field.
3.4. Development of safety monitoring and early warning module
With the aim of applying the finite element analysis results to the 
safety monitoring and early warning model establishment, it is neces -
sary to integrate the FEM analysis module into this module with an 
interface. After importing valid monitoring data from the valid database 
into this module, the generated processing files are further transferred to 
the finite element analysis module for computation. The computation 
results are then stored in the model database, allowing the establishment 
of various safety monitoring models. In the model database, the safety 
monitoring models include physical models, data-driven models and 
integrated models. The physical models are established by FEM based on 
the actual physical structure of the dam, these models simulate the dam 
mechanical behavior, deformation, seepage, and other factors. They 
typically consider the dam ’ s geometry, material properties, external 
loads, and more. The data-driven models are built by analyzing large 
amounts of historical data using statistical and machine learning 
methods to assess dam safety and operational conditions. The integrated 
models combine physical models and data-driven models, performing 
comprehensive analysis using multi-source data to provide more accu -
rate monitoring results. For the early warning models, there are three 
types: threshold-based early warning models, dynamic early warning 
models, multi-factor comprehensive early warning models. Threshold- 
based early warning models set threshold values for key parameters 
(such as water levels, displacement, stress, etc.). When these parameters 
exceed the safety range, an early warning is triggered. Dynamic early 
warning models analyze real-time monitoring data and use machine 
learning, fuzzy logic, and other methods to dynamically assess risks and 
issue early warnings. Multi-factor comprehensive early warning models 
assess the risks to the dam by considering the combined effects of 
multiple monitoring indicators (such as hydrological, meteorological,

4. Practical engineering application and analysis
In this section, the proposed system is tested in Jinping-I super high 
arch dam. The sketch map for the geographical location of the arch dam 
and its photo are shown in Fig. 15 . It is a remarkable engineering 
achievement located on the Yalong River in Sichuan Province, China. As 
one of the tallest dams in the world, it stands at an impressive height of 
305 m. The dam is a double-curvature arch dam, designed to efficiently 
handle the immense pressure of the reservoir water by transferring the 
load to the surrounding rock walls. Constructed primarily for hydro -
power generation, the dam supports the Jinping-I hydropower station, 
which has a total installed capacity of 3,600 MW. In addition to its en -
ergy production, the dam plays a significant role in flood control, irri -
gation, and water supply for the region.
4.1. Digital twin platform construction
Based on the next-generation models and real-time rendering effects, 
additional components such as a 3D visualization model, original 
database, valid database, monitoring information, safety monitoring

5. Conclusions
This study proposed a real-time NGM-enabled visualization system 
for dam safety monitoring with application of digital twin platform, 
which embeds computational programming, data processing, structural 
analysis, and health diagnosis domain knowledge. In the case of Jinping 
I super-high arch dam, the proposed system was tested to verify its 
specific functions. The results demonstrate that the developed system 
performs a more diverse set of functions as independent research and 
development without relying on existing software. The test results 
demonstrate the proposed system ultimately contributes to the overall 
structural safety status evaluation, and continuous supporting decision- 
making for dam safety monitoring. The main innovations and future 
work are the following as below. 
(1) It presents a construction method of the digital twin platform 
based on NGM and real-time rendering technology. The con -
struction result shows that the precision of 3D visualization 
model featuring a relative high precision, which is able to show 
engineering scenarios under different situations and helps to 
grasp the specific operation states of projects in real-time. This 
system incorporates digital twin platform with automatic data 
management module, dam monitoring field construction module 
and safety monitoring and early warning module for information 
visualization. In this case, the proposed system allows for dam 
monitoring data, structural behavior, safety monitoring result 
and early warning result visualization in real-time. With the 
application of the proposed system, it allows for more precise 
maintenance, helps in the early detection of potential problems, 
optimizes maintenance strategies, reduces the burden of manual 
inspections, and enhances operational efficiency.
(2) Meanwhile, the proposed system can integrate diverse data 
sources (e.g., on-site sensors, drone monitoring, meteorological 
data) into a unified database, facilitating information sharing 
among various departments in dam projects. Users can commu -
nicate and collaborate based on the same set of data, reducing 
information silos and improving overall efficiency. The digital 
twin platform in this system is not limited to safety monitoring, it 
can also simulate the surrounding environment. Through real- 
time monitoring and simulation, users can gain a better under -
standing of dam real-time operation state.
(3) Although the proposed system can realize continuous safety 
monitoring of dam characteristics and map the physical space 
into a virtual space, as verified in the application, there are still 
some limitations. For example, it can not predict how the dam 
might behave under extreme conditions such as earthquakes and 
floods in the virtual space, which still need further investigation. 
Besides, research on the database of emergency response plans in 
the system for preemptive actions to prevent disasters is also 
essential in the future work.
