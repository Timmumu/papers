ABSTRACT
In view of the current status and main problems of the construction of municipal pipelines in Shanghai Oriental Hub, based on
the digital twin technology, this paper proposes and constructs a full-cycle digital twin platform for municipal pipelines in the
digital era, and discusses the implementation path of the platform construction. On the premise of clarifying the planning and
management mechanism of underground pipelines, based on the establishment of a data standard system and safety control
scheme, based on building information modeling, IoT perception, arti ﬁcial intelligence, and other technologies, the deep inte-
gration of municipal pipelines and digital twin technology in the whole life cycle of planning, construction, operation and
maintenance (O &M) is promoted, which plays an important role in promoting the intensive and ef ﬁcient construction and
safe operation of municipal pipelines in the Oriental Hub. This paper preliminarily discusses the application of digital twin
technology in the scenario of Shanghai Oriental Hub Municipal Pipeline Platform, which can provide a reference for 3D pipeline
approval, emergency support, and urban physical examination and evaluation.
On February 19, 2024, the State Council of ﬁcially approved the
overall plan for the construction of the Shanghai Oriental Hub
International Business Cooperation Zone, marking the of ﬁcial
launch of the Shanghai Oriental Hub project. As a world-class
large-scale integrated transportation hub, it is an important stra-
tegic spatial deployment of a networked urban structure cluster
with multiple centers, levels, and nodes [ 1], Similar to Xiong ’an
New Area, Shanghai Oriental Hub, as a newly constructed area,
has a small historical burden and relatively simple utilization of
underground space. The pipeline management system is becom-
ing increasingly perfect, which is conducive to introducing digital
twin technology into the construction and application of munic-
ipal pipeline platforms. In the Oriental Hub, it gradually realizes
the advanced stage of smart city—digital twin city [2]—providing
examples and references for other cities to carry out similar work.
The traditional pipeline geographic information platform has
realized the integrated development from 2D to 3D and from a
single application to multiple technologies, such as the visualiza-
tion integration platform of VISTA in the United Kingdom [ 3]
and the underground integrated pipe network platform estab-

digital twin platform for municipal pipelines for the smart Ori-
ental Hub, which is an important part of the overall architecture
of the digital twin city, which is a physical space twin, a multidi-
mensional information integration and a people-oriented percep-
tion body with the water, electricity, gas, information and other
municipal pipelines and comprehensive pipe corridors and their
ancillary facilities of the Oriental Hub as the research object. The
twin of the physical space is represented by the all-element and
reﬁned simulation of the underground pipe network, the

1 | Basic Information of Shanghai Oriental Hub
1.1 | Project Overview
The Shanghai Oriental Hub is a “super project” that is related to
the implementation of national strategies and the long-term
development of Shanghai, aiming to create a world-class large-
scale comprehensive transportation hub. The project is located in
Zhuqiao Town, Pudong New Area, Shanghai, with a total
planned area of 155 km 2, including 84 km 2 of main functional
area and 71 km 2 of expansion area. Its core area is centered
around Shanghai East Railway Station, connected to Pudong
International Airport to the east, forming a transportation pat-
tern of “air rail integration, ” which will further enhance Shang-
hai’s transportation ef ﬁciency and overall competitiveness.
The Oriental Hub is a comprehensive transportation hub created
by the strong alliance between Shanghai Pudong International
Airport and Shanghai East Railway Station (under construction),
integrating air and rail transportation. Its speci ﬁc location is the
area enclosed by the Chuanyang River, the eastern perimeter of
Pudong Airport, Xiayan Road, and Pudong Canal. In terms of
spatial layout, the plan forms an open and chessboard style over-
all spatial pattern of “one center, two axes, two rings, four wings,
and nine clusters. ” (Figure 1)

1.2 | Overview of Municipal Pipeline Network for
the Project
The municipal pipe network of the Shanghai Oriental Hub is an
important part of its overall planning and construction, aiming to
ensure the normal operation and ef ﬁcient management of the
hub area. The following is only a list of water supply and energy
to illustrate the degree of the municipal pipe network of the
Shanghai Oriental Hub.
In terms of water supply, the maximum planned daily water
demand of the Eastern Hub is about 43,000 m
3/day. The pressure
at the end of the water distribution network is not less than
160 kPa, and the leakage rate of the water supply network is
less than 3 %. The water supply plan is jointly supplied to the
region by Huinan Water Plant, Nanhui North Water Plant, and
Yingbin Water Plant. Unconventional water sources, such as
collected rainwater, are used for miscellaneous water —such as
greening and watering —and municipal tap water is used as a
supplementary water source. The water from Huinan Water
Plant, Nanhui North Water Plant, and Yingbin Water Plant is
transported to Zhuqiao Pumping Station along the G1503 current
water pipeline and Zhoudeng Highway planned water pipeline
respectively, and the water supply is supplied to the surrounding
area after being pressurized by the pumping station. Implemen-
ted the Zhuqiao Pumping Station Renovation Project to ensure
water supply in the area. Within the planning scope, it is planned
to form a “four horizontal and three vertical ” water supply back-
bone pipe network layout, which are DN500 water supply pipe of
Wenju Road, DN500 water supply pipe of Zhupan Road, DN500
water supply pipe of Zhuqin Road and DN500 –DN600 water
supply pipe of Jinting Highway. The “three verticals ” are the
DN500 water supply pipe of Chuannanfeng Highway, the
DN500 and DN600 water supply pipes of G1503 Highway and
the DN500 water supply pipe of Zhandong Road. DN300 water
supply pipes are laid under other roads, and the water supply
pipe network is connected into a ring to meet the water supply
safety during peak hours, accidents, and ﬁre ﬁghting. Through
DMA partition management, online supervision and other mea-
sures, the leakage rate of the pipe network is reduced.
In terms of energy, the energy system of the Eastern Hub focuses
on improving the layout of pipeline network facilities. The ﬁrst is
to improve the regional power grid around the 500 kV far East
substation in accordance with high reliability standards. The area
will build seven new 220 kV substations, renovate four 220 kV
substations, and add 18 new 110 kV substations. Two high-sub-
high pressure regulator stations and four high-medium pressure
regulator stations will be built, and a high-standard high-pres-
sure ring network system will be built to promote the integrated
supply of regional gas services and greatly improve the regional
power supply and gas supply capacity. The second is to focus on
the integrated layout of the energy network. Three 220 kV sub-
stations and 12 110 kV substations were newly built, and the
pipeline network layout was optimized by using multiple gas
sources from West Gas and Sichuan Gas. It is the ﬁrst in the
country to realize the construction of inter-provincial distribu-
tion networks of 110 kV and below, explore the establishment of
an integrated regional electricity and gas information service
platform, and transform from the end of energy facilities to inter-
connection nodes, so as to greatly improve the reliability of
energy supply and service levels.
1.3 | Main Existing Problems
First, the leading role of planning is not enough. At present, the
Oriental Hub is playing a leading role in planning [ 4], designing
the top-level structure of the digital transformation of the under-
ground pipe network, and creating a demonstration and leading
model area. However, there are still deﬁ ciencies in comprehen-
sive consideration of urban road planning, sponge city construc-
tion, comprehensive construction of municipal pipelines and
pipe corridors, and overall demand promotion, and have not
yet been able to establish a working system of planning a map,
building a game of chess, and supervising a network. Second, the
efﬁciency of information coordination and sharing needs to be
improved. At the planning level, factors, such as the basic con-
ditions, construction dif ﬁculty, and landscape requirements of
pipeline construction were not considered as a whole. At the
data level, problems, such as insuf ﬁcient co-construction and
sharing still exist. Therefore, it is urgent to build a pipeline shar-
ing information platform based on uni ﬁed data standards, sys-
tems and speci ﬁcations for the whole life cycle of municipal
pipelines, promote business integration and open up data ﬂow
through the platform, and then realize the data integration and
resource sharing of integrated pipelines and professional pipe-
lines. Third, the comprehensive empowerment of digital and
intelligent transformation is insuf ﬁcient. The Eastern Hub has

1.4 | Comparison With Existing Practices
To highlight the innovative value and scenario adaptability of the
proposed platform, this section compares it with typical domestic
and international digital twin practices in municipal pipeline
management from three core dimensions: technical architecture,
application scenarios, and management mechanisms. The com-
parison clari ﬁes the differentiated advantages of the platform,
providing a theoretical basis for subsequent construction ideas
and implementation paths (Table 1).
As illustrated in the comparison, existing practices have obvious
limitations in adapting to the re ﬁned management needs of hub-
type pipeline networks: Xiong ’an’s digital twin focuses on macro
urban governance, traditional Shanghai platforms lack full-life-
cycle coverage, and international cases are not tailored to “air-rail
integration” scenarios. This platform, centered on the Oriental
Hub’s unique requirements, integrates full-lifecycle data ﬂow
and multi-stakeholder collaboration, ﬁlling the gap between
macro system frameworks and micro pipeline management. Its
differentiated design not only ensures the practicality of the proj-
ect but also provides a replicable paradigm for digital twin appli-
cations in similar hub-type municipal pipeline projects .

2 | Construction Ideas for the Full Cycle Digital
Twin Platform of Municipal Pipelines in the
Oriental Hub
2.1 | Construction Objectives
This paper focuses on the critical window period of the planning,
construction, and management of the Oriental Hub, combined
with the construction requirements of Shanghai ’s urban digital
transformation and high-quality development, studies and for-
mulates the implementation path of the full-cycle digital twin
platform of the Oriental Hub municipal pipeline, and innovates
the new concepts, technologies, and models of digital and intelli-
gent management of the municipal pipeline of the Oriental Hub
in a way empowered by the platform.
2.2 | Platform Roles and Functional Decomposition
Across the Full Lifecycle
To address the core challenges of “information silos ” and “dis-
connected processes” in municipal pipeline management, this
platform is designed as a central enabling hub that integrates
the planning, design, construction, and O &M stages. Its role
and primary functions evolve throughout the project lifecycle,
aiming to create a seamless digital thread for data ﬂow and value
addition.
During the planning stage (corresponding to BIM1 –BIM3), the
platform primarily serves as a 3D collaborative planning and
simulation veri ﬁcation tool. It tackles pain points like frequent
planning clashes and difﬁculties in assessing layout rationality by
integrating multisource data (e.g., geology, road networks, and
existing utilities) to create a 3D digital context of the hub area. Its
key functions include automated 3D clash detection, utility cor-
ridor simulation, and con ﬂict analysis with above-ground struc-
tures. This facilitates scheme comparison and optimization,
translating planning intent into precise, actionable 3D spatial
layouts (BIM3 models), thereby reducing late-stage changes
and providing a deﬁ nitive basis for the design stage.
In the design stage (corresponding to BIM4), the platform trans-
forms into a multidisciplinary collaborative design and auto-
mated compliance checking environment. It addresses issues,
such as con ﬂicts between different utility designs (water, gas,
power, and telecom) and inefﬁ cient manual code reviews. Oper-
ating on a uni ﬁed BIM data standard, it provides a collaborative
workspace. A core mechanism is its built-in regulatory knowl-
edge database and automated rule-checking engine, which
validates designs against mandatory codes. Furthermore, it
ensures the detailed design model (BIM4) aligns with the plan-
ning-stage BIM3 output, accurately conveying planning intent to
construction documents and signiﬁcantly enhancing design qual-
ity and ef ﬁciency.
At the construction stage (corresponding to BIM5 –BIM6), the
platform acts as a digital construction management and as-built
veriﬁcation system. To prevent deviations from design and
enable structured management of process data, its central func-
tion is real-time comparison between the as-built model (BIM5)
and the design model (BIM4), with alerts for signi ﬁcant discre-
pancies. It also integrates ﬁeld data on progress, quality, and
safety, driving comprehensive digitalization of the construction
process. Ultimately, it orchestrates data collection to generate a
high-ﬁdelity As-built digital asset (BIM6 model), forming a reli-
able foundation for subsequent O &M.
For the O &M Stage (corresponding to BIM0), the platform
evolves into an IoT-driven smart O &M and decision-support
core. Confronting traditional O &M hurdles like unknown asset
status and slow emergency response, the platform ﬁrst estab-
lishes the as-built model (BIM6) as the authoritative “As-is Base-
line Model (BIM0). ” By fusing this model with real-time IoT
sensor data (pressure, ﬂow, leak concentration, etc.), it enables
comprehensive status monitoring, health assessment, and risk
prediction.
Its advanced capabilities include emergency scenario
simulation and mitigation strategy evaluation based on the digi-
tal twin, fundamentally shifting the O &M paradigm from reac-
tive repair to proactive alerting and predictive maintenance.
In summary, by assuming distinct yet coherent core roles across
these four stages, the platform establishes a continuous digital
thread covering “planning–design–construction–O&M.” This
effectively fosters the integration of data, processes, and decision-
making across phases, providing a uni ﬁed platform for the
reﬁned, full-lifecycle management of the Oriental Hub ’s munici-
pal pipelines.

2.4 | Construction Principles
The construction of the full-cycle digital twin platform for munic-
ipal pipelines in the Oriental Hub adheres to four core principles,
which are closely aligned with the project’s characteristics of “air-
rail integration ” and the requirements of full-lifecycle re ﬁned
management. These principles guide the platform ’s functional
design, technical integration, and mechanism construction to
ensure practicality, operability, and sustainability.
2.4.1 | Full-Cycle Coverage
The platform ensures continuous coverage of the entire pipeline
lifecycle, from planning and design through construction to
O&M. This coverage is not merely the sequential inclusion of
each stage but is fundamentally about maintaining an unbroken
digital thread of information. As detailed in Section 2.2, the plat-
form assumes speciﬁc roles at each stage to address stage-speciﬁ c
challenges. Underpinning these roles is the consistent use of 3D
BIM models as the core data carrier, which enables the complete
preservation, transmission, and traceability of information across
phases.
The lifecycle stages, as illustrated in Figure 3,a r es e a m l e s s l yc o n -
nected through this digital thread: the planning stage (BIM1–BIM3)
establishes the foundational layout; the design stage (BIM4) reﬁnes
the details; the construction stage (BIM5 –BIM6) captures as-built
conditions; and the operation stage (BIM0) continuously updates
the model with real-time status. By maintaining this chain, the
platform prevents information fragmentation and ensures that
the ﬁnal built asset aligns with the original planning intent.
Moreover, the platform ’s data management strategy, which will
be elaborated in the following subsections, ensures that informa-
tion is not only passed forward but also can be traced back, support-
ing accountability and continuous improvement throughout the
pipeline’s life.
This seamless coverage is technically underpinned by a robust set
of data and model management mechanisms, as detailed in the
following section.
2.4.2 | Data and Model Management Mechanisms
To ensure the reliability and dynamism of the full lifecycle digital
thread, the platform establishes a core set of mechanisms govern-
ing model speci ﬁcation, version control, change coordination,
and baseline management.
2.4.2.1 | Model Level of Detail (LOD) Grading and
Lightweight Flow. The platform deﬁ nes minimum LOD
requirements for models at each stage (e.g., LOD300 for sche-
matic planning and LOD500 for as-built with complete compo-
nent attributes), following the technical speci ﬁcations for digital
twin of municipal pipelines (DB31/T 1289-2023) [ 5]. To balance
precision with system performance, detailed design and con-
struction models undergo geometry lightweighting and informa-
tion extraction upon ingestion. This process retains essential
spatial topology, key attributes (e.g., diameter, material, and pres-
sure rating), and connectivity. Crucially, a traceability link is
maintained to the source model, allowing users to retrieve the
full-detail version for specialized engineering analysis when
needed.

2.4.2.3 | Cross-Stage Change Coordination Workﬂ ow. A
standardized change coordination process is triggered for design
changes or construction adjustments. The work ﬂow includes:
initiation and automated impact analysis (leveraging platform
clash detection and dependency mapping), online multi-stake-
holder review/approval, automated propagation of updates to
all affected models/documents, and noti ﬁcation pushes to rele-
vant parties (e.g., downstream contractors and O&M). This work-
ﬂow ensures that a change approved in the design model (BIM4)
is automatically re ﬂected in the ongoing construction model
(BIM5) and future maintenance plans, maintaining consistency
across stages.
2.4.2.4 | Operational Baseline Establishment and Update
Rules. The digital delivery and acceptance process via the plat-
form is critical. The approved as-built model (BIM6) is formally
locked and designated as the “Operational Baseline Model”
(BIM0), serving as the single source of truth for all subsequent
O&M and analysis. Any future physical alteration (e.g., pipeline
retroﬁt) requires the creation of a change order against this base-
line. The update process generates a new child version (e.g.,
BIM0.1) of the baseline after ﬁeld veriﬁcation, ensuring the digi-
tal twin evolves in lockstep with the physical asset while preserv-
ing an accurate change history.
Collectively, these mechanisms ensure that the digital thread
is not only continuous but also auditable, controllable, and always
representative of the latest physical reality, directly supporting the
seamless phase transitions demonstrated in the pilot (Section 4).
2.4.3 | Multi-Party Participation
The platform establishes a multi-stakeholder collaborative mech-
anism involving urban planning departments, municipal pipe-
line enterprises (water, electricity, gas, and communication),
construction units, and O &M teams. Through permission-based
information sharing services, different professional entities can
access customized data and functional modules: planning depart-
ments obtain integrated planning data for approval; construction
units retrieve design and construction standards to avoid rework;
and operation teams access real-time monitoring data for daily
maintenance. This multi-party participation model breaks the
traditional “departmental isolation ” and promotes collaborative
decision-making throughout the pipeline lifecycle.
2.4.4 | Safety and Controllability
To ensure the secure circulation of sensitive pipeline data (e.g.,
key hub pipeline layouts and pressure parameters), the platform
adopts a “classiﬁed grading + decryption processing” mechanism
(Figure 4).
First, pipeline data is classi ﬁed into “internal con ﬁ-
dential data ” (e.g., core hub pipeline coordinates) and “publicly
shared data ” (e.g., general pipeline layout) based on security
levels. Con ﬁdential data undergoes desensitization and decryp-
tion processing before being shared with authorized entities. Sec-
ond, a re ﬁned permission management system is established to
deﬁne data access rights for different subjects (refer to Section
2.3.4 for details). Finally, the platform implements full-process
data traceability, recording all operations such as data access,
modiﬁcation, and sharing to ensure accountability. This mecha-
nism balances data sharing ef ﬁciency and security, complying
with national and Shanghai municipal regulations on under-
ground pipeline information management [ 6].

2.4.5 | Compatibility and Extensibility
The platform is designed with strong compatibility to adapt to the
existing planning and management systems of the Oriental Hub.
Its functional modules (planning, design, construction, and oper-
ation) can operate independently according to professional needs
or be integrated into a uni ﬁed management system. In terms of

large models for intelligent decision-making) and new scenarios
(e.g., carbon emission monitoring of pipelines) to meet future
upgrade needs. Through data expansion and functional iteration,
the platform can continuously adapt to the deepening of the
Oriental Hub’s digital transformation [ 7].
