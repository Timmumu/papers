Abstract
Federated learning (FL) is compelling for construction safety because sensitive, site-specific data can remain on devices; 
however, hyperparameter optimization (HPO) in FL is often impractical on resource-constrained edge nodes due to multi-
round tuning and communication overhead. Existing FL-HPO approaches (e.g., weight sharing and server-only selection) 
either incur iterative client-server loops or underutilize client heterogeneity, thereby raising privacy and governance concerns. 
We propose an asynchronous, one-shot local Bayesian optimization on each client using a small data subset, followed by 
loss-weighted aggregation of client-proposed hyperparameters on the server to produce a single global set for collaborative 
training. The pipeline is training-method agnostic and integrated into a Digital Twin (DT) platform for device registration, 
orchestration, and telemetry. Across public datasets (MNIST, EEGEyeState, Weather) and a private worker-safety dataset, our 
approach improves convergence and accuracy/MAE relative to simple averaging and reduces response time on Raspberry Pi 
clients, yielding an average improvement of 6.44% in end-to-end performance while remaining deployable on constrained 
AIoT hardware. In safety prediction, faster convergence and lower error enable earlier and more reliable hazard indicators, 
with on-device inference supporting near–real-time alerts to supervisors and wearables. We also discuss scalability, privacy 
of shared hyperparameters, and task complexity as directions for future work in safety-critical FL.

In construction safety, sites combine dynamic hazards 
(fall risks, struck-by equipment, heat, and weather expo -
sure) with heterogeneous sensing (wearables, tool teleme -
try, location beacons, environmental and structural sensors) 
[21]. Data are privacy-sensitive (worker physiology/loca -

ing for preventive alerts, yet edge devices (e.g., Raspberry 
Pi class) face tight compute, memory, and power budgets 
[50]. These constraints directly motivate FL with on-device 
inference but make hyperparameter selection especially 
costly: repeated, communication-heavy tuning can delay 
deployment, and exchanging intermediate model states 
may raise privacy or governance concerns within contrac -
tors and unions. Effective FL-HPO for safety must therefore 
minimize rounds, protect privacy, and remain robust under 
heterogeneous devices and data [46]. However, current FL-
HPO methods either require iterative, multi-round coordina-
tion or centralize key decisions, which are misaligned with 
edge constraints and can underuse client heterogeneity. This 
creates a practical gap for safety-critical, resource-limited 
deployments.
This study focuses on continuous safety-index prediction 
(site- and worker-level risk scoring) rather than on Personal 
Protective Equipment (PPE) compliance checks or unsafe 
behavior detection. The model takes in three input catego -
ries: (i) Weather – wind speed, temperature, precipitation; 
(ii) Building/structural – load, strain, inclination; and (iii) 
Worker/physiological & location – heart rate and position. 
The predicted safety index supports threshold-based alerts 
for wearables and dashboards. Other safety functions (e.g., 
PPE detection, video analytics) are beyond the scope of this 
paper.
While HPO is well explored in centralized settings, it 
is challenging in FL, where data remain distributed across 
clients [27]. Exhaustive or iterative HPO can be resource-
intensive due to repeated training and communication [ 61]. 
Simple, parallelizable strategies (e.g., random search) are 
therefore strong baselines in FL [35], aligning with practical 
constraints in emerging applications (e.g., transportation). 
Related work in FL spans algorithmic advances, heterogene-
ity handling, personalization, fairness, systems, and privacy 
[62]. However, comparatively fewer studies focus squarely 
on FL-HPO [ 25]. Federated Bayesian Optimization (FBO) 
[13] leverages cross-client information to improve a single 
participant, rather than yielding global hyperparameters for 
a collaborative model. Other efforts adjust specific hyper -
parameters such as the learning rate in SGD-based FL [42]. 
FedEx draws on NAS weight-sharing to guide hyperparam-
eter selection under successive halving [ 28], and FLoRa 
explores single-shot, server-side HPO [65].
Limitations of existing FL-HPO: Current FL-HPO 
approaches leave three practical gaps for safety-critical, 
edge deployments. First, many rely on iterative, multi-
round tuning (e.g., Bayesian optimization loops or suc -
cessive halving), which increases client–server traffic and 
latency. Second, weight-sharing/NAS-style methods (e.g., 
FedEx) often assume SGD-centric pipelines and exchange 
model artifacts that may be undesirable for privacy/gover -
nance. Third, server-only single-shot schemes (e.g., FLoRa) 
reduce communication but can neglect client-side heteroge-
neity during proposal, yielding suboptimal global settings 
under non-IID data. These gaps motivate a design that (i) 
performs lightweight client-local search once, (ii) aggre -
gates proposals without exposing model weights, and (iii) 
remains optimizer-agnostic and heterogeneity-aware. We 
next categorize prior approaches to situate our method in 
context. We group prior work into four categories. (A) Cen-
tralized HPO (grid, random, BO, evolutionary; multi-fidelity 
Hyperband/BOHB). Highly effective with full data access, 
but their iterative evaluations become impractical under 
FL’s communication and privacy constraints. (B) FL with 
NAS/weight sharing (e.g., FedEx and derivatives). Reduce 
search cost by sharing weights/architectures, yet typi -
cally assume SGD-centric training and require exchanging 
model artifacts, which elevates privacy/governance risk and 
device overhead. (C) Adaptive/learning-based HPO for FL 
(RL, meta-learning, per-client tuning, clustering, encrypted 
aggregation). These approaches tailor hyperparameters to 
clients or rounds, but often retain multi-round communica -
tion and, in many cases, rely on model access or heavier 
control logic that strains edge devices. (D) Single-shot or 
server-focused HPO for FL (e.g., FLoRa). Cuts communica-
tion by collapsing to server-side selection; however, it can 
underuse client-side heterogeneity during proposal and may 
inherit privacy concerns from shared hyperparameters.
In contrast to these, our approach is training-method 
agnostic (applicable to SGD and non-SGD) and aims to 
minimize communication and on-device compute by con -
solidating client proposals into a single global hyperparam-
eter set. We propose asynchronous, one-shot local Bayesian 
optimization on each client using a small local subset, and 
a loss-weighted, Top-K aggregation at the server to produce 
one global configuration without sharing model weights. 
The pipeline is operationalized in a Digital Twin (DT) plat-
form for device registration, orchestration, and telemetry.

2 Related work
2.1 Digital twin and sensing for construction safety
Construction sites are dynamic, hazard-rich environments, 
making them a natural target for DT-enabled monitoring. 
Prior work has linked IoT and DT platforms to track the 
state and location of workers in real time via RFID/BLE 
infrastructures [64]. Wearable DT systems using IMUs have 
captured posture and movement and delivered immediate 
feedback (e.g., via AR) to reduce unsafe kinematics [ 4]. 
DT-driven behavioral simulations have been used to study 
how workers respond to safety interventions and to inform 
training design [ 17]. Closed-loop DT frameworks [ 47] 
that integrate machine learning and case-based reasoning 
have been explored for proactive safety control during live 
projects. Task-specific DT applications, such as for crane/
hoisting operations, have modeled spatiotemporal risk and 
visualized evolving hazards on site [37]. Together, these ini-
tiatives show that DTs have great potential for safety man -
agement; however, many systems target narrow use cases, 
operate at limited scale, or rely on centralized processing 
that raises privacy and latency concerns in outdoor, multi-
worker contexts. Our study complements this line of work 
by focusing on on-device safety-index prediction under fed-
erated training, and by proposing an FL-HPO mechanism 
that respects privacy, bandwidth, and heterogeneity con -
straints common to construction deployments.
2.2 Classic hyperparameter optimization 
techniques in centralized settings
HPO is indispensable for system enhancement when objec-
tive functions are non-analytical, non-convex, or non-dif -
ferentiable. It involves tuning hyperparameters, set prior 
to training, that significantly impact model performance. 
In centralized learning, common HPO techniques include 
grid search [ 5], random search (RS) [ 18], Bayesian Opti -
mization (BO) [ 55], and evolutionary optimization [ 29]. 
These methods, while effective, rely on iterative trial-and-
error processes to strike a balance between exploration 
and exploitation, making them computationally expensive. 
Model-free approaches, such as grid search and RS, explore 
predefined configurations without relying on parameter 
models, offering simplicity but often with lower efficiency. 
In contrast, model-based methods like BO leverage surro -
gate models to approximate the objective function, resulting 
in more efficient search processes [48]. The primary distinc-
tions among these approaches lie in the choice of surrogate 
models and their strategies for selecting subsequent evalua-
tion points [22, 36]. Evolutionary algorithms (EAs), includ-
ing differential EAs [2, 52], iteratively refine populations to 
achieve optimal performance.
The high computational costs associated with training 
deep neural networks on large datasets make full-fidelity BO 
evaluations impractical. To mitigate this, researchers often 
employ strategies such as training on smaller data subsets or 
reducing the number of iterations to balance accuracy and 
computational efficiency. Hyperband [ 33], using the Suc -
cessive Halving Algorithm (SHA) [23], exemplifies a multi-
fidelity approach by iteratively refining configurations.

However, SHA’s random selection of initial candidates

most HPO benchmarks center on centralized models, there-
fore ignoring the challenges of distributed settings.
2.3 Hyperparameter optimization in federated 
learning
At a high level, prior FL-HPO work falls into four buckets: 
(A) NAS/weight sharing (e.g., FedEx and derivatives), (B) 
adaptive/learning-based HPO (RL/meta-learning, per-client 
tuning, clustering/offloading such as FedCust), (C) single-
shot/server-side selection (e.g., FLoRa), and (D) other 
decentralized or proxy schemes. In FL, the decentralized 
nature of training amplifies the communication overhead 
of traditional HPO: methods effective centrally (e.g., BO) 
become costly due to iterative client–server loops [54]. Sev-
eral decentralized/proxy approaches (e.g., HyperDistill [32]) 
aim to adapt hyperparameters rapidly with limited data, but 
they still incur high communication costs in privacy-sen -
sitive settings. Meta-learning (e.g., MAML [ 10]) likewise 
struggles with iteration and privacy in FL. (A) NAS/weight 
sharing. FedEx-style wrappers [ 26] reduce search cost by 
sharing weights/architectures, but typically assume SGD-
centric pipelines and exchange model artifacts, which can 
raise privacy/model-access concerns [ 56, 63]. Offline NAS 
variants [ 58, 66] also incur heavy client–server exchange 
and limit client participation. (B) Adaptive/learning-based 
HPO. Works such as Auto-FedRL [19], FedHPO [30], Fed-
Tune [60], and Genetic CFL [ 3], and clustering/offloading 
frameworks like FedCust [ 59] adjust hyperparameters per 
round/client or group; they can reduce convergence time 
but often retain multi-round control traffic and may require 
model/state access. Privacy-preserving variants (e.g., PRIV-
TUNA [39]) mitigate leakage but add cryptographic over -
head. (C) Single-shot/server-side. FLoRa [ 65] aggregates 
client hyperparameters into a server loss surface and selects 
a single global setting, sharply reducing rounds. However, it 
relies on default search spaces and shared HPs that can leak 
information; it also underutilizes client-side heterogeneity 
during proposal.
Despite these advances, many approaches remain com -
munication-heavy, assume access to model artifacts, or 
are brittle on constrained AIoT devices. In contrast, our 
method is designed for edge-constrained, privacy-sensitive 
deployments:
 ● One-shot local BO on each client: tune on a small lo -
cal subset (no multi-round loops) to keep client compute 
and uplink traffic low.
 ● Loss-weighted Top-K aggregation of hyperparameters 
(not weights): consolidate only the best proposals into 
a single global HP set, preserving client specialization 
during proposal while avoiding model-artifact exchange.
 ● DT-assisted orchestration: a lightweight control surface 
to register devices, schedule HPO/training, and collect 
telemetry–no additional learning logic on the server.
Compared with NAS/weight sharing (FedEx), we avoid 
model-artifact exchange and SGD-specific wrappers; com -
pared with adaptive RL/meta approaches, we avoid multi-
round control and model access; compared with server-only 
single-shot (FLoRa), we retain client heterogeneity during 
proposal yet still commit to one global HP via robust, loss-
aware aggregation.
In short, existing FL-HPO methods tend to be commu -
nication-heavy, require model artifacts, or underuse client 
heterogeneity. Our design targets edge constraints by one-
shot local BO and loss-weighted Top-K HP aggregation (no 
weight sharing), coordinated via a lightweight DT layer.
3 Proposed methodology
The conceptual framework for the proposed weighted 
hyperparameter aggregation mechanism aimed at enhanc -
ing model prediction in federated learning is depicted in 
Figure 1. The process includes two phases: the HPO phase 
and the FL phase. The HPO phase begins with each client 
initializing its hyperparameter search space and defining an 
objective function, typically based on performance metrics 
like loss. Clients then apply Bayesian optimization to deter-
mine optimal local hyperparameters. Upon finding these, 
clients evaluate their local model performance and transmit 
both the optimized hyperparameters and corresponding loss 
values to the federated server.
The server aggregates the hyperparameters and loss val -
ues from all clients, assigns weights based on the loss val -
ues, and selects the top K clients. We use K = ⌈0.3· N ⌉ 
unless noted; Sec. 5 keeps K fixed per experiment. The 
hyperparameters from these top K clients are aggregated

5 Performance evaluation
5.1 Datasets, model and evaluation metrics
The worker safety dataset, provided by the research center 
at Jeju National University, consists of 30,000 rows used 
for training and validation. The data is distributed across six 
devices, each representing a worker in an outdoor construc-
tion environment. The target variable is the worker safety 
index, with input features including weather (temperature, 
wind, precipitation), building conditions (strain, inclinom -
eter, load), and worker status (location, heart rate).
For the extended experiments purpose, three publicly 
available datasets are used: the MNIST [ 14], EEGEyeState 
[45], and Weather datasets [ 24]. The MNIST dataset con -
tains 70,000 grayscale images of handwritten digits (0–9), 
each 28x28 pixels, commonly used for image classification 
tasks. The EEGEyeState dataset includes 14,980 EEG sig -
nal readings with 14 continuous features, used to classify 
whether a subject’s eyes are open or closed. Additionally, 
the Weather dataset comprises daily meteorological data, 
including temperature, humidity, wind speed, and weather 
conditions, making it suitable for predictive modeling and 
weather analysis tasks.
Deep Neural Networks (DNNs) [ 31] are machine learn -
ing models that consist of multiple layers of interconnected 
neurons that enable to learn complex patterns from large 
datasets. They have been widely used for various tasks, 
including image recognition, natural language process -
ing, and predictive analytics due to their high accuracy and 
adaptability. Worker safety is predicted and trained using 
a DNN model. Two hidden layers with 64 and 32 neurons 
each, as well as an input layer, are all part of this multilay -
ered neural network. The model considers a number of input 
factors, including location, inclinometer measurements, 
wind, temperature, precipitation, load, strain, and heart rate. 
A safety index, which is produced from 2,689 parameters in 
total, is the model’s output.
Model performance is evaluated using Mean Square 
Error (MSE) and Mean Absolute Error (MAE) for regres -
sion [ 43], and Accuracy, Binary Crossentropy [ 11], and 
Sparse Categorical Crossentropy for classification tasks [7].

fluctuations during training can be attributed to the inherent 
dynamics of FL, where aggregated updates from distributed 
clients are averaged during each round. This process intro -
duces variability, particularly in the early epochs, as clients 
have different data distributions and optimization progress.

sizes, varying local convergence rates, and client-specific

ated learning, client selection based on performance during 
model updates can prioritize reliable participants, while 
clustered aggregation groups clients with similar data dis -
tributions to stabilize training. Excluding underperforming 
clients could further enhance robustness, even in the pres -
ence of heterogeneous and non-IID data.

5.4 Worker safety monitoring result and discussion
The increasing urban population, expanding by 200,000 
individuals per day, is exerting a profound influence on the 
global construction industry [ 1]. As cities grow and con -
struction projects surge, ensuring worker safety becomes 
more challenging due to the dynamic and intricate nature 
of construction sites. Relying solely on physical monitor -
ing can lead to misleading assessments, especially under 
fast-changing on-site conditions. In response, decentralized 
training (FL with HPO) enables continuous safety moni -
toring and adaptive responses without constant physical 
observation.
To demonstrate this approach, we conducted a case study 
aimed at predicting and monitoring worker safety. Utilizing 
a globally trained model, optimized with the globally tuned 
hyperparameter (HP) set derived through a weighted aggre-
gation method, we simulated a one-hour-ahead prediction of 
a worker’s safety status. Data for inference was gathered at 
20-second intervals, resulting in 180 total observations. The 
comparison between the actual and predicted safety indices, 
as illustrated in Figure 14, highlights the model’s perfor -
mance across the collected data points.
The global model runs on-device and outputs a safety 
index every 20 s. The DT page only displays per-worker 
indices and relays basic notifications; inference and thresh-
olding occur on the device.

6 Conclusions
In conclusion, hyperparameters play a crucial role in the 
machine learning process, influencing the model’s learning 
and performance. While centralized machine learning has 
extensively explored HPO, FL presents unique challenges 
for HPO due to the decentralized nature of the data. The 
limitations of conventional HPO approaches in FL settings 
stem from the need for more access to the complete dataset 
and the high communication and training costs associated 
with training numerous models for various hyper-parameter 
configurations. One-shot local Bayesian Optimization is 
used to identify optimal hyperparameters for each client 
using a fraction of the dataset. Initially, FL clients determine 
and transmit their optimal hyperparameters to a central 
server, which uses a weighted consolidation strategy to cre-
ate and distribute a global hyperparameter set. This method 
improves model performance and convergence, ensuring 
scalable federated learning deployments in worker safety-
critical applications. Across different datasets, client distri -
butions, and evaluation metrics (accuracy for classification 
and MAE for regression), the weighted hyperparameter 
consolidation method demonstrates an overall improvement 
of approximately 6.44% across all datasets and client counts 
(increased accuracy for classification; decreased MAE for 
regression, each normalized and averaged) compared to the 
simple averaging approach. Despite the significance of HPO 
in FL, there remains ample opportunity for further explo -
ration and application across diverse domains. Our study, 
which mainly focused on worker safety data, underscores 
the importance of addressing these challenges to maximize 
the benefits of FLHPO in real-world scenarios.
The proposed mechanism enhances FL by optimizing 
hyperparameters through a weighted consolidation strategy, 
improving model performance and scalability in worker 
safety applications. However, privacy risks arising from 
exchanging unprotected hyperparameters highlight the need 
for approaches that safeguard information while enabling 
collaborative tuning. Additionally, addressing communica -
tion overhead in large-scale networks and benchmarking 
against advanced HPO methods remain key areas for future 
research to maximize FLHPO’s real-world impact.
Limitations and future work. Our evaluation involves 
up to 15 clients on Raspberry Pi–class devices; behavior at 
larger scales still needs investigation. The loss-weighted 
aggregation assumes honest, fairly calibrated client valida -
tion losses, and although only hyperparameters (not model 
weights) are shared, some basic information leakage is pos-
sible. We present results from a single seed per setting and 
focus on non-visual, tabular, and temporal signals. Vision 
tasks (e.g., PPE detection) and additional baselines beyond 
Simple-A VG and a server single-shot proxy are planned for 
future work. Moving forward, we will explore larger client 
populations (such as partial participation and lightweight 
multi-fidelity search), improve robustness against noisy or 
misreported losses (e.g., with robust weighting and secure 
aggregation), and combine the method with lightweight 
vision models.
