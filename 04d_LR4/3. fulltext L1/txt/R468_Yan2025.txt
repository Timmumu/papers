Real-Time Digital Twin –Driven 3D Near-Miss Detection
System at Construction Sites
Haofeng Yan 1; Changyong Liu 2; Xincong Yang 3; and Kailun Feng 4
Abstract: To enhance on-site safety management, detecting near-misses in a timely and accurate manner is crucial. However, traditional
monitoring methods that rely on inspectors are time-consuming, labor-intensive, and limited in their ability to detect three-dimensional (3D)
incidents. Digital twin technology presents a promising solution by integrating real-time monitoring, simulation and interactivity capabilitie s
in three dimensional virtual environments. Therefore, we propose a real-time digital twin –driven near-miss detection system to facilitate
safety management at construction sites. Using advanced stereovision techniques, the system reproduces the construction site as a digital
twin model, enabling the real-time 3D monitoring of near-miss events. Additionally, the system incorporates an early warning mechanism to
alert workers and managers of potential safety hazards. To demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed system, a lifting project under
various exposure conditions was investigated. The results demonstrate the system ’s ability to detect and visualize near-misses in three-
dimensional space and provide proactive warnings of potential hazards. DOI: 10.1061/JCEMD4.COENG-15583. © 2025 American Society
of Civil Engineers.
Author keywords: Near-miss detection; Real-time three-dimensional (3D) monitoring; Digital twin; Safety management.
Introduction
Safety is a crucial concern in the construction industry, because
lack of safety leads to fatalities, injuries, and accidents that result
in substantial costs and irreversible damages every year ( Rokooei
et al. 2023 ). A comprehensive investigation of numerous construc-
tion safety accidents found that almost every occurrence of a safety
accident is not a coincidence without premonitory signs ( Shen and
Marks 2016 ). Construction sites can be high-risk areas for serious
safety accidents due to the accumulation of various incidents influ-
enced by hazardous factors associated with building processes.
However, in certain situations, these risk factors can be mitigated
or eliminated, preventing any actual harm or significant damage
(Khakzad et al. 2014 ). Thus, only minor injuries and minimal
losses may occur ( Zhang et al. 2019 ), leading to what is known
as near-miss events at construction sites ( Raviv et al. 2017 ). The
Occupational Safety and Health Administration (OSHA) defined
a near miss as a critical incident that narrowly averted an accident
due to timely intervention ( OSHA 2016 ).
Heinrich’s law ( Heinrich 1941 ) suggests that for every major
accident, there are typically 29 minor accidents and 300 potential
hazards. This implies that injuries or fatalities are not isolated in-
cidents, but rather the result of a series of events occurring one after
another, even if the incident itself happens suddenly at a specific
moment (Fig. 1). Near-miss events serve as precursors to accidents,
highlighting the importance of paying attention to these near-misses
in order to prevent safety incidents (Saleh et al. 2013). Consequently,
the real-time and effective detection and analysis of near-miss
events can reduce the frequency of safety accidents and enhance the
overall safety manageme nt at construction sites ( Duan and
Zhou 2023 ).
However, the real-time detection of near-miss events at construc-
tion sites poses a complex and formidable challenge ( Goldenhar
et al. 2003 ; Hajdasz 2015 ; Yang and Cai 2024 ), highlighting the
dynamic and unpredictable nature of construction environments.
The traditional approach to on-site safety inspections, which is
heavily reliant on professional surveyors, demands significant
workerpower and material resources. Moreover, manual monitor-
ing is prone to errors and is less reliable due to factors such as staff
fatigue or prolonged strain on vision ( Cheng et al. 2024 ), resulting
in an underestimation of risk factors at the construction sites.
To address these issues, various solutions have been proposed in
both academia and industrial settings. An innovative safety model
employing the Internet of Things (IoT) has been designed to detect
near-miss events in real-time through vigilant monitoring of site
personnel and environmental conditions ( Chung et al. 2023 ), with
the goal of reducing accident rates. Recently, blockchain technol-
ogy has emerged as a cutting-edge tool for monitoring near-misses
at construction sites ( Li et al. 2023 ) laying the groundwork for
an extensive database of incident records and an early warning sys-
tem for near-miss detection. Additionally, 2.4-GHz Bluetooth Low
Energy (BLE) technology has been utilized to develop a real-time
location system to accurately gather and tally near-misses at con-
struction sites ( Li et al. 2016 ). However, contemporary research
on near-misses detection predominantly has focused on two-
dimensional analysis, and the transition to more-comprehensive
three-dimensional (3D) tracking is still pending. This is due to

In recent years, the construction industry has begun exploring
various new technologies, including digital twin (DT) technology.
This technology enables the real-time collection of construction-
related information, such as workers, materials, equipment, and so
forth, which is displayed intuitively on a general platform. Individ-
uals at different phases of the construction process can access this
information for strategic planning in their next steps. Moreover,
digital twin technology plays a vital role throughout the entire life
cycle of a building, encompassing the design, construction, and
operation and maintenance phases. To optimize the planning sched-
uling and execution of prefabricated components at construction
sites, a real-time synchronous digital twin system has been pro-
posed (Jiang et al. 2022c ). In addition, digital twins also have been
applied to anomaly detection and monitoring during the operation
and maintenance phases of construction projects ( Lu et al. 2020 ).
Another notable application is the use of a vision-based frame-
work for digital twinning and threat assessment, which identifies
and evaluates the characteristics and potential impacts of wind-
borne debris within a virtual simulation model of a construction
site (Kamari and Ham 2022). However, current research has not yet
explored the application of digital twins for the detection of near-
misses at construction sites.
Therefore, this paper proposes a digital twin –driven near-
miss detection system at construction sites in a timely manner.

Related Works
Near-Misses in Construction
Although incidents may pose risks of injury or death, they can be
prevented with proactive measures ( Saurin et al. 2015 ). Despite
the importance of gathering detailed data on near-miss incidents
for construction safety management, too few of these events are
identified and reported. At present, the existing research on near-
misses in the construction field is divided into two categories.
One category traces the roots of near-misses at construction
sites. Wu et al. ( 2010a) established a systematic approach to ad-
dress and prevent precursors and immediate factors (PaIFs) at con-
struction sites, enabling the extraction of valuable insights from
accident histories to enhance site safety. Zhou et al. ( 2019) intro-
duced a human dynamics model using attempt data to uncover
the shared dynamic characteristics of accidents stemming from
unsafe behaviors among construction workers. Li and Ding ( 2019)
proposed a deep learning-based video surveillance method for
automatically detecting and analyzing the possible causes of near-
miss events at construction sites, forming an exhaustive case base
from real-time monitoring for safety assessment, training, and
safety management.
Another category monitors near-miss events for analysis to
guide construction. Wu et al. ( 2010b) proposed an autonomous
real-time tracking system of near-miss accidents (ARTTS-NMA)
at construction sites to monitor near-misses. Yang et al. ( 2014)
introduced an inertial measurement unit (IMU)-based method for
detecting attempted falls, which provided quantitative data for fall
risk assessment of steel workers. Teizer and Cheng (2015)p r o p o s e d
a proximity hazard indicator to automatically identify hazardous
areas at construction sites and to collect and analyze spatiotemporal
conflicts between walking workers and identified hazards.
The evolution of near-miss detection technologies has been
marked by advancements in wearable devices, mobile technology,
and artificial intelligence. Early efforts utilized wearable inertial
measurement units to monitor workers ’ movements and detect po-
tential falls ( Aria et al. 2014 ). Subsequent research refined these
systems with automated detection methods and threshold-based
algorithms ( Yang et al. 2014 , 2015, 2016).
Mobile technology introduced a new paradigm, leveraging
smartphone sensors and artificial neural networks (ANNs) for event
recognition, capitalizing on the ubiquity of smartphones ( Zhang
et al. 2019). However, this approach faced limitations in battery life
and computational power. Proximity-based systems used Global
Positioning System (GPS) and radio-frequency identification
(RFID) technologies to assess risks associated with hazardous
proximity, providing valuable insights into potential near-miss
incidents ( Golovina et al. 2016 ). Real-time location tracking with
ultrawideband (UWB) systems further enhanced this approach by
offering precise tracking of workers ’ movements in relation to
hazardous zones or equipment ( Teizer and Cheng 2015 ). How-
ever, these methods are hindered by high costs and installation
complexities.
Despite progress, current technologies face limitations includ-
ing discomfort and usability issues with wearables, battery and
processing constraints with mobile devices, and cost and com-
plexity challenges with proximity-based systems. To address these
limitations, computer vision offers a promising alternative. By an-
alyzing video feeds from cameras strategically placed on construc-
tion sites, computer vision systems can detect near-miss incidents
with high accuracy and minimal user discomfort. This approach not
only reduces the reliance on wearable devices and mobile technol-
ogy but also provides a more comprehensive view of the construc-
tion environment, enhancing the overall safety monitoring system.

struction environment pose challenges in acquiring multisource
information at construction sites. Additionally, there is a lack of
interaction between the data, such as the interaction between
workers and buildings, construction vehicles, and so forth, which
hinders the real-time and accurate management of construction
site safety.
In summary, DT holds significant potential for real-time mon-
itoring of near-miss events at construction sites. Firstly, DT can
provide a three-dimensional representation of near-miss incidents
by mirroring the physical environment in a virtual space, allowing
for the capture of detailed and accurate data that might otherwise be
missed. This capability enables the identification of subtle anoma-
lies or deviations from safe practices that could lead to accidents.
Secondly, by leveraging advanced analytics and machine learning
algorithms, DT can process this real-time data to perform predic-
tive analysis, issuing early warnings of potential safety risks before
they escalate into actual accidents. Furthermore, DT supports low-
cost remote simulation, computation, analysis, optimization, and
management processes ( Long et al. 2024 ), thereby enhancing op-
erational efficiency and safety management across construction
sites. However, few studies have demonstrated the application of
digital twin technology in the field of near-misses detection at
construction sites, and its effectiveness needs to be explored. There-
fore, based on the concept of digital twin, this study utilized com-
puter vision technology to integrate multisource information of the
construction fields and establish a near-miss detection system for
construction sites.

Classification of Near-Misses at Construction Sites
To provide a more comprehensive analysis of site operations and
interactions, we referred to relevant literature ( Li et al. 2016 )o n
near-miss accidents and, based on this, combined with discussions
with on-site construction management personnel, propose the clas-
sification principles of near-miss accidents. The paper categorizes
the construction site into two distinct groups: static elements, which
include structures and construction materials that experience min-
imal changes over short periods, often displaying cyclical patterns
during the construction phase, typically over the span of a week
to 2 weeks; and dynamic elements, which are entities that change
in real-time, such as workers, materials being transported, and con-
struction machinery.
Correspondingly, hazardous regions at the construction site are
categorized into two categories: static fixed areas, in which hazards
result from interactions between individuals and static materials or
structures; and dynamic changing areas, in which hazards arise
from interactions between individuals and dynamic materials or
construction machinery. Moreover, the hazard levels are divided
into three categories: safe, in which workers are not in any danger
in this area; hazardous, in which where workers may be in danger in
this area; and admonitory, which is set around determined hazard-
ous areas.
There are two types of near-misses in static fixed areas. The first
type exhibits diffusive characteristics, and is denoted S1 [Fig. 2(a)].
A typical example is a temporarily excavated foundation pit, in
which the boundary of the pit defines the hazardous zone. The
fencing around the pit, designed to keep workers at a safe distance,
defines the safe area. The region between the fence and the pit ’s
edge serves as an admonitory area. Another common example is an
aerial work platform or scaffolding, in which the area occupied by
workers is considered safe. Workers receive warnings when they
approach the edge, and a dangerous area lies beyond the edge.
These near-misses demonstrate anti-diffusion-like characteristics,
and are denoted S2 [Fig. 2(b)].
There also are two types of near-misses in dynamically chang-
ing areas. One involves horizontal hazards, and is denoted S3
[Fig. 2(c)]. When construction machinery, such as vehicles and
cranes, is in motion, workers may face accidental injuries resulting
from falls and collisions. Given the mobility of both workers and
hazard sources, the dynamically uncertain hazard source area is
defined by establishing the maximum effective area boundary
within a fixed distance. Conversely, the other type of danger arises
from the vertical direction, such as various construction materials
being hoisted on site, in which workers may face the danger of
falling objects from high altitudes, and is denoted S4. Therefore,
the space below the suspended object is designated as a hazardous
area [Fig. 2(d)].
Design Framework of the System
To monitor the four types of near-misses in real time, especially on
complex and chaotic construction sites, the system in this paper
was built based on the multisource information of construction
sites, including buildings, construction personnel, construction
materials and construction machinery and equipment at the con-
struction sites. Considering the importance of near-misses in
construction site safety management ( Jin et al. 2020 ), digital-twin
technology is introduced to the field of construction site near-
misses detection in this paper.
The digital twin concept, comprising five components —
physical part, virtual part, connections, data, and services ( Tao
et al. 2019 )—is illustrated in the system design framework pre-
sented in Fig. 3. The platform operates as follows. First, images of
the construction site, which are transmitted to the image processing
model including target recognition, tracking, and stereo matching
algorithms, are collected in real time using drones and binocular

Physical Module
Multisource Information of Construction Sites
Construction sites often are chaotic and complex scenes in which
various elements interact ( Haslam et al. 2005 ), including workers,
materials, structures, and machinery (Fig. 4). To ensure workers ’
safety by monitoring near-misses, it is particularly important to
obtain multisource information at the construction sites ( Ye et al.
2023). These multisource data, including details about workers,
materials, machinery, the building structure itself, and the sur-
rounding environment, form the essential foundation for the dig-
ital representation and simulation in our digital twin approach.
By capturing and integrating these complex interactions and dy-
namics within the construction site into the digital model, we are
able to conduct in-depth analysis and make informed decisions to
enhance safety and optimize construction processes.
Data Acquisition and Image Processing Module
To effectively obtain information at the construction sites in real
time, drones and binocular cameras are used to obtain informa-
tion on static elements and dynamic elements of the construction
site respectively. Uncrewed aerial vehicles (UA Vs) and surveil-
lance cameras deployed at the construction sites transmit the col-
lected image data to the image processing model in real time for
image processing (Fig. 5). The workflow of the model is as fol-
lows: the images collected by the drone that are passed into the
image processing model are used directly to establish the build-
ing information modeling (BIM) model of the construction site,
whereas the left and right images collected by the binocular
camera are used for target recognition, target tracking, and stereo
matching.
The target recognition algorithm is based on Y olov5, which is
efficient and effective in identifying workers, materials, and con-
struction machinery in each frame of image ( Tan et al. 2021 ; Zhou
et al. 2022 ). Y olov5 utilizes a single neural network to predict
bounding boxes and class probabilities directly from full images in
one evaluation, making it highly efficient and capable of real-time
processing. Its architecture, which includes a backbone for feature
extraction, a neck for feature aggregation, and a head for prediction,
ensures high accuracy and robustness in complex environments.
The identified targets are tracked based on Deep SORT version
1.2 that has the advantages of high real-time performance, high ac-
curacy, strong robustness, and strong scalability ( Maung and Myint
2022). Deep SORT extends the traditional sort algorithm by incor-
porating appearance information and deep learning features to han-
dle occlusions and track identity switches more effectively. This
integration enhances the tracker ’s ability to maintain consistent
identities across frames, even in crowded or dynamic scenes.
To obtain the three-dimensional world coordinates of the target,
stereo calibration, stereo rectification, and stereo matching are re-
quired. This paper utilizes ( Zhang’s 1999 ) calibration method for
the visual localization system. Zhang ’s calibration method is a
widely used technique for camera calibration due to its simplicity
and accuracy, allowing for the precise determination of camera
parameters from a set of images of a flat pattern.
Subsequently, the internal and external camera parameters are
imported seamlessly into OpenCV version 4.7.0. A stereo matching
algorithm, which was developed based on semiglobal matching
(SGM) ( Hirschmuller 2008 ), is employed for the stereo matching
step. SGM is a robust algorithm that computes disparity maps by
minimizing a global energy function, and is particularly effective in
handling occlusions and textureless regions. This method ensures
accurate and dense disparity maps, which is crucial for precise 3D
reconstruction.
Ultimately, the three-dimensional information is computed us-
ing the principles of similar triangles. Static-element messages and
dynamic-element messages then are passed to the digital twin mod-
ule for construction site reconstruction.

Digital Module
Construction Site Digital Twin Model
The construction site virtual model is built on Unreal Engine
version 5.1.1 ( Qiu and Y uille 2016) based on static element mes-
sages and dynamic element messages. The BIM model built based
on static element information is imported into Unreal Engine, and
surrounding environmental elements are added to model the inter-
actions between building components and environments. The BIM
model typically is updated on a daily basis, with dynamic elements
being updated in real-time. This frequency generally is adequate for
detecting and warning about near-miss events at construction sites.
It is acknowledged that different construction sites have unique
characteristics, and the update frequency can be adjusted based
on the specific project ’s needs.

3D Near-Miss Detection Method
To automatically detect and analyze near-misses at construction
sites, additional descriptive information for each individual re-
source involved in a near-miss event is necessary. For example, ac-
cording to the characteristics of each object, a protective envelope,
which users can customize according to different situations, is set
to expand a certain range around it. To save computing time, the
protective envelope typically is modeled as a cube, sphere, or cyl-
inder in this paper.
Unless specified otherwise by a user, each resource boundary is
surrounded by its own protection envelope that remains transparent
during operation (Fig. 9). The protection envelopes are determined
based on the specific characteristics and operational parameters of
the resources involved in the construction site. For the static parts
of the construction site, such as structural holes, foundation pits,
and unfenced floor edges, the protection envelopes are predefined
based on the hazardous nature of these areas. For example, a struc-
tural hole might have a protection envelope that extends 2 m
beyond its edge to ensure a sufficient safety buffer for workers. For
the dynamic parts of the construction site, the protection envelope
for workers is set as a cylinder with a radius of 1.5 m, derived from
the average distance a person can walk in 1 s, react, and come to a
complete stop. The protection envelope for construction materials
varies based on their characteristics; for example, large steel beams
being lifted by cranes would have larger protection envelopes than
a stationary piles of bricks, accounting for their movement and po-
tential risk radius. For construction machinery, several parameters
are considered, including operating speed, operating angle, equip-
ment size, and even ground conditions that might affect the machi-
nery’s braking distance.
The current protection envelopes for static and dynamic ele-
ments were determined by referring to some existing relevant
literature (Speiser and Teizer 2023 ) and in collaboration with con-
struction site management personnel. The protection envelopes are
based on scenarios from the majority of pilot studies, making them
applicable to similar construction scenarios. For different projects,
users can directly adjust the size of the protection envelopes accord-
ing to the aforementioned principles by inputting scale-up or scale-
down factors, based on the specific characteristics of the project.
For example, in a high-rise building project, cranes lifting heavy
materials will have a larger protection envelope to ensure safety
considering the height and the swing radius of the crane arm. In a
road construction project, vehicles such as asphalt pavers and roll-
ers will have protection envelopes adjusted based on their operating
speed and the width of the road being constructed. During the con-
struction of a bridge, protection envelopes around the edge of the
bridge deck will be significantly extended due to the high risk of
falls and the complexity of the operations involved.
Near-misses, as defined in this study, refer to close incidents
between one or more pedestrian workers and hazardous objects,
resulting in workers being exposed to danger. For example, for two
resources A and B, near-misses are defined as collisions of protec-
tive envelopes between the two. The near-misses detection algo-
rithm determines their collision start events and end events, and
records the timestamps of the collision start and collision end to
calculate the duration of the near-misses event.
Service Module
3D Near-Miss Detection Service
The real-time detection of near-misses is displayed on a digital
twin interface, and an intelligent risk accumulation system logs
these incidents (Fig. 10). This system enables users to modify the
threshold to suit various construction project needs. Defaulting to
Heinrich’s law ( Heinrich 1941 ) and for general applicability, the
threshold is preset at 300; surpassing this number prompts the sys-
tem to alert managers and workers, warning them of the potential
for minor injuries. However, users can adjust this threshold accord-
ing to the specific characteristics and risks associated with different
construction activities, allowing for better customization and im-
proved safety management.
Additionally, the analysis of data on near-misses collected from
the construction sites is tremendously valuable to both workers and
managers. For the workers, these data serve to enhance their aware-
ness and conduct regarding safety. Through the analysis of near-
miss incidents, workers can gain a better grasp of potential hazards
and proactively take measures to prevent accidents. This not only
helps in diminishing the risk of workplace injuries but also elevates
the overall safety of the work environment.
For managers, the data on near-misses offer critical insights that
can be leveraged to increase the safety of the entire construction
project. By examining these data, managers can pinpoint potential
security loopholes and areas of concern, enabling them to refine
work processes, conduct relevant training, upgrade equipment, or
institute new safety protocols. This proactive approach does not
only mitigate the risk of accidents, it also contributes to lowering
project costs and enhancing productivity.

Pilot Study
To assess the effectiveness of the system introduced in the previous
section, an industrial park project located in Foshan City of Guang-
dong Province in China was selected as a case to demonstrate its
implementation performance. The project is composed of six fac-
tory buildings, three single-story workshops, and two three-story
ramps. This paper focuses on the steel structure hoisting operations
on the top floor of the B2 factory building. The structure had not yet
been capped, and there are relatively few safety protection mea-
sures at the construction site. In addition, during the construction
process, workers exhibited many irregular behaviors, which created
numerous safety hazards during the steel beam hoisting operations.
This case study aimed to improve the safety management of the
construction site and provide a strong guarantee for the smooth
progress of the project through real-time monitoring of near-misses
during steel beam hoisting construction and providing real-time
safety warnings.

Discussion
This study introduces an image-based digital twin system that,
compared with traditional point cloud –based counterparts, demon-
strates significant enhancements in real-time capability and light-
weight processing. The system has been trained to recognize and
track common elements found on construction sites, such as per-
sonnel, materials, and machinery, and has established a dynamic
element model database. This foundational training ensures that the
system is broadly applicable to a wide range of construction envi-
ronments. However, it is acknowledged that certain specialized
equipment and materials may require additional training before
deployment. This pretraining process is designed to be efficient,
and does not entail an extensive time commitment, allowing for
rapid adaptation to specific site requirements. The system ’s modu-
lar design and the use of advanced stereovision techniques facilitate
this scalability, making it a robust and flexible tool for enhancing
safety management across diverse construction projects.
During field trials, the real-time digital twin –driven system
for detecting near-misses demonstrated exceptional performance,
achieving a perfect score in both precision and recall for Type S1
near-miss detection. This high level of accuracy may be attributed
to the relatively uncluttered environment surrounding the Type S1
near-misses during testing, which were characterized by a lower
number of workers and less visual confusion. Additionally, the
proximity of Type S1 near-misses to the installed binocular camera,
resulting in clearer video imagery, likely contributed to the system’s
enhanced detection capabilities.
Additionally, the impact of lighting on computer vision is a
pivotal factor to take into account, because it greatly influences the
visual quality of captured images. V ariations in lighting conditions

This paper presents an analysis of the system ’s stability under
varying exposure conditions through experimental evaluation. The
noticeable decrease in precision for Type S3 near-misses in over-
exposure scenarios can be attributed to several factors:
 image degradation: overexposed areas within the image can
become excessively bright, resulting in a loss of detail;
 feature distortion: overexposure often leads to prominent high-
light areas that can overshadow critical features such as edges,
shapes, textures, and colors; and
 Data set limitations: the model was trained on a data set lacking
diverse exposure scenarios, which hinders its ability to accu-
rately recognize and adapt to real-world exposure variations.
To mitigate these issues, the following methodologies could be
considered for future improvements:
 image preprocessing: implementing automatic exposure correc-
tion as part of the preprocessing stage can diminish the influence
of inconsistent lighting on the system ’s performance;
 dynamic range extension: employing image sensors with
a superior dynamic range can help alleviate the issues of
overexposure and underexposure, thus preserving more detail in
the imagery; and
 data augmentation: enhancing the training data set to include a
wider array of images with varying exposure levels can enable
the model to more effectively learn features across different
lighting conditions.
The lower recall rates for Type S3 near-misses observed across
the three test sets, compared with other types, may be attributable
to the densely populated construction machinery present around the
S3 areas within the site. The ensuing chaotic and complex environ-
ment could result in certain S3 near-misses being undetected by the
system, thus contributing to a marginally reduced recall relative to
other incident types.
Conclusions and Future Work
The construction industry is characterized by its complexity and
variability, leading to a high frequency of safety incidents. To mit-
igate these risks, it is crucial to implement a real-time monitoring of
near-misses at construction sites. Although these near-misses may
not result in immediate serious harm, they serve as indicators of
underlying risks and vulnerabilities that could precipitate severe
injuries or damages. Through proactively analysis of these near-
misses, construction management teams can pinpoint and rectify
unsafe conditions or practices, effectively forestalling the occur-
rence of actual accidents. Furthermore, this vigilant monitoring
process fosters a culture of safety and heightens the safety con-
sciousness among construction workers, contributing to an overall
safety climate.
Digital twin technology has gained significant attention as a
state-of-the-art simulation tool across various industries. Therefore,
we introduced the application of digital twin technology to the field
of near-misses monitoring at construction sites by presenting a
digital twin –driven near-miss detection system which distills im-
plicit ontological knowledge within engineering realms. This sys-
tem adeptly detects near-misses within a three-dimensional space,
providing early warnings that underpin a scientific approach to
safety management at construction sites. The key contributions of
this research are as follows:
 The paper classifies near-misses and multisource data at con-
struction sites, and proposes a real-time information acquisition
method based on computer vision, enabling the establishment
of a digital twin model for the continuous monitoring of near-
misses at construction sites.

However, the research in this paper still has some limitations.
Constrained by the inherent limitations of computer vision tech-
nology, there is an issue of incomplete information capture when
personnel and machinery at the construction site overlap. In addi-

the stability of the digital twin –driven system for detecting near-
misses. Furthermore, current vision-based digital twin system op-
erates effectively within a camera field of view of up to 20 m, which
is not suitable for residential houses, high-rise buildings, and large
classroom buildings.
Future research will address the occlusion problem by inte-
grating multiple perspectives to broaden the system ’s applicabil-
ity and enhance its suitability for large-scale complex scenarios.
Additionally, enhancements in both hardware and algorithms will
be considered to mitigate the impact of lighting conditions, with the
overarching goal of continuously refining the system to further the
advancement of safety management in the construction industry.
Furthermore, future studies will conduct detailed variable analysis
of different update frequencies to balance efficiency and cost,
enhancing the practical application of DT technology in various
construction settings.
