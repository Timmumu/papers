Abstract 
Eﬀective management and safe operation of tunnel infrastructure are critically challenged by the persistent vehicle tracking across 
non-overlapping
 camera domains and the lack of real-time situational awareness for emergency incidents. This work proposes a dynamic 
digital twin framework to create a high-ﬁdelity virtual representation that synchronously updates with physical tunnel environments by 
leveraging existing Closed-Circuit Television (CCTV) networks. The core is a novel Tunnel Re-identiﬁcation Model (TRIM) that can 
create discriminative feature embeddings for seamless vehicle tracking and re-identiﬁcation across multi-camera domains. Comparative 
validations unequivocally demonstrate that the TRIM signiﬁcantly outperforms Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) and standard 
Vision Transformer backbones, achieving a state-of-the-art mAP@10 of 0.684. Analysis of attention heatmaps reveals its ability to gen-
erate a comprehensive and granular feature representation, focusing on preserving high-quality characteristics for speciﬁc vehicle iden-
tity. Furthermore, the emergency response ability of the digital twin is augmented with a vision-based ﬁre detection module to real-time
localize and visualize emergent tunnel ﬁre events in the digital twin. By consolidating and distilling vision data from CCTV cameras into
an intuitive representation, the proposed framework enhances situational judgment for operators and signiﬁcantly reinforces the safety
resilience of tunnel infrastructure without requiring extensive hardware redevelopment.
Keywords: Digital twin; Vehicle re-identiﬁcation; Emergency response; Traﬃc management; Smart tunnel

 operation lacks responsiveness to the dynamic

Digital twins, as a bridge connecting the physical world 
and the digital space, oﬀer a revolutionary approach to 
solving the aforementioned problems and achievi ng smart
tunnel operation ( Wu et al., 2 025). Its data-driven integra-
tion with tunnel facilities and historic records enables real-
time awareness and real-scene modeling of digital twin
models for tunnel scenarios (
Yu et al., 2021 ). This further 
facilitates traﬃc management and emergency response by 
prompt vehicle positioning and immediate data transmis-
sion, thereby providing a visual platform for data integra-
tion and judgment ( Ye et al., 2023 ). Furthermore, the 
implementation of digital twins allows a uniﬁed data plat-
form to interconnect diﬀerent functional modules and sub-
systems, such as equipment signal controllers and ﬁre
services ( X. Zhang et al., 2024b ).

Despite these beneﬁts, how to fully utilize data, perform 
real-time updates, and reﬂect changes of the real world
remains the focus for tunnel digital twins ( Liu et al.,
2021). Current solutions have limited practicality because 
of the lack of cross-domain perception and the incompati-
bility of multi-source sensor data. This data silo degrades 
tunnel digital twins into low time-sensitivity systems with 
poor interaction between physical devices and virtual mod-
els. One of the challenges lies in persistently tracking vehi-
cle ﬂow inside tunnels, as well as immediately reacting to
emergency incidents such as ﬁres (
Gao et al., 2021 ). 
Given these gaps, this study proposes a dynamic tunnel 
digital twin framework for simultaneously updating vehicle 
behaviors and reﬂecting occurrences in accordance with the 
physical world. Rather than functioning as a fully auto-
mated closed-loop digital twin, the proposed framework
serves as an advanced data fusion and perception layer
within the comprehensive digital twin architecture (
Chang 
et al., 2024 ). By employing a Transformer-based re-
identiﬁcation model (TRIM) to conduct visual feature 
extraction and matching, the constant tracking and re-
identiﬁcation (Re-ID) of the same vehicle from multiple 
camera areas can be performed within the full-domain dig-
ital twin. Along with the regular traﬃc monitoring func-
tion, a vision-based ﬁre detection unit is coupled into the 
digital twin, which provides accurate ﬁre coordinates and 
risk estimations. This dynamic tunnel digital twin is estab-
lished directly on Cl osed-Circuit Television (CCTV) net-
works, meaning it can be conveniently deployed in
existing and upcoming tunnels without redevelopment.
By consolidating multi-camera vision data, it establishes
a fundamental data-fusion platform, thereby providing
the essential prerequisite data infrastructure for future
intelligent decision-making and automated facility control.
2 Literature revie w
2.1 Digital twin in tunnel lifecycle
Over the period of tunnel construction, uncertain and 
changeable geological conditions will be encountered, 
which are gradually exposed as excavation or blasting pro-
gresses ( Desai et al., 2022 ). Through integrating on-site 
sensing methods, including image recognition, oblique 
photography, laser scanning, and various physical sensors,
digital twins are allowed to quickly and accurately access
geotechnical and structural responses, thereby supporti ng
tunnel construction ( L. Zhang et al., 2024 ). Considering 
that tunnel construction involves multidisciplinary knowl-
edge that contains mechanics, thermodynamics, geology, 
and materials science, digital twins present computability 
and pr edictability to simulate multi-physics coupling and
avoid potential structural failures with ﬁne-grained descrip-
tions ( Fang et al., 2024 ). 
During the operation and maintenance phase, digital 
twins serve as a comprehensive and intelligent management 
platform, providing a holistic and real-time overview of the 
tunnels’ performance and environment. By integrating with
disparate systems, such as electromechanical equipment
(
Yu et al., 2023), lighting controls (Shen et al., 2022), traﬃc 
signals (Zhao et al., 2022 ), and structural health mo nitor-
ing (Wang et al., 2025 ), digital twins create a uniﬁed repre-
sentation of tunnel operational conditions. A practical 
exempliﬁcation involves designing a digital twin framework 
to ad opt surveillance videos to reconstruct an immersive
virtual tunnel and couple with tunnel facilities (
Z. Wu 
et al., 2022 ). This enables operators to predict the future 
performance of these assets and minimize the losses of traf-
ﬁc accidents, moving beyond reactive problem-solving to a
more predictive and optimized management paradigm
(
Khan, 2025 ). Historic traﬃc patterns can be superposed 
onto this digital twin model, allowing for the proactive 
adjustment of traﬃc managem ent strategies at the speciﬁc
time point to ensure smooth and eﬃcient transit (
Rezaei 
et al., 2 023). Furthermore, tunnel digital twins enable the 
sophisticated optimization of critical subsystems. For 
instance, ventilation and lighting systems can be dynami-
cally adjusted based on actual traﬃc volume and air quality
data, thereby signiﬁcantly reducing energy consumption
and operational costs while adhering to safety standards
(Yang et al., 2024 ). 
Although these applications have signiﬁcantly empow-
ered smart operation and maintenance of tunnels, current 
studies in this ﬁeld are still limited by data silos (
Chang 
et al., 2024). They lack a uniﬁed mechanism for associating 
and integrating tunnel lifecycle data, which restricts contin-
uous environmental perception of tunnel scenarios. Exist-
ing methods have diﬃculty enabling mutual interaction 
between various data, including CCTV videos, sensor mea-
surements, and facility conditions, resulting in digital twin
frameworks that fall short in supporting operational and
management decisions.
2.2 Digital twin in emergency response
In the domain of emergency response and disaster miti-
gation, digital twins are emerging as a cornerstone for 
achieving new levels of eﬃciency, saf ety, and sustainability
(
Fan et al., 2 021). This high-ﬁdelity replica of the physical 
world makes key emergency information available for 
security staﬀ from Internet of Things (IoT) networks, traf-
ﬁc systems, and environmental monitors (
Huang et al .,
2022). The resulting dynamic twin facilitates a holistic
understanding of the operational state, enabling a shift 
from reactive to predictive response. Advanced analytics 
and machine learning algorithms applied to digital twins 
can assist with ﬂood prevention by integrating rainfall data
and river water level information from sensors in ﬂood-
prone areas (
Thakur, 2024). Regarding urban wildﬁre con-
trol measures, digital twins oﬀer a systematic pipeli ne that
integrates remote sensing data ( Wang et al., 2024), fuel dis-
tribution ( Lewis et al., 2024 ), meteorological infor mation
(Hyeong-su et al., 2019 ), and aerial ﬁeld images ( Shao 
et al., 2023 ) to drive complex ﬁre spread models. This 
enables emergency departments to predict the spread path 
and speed of wildﬁre and its products in a virtual environ-
ment, determining the eﬃciency of diﬀerent ﬁreﬁghting
strategies and evaluating residential impact (
Halem et al.,
2024).

The application of digital twins fundamentally trans-
forms emergency response within tunnel environments, 
where rapid and informed decision-making is paramount
(Yu & He, 2022 ). In the event of an incident, such as a ﬁre 
or collision, the digital twin provides immediate and com-
prehensive situational awareness to emergency services and
tunnel operators by tracking high-ﬁre-risk vehicles and
forecasting potential ﬁre power (
X. Zhang et al., 2024b ). 
For example, it can predict the propagation of ﬁre and 
the dispersion of smoke with high accuracy, considering 
ventilation status and tunnel geometry through communi-
cating with external artiﬁcial intelligence (AI) models (
X. 
Wu et al., 2022 ). This predictive capability is invaluable 
for formulating eﬀective response strategies, such as opti-
mizing the activati on of ﬁre suppression systems and
dynamically adjusting ventilation ( Zeng, 2024). Moreover, 
digital twins can simulate multiple evacuation scenarios in 
seconds, identifying the most eﬃcient egress paths and 
communicating this guidance dynamically to travelers via
dynamic signage ( Han et al., 2020; Wong et al., 2025 ). 
Overall, the ability of digital twins for simulation, pre-
diction, and guidance provides an advantage that enables 
responders to mitigate the negative impact, protect human 
life, and restore normal operations with minimized delays. 
However, to capture changing ﬁre events and support time-
critical emergency response, low-delay information syn-
chronization between real tunnels and digital twins is
required (
Wolf et al., 2022 ). Most investigations have 
selected sensors already positioned in tunnels as data 
sources to implement digital twins, where situational 
awareness is limited compared to CCTV networks. This
may cause the critical unreliability of emergency
information.
2.3 Vision-based sensing in tunnels
While the aforementioned applications highlight the 
potential of digital twins in tunnel management and emer-
gency response, realizing these capabilities heavily depends
on real-time data acquisition and sensing ability (
Liu et al.,
2025). Situational awareness in digital twins has been lim-
ited by a reliance on single-point-based sensors, which 
can cause a critical latency between the emergency occur-
rence and response. To overcome this sensory bottleneck, 
recent research has increasingly turned towards vision-
based sensing. With technical advances in computer vision, 
it has become increasingly mature to take full advantage of
widespread CCTV camera networks to sense internal tun-
nel environments without extra installations of expensive
LiDARs or motion detectors (
Diren & Alth en, 2023;
Rios-Cabrera et al., 2012 ). Meanwhile, vision data pro-
vides more diverse information than that provided by 
lasers or millimeter wave s, which supports the real-world
modeling and awareness of tunnel scenes (
Attard et al.,
2018). Some investigations have demonstrated the reliabil-
ity and eﬃciency of vision data on tunnel construction ( Y. 
Li et al., 2024 ), 3D digitalization ( Xue et al., 2021 ), defect 
inspection ( Li et al., 2023 ), traﬃc management ( Lee & 
Shin, 2019; Tahir et al., 2023 ), and emergency response
(Cheng et al., 2025; X. Zhang et al., 2024a ). 
Despite these advancements in single-camera data ana-
lytics, a critical gap remains in the continuous tracking of 
dynamic entities across the entire tunnel infrastructure. A 
signiﬁcant amount of information in the visual data cap-
tured by CCTV networks has not been used eﬃciently, 
remaining conﬁned to processing and analysis within a sin-
gle camera domain. The information isolation between 
cameras with non-overlapping coverage areas results in dif-
ﬁculties in real-time visualization and cross-region vehicle
re-identiﬁcation in tunnels. This limitation directly moti-
vates the development of the digital twin framework pro-
posed in this study, which seeks to transform disjointed
camera feeds into a full-domain perception layer for the
dynamic digital twin.
3 System archi tecture
3.1 Top-level desig n
The proposed dynamic digital twin adopts CCTV cam-
eras as the data source for tunnel environment sen sing,
which can signiﬁcantly minimize hardware dependency.
As shown in
Fig. 1 , the digital twin framework employs 
a multi-stage pipeline to transform multi-source video feeds 
into a coherent and dynamic representation. It ﬁrst detects 
and tracks vehicles across real-time streams. A Re-ID mod-
ule then extracts vehicle features, computes similarity 
scores against a feature gallery, and assigns a global iden-
tiﬁer (GID) to each vehicle. In the event of a ﬁre, the frame-
work rapidly localizes the ﬁre disaster and visualizes it on
the user interface, providing critical situational awareness
to initiate swift and targeted emergency responses.
This dynamic tunnel digital twin consists of multi-
functional submodules, which support video decoding, 
vehicle tracking, vehicle Re-ID, ﬁre detection, and model 
rendering. Ther efore, the architecture is realized through 
a component-based design that enhances systematic 
scalability. Uniﬁed Modeling Language (UML) is a useful
way to illustrate the data interaction between various com-
ponents (see Fig. 2 ) ( Medvidovic et al., 2002 ). The data 
ﬂow initiates with the ingestion and decoding of cross-
region video streams from a network of CCTV cameras 
distributed throughout the tunnel infrastructure. An object 
detection and tracking module processes these streams to
identify and assign transient local identiﬁers (LID) to vehi-
cle entities within individual camera views.

The core of the digital twin is the vehicle Re-ID module, 
which tracks vehicles across non-overlapping camera 
views. It extracts a feature vector from each detected vehi-
cle and matches it against a dynamic feature dictionary. By 
calculating similarity scores, the syst em correlates vehicle
instances across diﬀerent cameras to assign persistent glo-
bal identiﬁers. Furthermore, an identity corrector inte-
grates spatiotemporal data, such as camera layout and
sequence, to validate these identiﬁers. This module assesses 
the coherence of each detected vehicle trajectory by verify-
ing its conformity with the known camera topology and 
ensuring that the cross-camera transition times are physi-
cally plausible. Filtering out illogical Re-ID results main-
tains a high-ﬁdelity representation of the tunnel
environment and ensures the accuracy of continuous vehi-
cle tracking.
Furthermore, the architecture supports functional mod-
ularization, exempliﬁed by an auxiliary ﬁre detection mod-
ule. The ﬁre detection task is performed by a ﬁne-tuned 
YOLOv8 model trained for 150 epochs on 70 000 images. 
This integration demonstrates the framework’s extensibil-
ity for concurrent task processing without bottlenecking 
the primary tracking modules. Crucially, the vehicle
Re-ID and ﬁre detection modules do not operate in isola-
tion. They are coupled at the data fusion layer to achieve 
comprehensive situational awareness. While the ﬁre detec-
tor yields spatial coordinates and event triggers, the Re-ID 
workﬂow supplies persistent vehicle identities. By mapping 
both data streams onto a uniﬁed spatiotemporal frame-
work, the digital twin inherently links ﬁre events with vehi-
cle trajectories. Consequently, the system can instantly
ascertain hazard locations alongside the quantities of
trapped vehicles. Based on the reserved modular interface,
this data link can further dictate the scale, priority, and
urgency of subsequent emergency responses via advanced
ﬁre prediction technologies.

3.2 Vehicle Re-ID strategy
The vehicle re-identiﬁcation strategy is underpinned by a 
deep learning architecture for extracting high-dimensional 
features and matching vehicle entities. While convolutional 
neural networks (CNNs) have been widely adopted for 
visual tasks, their reliance on local receptive ﬁelds makes
them vulnerable to the unique challenges of tunnel environ-
ments, such as cluttered backgrounds, variable illumina-
tion, a nd p artial occlusions. CNNs often struggle to 
capture the holistic structure of vehicles and are easily dis-
tracted by dominant global features like color, resulting in 
perceptual aliasing among vehicles with high inter-class 
simila rity. Vision Transformer (ViT) architectures over-
come these problems through their self-attention mecha-
nism, which inherently possesses a global awareness
capability ( He et al., 2021 ). However, universal ViTs often 
exhibit overly sparse attention, missing ﬁne-grained local 
details critical for instance-level identiﬁcation. Therefore, 
a vision-based tunnel re-identiﬁcation model (TRI M) is
speciﬁcally designed based on the ViT paradigm to solve
these drawbacks, as presented in Fig. 3.
Instead of processing the entire vehicle image at once, 
the model employs a sliding window approach to divide 
the image into a sequence of overlapping 2D patches. This 
sequence allows TRIM to convert each patch into a high-
dimensional vector, with spatial relationships preserved 
by embedding position information into each patch. A glo-
bal aggregation function is also included to summarize the 
entire sequence. Furthermore, a random combination
strategy aggregates subsets of local features, forcing the
model to learn discriminative details rather than over-
relying on a single vehicle part. This strategy can generate 
a multi-granularity feature representation that captures 
both the holistic appearance and ﬁne-gra ined local details
of a vehicle, ensuring reliability against the perceptual chal-
lenges inherent in tunnel environments.

4 Demonstrations and analyses
4.1 Visual ization
The dynamic digital twin framework features a graphi-
cal user interface that provides ope rators with comprehe n-
sive situational awareness (as shown in Fig. 4). It eﬀectively 
integrates raw visual data with a synthesized representation 
of the tunnel environment. The surveillance panel displays 
synchron ized video streams from multiple cameras along
the tunnel. These live feeds are enriched with real-time
visual augmentations, including bounding boxes for vehicle 
tracking and immediate alerts for environmental hazards 
like ﬁres. By combining direct visual evidence with
machine-generated annotations, the interface allows tunnel
operators to rapidly verify and assess emergency events.
A spatiotemporal replica of the tunnel environment is 
juxtaposed beneath the video feed, which is the core ele-
ment of the digital twin. This view abstracts the complex, 
multi-perspective video data into a single, coherent opera-
tional map of the entire tunnel length. It plots the precise 
longitudinal position of every tracked entity, assigning
each vehicle a persistent identiﬁer and annotating it with
dynamic attributes such as its current velocity. Crucially,
this digital twin also visualizes detected events, such as
the ﬁre, at their exact location within the tunnel
infrastructure.
In terms of implementation details, once the ﬁre detec-
tion module identiﬁes a ﬂame within a camera’s video feed, 
the system instantly extracts the bounding box parameters 
and projects these 2D pixel coordinates onto the spatiotem-
poral operational map of the digital twin. This transforma-
tion converts a basic visual alert into precise spatial 
coordinates, visualizing the exact location of the ﬁre along-
side camera positions, blind zones, and the dynamic trajec-
tories of surrounding vehicles. By integrating this localized 
event data with persistent veh icle identiﬁers, the digital
twin highlights the synergistic data linkage between the ﬁre
detection and Re-ID modules. Tunnel operators can
instantly identify which speciﬁc vehicles are trapped
upstream of the ﬁre disaster. This cooperation translates
isolated visual detections into a cohesive narrative, empow-
ering operators to accurately assess the spatial relationship
between the ﬁre and trapped vehicles.

4.3 Quantitative analys is
The quantitative performance of the TRIM is evaluated 
through feature-level similarity analysis and practical 
instance retrieval tasks. A feature confusion matrix pro-
vides insights into the discriminative power of the TRIM
for the vehicle feature embeddings (see Fig. 5). Vehicle fea-
tures are extracted and vectorized from images by the 
TRIM to calculate the cosine similarity score. This matrix 
visualizes pairwise similarity scores between vehicles cap-
tured by three non-overlapping cameras.
A critical observation is that the intra-class similarity 
scores are consistently and signiﬁcantly higher than the 
inter-class similarity scores for diﬀerent vehicles when com-
paring images of the same vehicle across diﬀerent camera 
views. For instance, the cross-camera similarity for vehicle 
GID 2 remains exceptionally high (e.g., a score of 0.957
between Cam 1 and Cam 2, and 0.942 between Cam 2
and Cam 3), whereas its similarity to other vehicles is
markedly lower. This pattern is consistent across all evalu-
ated vehicle identities, demonstrating that the TRIM model 
successfully generates a feature representation that is both 
invariant to the substantial viewpoint and illumination 
shifts between cameras and highly speciﬁc to each vehicle.
This conﬁrms its capacity to mitigate feature confusion and
form a reliable basis for identity persistence.

4.4 Comparative va lidations
To qualitatively validate the eﬃcacy of the feature 
extraction strategy employed by the TRIM model, a com-
parative analysis of its feature extraction mechani sm was
conducted against two prominent backbone architectures,
namely a CNN-based ResNet-152 (
He et al., 2016 ) and a 
standard Vision Transformer (ViT) ( Dosovitskiy et al.,
2020). The gradient-weighted class activation mapping 
(Grad-CAM) algorithm was employed to visualize the 
attention heatmaps generat ed by each model for a diverse
set of vehicle images ( Selvaraju et al., 2017 ). It is possible 
to discern where each architecture focuses its attention to
extract discriminative cues for vehicle re-identiﬁcation
(see Fig. 7 ). The results can clearly illustrate the superior 
and more task-appropriate feature learning of the TRIM
model.
The R esNet-152 baseline produced coarse and diﬀuse 
attention maps that frequently spilled into irrelevant back-
ground areas, such as roads or tunnel walls. While capable 
of general vehicle detection, the model struggles to isolate 
the subject from environmental clutters, which severely 
compromises its eﬀectiveness of instance-level feature iden-
tiﬁcation. In contrast, the standard ViT suppressed back-
ground noise and conﬁned its attention to vehicle 
instances. However, its focus was overly sparse, concen-
trating only on high-contrast scattered patches. Although
it successfully pinpoints discriminative regions, it fails to
capture the vehicle’s holistic appearance. This reliance on
a few discrete features renders the ViT model highly vulner-
able to occlusion, motion blur, and challenging lighting
conditions.
The attention mechanism of TRIM synthesizes the 
strengths of the preceding models while mitigating their
respective weaknesses. The heatmaps reveal that TRIM 
learned a feature representation that was simultaneously 
comprehensive and granular. The attention ﬁeld accurately 
conformed to the vehicle’s overall contour, eﬀectively seg-
menting it from the background, yet it also exhibited dense 
and high-intensity activations across a distributed set of 
detailed local regions. This included taillight clusters, win-
dow contours, rooﬂines, and other unique structural ele-
ments. This visualization implies that the TRIM mo del is
adept at capturing a rich, multi-scale set of preserving iden-
tity features across the entire object. This holistic and
detailed attention strategy explains the enhanced perfor-
mance of TRIM in reliably reidentifying vehicles under
the challenging and variable conditions of a real-world tun-
nel environment.

5 Dis cussions
5.1 System eﬃci ency
To rigorously substantiate the real-time and dynamic 
capabilities of the proposed digital twin framework, a com-
prehensive end-to-end late ncy benchmark was conducted
under high-load conditions. As shown in Table 2, the sys-
tem was stress-tested by concurrently processing synchro-
nized video streams from 19 non-overlapping CCTV 
cameras. During the evaluation, the tunnel environmen t
maintained a dense traﬃc ﬂow, yielding an average of
228 vehicles per system processing step.
The end-to-end pipeline latency is primarily distributed 
across four modular stages: vehicle detection, parallel vehi-
cle tracking, TRIM-based Re-ID, and global map render-
ing. The vehicle batch detection stage demonstrated its 
eﬃciency with a mean latency of 78.60 ms. The parallel 
tracking and the core TRIM-based Re-ID modules 
recorded mean processing times of 133.11 ms and 
145.36 ms, respectively. The 95th percentile (P95) and 
99th percentile (P99) metrics indicate that even under local-
ized computational spikes caused by sudden increases in
vehicle entities, the TRIM module consistently completes
feature extraction and dictionary matching within
372.43 ms. The rendering of the digital twin interface incurs
a negligible computational overhead within 1.19 ms.

5.2 Implications for smart tunnel operation
While t his perception pipeline establishes the physical-
to-virtual data ﬂow, the mature architecture relies on a 
bidirectional feedback loop. The analytical conclusions 
derived within the virtual digital twin, such as vehicle posi-
tions and early ﬁre coordinates, serve as immediate trigger 
inputs for physical space actuation. This virtual-to-physical
feedback will guide physical tunnel infrastructure, enabling
automated facility adjustments and informed human-in-
the-loop interventions. As shown in
Fig. 10 , the conver-
gence of vision-based dynamic digital twins with technolo-
gies centered on human interaction and emergency 
response presents profound implications for enhancing
tunnel safety operation and risk resilience.
The fusi on with the Artiﬁcial Intelligence of Things 
(AIoT) creates a symbiotic loop between the physical tun-
nel and its virtual counterpart, enabling high-ﬁdelity mon-
itoring and automated control (
Xie et al., 2 025 ). 
Concurrently, the incorporation of patrol robots, guided 
by the digital twin, can facilitate automated inspections 
of structural integrity and equipment status, mitigating 
the expense associated with human-led inspecti ons and
tunnel reconstructions (
Deng et al., 2 026). The integration 
of an Intelligent Dynamic Exit Sign (IDES) system can 
guide occupants to the safest egress path by altering sig-
nage in real-time according to on-site situations ( Wong 
et al., 20 25). It is a critical factor in minimizing evacuation 
time and potential casualties. Large language models 
(LLMs) provide a natural language interface for interact-
ing with tunnel sensor data, allowing actionable insights 
for streamlining maintenance and operational processes
(Otal et al., 2024 ). This eﬀectively realizes the closed-loop 
feedback between the virtual digital twin and physical tun-
nel facilities. The digital twin aggregates physical percep-
tion data into virtual conclusions, and LLMs
subsequently translate them into physical execution com-
mands (
Xie et al., 2026 ). These integrated approaches also 
include a shift from reactive to predictive maintenance 
strategies, such as optimized energy consumption through 
intelli gent ventilation and lighting control, improving the
lifecycle management of tunnel infrastructure.

The primary engineering design of this vision -driven 
framework is rooted in proactive early intervention. While 
purely vision-based systems inevitably face severe degrada-
tion under extreme late-stage ﬁre conditions, such as dense 
smoke or complete power outage, this digital twin is 
designed to capitalize on the critical early time window of 
a ﬁre incident. By providing sub-second situational aware-
ness during the incipient stages of a ﬁre, the framework
empowers operators to rapidly execute ﬁreﬁghting proto-
cols and activate ventilation systems before severe environ-
mental degradation occurs. This will actively prevent the
tunnel from deteriorating into an extreme scenario.
5.3 Limitations and future work
The current implementation primarily solves the chal-
lenges of situational perception and data fusion of tunnel
environments, w hich are the underlayer of a fully opera-
tional digital twin system. The focus of future research will 
remain on how to achieve subsequent upper-level intelli-
gent decision-making and closed-loop facility control based 
on the proposed framework. This involves establishing a 
bidirectional interaction mechanism. Digital twins will 
not only sense the physical world in real-time but also com-
mand infrastructures based on the decisions made within
the digital space. This transition will ultimately evolve the
framework from a passive perception platform into an
active, automated management agent.
Although the vision-based vehicle re-identiﬁcation 
approach designed by this work has proven its eﬃciency 
and reliability, it may be aﬀected by visual features of 
vehicles, particularly in handling h igh inter-class similar-
ities.
Figure 1 1 provides a similarity matrix across six 
distinct vehicles that substantiates this challenge. For 
example, two diﬀerent white vehicles, Veh_2 and 
Veh_4, exhibit a high similarity score of 0.753. This 
demonstrates that vision-based models are still limited 
by over-reliance on dominant, non-unique global features 
like color and vehicle shape, leading to perceptual alias-
ing in the feature embedding space. This issue is further 
compounded by high intra-class variation (e.g., the same 
vehicle appears diﬀerent due to viewpoint, illumination,
or occlusion), motion blur, and inappropriate installa-
tions of cameras.

The current spatiotemporal identity correction module 
relies on heuristic assumptions regarding camera topology 
and expected transition times. While highly eﬀective under 
normal traﬃc conditions, this purely vision-based 
approach exhibits inherent physical limitations and failure 
cases in high-density traﬃc and extreme ﬁre scenarios. For 
instance, when vehicles break down or stop within a cam-
era blind zone, or during severe traﬃc congestion, the
actual inter-camera transition time can vastly exceed the
algorithm’s permissible temporal window. Under these
anomalous cases, the failure of visual sensing and heuristic
topological assumptions inevitably leads to ID switches or
target loss.
To fundamentally overcome these limitations, a crucial 
advancement is to transition towards multi-source data 
fusion of external perceptive devices, especially LiDARs 
and millimeter-wave radars. This combination is envi-
sioned to provide continuous, absolute spatiotemporal 
coordinates. Unlike purely visual tracking, these data 
modalities are immune to illumination variations and 
visual occlusions. By fusing this high-precision physical 
data with visual ve hicle features, the system enables the
re-ranking of the visual matching list to prioritize spa-
tiotemporally most-probable matches. This multi-modal
synergy will ensure stable identity persistence and high-
ﬁdelity situational awareness even under extreme traﬃc
anomalies or heavy congestion.
Additionally, while the YOLO-based ﬁre detection mod-
ule demonstrates the extensibility of the proposed frame-
work, deploying a reliable ﬁre detection system in real-
world tunnels requires rigorous quantiﬁcation. It is diﬃcult 
to accurately deﬁne system performance and sensitivity 
using open-source static datasets due to complex tunnel 
environments. Therefore, a full-scale ﬁre experiment is cur-
rently being conducted in a realistic physical tunnel. The
quantitative results, boundary deﬁnition, false-alarm resis-
tance (e.g., distinguishing taillights from ﬂames), and ﬁre
detection sensitivity of this speciﬁc vision-based module
will be systematically reported in a dedicated follow-up
study.
6 Conc lusions
This study introduced a dynamic digital twin framework 
designed to enhance the operational management and 
safety of tunnels by leveraging existing CCTV networks. 
Quantitative evaluations clearly validated the superior per-
formance of the TRIM, with an mAP@10 of 0.684, outper-
forming 0.335 for a CNN structure, 0.245 for a standard 
Transformer structure, and 0.375 for a hybrid structure. 
The TRIM model demonstrates a satisfactory ability to 
generate highly discriminative feature embeddings, which
remain invariant to shifts in viewpoint and illumination
while being highly speciﬁc to individual vehicles. This capa-
bility is proven through retrieval experiments where TRIM
achieved the highest NPS result for correct vehicles among
nearly all camera views, which indicates the consistency of
a speciﬁc vehicle instance.
A comparative analysis of attention heatmaps reveals 
the mechanism underpinning this success. Unlike the dif-
fuse focus of the CNN model or the overly sparse attention 
of the standard ViT, TRIM presents a feature representa-
tion that is simultaneously comprehensive and granular. 
This feature extraction strategy directly translates to reli-
able re-identiﬁcation, even in challenging scenarios involv-
ing visually similar vehicles where other models fail. By
successfully integrating high-ﬁdelity tracking and emergent
event detection, the proposed digital twin system provides
an intuitive operational platform that enhances situational
judgment for tunnel operators without requiring extensive
hardware reconstruction.
The outlook lies in evolving this vision-driven digital 
twin system into a comprehensive perception and control 
framework. Data from heterogeneous environmental mon-
itors is envisioned to be combined with this framework for 
more unambiguous situational awareness. This integration 
will advance the system from passive monitoring to active 
closed-loop control. By establishing bidirectional data ﬂows 
with tunnel facilities, the digital twin will gain the capability
to intelligently adjust tunnel facilities, such as autono-
mously activating ventilation systems, dynamically manag-
ing lighting systems, and coordinating exit signs. It enables
a truly proactive paradigm for smart operation and auto-
mated emergency response in tunnel infrastructure.
