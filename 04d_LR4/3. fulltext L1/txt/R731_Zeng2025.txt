Abstract
Purpose – The application of steel structures in infrastructure construction may exhibit early-stage damage 
characteristics caused by dynamic load fluctuations and connection imperfections, which, if undetected, could 
pose significant safety risks. The paper aims to discuss this issue.
Design/methodology/approach – This study presents a dynamic health monitoring framework for steel 
structures under construction, integrating multi-source sensing, wavelet–Kalman signal preprocessing, 
spatiotemporal feature extraction via a CNN–LSTM neural network and maintenance decision inference 
using a Bayesian probabilistic graphical model. Such components are embedded within a digital twin platform 
that supports end-to-end status assessment and strategy mapping. The system adopts a four-layer architecture, 
comprising sensing (STM32 MCU), edge computing (Raspberry Pi 4), cloud-based inference (GPU) and real-
time visualization (Web Dashboard).
Findings – The framework was deployed on a 50-m cable-stayed bridge with 60 strain gauges and 30 
accelerometers, continuously collecting 260 m data points over a 30-day period from the deployed sensor 
network, the results show that the proposed method achieves 93.6% classification accuracy and a 20% 
improvement in early warning sensitivity compared to conventional frequency-domain thresholds and offline 
probabilistic neural network models.
Originality/value – The system provides a robust solution for intelligent structural health monitoring and life-
cycle infrastructure management.
Keywords Structural health monitoring, Steel structure, Digital twin, Algorithm optimization

Introduction
Steel structures, owing to their high strength-to-weight ratio and construction efficiency, have 
become the preferred structural system for the transportation, industrial engineering, and high-
rise building. However, if the constructors fail to dynamically monitor load fluctuations, 
welding-induced stress concentrations, and installation deviations during construction, this 
may not only cause micro-damage before structural commissioning but also increase 
operational safety risks during subsequent service (Singh et al., 2021). During construction, 
the dynamic structural health monitoring (SHM) technology enhances structural safety 
through high-frequency, multi-dimensional data collection and real-time analysis (Wang and 
Li, 2023). This approach enables precise structural status perception and timely anomaly 
warnings, while providing a scientific basis for subsequent maintenance decisions.

In addition to frequency-domain thresholding, vibration-based operational modal analysis 
(OMA) methods have been extensively utilized for structural feature extraction and health 
assessment. Nevertheless, these methods frequently encounter difficulties in processing non-
stationary signals and achieving real-time adaptation throughout dynamic construction 
phases. Consequently, a crucial demand persists for more intelligent and adaptive monitoring
methodologies. 
Furthermore, vibration-based operational modal analysis (OMA) methods have been 
extensively adopted for structural feature extraction and health assessment, especially under 
ambient excitation conditions. However, these methods often face challenges in handling non-
stationary signals and achieving real-time adaptability throughout dynamic construction 
phases, which constrains their applicability in construction-phase monitoring.
Recent advancements in digital twin technology have shown great potential in enhancing 
the fidelity and predictive capability of SHM systems. For instance, digital twins enable virtual 
representation and real-time synchronization with physical structures, facilitating predictive 
maintenance and decision support (Chen and Wang, 2025). Similarly, the integration of AI 
with OMA has been explored to improve feature extraction under varying operational 
conditions (Zhang et al., 2022). However, the combination of these approaches into a unified, 
real-time monitoring framework for construction-phase steel structures remains 
underexplored. This study aims to bridge this gap by integrating multi-sensor data, AI-
driven feature extraction, and probabilistic decision-making within a digital twin environment.
Conventional SHM methods primarily depend on frequency-domain feature extraction and 
threshold-based discrimination for maintenance strategy formulation or damage localization 
(Honarjoo et al., 2024). However, these methods are unsuitable for capturing non-smooth and 
heterogeneous features in construction-phase signals and may experience sudden accuracy 
degradation under real-world interference. Machine learning and deep learning have 
demonstrated significant advantages in damage recognition, including improved accuracy, 
enhanced efficiency, and superior generalization performance; nevertheless, these methods are 
largely confined to offline analysis and post-processing, with limited research on online 
incremental learning and practical deployment. Furthermore, research integrating digital twin 
frameworks with probabilistic graphical models (PGMs) for operational decision-making 
remains primarily theoretical, lacking seamless integration with construction-phase monitoring 
systems. The widespread implementation of end-to-end real-time early warning systems remains 
limited, which constrains the effectiveness of maintenance decision-making (Smith et al., 2024).
To address these challenges, this study develops an end-to-end dynamic monitoring 
framework encompassing sensor data acquisition, intelligent analysis, and decision mapping. 
A hybrid denoising scheme, based on wavelet transform and Kalman filtering, enhances the 
signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) by approximately 30%, ensuring robust feature extraction. 
A convolutional neural network (CNN)–long short-term memory network (LSTM) hybrid 
model, equipped with an online incremental learning mechanism, adaptively fine-tunes 
spatiotemporal feature representations, achieving a classification accuracy of 93.6%. Finally, a 
digital twin decision platform employing a Bayesian network maps health indicators to 
maintenance strategies, effecting dynamic warning triggers and prescriptive maintenance 
recommendations. By integrating the sensing layer, edge computing layer, and cloud-based 
algorithm layer through digital twin technology, the system achieves accurate health 
identification of steel structures during construction. This integration provides technical 
support for ensuring structural stability throughout the construction phase (Brenner 
et al., 2023).

The proposed framework integrates four key methodological components: multi-sensor

The CNN-LSTM hybrid was chosen over a pure LSTM to exploit the CNN’s ability to 
extract spatial features (e.g. strain distribution patterns) from multi-sensor data, which the 
LSTM alone cannot efficiently capture. This is particularly important for structural health 
monitoring where damage often manifests as localized spatial anomalies.
Theoretical framework construction
A sensor network comprising strain gauges, triaxial accelerometers, and fiber Bragg grating 
(FBG) sensors was deployed according to structural stress-concentration zones and modal 
response characteristics. Strain gauges—affixed at critical weld seams—offer 1 μ ε sensitivity 
to capture incipient micro-strain variations induced by localized stress risers. Triaxial 
accelerometers, rated to ± 16 g, were mounted at mid-span to record fundamental bending and 
torsional vibration modes (Johnson and Wang, 2025). FBG sensors—optical fibers whose 
reflected Bragg wavelength shifts proportionally with axial strain and temperature—were 
installed adjacent to bearing supports, providing 0.5 μ ε resolution for precise monitoring of 
support displacement and thermal effects. Strain gauges and accelerometers sampled at 2 kHz, 
whereas FBG channels operated at 1 kHz; all devices underwent two-point calibration against 
reference shakers and static load cells prior to deployment.

Dynamic decision-making and digital twin
A probabilistic graphical model (PGM) instantiated as a Bayesian network integrates three 
node categories: structural health state SSS, maintenance decision D, and observation vector O 
(Figure 1). Conditional probability tables (CPTs) were initialized based on expert knowledge 
regarding the relationships between load cycles, observed features, and damage states. These 
priors were then updated and refined using a historical dataset of 500 labeled damage events 
(from similar structures and laboratory tests) via Bayesian parameter learning. The resulting 
CPTs were validated through 5-fold cross-validation, showing consistent inference results

System architecture overview
The proposed system adopts a four-tier cyber-physical architecture (Figure 2), comprising a 
sensing layer, edge computing layer, cloud-based algorithm layer, and visualization layer. This 
modular structure ensures adaptability to complex on-site construction environments, while 
satisfying latency, accuracy, and scalability requirements. At the sensing layer, STM32 
microcontrollers (MCUs) are employed to acquire synchronized multi-modal signals from 
strain gauges, triaxial accelerometers, and fiber Bragg grating (FBG) sensors. Sensor 
triggering is controlled via timestampcalibrated interrupts to ensure temporal alignment across 
all channels (Li et al., 2024). The raw signal xðtÞ ∈ R n is sampled at a rate of 2 kHz (1 kHz for 
FBGs), and stored with metadata tags for device ID, sampling position, and physical modality. 
The edge computing layer, implemented on Raspberry Pi 4 devices, performs real-time 
preprocessing, including wavelet-based denoising and Kalman filtering. The Raspberry Pi 4 
was selected for its balance of computational capability, power efficiency, cost-effectiveness, 
and support for real-time Linux kernels, which are sufficient for preprocessing tasks such as 
wavelet denoising and Kalman filtering. More powerful processors were deemed unnecessary
for this edge layer, as heavy computation is offloaded to the cloud. The filtered signal x

Probabilistic decision-making and digital twin integration
Bridge monitoring case study
To evaluate the practical applicability and robustness of the proposed monitoring framework, 
an in situ deployment was conducted on a 50-m-span cable-stayed bridge located in an active 
construction zone. A total of 60 strain gauges and 30 triaxial accelerometers were installed on 
critical locations, including stay cables, pylons, and deck segments. Sensor locations were 
determined via modal analysis and expert-defined stress concentration regions (Yaoyama 
et al., 2024).
Ground truth labels for model training and validation were obtained through scheduled 
manual inspections conducted weekly using non-destructive testing (NDT) methods, 
specifically ultrasonic testing (UT) for crack detection and strain gauge validation against 
theoretical models. For this study, ‘minor damage’ was quantitatively defined as a localized 
strain reading between 30 and 60% of the nominal yield strength of the steel (345 MPa), 
accompanied by no visible deformation. ‘Severe damage’ was defined as strain exceeding 
60% of yield strength and/or the presence of visible permanent deformation or crack patterns 
identified by UT. For this study, ‘minor damage’ was quantitatively defined as a localized 
strain reading between 30 and 60% of the nominal yield strength of the steel (345 MPa), 
accompanied by no visible deformation. ‘Severe damage’ was defined as strain exceeding 
60% of yield strength and/or the presence of visible permanent deformation or crack patterns 
identified by UT.

Core results and health state evolution
Definition of Early Warning Sensitivity: Early warning sensitivity is defined herein as the ratio of 
true positive damage detections occurring before a damage severity threshold is reached to the 
total number of actual damage events. A warning is considered “early” if it is triggered at least 
10 min before the measured strain exceeds 50% of the material’s yield strength, which is defined 
as the transition point from “minor” to “severe” damage state for the purpose of this study.

This case study validates the proposed system’s capacity to operate reliably under field 
conditions, adapt to evolving load profiles, and produce actionable insights through a digital 
twin–enabled inference pipeline. The integration of edge intelligence and probabilistic 
reasoning proves critical in maintaining both responsiveness and decision interpretability 
during construction monitoring.
While the proposed framework demonstrates promising results, several limitations should 
be acknowledged. The accuracy of the Bayesian decision model is contingent upon the quality 
of the expert-elicited priors and the historical data used for learning CPTs, which may 
introduce bias. The current system’s latency, though acceptable for this application, may be 
prohibitive for scenarios requiring sub-second responses. Future work will focus on 
developing lightweight CNN-LSTM models deployable directly on edge devices to reduce 
latency, investigating semi-supervised learning techniques to minimize reliance on 
extensively labeled NDT data, and exploring federated learning approaches for 
collaborative health monitoring across multiple similar structures.
Conclusion
Conventional inspection methods often suffer from limited accuracy and lack real-time 
feedback capabilities when identifying early-stage minor damage. To address it, this study 
proposes a computerized intelligent algorithm-driven dynamic health monitoring framework 
for enhancing the safety maintenance of steel structures during civil engineering construction.

hybrid signal preprocessing, spatiotemporal feature modeling via a CNN–LSTM hybrid 
neural network, and maintenance decision-making through a Bayesian probabilistic graphical 
model (PGM). All components are embedded within a digital twin platform to form a closed-
loop solution that spans from data acquisition to actionable decision-making. The system is 
implemented with a layered architecture, offering high real-time responsiveness, scalability, 
and suitability for field deployment.
In a real-world application on a 50-m-span cable-stayed bridge, the system operated 
continuously for 30 days, acquiring over 260 million data points. Experimental results 
demonstrate that the proposed method significantly outperforms traditional frequency-domain 
thresholding and offline neural network models in terms of classification accuracy, early 
warning sensitivity, and robustness. The system demonstrates feasibility and effectiveness in 
engineering practice through its high-frequency monitoring capability and adaptive inference 
under complex construction conditions.
To address limitations in existing steel structure monitoring technologies, this study 
proposes a more reliable intelligent monitoring solution. This system enables constructors to 
transition from single-stage construction monitoring to a comprehensive lifecycle perception 
system with active decision-making capabilities based on real-time data. Future work will 
focus on enhancing multi-modal sensor integration, lightweight model deployment, and 
collaborative monitoring and risk prediction across structural systems.
