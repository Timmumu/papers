Scenario Modeling for Urban Road Emergency
Management: A Cognitive Digital Twin –Based Method
Gang Yu 1; Jialin Li 2; Jing Xiong 3;M i nH u 4; Ruoceng Zeng 5;
and Vijayan Sugumaran 6
Abstract: The cognitive digital twin (CDT) concept provides a promising way to organize and integrate the full life-cycle data of urban road
emergency scenarios, as well as facilitate scenario deduction and prediction. The construction of twin models constitutes the prerequisite for
the practical implementation and application of CDT. However, the existing research on emergency scenario twin modeling falls short in
providing a unified modeling method that comprehensively captures the multidimensional characteristics of scenario elements. This defi-
ciency, coupled with inadequate analysis of interelement interactions and constraints posed by semantic inconsistencies and interoperability
among twin models, hampers the accurate mapping of physical scenarios and their evolution dynamics. In response to the multielement,
uncertainty, and dynamic nature of urban road emergency scenarios, this paper proposes a CDT modeling method for urban road emergency
scenarios. First, the definitions of element-level and scenario-level twin models are elucidated in three dimensions —geometry, attribute, and
behavior—in conjunction with resilience theory. Second, a modeling method based on SysML and ontology is proposed for twin models at
different levels, and a SysML2Ontology transformation tool is developed to enable the systematic construction of emergency scenario cog-
nitive digital twin (ESCDT). Furthermore, a CDT framework for urban road emergency management centered on twin models is introduced.
Finally, the North-South Elevated Road in Shanghai, China, is used as the engineering application object. The feasibility of our modeling
methodology is validated through a case study, which involves the construction of the ESCDT for a collision accident. The model can be
further utilized for emergency scenario deduction, ultimately elevating the effectiveness of emergency management. DOI: 10.1061/JCCEE5.

Introduction
With the rapid growth of the world’s population and the acceleration
of urbanization, vehicles and traffic flows are soaring, increasing
traffic emergencies such as bad weather, traffic accidents, or road
failures. They can lead to disruptions in the operation of the trans-
portation system, placing great pressure on the reliable travel and
robust operation of urban roads (Stefaniec et al. 2020), directly threat-
ening the resilience of urban road systems ( Osman 2021). The resil-
ience of urban road systems is generally considered to be the ability
to absorb, adapt, and quickly recover after being disturbed (Bešinovi´c
2020). When a traffic accident occurs, resilience is mainly ensured
through technical and organizational means, that is, urban road emer-
gency management (Y u et al. 2024). Effective urban road emergency
management for enhanced resilience necessitates the integration of
the unique characteristics of the urban road during traffic accidents,
real-time situational awareness, and foresight of potential accidents.
(Ford and Wolf 2020 ). To satisfy these requirements, scholars have
been applying digital twin (DT) technology to improve the resilience
of urban transportation systems ( Miglani and Kumar 2019 ). Braik
a n dK o l i o u(2023) proposed a DT framework for emergency manage-
ment of transportation infrastructure during natural disasters that fo-
cuses on data acquisition, analysis, and reasoning, integrating
physics-based and data-driven models to simulate and predict facility
performance and assisting in the adjustment of emergency strategies
and planning. Feng et al. (2023) analyzed the data sharing problem in
the Internet of vehicles transportation system from a DT perspective,
developed travel behavior models under uncertain events, and estab-
lished a blockchain-enabled DT transportation system to improve
traffic network resilience. However, the urban road system is com-
plex, consisting of people, vehicles, roads, and environments. The
interactions between various elements during traffic accidents are un-
certain and dynamic, which challenge the ability to effectively model,
understand, and predict system performance ( Alibrandi 2022).
Cognitive digital twin (CDT) empowers DT with enhanced cog-
nitive capabilities and realizes DT integration with the help of seman-
tic technologies such as ontology and knowledge graph (Zheng et al.
2022), which facilitates the integration of life-cycle data, informa-
tion, and knowledge of urban road emergency scenarios. CDT
has been studied in intelligent manufacturing ( Eirinakis et al.
2022), smart cities ( Liu et al. 2023 ), etc. Similar to DT, CDT takes
the twin models as the core, and the division and definition of the
models have distinct domain characteristics.

When urban road emergencies occur, twin modeling of emer-
gency scenarios requires recognizing the complex dynamic coupling
relationships between multiple scenario elements and focusing on the
chain process of scenario evolution and its mutation characteristics.
In response to addressing the issue of completeness in twin modeling,
this paper proposes the multilevel and multidimension definition for
emergency scenario twin models, informed by resilience theory and
accounting for the multifaceted, uncertain, and dynamic nature of
urban road emergency scenarios. Subsequently, in view of the char-
acteristics of twin models at different levels and dimensions, a sys-
tematic method of model construction and integration is proposed,
aimed at unraveling the underlying mechanisms governing the inter-
actions among elements. Finally, with the twin model as the core, a
CDT framework is proposed for urban road emergency management.
The remaining part of this paper is organized as follows.
First, the research related to DT-based emergency management is
reviewed in terms of both models and modeling approaches, and
the research gap is identified. The next section introduces and
meticulously discusses the CDT model construction and integration
method for urban road emergency scenarios, together with an ex-
position of the CDT framework tailored for this setting. Then, an
emergency scenario twin modeling case under a collision accident
is introduced to verify the proposed method. Finally, the results and
future directions of the study are discussed and summarized.
Related Work
DT provides a new way to synchronize the physical and virtual
worlds ( Grieves 2014 ). In the field of emergency management,
DT leverages high-fidelity models and dynamic simulations up-
dated in real time to help decision-makers generate insights into
current scenario during and after the emergency occurrence, thus
enabling enhanced emergency management and significantly in-
creased resilience ( Braik and Koliou 2024b ). However, existing re-
search shows that ensuring consistency and interoperability of
models is both important and challenging for DT-based emergency
management ( Deng et al. 2021 ). To address this challenge, recent
studies have combined semantic modeling and system engineering
with DTs to improve interoperability and connectivity among twin
models ( Rožanec et al. 2022 ). On this basis, the concept of CDT
has been introduced ( Adl 2016 ), and recent works have explored
the potential of CDT in smart manufacturing systems. Nevertheless,
in the field of emergency management, there remains a need for
further investigation into the CDT model and associated modeling
methodologies, which are the core of the CDT application.
Twin Models for Scenario-Based Emergency
Management
DT can be defined as a multiphysics, multiscale model where digital
models are simulated, analyzed, and updated based on data obtained
from the physical world (Longman et al. 2023). Therefore, the foun-
dation of DT-based emergency management is the twin modeling of
emergency scenarios in the physical world. Classical disaster theory
identifies hazard elements, affected elements, and environment ele-
ments as the constituent elements of emergency scenarios in the
physical world ( Blaikie et al. 2014 ). The hazard element and af-
fected element, respectively, denote the elements causing the emer-
gency and those affected by it, while the environment element serves
as the backdrop conditions that affect them. Moreover, response
plans outline action schemes aimed at mitigating the impact of
an emergency (Ni et al. 2023 ), their implementation driving system
recovery from the emergency ’s effects, and these plans themselves
becoming pivotal elements driving the evolution of scenarios (Wang
et al. 2020 ). Thus, the hazard element, affected element, environ-
ment element, and response plan element constitute the key physical
objects in constructing an emergency scenario twin model. The in-
terrelationships among these various scenario elements drive the
evolution of emergency scenarios. Therefore, the construction of
a twin model based on the interaction mechanism between the el-
ements of the emergency scenario can provide a panoramic descrip-
tion of the emergency scenario ( Kang et al. 2023 ).
The core of DT is a high-fidelity model that contains a multidi-
mensional fusion of geometry models, attribute models, behavior
models, and rule models, so it can truly depict and simulate the state
of physical entities (Tao et al. 2019). In the field of emergency man-
agement, Li et al. (2018) analyzed the dynamic interactions between
flood inundation and traffic flow and conducted real-time simulation
modeling of flood scenarios from the geometric and behavior dimen-
sions to predict traffic disruptions. Lv et al. ( 2022) used traffic ac-
cident intelligent algorithms to analyze the attribute characteristics
of involved elements, establishing a traffic accident prevention and
prediction system driven by DT and artificial intelligence. To en-
hance the understanding of the full-stage and overall posture of emer-
gency scenarios ( Arvin et al. 2023 ), resilience-based scenario
analysis and modeling methods have gradually gained attention.
Resilience embodies a system’s and its subsystems’ capacities to ab-
sorb, adapt to, and recover from adverse or abnormal states, serving
as an effective means of integrating information about scenario el-
ements and fostering a holistic comprehension of emergency scenar-
ios (Field et al. 2022 ). Jiao et al. ( 2021) established a storm disaster
scenario model by identifying the key factors influencing the resil-
ience of urban rail transit stations and their action paths. Y u et al.
(2023) applied the pressure-state-response theory to analyze the in-
teraction mechanisms between multidimensional scenario elements
and the characteristics of the resilience stage and established an
emergency scenario model from a resilience perspective. Resilience
provides a new perspective for in-depth exploration of the coupling
relationship and dynamic evolution pattern among scenario ele-
ments. Therefore, this paper considers that it is promising to integrate
multidimensional scenario element information within the resilience
framework to construct multilevel twin models of emergency sce-
nario cognitive digital twin (ESCDT).
Twin Modeling Approach for Emergency Scenario
Appropriate modeling methods facilitate the practice of twin mod-
els. Multiple studies have been carried out to explore the construc-
tion of emergency scenario twin models. For the construction of
geometry models, White et al. ( 2021) integrated building informa-
tion modeling (BIM), big data, and IoT sensor data to create a
DT-supported smart city, in which BIM was used to present the
collected information in 3D format. For the construction of attribute
models, Liu et al. ( 2020) collected indoor real-time operating in-
formation to extract the key attributes of the building and used ma-
chine learning models to automate the classification and severity
assessment of emergency scenarios. For the construction of behav-
ior models, Fang et al. (
2023) introduced a structural model to
simulate the safety performance of workers ’ behavior under differ-
ent intervention strategies by considering the behavioral decision-
making process under emergency scenarios and the interaction
between workers and the environment.
Existing research predominantly proposed modeling methods
for a single dimension in a certain application field. However,
an emergency scenario contains multiple elements, spans multiple
phases, and is a complex object with uncertainty, which requires a
systematic modeling approach to construct and integrate models in
different dimensions. SysML is a widely used system modeling

Ontology is often used to solve semantic issues because it en-
ables modeling data in a unified form and introduces the possibility
of data expansion ( Chen et al. 2020 ). Specifically, it represents
knowledge as a set of concepts within a domain, using a shared
vocabulary to represent the types, attributes, and interrelationships
of these concepts, providing an effective way to integrate hetero-
geneous data, information, and knowledge involved in the CDT
models ( Zheng et al. 2022 ). Jia et al. ( 2022) proposed a generic
ontology-based information fusion method that can construct
complex DT models with multiscale, multidimensional, and multi-
scenario information. Jiang et al. ( 2023) developed a building fire-
fighting ontology to fuse static geometry information and dynamic
sensory data and deployed this ontology as the DT model to help
respond quickly to fire events.
Relevant studies indicate that SysML and ontology are the main
enabling technologies for CDT modeling and integration. In this
study, we consider using SysML and ontology for twin modeling
of urban road emergency scenarios to solve the problem of repre-
senting and integrating multilevel, multidimensional information.
Knowledge Gaps
The review of existing literature highlights the following knowl-
edge gaps:
1. The existing research on emergency scenario twin models pre-
dominantly focuses on the geometrical modeling of scenario el-
ements, overlooking the definition and construction of other
dimensions such as attribute and behavior models. The absence
of attribute models hinders the description of crucial character-
istics of scenario elements, thereby impeding an accurate reflec-
tion of scenario state. Similarly, the lack of behavior models
precludes the depiction of the evolutionary behaviors and inter-
actions of scenario elements, making it challenging to illustrate
the dynamic evolution of the scenarios. Consequently, the fail-
ure to precisely construct and integrate multidimensional mod-
els of scenario elements results in an inadequate mirroring of
their real-world states, hindering the precise mapping of emer-
gency scenarios.
2. The urban road emergency scenario is a complex object, and
while geometric modeling methods for scenario elements have
matured, the construction of attribute models has not yet fully
established the mapping between the attribute models of sce-
nario elements and scenarios. The construction of behavior
models has also not delved into behavioral traits, response
mechanisms, and evolution patterns. Moreover, the analysis and
construction of the interactions and coupling relationships
among scenario elements are crucial for the assembly and inte-
gration of element-level models. However, there is a lack of
research on the assembly and integration of models from a
multilevel perspective, ranging from element-level to scenario-
level. Additionally, models exhibit deficiencies in semantic
consistency and interoperability, limiting their effective CDT-
based applications such as emergency simulation and deci-
sion-making.
Therefore, this study proposes a method to define, construct,
and integrate CDT models of urban road emergency scenarios.
First, this article combines resilience theory to define multidimen-
sional and multilevel twin models to achieve a complete depiction
of emergency scenarios. Further, based on the characteristics of
twin models at different levels and dimensions, a model construc-
tion and integration method based on SysML and ontology is pro-
posed, and a model transformation tool is developed, thereby
facilitating the systematic and automatic construction of ESCDT.
Building upon the constructed twin models, the study introduced
a CDT framework for urban road emergency management.
Methodology
Models for ESCDT
In this paper, the twin models for urban road emergency scenarios
are classified into element-level and scenario-level, and different-
level twin models (LTMs) are constructed from multiple dimen-
sions such as geometry, attribute, and behavior, as shown in Fig. 1.
Among them, the geometry model depicts the external manifes-
tation of emergency scenarios with information such as the geomet-
ric appearance and geometric structure of elements. The attribute
model (AM) is used to describe the characteristics of elements in
the scenario evolution process, including feature attribute (FA) and
association attribute (AA). FA refers to the inherent attributes of an
element, whereas AA denotes attributes that arise from physical
linkages or connections with other elements (called associated el-
ements). For example, vehicle cargo and vehicle hazard element
have associated attributes such as loadedOnVehicle and loaded-
Cargo, respectively, which shows a strong linkage between them.
Therefore, AA portrays the physical interdependencies between
elements (Rinaldi 2004). Such interdependencies can be classified
into compositional and noncompositional interdependencies. Com-
positional interdependencies denote that the associated element is a
component of the current element, and then the corresponding AA
defines the information such as function and structure related to the
associated element. The AA under noncompositional interdepen-
dencies describes the interactions of the elements, the degree of
influence, and the way they are associated. Changes in the attributes
of elements are driven by certain behaviors, so the behavior model
(BM) is employed to elucidate the interactions between elements.
BM involves four components, including the behavior element
(BE), behavior condition (BC), behavior logic (BL), and behavior
result (BR). BE is the set of scenario elements involved in behavior.
BC is the condition under which the behavior occurs, consisting
of a series of propositions that determine the attributes of the in-
volved elements. BC denotes receiving input from other elements,
so BM can characterize the functional interdependencies between
elements (Aghababaei and Koliou 2022 ). BL is the logic of behav-
ior and describes the process of attribute changes. BR embodies
the outcome of attribute alterations resulting from the behavior.
Employing the BM enables the portrayal of individual element evo-
lution and interdependencies, thereby elucidating the logical rela-
tionships among elements within emergency scenarios.

etry, attribute, and behavior models, the element-level twin model

extracting safety, function, time, and economy attributes from the
AMs of ELTM. Subsequently, the BMs of the ESLTM are formed
by integrating the BMs of ELTM into a panoramic view of scenario
evolution, guided by the concurrent and sequential relationships
among individual behaviors.
Modeling for ESCDT
A systematic modeling method with high fidelity, punctuality, and
efficiency is an essential support for the CDT model. Existing
relevant research shows that BIM is the most commonly used geo-
metric modeling technology, and its application has been extensively
explored (Pan and Zhang 2021 ). On account of this, a novel CDT
modeling method is proposed based on SysML and semantic tech-
nology, constructing and integrating AMs and BMs to build differ-
ent LTMs.

of semantic consistency and interoperability, which may lead to
information ambiguity and increased complexity when directly
combined. In contrast, ontology models, with their rigorous logical
structures and robust reasoning capabilities, excel at clearly organ-

well as facilitating the understanding and analysis of underlying sce-
nario characteristics. Consequently, this section presents an ontology-
based ESL TM construction method; the ontology base contains three
core ontologies: theEmergency ontology, theScenario ontology, and

Modeling of ESCDT
According to the definition in the section “Modeling of ESLTM, ”
ESLTM comprises three constituent ontologies: the Emergency
ontology, the Scenario ontology, and the ScenarioElement ontol-
ogy. To realize the systematic construction of ESCDT, a novel
approach is proposed in this section, which first proposes the
transformation rules and algorithms for converting SysML v2-
based ELTM to the ScenarioElement ontology and extracts the
relevant instance information. Second, corresponding ontologies
are generated based on the definitions of the Scenario ontology
and the Emergency ontology; the corresponding individuals of the
two ontologies are obtained by reasoning semantic web rule lan-
guage (SWRL) rules.
Based on the basic element categories of the ontology model,
the transformation rules (see Fig. 6) can be divided into three
major categories: class, property, and relationship. The owl classes
are developed based on the interested entities, so they can be ob-
tained through the conversion of part definition and item definition,
which represent such entities, as shown by Rules 1 and 2.
Furthermore, state definition is also translated into the owl classes,
as illustrated by Rule 3. The data properties are developed to de-
scribe the attributes of the entities, and, thus, they can be generated
from attribute definition , which encapsulates the entity ’s FA, as
demonstrated by Rule 4. The object properties are developed to
describe the interrelationships between entities. These are derived
from action definition and state definition , which describe the
entity’s BM, as well as item definition and part definition , which
capture the entity ’s AA, as shown in Rules 5 –9.

1. The association rules between scenario element and scenario
(Rules S1 –S19)
Rules S1–S19 serve to facilitate the inferential transformation
of individuals of the ScenarioElement ontology into individuals

and equivalentElement object properties. Rules S2–S4 map tem-

from the ScenarioElement ontology to corresponding attributes
in the Scenario ontology. Rules S5 –S19 relate the properties
of the ScenarioElement ontology with the resilience proper-
ties of the Scenario ontology from four aspects: safety, function,
economy, and time. In terms of safety, the properties of the
Scenario ontology include emergencyType and casualties. Rules
S5–S8 infer emergencyType property based on the behavior type
and target objects of the HazardElement class within the Scenar-
ioElement ontology. Rule S9 infers the Casualties property from
the Casualties property of the AffectedPeople class within the
ScenarioElement ontology. This property is true when someone
is injured or dead; otherwise it defaults to false. In terms of
function, the property of the Scenario ontology is roadPassibil-

property of the Road class and its associated lane class within
the ScenarioElement ontology. In terms of economy, the prop-
erties of the Scenario ontology include roadLoss and resource-
Type. Rules S11 and S12 describe the association of the
DamageCondition property of the Road class and Facility class
in the ScenarioElement ontology with roadLoss. Rule S13 infers
resourceType based on the type of response resource described
in the ResponsePlan class within the ScenarioElement ontology.
Lastly, in terms of time, Rules S14 –S19 employ built-in SWRL
functions such as subtract, greaterThanOrEqual, and lessThan
to calculate durations and match time intervals, thereby deter-
mining the responseDuration, disposalDuration, and emergen-

Proposed Framework for ESCDT
Based on the concept explanation and construction method for
ESCDT, the overview of the proposed framework is illustrated in
Fig. 11. It primarily consists of the physical layer, data layer, twin
layer, knowledge layer, and service layer.
1. The physical layer aims to process information about relevant
entities in the physical scenarios through data collection, map-
ping, and transmission. In urban road emergencies, relevant
entities include the vehicle hazard element, vehicle affected
element, road affected element, people affected element, road
environment element, meteorology environment element, re-
sponse action element, and resource element. Data acquisition
equipment includes roadside equipment and external systems.
Roadside equipment mainly collects various traffic parameters,
vehicle types, weather, etc. External systems collect data from
external channels, such as traffic and travel speed provided by
the toll collection system, real-time speed and congestion con-
ditions reported by the navigation map platform, operation plans
recorded by the maintenance management system, and real-
time location data provided by the rescue vehicle management
system. Then, standard data interfaces are required to map these
initial data into data catering to DT modeling. Moreover, real-
time data transmission is made possible by edge computing and
5G technology ( Liu et al. 2021 ). Thus, dynamic monitoring
information about physical entities can be provided to the virtual
space.
2. The data layer is used to collect and store twin data. The twin
data can be divided into physical data, virtual data, and knowl-
edge data ( Y u et al. 2021
). The physical data originate from the
physical layer, which are collected by access devices. These data
reflect state changes of relevant entities, furnishing a fundamen-
tal basis for emergency decision-making. The virtual data are
generated through the construction and operation of twin mod-
els. On the one hand, multidimensional models are constructed
using data that reflect the characteristics across various

3. The twin layer aims to digitally construct ESCDT with cognitive

ables the twin to have a rich semantic foundation and provides a
shared knowledge base for cognitive services. Based on these
twin models, the systematic construction of ESCDT is achieved
through model transformation and geometric alignment.

emergency response cases. The generated knowledge is stored
in the dynamic knowledge base. The analytical model includes
reasoning analysis models, and uncertainty analysis models,

under the influence o f different emergency plans, thereby en-
abling intelligent emergency decision-making. After a traffic
accident, decision-makers use CDT models to simulate and
evaluate the effects of various emergency plans, thereby gen-
erating new insights and refining current emergency plans to
enhance the efficacy of future responses.
Case Study
Shanghai Urban Express Road Network is the backbone of trans-
portation in Shanghai, China. The North-South Elevated Road in
Shanghai Urban Express Road Network starts at the intersection of
Longyao Road Tunnel and Jiyang Road and ends at the S20 Outer
Ring Expressway (shown in red in Fig. 12). The total length of the
road is about 22.2 km (Guan 2022), and the total flow rate of the main
line is about 4,277 veh=h during the morning peak (Xu et al. 2024).
It is one of the main elevated roads in Shanghai, bearing a substantial
portion of the traffic and experiencing frequent traffic accidents.
At about 12:25 a.m. on January 17, 2022, a box truck and a car col-
lided on the Huma section of Gongjiang Road on the North-South
Elevated Road. The accident resulted in one person being injured,
garbage and oil stains appearing on the road, and damage to one anti-
noise screen. After rescue, firefighting, towing, and rescue emer-
gency units carried out rescue operations, the lane returned to
normal. This study selects the above-mentioned two-vehicle colli-
sion accident (depicted in Fig. 13) as a case study to demonstrate
the feasibility of the proposed ESCDT modeling method.
