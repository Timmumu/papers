ABSTRACT 
The industries engaged healthcare, manufacturing, and construction automated with exquisite 
creativity and innovation at the doorstep of Industry 4.0. In this process, digital twins are dili -
gently used to supplement industrial processes, reduce costs, monitor assets, streamline mainte -
nance, reduce downtime, and enable the creation of connected goods. The research is aimed at 
finding the digital representation of electro-mechanical railway interlocking elements are simu -
lated and its functionality is verified accordingly. With respect to carry out this research, the 
Indian Railways Mangalagiri Railway Station blueprints were collected to represent the physical 
Model which consists of signals, points, a crank handle, an LC gate, and many more elements. In 
this paper, three interlocking elements such as points, crank handle, and LC gate, are analysed, 
modelled, and simulated by using Model Sim ME of Libero SoC software. Representation of these 
digital elements were developed using a smart design canvas. Therefore, the proposed method -
ology will enhance the safety measures and security levels of the train movements and reduces the 
time required to replace the faulty relay. Similarly, in future, the proposed model could be 
dumped into the FPGA kit to verify the real-time data for finding the faulty relay in the relay- 
based interlocking system. 
1. Introduction 
In the demanding era of industry 4.0, digital twins (DT) are used as a tool to connect physical and digital worlds. A virtual rep -
resentation of a physical system, process, or product can be used for diagnostics and design adjustments is called a Digital Twin. 
Manufacturing companies are using the leverage of DT technology to reduce development costs of the next generation of machines by 
50 %. Digital twins are being used by businesses to optimise their products in ways that were previously either impossible or unrealistic 
[1 – 3] . It serves as a link between the physical and digital worlds by using sensors to gather information in real-time situation about a 
physical object which can then be utilised to construct a digital replica of the object. It can receive inputs from sensors gathering data 
from a real-world counterpart, simulate the physical object in real-time, and provide insights in the form of performance and potential 
problems [4] . A digital twin can help to troubleshoot the potential faults which ultimately improves customer satisfaction. It also helps 
with product differentiation, product quality, and add-on services. Primarily, the digital twin is composed of physical entities in the 
real world, their virtual models and the connected data that tie the two worlds [5] .

2. Railway interlocking system 
There are three kinds of railway interlocking systems present such as mechanical, electro-mechanical (relay) and computer-based 
systems [21] . In this research, the virtual modelling of a relay-based interlocking system was considered as a part of the imple -
mentation of the digital twin [21] . The Fig.2 . represents the relay-based, metal-to-carbon type relay interlocking system including a 
VDU panel, relays, data logger and field gears. Whenever the SM wants to allow/dispatch a train into a station, he will operate the 
signal from the panel to meet the required operation based on the status of the relays and conditions of interlocking [ 22 , 23 ]. The status 
of relays stores in the data logger from time to time [ 24 , 25 , 14 ]. In the process of this research, digital modelling was carried out on 
route setting devices such as points, crank handle (CH) and level crossing (LC) gates. So, an elaborated working principle of these three 
elements were discussed in the following section. 
2.1. Point 
A point is a motor that allows the movable rails to either be normal or reverse that allows train to be either on the main track or 
crossing respectively [26] . Let ’ s say a point is set to normal state, in which case the train goes from A to B; if the point is set to reverse, 
the train goes from A to C as shown in Fig 3 . The general and practical representation of the element “ point ” is shown in Figures 3(a) 
and 3(b). Points can be operated in two ways such as rod operated, and motor-operated. In this research, motor-operated points are 
considered for implementation of the virtual model. 
2.2. Crank handle 
If the operation of a point fails due to any error, then the point can be operated via CH. After a signal has been set, manual operation 
of the point could affect the train ’ s ability to run, therefore CH can be appropriately linked with signals. As there are several points in a 
station, giving CH interlocking for each point separately is not feasible [27] . Additionally, it is inappropriate to use a single crank

handle for all the points. So, to obtain maximum flexibility, points are grouped, and the concept of CH is included in interlocking 
elements as follows: 
• The relevant CH, which is stored inside an electrical key transmitter (EKT), cannot be unlocked once a signal has been set across the 
points. 
• When CH is removed, it is not permitted to operate the panel points along with its related signals. 
• No other group point machine may accept the crank handle that was removed from another group. 
To achieve the aforementioned interlocking, the crank handle must be linked and welded to the EKT key. The insertion of a key into 
the EKT and its subsequent clockwise twist causes the CH in locked relay (CHLR) to accelerate and sustain front contact, as seen in 
Fig. 4 . The crank handle EKT is kept in the provided glass-fronted box secured with a padlock [28] . Each time the crank handle is 
released to manually operate the point, SM must enter data in the CH register.

The purpose of the LC gate is to ensure traffic movement across them in a controlled way and in accordance with the established

LC Gate in block section – It is provided with a gate signal at 180 m from the gate. It displays green when the gate is closed and red

SM ’ s assistance, Gateman has no control over the LC gate ’ s opening.

3.2.2. Crank handle modelling 
When an electrical point fails, crank handles are available to manually operate motor-operated points. These are arranged in the CH 
zone according to working convenience. Signals are interlocked with crank handles. CH cannot be removed while signals are turned 
"off," and vice versa. Figs. 9 (a) and 9 (b) show the physical and DT of a CH element. Mangalagiri Railway Station consists of six points 
which are grouped into three elements named CH1FR, CH2FR, and CH3FR respectively. 
CH1FR will be dropped when a signal is on. At that point, CH1LR does not drop if SM tries to withdraw the crank handle by pushing 
the economizer push button since the feed extended to CH1LR through CH1FR back contact, as seen in Fig. 4 . The CH key cannot be 
withdrawn as a result, and the EKT lock coil isn ’ t energised. The signals that are sent off continue to exist in the same state at the same 
time. CH1FR stays energized if a signal is not shut off. CHLR decreases when SM hits the push button for the economizer. The key is 
removed and the lock coil is energized. As a result, the signals that are interlocked with it cannot be removed. The CHLR circuit is built 
in such a way that when the economizer push button hits to remove the crank handle, it cuts off power to CHLR and CHLR drops, 
ensuring that CHLR drops before the actual extraction of the key. Crank handle extraction is not possible till CHLR drops. 
For implementing the crank handle, need to know about the relay called CHFR. If this relay is free, then the CH is used for setting 
the points. CH1FR will be in an energised state when all the relevant signal dials are normal, meaning that the RRs have been dropped. 
After that, the CH box ’ s crank handle free indicator will be lit. As a result, it lets SM know that CH1 can be removed if necessary. The

5. Conclusion & future scope 
In this paper, the importance of Digital Twin concept in Industry 4.0 is discussed. For creating a digital twin, the physical asset 
should be modelled, simulated and it should be implemented. As a part of creating a digital twin of railway interlocking system, the 
interlocking elements such as points, crank handles, and LC gates are modelled and simulated. By the above evidence of waveforms, it 
can be said that the modelling and simulation were done. Later the modelling of signals and corresponding elements has to be modelled 
and it should be simulated and implemented on an FPGA board which shows the virtual asset of a physical interlocking system. It 
should connect the physical system and virtual system by using interface protocol UART, which helps creating a digital twin for the 
railway’s entire interlocking system. Particularly in India, these initiatives are still in the exploratory stage and have not yet been 
carried out realistically. As a result, we took time to conduct research it thoroughly and develop a virtual model. According to data 
available on the previous train accidents, our current system needs to be automated in a way that is more effective, accurate, and fail- 
safe. 
We conclude that a Digital Twin-based system would prove to be more efficient in the current relay-based technology is used in 
India because the entire regulatory action would become automated. This would lower the time required to substitute the faulty relay 
efficiently and affordably.
