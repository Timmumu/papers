ABSTRACT
Digital twins (DTs) are increasingly applied throughout the life cycle of structures for health monitoring and 
decision-making. To meet real-time requirements, this paper presented an efficiency-enhanced DT framework 
based on an edge-cloud collaborative architecture and three innovations. First, a Python-based finite element 
(FE) analysis program with a precomputed stiffness matrix strategy significantly accelerated structural load 
identification and response computation. Second, a deviation severity coefficient with adaptive weighting 
effectively detected sensor faults and improved identification robustness. Third, a modified particle swarm 
optimization algorithm with population differentiation and local acceleration strategies further enhanced 
identification accuracy and efficiency. The system was validated in a bridge pier replacement project under five 
construction scenarios. The results demonstrated a sub-300 ms total response time, a 70-fold reduction in one- 
time FE computation, a 70 % decrease in optimization cost, stress prediction errors below 10%, and safety 
warnings for stress levels exceeding design values by over threefold.
1. Introduction
This section introduces the technical context required for under -
standing the proposed structural health monitoring (SHM) framework. It 
first discusses the fundamental principles and practical challenges of 
SHM, then outlines the motivations driving this study and summarizes 
its main contributions.
1.1. Background of structural health monitoring
Structural safety assurance necessitates a systematic evaluation of all 
foreseeable limit states and accidental risks during the design phase, 
encompassing the structure ’ s entire service life [ 1 , 2 ]. This approach 
requires rigorous deterministic and probabilistic analyses of extreme 
loading scenarios, such as peak operational loads, foundation displace -
ment thresholds, and accidental overload conditions [ 3 – 7 ]. Under these 
limit states, induced stresses and deformations must be maintained 
below both the ultimate material strength and permissible structural 
deformation limits to achieve quantified system reliability metrics. 
However, the following limitations significantly constrain the precision 
of existing methodologies. First, the structural system inherently di -
verges from its as-designed configuration due to material heterogeneity, 
coupled with performance variability, progressive degradation, and 
uncertainties emerging throughout construction and operational phases 
[ 8 – 11 ]. Applying idealized design models introduces inherent discrep -
ancies that critically undermine structural integrity assessments. Sec -
ond, boundary conditions such as soil-structure interactions resist 
precise numerical simulation due to their environmental sensitivity and 
temporal variability [ 12 – 14 ]. Consequently, the boundary conditions 
assumed during design-phase safety evaluations frequently diverge from 
field observations. Furthermore, critical scenarios often remain unad -
dressed in conventional design frameworks due to methodological lim -
itations, economic constraints, or the probabilistic nature of unforeseen 
extreme events [ 15 – 17 ]. As a result, the deployment of SHM systems 
throughout the structural life cycle has become imperative to mitigate 
risks arising from indeterminate material properties, fluctuating 
boundary constraints, and stochastic loading scenarios [ 18 , 19 ]. Real- 
time and continuous monitoring of loading spectrum evolution and

A SHM system typically comprises two core components: sensing 
networks and analytical diagnostics. It integrates sensor network mea -
surements of structural-environmental parameters with data-informed 
prognostics to evaluate real-time integrity and forecast evolutionary 
performance states [ 22 , 23 ]. Currently, the sensing network prioritizes 
specialized sensors and advanced transmission techniques to enable 
real-time state acquisition. For example, Trapani et al. [ 22 ] imple -
mented microcontroller-integrated piezoresistive ceramic sensors to 
directly quantify stress variations in masonry systems via electrome -
chanical coupling, circumventing conventional strain-derived stress 
computation. As for data transmission, Ma et al. [ 24 ] implemented a 
long range radio (LoRa) wireless ad hoc network to transmit sensor data 
from monitoring nodes to local terminals, with General Packet Radio 
Service (GPRS) networks relaying the data to a centralized service 
platform. Hu et al. [ 25 ] utilized HTTP web protocols over wireless local 
area networks (WLANs) to enable communication between sensors and 
computer networks. Abner et al. [ 26 ] adopted a Bluetooth Low Energy 
(BLE) mesh network to facilitate multi-hop communication between 
sensor nodes, relay nodes, and station nodes, leveraging self-healing 
capabilities for dynamic path optimization. Mukherjee et al. [ 27 ] 
employed 5G networks to transmit sensor data from edge devices to 
remote cloud servers. Xu et al. [ 28 ] developed a dynamic wireless sensor 
network (DWSN) using tree-topology routing with address reallocation 
mechanisms to adapt to structural movements.
The analytical diagnostics emphasizes surrogate model development 
and deployment, typically leveraging finite element modeling (FEM) as 
the predominant paradigm to balance computational efficiency with 
high-fidelity structural representation [ 29 , 30 ]. Structural integrity 
assessment is performed through systematic comparison of field- 
measured structural responses against model-generated predictions,

1.2. Motivation
As outlined above, SHM has evolved into a multidisciplinary field 
integrating hardware innovation with advanced system analytics. These 
advancements have progressively addressed the critical demands for 
real-time performance and accuracy in SHM systems. However, existing 
methodologies still face persistent limitations that arise from the 
inherent trade-off between accuracy and computational efficiency. As a 
result, monitoring performance remains suboptimal, underscoring 
research gaps that require systematic investigation and methodological 
innovation.
Dense sensor arrays with high sampling rates are always employed to 
achieve comprehensive structural characterization [ 21 ]. Bridge health 
monitoring systems, for instance, can routinely generate multi-gigabyte 
daily datasets through continuous sensing modalities, creating sub -
stantial challenges in data transmission bandwidth and computational 
resource allocation [ 37 ]. Although high-bandwidth communication 
channels are theoretically essential for maintaining data fidelity and 
supporting real-time analysis [ 38 ], practical deployment is often con -
strained by harsh environmental conditions and hardware limitations, 
which make such infrastructure difficult to implement in the field [ 39 ]. 
Furthermore, raw sensor outputs predominantly contain extraneous 
information unrelated to structural integrity assessment, necessitating 
advanced signal processing techniques (e.g., Fourier spectral analysis, 
wavelet transform, time-domain feature extraction) to distill physically 
meaningful indicators from the acquired datasets [ 25 , 37 , 40 ].
Data-driven surrogate models, particularly ML approaches, have 
gained traction as computationally efficient alternatives to FEM for real- 
time SHM in large-scale systems. Nevertheless, their practical imple -
mentation is constrained by three fundamental limitations: the sampling 
bias inherent in training datasets, the architectural constraints of sur -
rogate models, and the absence of mathematically rigorous frameworks

for quantifying error propagation and generalization boundaries. Cur -
rent implementations demonstrate acceptable accuracy within their 
trained parameter spaces but exhibit significant performance deterio -
ration under out-of-distribution (OOD) scenarios, particularly when 
confronted with uncharacterized loading patterns or emerging struc -
tural degradation modes [ 33 ]. Furthermore, architectural mismatch and 
data-model imbalance, which manifest as overfitting and representation 
gaps, remain poorly quantified when relying on conventional training- 
accuracy metrics [ 41 , 42 ]. Notably, the severe scarcity of anomaly data 
relative to normal operational measurements introduces intrinsic sam -
pling skewness, while the topological complexity of engineered systems 
compounds diagnostic ambiguity, frequently preventing precise 
subsystem-level anomaly attribution. Although domain-informed ML 
approaches (e.g., physics-informed neural networks, PINN) show 
promise in enhancing model generalization and robustness [ 43 , 44 ], 
FEM retains superior predictive fidelity and reliability for safety-critical 
engineering applications [ 45 , 46 ].
1.3. Contributions and outline
To address persistent challenges in reconciling computational effi -
ciency with diagnostic accuracy for real-time SHM, this study developed 
a DT framework integrating the following three core innovations: 
• Real-time FE execution : A Python-based FE program was devel -
oped and implemented to execute structural response analysis 
through cloud computing. Computational efficiency was enhanced 
through precomputed stiffness matrix and sparse matrix protocols, 
satisfying sub-second latency constraints for real-time safety 
assessments.
• Robust sensor fault detection : A deviation severity coefficient was 
formulated to detect abnormal sensors. Adaptive weighting was 
applied to balance heterogeneous sensor data, thereby improving the 
robustness of system identification.
• Efficient system identification : A modified particle swarm opti -
mization variant was designed to expedite system parameter iden -
tification, yielding times of convergence improvements without 
compromising precision for time-constrained diagnostics.
Although advanced hardware technologies such as edge-cloud inte -
gration, cloud computing, and 5G wireless networks are still employed 
in the proposed DT system for rapid data transmission and processing, 
the three innovations presented in this paper primarily target method -
ological advances in generic system simulation and identification. These 
contributions aim to provide adaptable improvements for future health 
monitoring tasks across different structural types, varying levels of 
complexity, and diverse hardware conditions. In addition to the funda -
mental concepts and recent advancements in SHM and DT presented in 
Section 1, the remainder of the paper is structured as follows: Section 2 
delineates the constituent modules of the DT framework while eluci -
dating technical implementations of three core contributions; Section 3 
demonstrates the implementation process and device configurations of 
the proposed system through an engineering application; Section 4 
conducts a comprehensive analysis of experimental findings, employing 
systematic comparison with conventional approaches to validate the 
enhanced performance of the developed strategies; and Section 5 syn -
thesizes principal conclusions and outlook for future research.
2. Methodology
This section systematically describes the functional architecture of 
the proposed DT framework, summarizing the roles, data flow, and in -
teractions among its core modules. These technical elements serve as the 
basis for the three methodological contributions that are elaborated in 
the subsequent three subsections.

2.2. Python-based finite element program with cloud computing
Conventional structural FE analysis predominantly relies on com -
mercial desktop-based softwares (e.g., ABAQUS, ANSYS, Midas Civil, 
etc.), necessitating manual or scripted data transfer for sensing inputs 
and analytical outputs. This workflow fragmentation introduces 
computational bottlenecks that critically compromise structural moni -
toring system automation and efficiency. While commercial platforms 
emphasize visualization capabilities, especially in modeling and analysis 
as the key distinguishing feature, such functionality becomes non- 
essential in well-constrained application scenarios (e.g., specific load- 
response solutions at predefined critical nodes). Furthermore, graph -
ical processing demands substantial GPU resources for rendering and 
computation, making visualization impractical for typical resource- 
constrained monitoring system architectures with limited computa -
tional and storage capacity. As illustrated in Fig. 2 , the developed FE 
program only preserves the formulation, assembly, and computation of 
stiffness and force matrices [ 47 ]. To overcome client-side constraints in 
data and workflow management, the Python-implemented system em -
ploys MySQL for database operations, enabling full cloud-based 
functionality.
To ensure modeling efficiency for both engineering application and 
subsequent projects, the preprocessing module incorporates the pre -
defined Element Library with common element types and shape func -
tions, the Material Library containing elastic moduli, densities, and 
Poisson ’ s ratios for diverse concrete and steel grades, and the Boundary 
Library defining various constraint and load conditions. The Python- 
based solver module enables flexible equation system resolution based 
on structural characteristics. For sparse global stiffness matrices, the 
SciPy sparse solver ( “ scipy.sparse.linalg.spsolve ” ) is employed, while 
models with limited degrees of freedom utilize the direct solver from 
NumPy ( “ numpy.linalg.solve ” ) to optimize computational efficiency. In 
the post-analysis module, Python APIs extract nodal and elemental 
displacement and stress responses, enabling specialized analyses such as 
staged construction simulation, load combination evaluation, and 
structural configuration optimization. Python ’ s interoperability facili -
tates seamless integration of analysis results with other digital systems, 
including SHM platforms and engineering information management 
frameworks. This flexibility supports cross-platform data sharing, sec -
ondary development, and collaborative workflows.
In addition to leveraging Python to achieve intensive FE analysis 
capabilities, the program employs two strategies to optimize SHM effi -
ciency. The first is a computation acceleration strategy based on the 
fixed stiffness matrix. In structural analysis, when the topology and 
geometric configuration of the structure remain unchanged, the global 
stiffness matrix theoretically remains constant across varying external 
loading conditions. By reusing the precomputed global stiffness matrix 
for multi-load case analyses, the program minimizes redundant element- 
level stiffness calculations and global matrix assembly processes, sub -
stantially accelerating real-time structural response evaluation. The 
second approach focuses on sparse matrix storage and in-memory 
computing. Given the high sparsity of the FE-derived global stiffness 
matrices in the engineering application, the program adopts the Coor -
dinate (COO) format to reduce storage requirements. Optimized data 
structures and storage schemes enable core computations, particularly 
the solution of large-scale linear systems, to be performed primarily in 
memory. This memory-centric strategy minimizes interactions with the 
hard disk, limiting them to necessary data output at the end of the 
analysis, thereby further reducing computational cycles and enhancing 
overall efficiency.

trained on representative FE simulation datasets, replace the full FE 
model during optimization [ 57 – 59 ]. Additionally, the standard PSO 
employs uniform behavioral patterns, update strategies, and weighting 
parameters across the swarm, potentially inducing premature conver -
gence to local optima, insufficient balance between global exploration 
and local exploitation, and suboptimal convergence rates [ 60 ].
To mitigate these limitations, this study proposed a modified PSO 
(Fast-PSO) algorithm integrating dynamic population differentiation 
with adaptive group allocation. Furthermore, high-potential solution 
subspaces (HPSS) are dynamically identified and modeled using inde -
pendent surrogate models, while direct mathematical optimization 
techniques are applied to accelerate solution convergence within these 
subspaces. As outlined in Algorithm 2 and Table 1 , particles are classi -
fied into three behavioral groups, namely exploratory, exploitative, and 
stagnant, following multiple iterations of the standard PSO framework. 
This classification employs the Exploration Dynamism Index as Eq. 7 in 
Table 1 , which quantifies convergence stagnation via the Euclidean 
distance between a particle ’ s current and historical best positions. 
Exploratory particles exhibiting high dynamism retain standard PSO 
update rules to intensively probe unexplored regions of the solution 
space. Exploitative particles with moderate dynamism leverage low- 
fidelity surrogate models (e.g., quadratic response surfaces) to map 
localized HPSS, enabling rapid mathematical optimization techniques 
(e.g., gradient-based convergence) within these refined regions. Stag -
nant particles demonstrating minimal dynamism trigger L ´evy-flight- 
aided relocation to escape local optima as Eq. 10 [ 61 ], redistributing 
them toward unexplored regions.
As formulated in Eq. 11, the algorithm monitors the evolution of the 
global best solution within the most recent five generations to determine 
whether the search process has stagnated, and adjusts the proportions of 
exploratory and exploitative particles accordingly. If no improvement is 
observed, the system identifies stagnation and increases the proportion 
(e.g., 5 %) of exploratory particles while reducing exploitative ones to 
enhance global search ability and escape local optima. Conversely, if 
improvement occurs, the system increases the proportion (e.g., 2 %) of 
exploitative particles to focus the search on promising regions and 
accelerate convergence. After the target proportions are set, particle 
classification in the current iteration is performed based on the

The proposed Fast-PSO systematically balances global exploration, 
local exploitation, and convergence recovery while preserving core PSO 
mechanics. Its innovations manifest in two primary mechanisms. First, 
dynamic particle categorization, guided by real-time convergence status 
and diversity feedback, enables efficient computational resource distri -
bution. This allows strategic prioritization of critical search behaviors 
across optimization phases: emphasizing exploration for rapid identifi -
cation of promising regions during initial stages, and shifting toward 
exploitation to accelerate convergence to precise solutions later. Such 
adaptive balancing mitigates redundant searches in low-potential re -
gions and prevents premature convergence from insufficient exploration 
or excessive exploitation, thereby significantly reducing high-quality 
solution discovery time and directly enhancing optimization speed.
Second, the dynamically constructed surrogate models for HPSS 
leverage available real-evaluated data points to form local approxima -
tions using explicit and tractable mathematical representations. This 
formulation enables the direct application of efficient mathematical 
optimization techniques to rapidly obtain optimal solutions, thereby 
bypassing computationally expensive iterative particle evaluations. The 
resulting local optimum provides guidance for Exploitation particles, 
significantly accelerating convergence within potential basins and 
playing a decisive role in global optimization efficiency.

3. Engineering application of the proposed digital twin
This section introduces the engineering application used to demon -
strate the implementation of the proposed DT framework. It presents the 
construction background, critical working scenarios, sensor deploy -
ment, and system configuration, forming the basis for the subsequent 
evaluation of the system ’ s operational performance.
3.1. Background of the application
In the newly constructed Chengdu – Chongqing Middle Line, the 
Jingkou Double-Track Bridge intersects the existing Xinjingkou Jialing 
River Bridge on the Shanghai – Chengdu Line via a grade-separated 
crossing. As shown in Fig. 3 , the two lines intersect at an angle of 
approximately 13
◦
, with an overlap length of 115 m. To minimize the 
longitudinal elevation of the new line, an underpass scheme was adop -
ted. The spatial overlap between the new and existing piers required the 
demolition of gravity piers No. 23 and No. 24 of the Xinjingkou Jialing 
River Bridge, which were replaced by portal piers to accommodate the 
new alignment. The entire construction process comprised over 20 tasks 
categorized into three phases: construction of the new portal piers, 
transfer of the existing bridge loads to these piers, and removal of the 
existing gravity piers.
This application focuses on five representative scenarios described in 
Table 2 . As illustrated in Fig. 4 , the steel crossbeam of the newly con -
structed portal pier rested on temporary steel tubular trestles (Trestle 
No. 1). The steel crossbeam bore the dead load transmitted from the 
main girder and underwent deformation. As a result, Trestle No. 1 was 
subjected to both the load transferred through the steel crossbeam and 
direct loading from the construction platform. The removal of existing 
pier caps required lateral displacement using winches mounted on 
sliding trestles (Trestle No. 2). As a result, Trestle No. 2 primarily 
resisted horizontal forces exerted by the winches while simultaneously 
supporting vertical loads from existing pier caps.
The construction process entailed significant complexity, tight 
scheduling, and elevated safety risks. Crucially, the trestles bore most 
structural loads, making their stability and safety paramount to project 
integrity. To ensure their structural integrity, stress sensors were 
installed at the most critical sections for real-time monitoring as illus -
trated in Fig. 5 , which were identified through prior analysis. However, 
deviations from design conditions, including varying mechanical and 
human-induced loads and boundary condition discrepancies, limit the 
reliability of localized monitoring. It motivated the full-field stress dis -
tribution analysis to comprehensively assess structural behavior in this 
application.

4. Results and discussion
This section comprehensively examines the performance of the 
proposed DT framework. First, the computational efficiency of the entire 
process, from data acquisition to stress distribution visualization, is 
quantitatively analyzed, with particular emphasis on evaluating the 
contribution of the two proposed strategies to improving overall effi -
ciency. Subsequently, the effectiveness of the proposed damaged sensor 
identification strategy is demonstrated. Finally, by comparing the stress 
data collected from the physical entity with those predicted by the DT 
under five different scenarios, the accuracy and comprehensiveness of 
the twin ’ s predictions, as well as its capability to identify potential 
structural risks, are validated.
4.1. Efficiency assessment
As shown in Table 4 , the total time required from data acquisition to 
global stress visualization across all five scenarios does not exceed 0.3 s 
(300 ms), demonstrating the real-time capability of the DT system and 
its compliance with the fundamental requirements for risk early- 
warning tasks in SHM [ 62 ]. It is noteworthy that the time spent on 
hardware and network-related operations accounts for less than 20 % of 
the total duration. This observation underscores the significance and 
necessity of the efficiency enhancements introduced in this paper for the 
remaining modules, particularly structural simulation and system 
identification, which together account for the majority of the compu -
tational time.
The generation and assembly of the stiffness matrix constitute the 
establishment of the structural FE model, enabling the calculation of 
structural responses under applied external loads. Fig. 7 illustrates the 
specific impact of different stiffness matrix generation methods on the 
execution time for structural stress distribution calculation, in which the 
stiffness matrix (i.e., the FE model) is required to be invoked once. The 
results demonstrate that directly calling the precomputed stiffness ma -
trix (requiring approximately 20 – 30 ms) reduced the execution time by 
up to 70 times compared to computing and calling the stiffness matrix 
(requiring approximately 1600 – 1900 ms). It can thus be anticipated that 
using precomputed stiffness matrices will lead to significant time sav -
ings in modules such as deviation severity determination of sensors and 
load identification, in which repeated invocation of the FE model is 
required. Given the assumptions adopted in this application, including 
material linearity, small deformations, and fixed boundary conditions, 
the use of this approach is well justified. For example, the temporary 
steel trestles examined here are expected to remain within the elastic 
range under service loads [ 63 , 64 ]. Moreover, Table 4 shows that the 
time required for global stress computation constitutes only 10 – 15 % of 
the total workflow duration, suggesting that the framework is likely to 
maintain satisfactory performance when applied to larger and more 
complex structures.

5. Conclusions
This paper presented an efficiency-enhanced digital twin (DT) sys -
tem tailored to the real-time requirements of structural health moni -
toring. A bridge pier replacement project was selected as an engineering 
application, where six pre-installed sensors were used to identify 
external forces and compute stress distributions of two steel trestles 
under five representative scenarios. The main conclusions are as follows: 
• The DT system adopted edge-cloud integration, cloud computing, 
and 5G mobile network at the hardware level, and employed a 
Python-based finite element (FE) program and swarm-intelligence- 
based optimization at the software level. The total time from data 
acquisition to stress visualization remained below 300 ms, demon -
strating strong real-time capability and suitability for risk warning in 
health monitoring.
• The developed FE program, combined with precomputed and fixed 
stiffness matrix, significantly accelerated load identification and 
global stress computation, reducing FE computational time to 1/70 
of the original.
• The proposed deviation severity coefficient with adaptive weighting 
accurately identified abnormal sensors, ensuring prediction- 
measurement stress errors below 10 % in most cases.
• The modified particle swarm optimization (Fast-PSO) algorithm, 
incorporating population differentiation and local acceleration, 
improved both accuracy and efficiency. It reduced computational 
time by approximately 70 % in the application and achieved up to a 
tenfold accuracy improvement over the standard PSO on 12 
CEC ’ 2022 benchmark problems.
• The DT system effectively identified critical stress regions, with 
predicted maximum stresses consistently exceeding those considered 
during design, in some cases by more than threefold.
Overall, this paper presented an effective solution for real-time 
monitoring of engineering structures and offered technical guidance 
for efficient simulation and system identification. While some proposed 
approaches were developed under certain simplifying assumptions, such 
as linear elasticity and small deformations, these do not diminish their 
practical relevance for most engineering applications. Future research 
will aim to further enhance the framework ’ s adaptability and compu -
tational robustness, enabling its extension to more complex scenarios, 
including systems with varying stiffness or nonlinear responses.

Appendix A. Validation of the finite element implementation 
and Fast-PSO performance
This appendix provides supplementary validation for the methods 
presented in this paper. The first part verifies the developed finite 
element program, while the second evaluates the performance of the 
proposed Fast-PSO algorithm.
A.1. Validation of the developed finite element program
This section validates the accuracy of the developed Python-based FE 
program. The verification case involves a half-through concrete-filled 
steel tubular (CFST) tied-arch bridge. As shown in Fig. 12 , the bridge has 
a total length of 1130 m, with a main span of 252 m, side spans of 60 m, 
and a width of 27 m. All structural components were assigned a density 
of 7.85 g/cm
3 
and an elastic modulus of 200 GPa.
The bridge model was constructed within the self-developed Python 
program following a standard FE modeling procedure, whose general 
framework is described in Section 2.2 "Python-based finite element 
program with cloud computing". The bridge geometry was first drafted 
in Autodesk CAD based on the design drawings. The Python program 
then extracted the three-dimensional coordinates of key feature points 
to generate primary nodes. Additional nodes were created by linear 
interpolation along the geometric centerlines of the main components 
(e.g., girders, arch ribs, and hangers). These continuous components 
were discretized into a series of connected three-dimensional beam el -
ements. To ensure computational accuracy, denser mesh divisions were 
applied in regions with expected high stress gradients, such as near 
joints and supports. The final FE model consisted of 1050 nodes and 
6300 degrees of freedom.
Fixed boundary constraints were applied at both bridge supports, 
and the self-weight of the structure was considered as the applied load. 
Before computation, the program automatically executed the load 
application process by traversing each beam element, calculating its 
volume and weight based on sectional properties and material density, 
and converting the weight into an equivalent set of nodal forces and 
moments acting at both ends of the element. Finally, all equivalent nodal 
loads were systematically assembled into the global load vector for 
subsequent structural response analysis.
The vertical nodal displacements under self-weight load were 
computed and compared with the results obtained from the commercial 
FE software Midas Civil. The results presented in Fig. 13 demonstrate 
close agreement between the two methods, confirming the accuracy of 
the developed program. Furthermore, the computational times were 
2.09 s for Midas Civil and 1.85 s for the developed program, reflecting 
the earlier conclusion that by retaining only core modeling and solving 
modules, the present FE program achieved higher computational effi -
ciency compared to commercial software. This advantage makes it a 
suitable tool for real-time SHM applications.
A.2. Evaluation of Fast-PSO with benchmark functions
This section conducts a comprehensive performance evaluation and 
comparison between Fast-PSO and the standard PSO using the CEC ’ 2022 
test suite, which consists of 12 rotated, shifted, hybrid, and composite 
minimization functions [ 65 ]. The aim is to demonstrate the computa -
tional efficiency improvements of Fast-PSO in a more standardized 
manner through widely recognized benchmark problems.
Table 5 presents the hyperparameter settings used for both the en -
gineering application and the benchmark problems. No hyperparameter 
optimization was performed; instead, classical parameter values 
commonly adopted in previous studies were directly employed [ 66 ]. 
The primary objective is to validate the performance improvements 
introduced by the Fast-PSO strategies, such as population differentiation 
and surrogate-model acceleration, over the standard PSO. Therefore, to 
ensure a fair and scientifically rigorous comparison, the principle of 
controlled variables was applied: the baseline parameters of Fast-PSO 
and the standard PSO were kept identical. This design ensures that 
any observed performance gains can be attributed to the proposed 
mechanisms rather than from any incidental advantage due to hyper -
parameter tuning. For the benchmark problems, both algorithms were 
evaluated and compared under two relatively high dimensions (D = 10 
and D = 20) in the following two aspects: 
• Fast-PSO was repeated 30 times for each of five different numbers of 
objective function evaluations (100, 150, 200, 250, and 300). The 
mean and confidence interval of the final objective function values, 
as well as the average execution time, were recorded. The results are 
presented in Fig. 14 .
• Both Fast-PSO and the standard PSO were repeated 30 times under 
approximately the same execution time (with 270 objective function 
evaluations for Fast-PSO and 2000 for the standard PSO). The mean, 
standard deviation, and best values of the final objective function 
values were obtained. The results are summarized in Table 6 .
The results in Fig. 14 demonstrate that the objective function value 
decreased as the number of function evaluations increased, indicating a 
correct optimization trend of the modified algorithm. The narrow con -
fidence intervals further reflect the high stability of its optimization 
performance. Due to strategies such as population differentiation which 
introduce higher complexity and more sophisticated algorithmic 
behavi
