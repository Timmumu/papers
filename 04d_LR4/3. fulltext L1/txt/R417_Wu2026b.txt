ABSTRACT
Large diameter shield construction (LDSS) safety is particularly important. To ensure safe excavation in LDSS 
projects, this paper proposed a digital twin (DT) platform integrated with knowledge-data driven method to 
achieve prediction and control of tunnel face stability (TFS) during LDSS construction. The DT platform enables 
acquisition of physical data and expert knowledge, facilitating bidirectional synchronization between physical 
entities and virtual counterparts. DT platform utilizes Bayesian Optimization (BO), Graph Convolutional 
Network (GCN), Bidirectional Long Short-Term Memory (BiLSTM) networks, and SHapley Additive exPlanations 
(SHAP), driven by physical data, together with expert knowledge, to achieve knowledge-data driven prediction 
and control of Tunnel Face Stability (TFS) during LDSS construction. A case study of Wuhan Metro Line 12 
construction demonstrates the platform's effectiveness, with key findings revealing: (1) The DT platform achieves 
accurate TFS prediction across six Geological types, showing average R
2 
values of 0.935 and root mean square 
error (RMSE) of 0.239. (2) Key construction parameters are identified through the knowledge-data driven 
method, including air chamber pressure, grouting pressure, advance rate, cutterhead rotation speed, and cut -
terhead torque. (3) The DT platform enables control of the TFS during LDSS construction by maintaining the 
slurry pressure (SP) within an optimal range. The DT platform developed in this study fills TFS research gaps in 
LDSS construction and provides valuable references for similar complex projects.
1. Introduction
With the rapid advancement of tunneling engineering technology, 
large-diameter slurry shield (LDSS) technology has emerged and gained 
widespread adoption ( Chen et al., 2024 , 2025 ; Wu et al., 2023 ). it has 
been applied in significant projects such as the Slender West Lake Tunnel 
in Yangzhou ( Chenn kui, 2014 ) and the Sanyang Road Tunnel in Wuhan 
( Wang, 2015 ). However, the diversity of the geological environments 
and the uncertainty of the construction scenarios have significantly 
increased the safety risks associated with LDSS operations ( Wu et al., 
2024 ), particularly the precise prediction and control of tunnel face 
stability (TFS) ( Xu et al., 2021 ). As the diameter of the shield increases, 
the geological structures encountered by the tunnel become more 
complex ( Li et al., 2017 ; Yu et al., 2023 ), and the frequency of 
encountering multilayered strata and composite strata with varying 
mechanical properties ( Chen et al., 2025 ), such as intermixed soft and 
hard clay and sand, significantly increases ( Cong et al., 2023 ). These 
challenges not only markedly increase the complexity of the excavation 
environment but also greatly increase the difficulty and intricacy of 
maintaining TFS. Therefore, the TFS is crucial for ensuring the safe 
construction of an LDSS.
Traditional research on TFS issues has been mostly centered around 
theoretical analysis, numerical simulation, and experimental studies. 
The recent progress in shield machine (SM) monitoring technology, 
coupled with the deep integration of data-driven methods, is now 
driving the field of shield tunnel construction towards a more advanced

This analysis highlights significant advancements over previous 
studies in the field but also notes certain limitations: 
(1) Theoretical analysis simplifies complex situations for easier un -
derstanding and computation, but this may reduce prediction 
accuracy by omitting some influencing factors.
(2) Numerical simulations need fine meshing and many iterations to 
meet high accuracy demands, which increases computational 
costs and makes large-scale simulations time-consuming, 
affecting their application efficiency in construction.
(3) Experimental studies in complex environments require substan -
tial investment in equipment and manpower, and they interrupt 
the construction process, leading to higher costs and safety risks.
(4) Traditional data-driven methods can build predictive models 
using field data but lack interpretability and struggle with highly 
complex parameter interactions and dynamic changes.
As a branch of data-driven methods, deep learning has been exten -
sively utilized by many scholars owing to its powerful data processing 
and analysis capabilities, as well as its capacity to reveal and capture 
complex nonlinear relationships within data ( Liu et al., 2021 ; Phoon and 
Zhang, 2023 ; Zhang et al., 2020 ).。However, existing research indicates 
that the construction parameters in SM construction exhibit spatiotem -
poral relationships; these relationships are difficult for conventional 
data-driven methods to capture and analyze, and their predictive per -
formances are reduced ( Fu et al., 2023 ; Zhang et al., 2024b ). Moreover, 
some existing studies mainly focus on the accurate prediction of TFS 
under traditional SM conditions; however, there is limited research on 
establishing suitable data-driven methods for precise prediction and 
control of TFS under LDSS conditions.
Among the various data-driven methods, LSTM is commonly used for 
long time series data but can only predict future moments based on past 
information ( Li et al., 2019 ). This is insufficient for LDSS construction 
parameters, which may also depend on future moments ( Chen et al., 
2022 ; Siami-Namini et al., 2019 ). To address this, BiLSTM, combining 
forward and backward LSTMs, is introduced to process LDSS tunnel 
construction data ( Graves and Schmidhuber, 2005 ). Additionally, TFS in 
LDSS construction is influenced by both past construction parameters 
and spatial relationships among parameters ( Fu et al., 2024 ). For 
example, different parameters are emphasized in soft soil and hard rock 
( Chen et al., 2022 ; Zhao et al., 2019 ). To capture these spatial re -
lationships, GCNs are introduced, representing data as graph structures 
and using the Laplacian matrix for convolution operations.
During the development of data-driven method ( Wu et al., 2026 ), 
hyperparameter optimization is a critical preliminary step aimed at 
identifying the hyperparameter configurations that achieve optimal 
model performance during both the training and testing phases ( Xu 
et al., 2025 ). While traditional optimization methods, such as manual 
operation (MO), grid search (GS), and random search (RS), provide re -
searchers flexibility in model tuning, they suffer from inefficiency, 
subjective bias, wasted computational resources, and inadequate opti -
mization ( Victoria and Maragatham, 2021 ). In contrast, Bayesian opti -
mization (BO) introduces a probabilistic acquisition function to 
efficiently explore the parameter space which significantly increases the 
likelihood of discovering the global optimum ( Chen et al., 2025 ). 
Therefore, to improve the prediction and control performance of 
data-driven methods, a hybrid BO-GCN-BiLSTM method is proposed.
Additionally, to address the research gap in key construction pa -
rameters for TFS during LDSS construction, this paper employs the 
SHapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP) to identify and analyze these key 
parameters. By utilizing this method, it is possible to accurately deter -
mine the construction parameters that significantly impact TFS ( Xu 
et al., 2026 ), thereby enabling precise control of TFS in LDSS 
construction.
Data-driven methods can improve prediction and control perfor -
mance but may lack reliability for practical engineering due to the 
absence of practical experience and expert knowledge ( Zhang et al., 
2024a ). Therefore, a knowledge-data driven method integrating expert 
knowledge is more suitable for guiding TFS during LDSS construction. 
This approach can select input parameters and identify key influencing 
factors based on expert knowledge and literature ( Su et al., 2025 ). 
However, most existing research focuses on developing prediction and 
control algorithms, with limited efforts on creating a unified platform 
for integrating knowledge-driven and data-driven methods with phys -
ical systems to enhance LDSS control and monitoring capabilities.
Digital twin (DT) technology integrates construction site data with 
virtual dynamic models to create a digital simulation platform, showing 
superior capabilities in tunnel operation and management ( Boje et al., 
2020 ; Yu et al., 2021 , 2023 ). Recent studies have applied the concept of 
DT to general shield tunnel excavation, enabling efficient remote 
interaction between physical and virtual entities by transforming 
physical equipment into digital models ( Pan and Zhang, 2021 ; Zhang 
et al., 2024b ). However, research on using DT platforms for data 
collection and TFS prediction and control in LDSS construction is 
limited. This paper proposes a DT platform that integrates data-driven 
methods (BO-GCN-BiLSTM and SHAP) with expert knowledge to form 
a knowledge-data driven method, enabling data acquisition and sup -
porting TFS prediction and control in LDSS construction.
In summary, the following issues motivate this paper: (1) how to 
analyze the spatiotemporal relationships among the various construc -
tion parameters affecting the TFS during LDSS construction; (2) what 
construction parameters are the key factors affecting the TFS during 
LDSS construction; and (3) how to implement prediction and control of 
TFS in LDSS construction. To address these issues, a knowledge-data 
driven method is developed for predicting and controlling TFS in LDSS

2. Methodology
To enable prediction and control of the TFS during LDSS construc -
tion, a DT platform has been developed. This platform includes a virtual 
twin layer with a virtual LDSS model, a data-driven module, and a re -
ality service layer. Specifically, the data-driven module integrates BO, 
GCN, and BiLSTM to capture the spatiotemporal relationships of shield 
construction parameters. Additionally, the reality service layer facili -
tates prediction, SHAP for key construction parameters, and control of 
TFS during LDSS construction using the knowledge-data driven method. 
Fig. 1 illustrates the framework of the DT platform.
2.1. Virtual twin layer
The establishment of the virtual twin layer is a core component of the 
DT platform. It achieves digital mapping and real-time interaction with 
the LDSS physical entity through high-fidelity modeling of the LDSS 
virtual model and multidimensional construction data fusion. This layer 
includes a data acquisition module and a virtual model.
During the LDSS tunnel construction process, the digital twin (DT) 
platform utilizes two types of data sources: physical data and expert 
knowledge ( Li et al., 2025 ). The former consists of real-time and his -
torical data collected from the construction site and the shield machine, 
including ring numbers, cutter head speed, cutter head torque, and 
tunneling speed; the latter primarily relies on experts who, based on 
their expert knowledge and relevant literature ( Chen et al., 2024 ), guide 
the selection of key parameters, thereby enabling 
knowledge-data-driven methods for TFS prediction and control during 
LDSS construction. These two data categories jointly support moni -
toring, prediction, and control functionalities.
However, they can only provide a static description of the con -
struction conditions and are unable to reflect the dynamic evolution of 
the LDSS – tunnel interaction in real time. To address this limitation, the 
platform establishes a virtual model based on the physical entities, fully 
digitizing the LDSS and tunnel system, and continuously updates it with 
real-time data. Interaction between the physical and virtual systems can 
be enabled through signal transmission via the Internet of Things (IoT) 
and 5th generation of cellular networks (5G) networks ( Zhang et al., 
2024b ). Field data from the physical LDSS are uploaded via signals to 
the project manager's online DT platform. The platform then performs 
key parameter prediction and control based on the virtual LDSS model, 
and the resulting instructions are transmitted back through signals to the 
physical LDSS on-site, guiding the operator's actions ( He et al., 2024 ). 
This bidirectional data flow establishes a closed-loop interaction be -
tween the physical entity and its virtual counterpart, as illustrated in 
Fig. 2 .

2.2.2. LDSS construction parameter selection
In the knowledge-data-driven method of the DT platform, the iden -
tification of input and output parameters is crucial for achieving reliable 
TFS prediction and control during LDSS construction. These parameters 
are determined collaboratively by experts based on their knowledge, 
LDSS operators, and project managers within the DT platform, with the 
parameter selection interface shown Fig. 4 . Notably, an intrinsic rela -
tionship exists between slurry pressure (SP) and TFS during LDSS 
operation ( Hou et al., 2022 ). The slurry serves not only to transport 
excavated soil but also acts as a pressure medium to balance the lateral 
earth and water pressures on the excavation face ( Wu et al., 2024 ). 
Additionally, it forms a protective filter cake that helps prevent face 
instability and collapse. Therefore, SP is established as the key output 
target in the TFS prediction model.
LDSS construction parameters are transmitted to the virtual twin 
layer via CSV data files from the LDSS monitoring system, containing 
various construction parameters. In this paper, the representative con -
struction parameters are selected based on previous research ( Cao et al., 
2023 ; Wang et al., 2021b ; Zhonghai et al., 2019 ; Zhou et al., 2023 ) and 
expert knowledge from virtual twin layer. Construction parameters 
chosen as inputs for the data-driven method include the air cushion

2.2.3. BO-GCN-BiLSTM construction
2.2.3.1. Bayesian optimization algorithm. BO is a sequential strategy for 
hyperparameter search that employs a probabilistic model to guide the 
search process ( Cao et al., 2024 ), primarily consisting of two essential 
elements: a probabilistic surrogate model and an acquisition function for 
guiding parameter selection ( Lei et al., 2021 ). As discussed in the 
introduction, traditional hyperparameter tuning methods often lack 
flexibility and suffer from high computational costs ( Liu et al., 2024 ), 
limitations that become more pronounced when applied to complex 
data-driven methods. Furthermore, given the diverse geological 
conditions encountered during LDSS construction, the hyperparameters 
of data-driven models require frequent adaptation ( Liu et al., 2023 ). 
Therefore, integrating Bayesian optimization is essential to enhance 
both the predictive performance and computational efficiency of 
data-driven approaches ( Xu et al., 2025 ). Gaussian process regression 
(GPR) is the most widely adopted probabilistic model in BO due to its 
high flexibility and strong generalization capability. The GPR model is 
expressed in Eq. (4) ( Mahmoodzadeh et al., 2022 ; Yang et al., 2023 ):

2.2.3.4. BO-GCN-BiLSTM. This paper propose a BO-GCN-BiLSTM 
method that integrates BO, GCN, and BiLSTM networks, with the aim 
to optimize the accuracy of TFS prediction; in particular, in parsing the 
spatiotemporal relationship among the LDSS construction parameters, 
our method demonstrates superior performance. In this study, a two- 
layer GCN is configured with 64 and 32 neurons in the first and sec -
ond convolutional layers, respectively, using the Tanh activation func -
tion. A three-layer BiLSTM is employed with 128, 64, and 32 
bidirectional neurons in each layer, respectively. The sequence length is 
determined based on the length of the time series collected under 
different geological conditions, and the Tanh activation function is also 
applied. The initial learning rate for both models is set to 0.001, serving 
as input for the BO process. Fig. 7 shows the construction logic of the BO- 
GCN-BiLSTM method.

2.3. Reality service layer
The established data-driven module within the DT platform can be 
further extended to provide reliable services for LDSS tunnel excavation 
( Cao et al., 2025 ). The reality service layer is a crucial component of the 
DT platform, with its core function being the integration of the virtual 
simulation capabilities of the virtual twin layer with the actual opera -
tional requirements of the LDSS physical entity. This enables monitoring 
of LDSS construction and prediction and control of TFS.
In the context of prediction and control of TFS during LDSS con -
struction, the reality service layer integrates physical data and expert 
knowledge to determine the input and output parameters for TFS pre -
diction. It constructs a knowledge-data driven method based on BO, 
GCN, and BiLSTM. This method leverages BO to optimize hyper -
parameters, thereby enhancing prediction accuracy and generalization 
capability. Simultaneously, it employs SHAP and expert knowledge to 
select critical parameters, ensuring that the model accurately identifies 
key construction parameters influencing TFS.
This process, based on the knowledge-data driven method, not only 
achieves prediction and control of TFS but also provides scientific 
guidance for adjusting the construction parameters of LDSS through 
feedback mechanisms. The training and evaluation of the BO-GCN- 
BiLSTM model, as well as the control flow for TFS, are described below.

payoff using Shapley values. This additive explanatory model is

2.3.3. TFS control strategy
To accurately simulate the control process of TFS, this paper in -
troduces a knowledge-data driven method based control strategy in the 
DT platform, where the latest control iteration results replace historical 
data, ensuring each iteration is based on the outcomes of the previous 
regulation. The main steps are as follows: 
a. Determine Control Objectives: By consulting relevant literature and 
expert knowledge within the virtual twin layer, and integrating 
practical construction requirements and standards, establish the 
reasonable range for control objectives in actual construction.
b. Identify Key Parameters: Experts utilize their own expert knowledge 
within the virtual twin layer and SHAP, determine the key con -
struction parameters and their adjustment ranges, which serve as 
input parameters for TFS control.
c. Control Iteration: Utilize physical data from the virtual twin layer 
and the data-driven module to perform control iterations, with each 
iteration result updating and replacing historical data.
d. Stop Condition: When the control objectives fall within the pre -
defined reasonable range, the iteration process stops.
This knowledge-data driven method not only enhances the timeli -
ness and adaptability of the prediction model but also provides a solid 
foundation for continuous control of LDSS construction safety and 
excavation efficiency. The specific control flow is illustrated in Fig. 8 .
3. Case study
3.1. Case background and DT platform interface
This paper is based on a section of the longest metro loop in Asia - 
Wuhan Metro Line 12 between Lingwucun Station and Guobo Center 
South Station; in this section, the Yangtze River tunnel segment is 2610 
m long and is excavated by two Φ 12.56-m LDSS machines. The tunnel is 
designed with a single-bore double-track layout and features an outer 
segment diameter of 12.1 m, an inner diameter of 11.1 m, a segment 
thickness of 0.5 m, and a ring width of 2 m. During shield advancement, 
all relevant construction parameters are recorded by the shield data 
acquisition system. Fig. 9 (a and b,c,d) shows the Location Map of Wuhan 
Metro Project, Construction site photos, Interior view of the tunnel, and 
Data acquisition, respectively. Fig. 10 shows the geological conditions 
for LDSS construction. And Fig. 11 shows the DT platform interface of 
LDSS construction.
3.2. Data collection
To address the inadequacies of traditional methods in the TFS during 
LDSS construction research and to achieve effective TFS prediction and 
control, the proposed knowledge-data driven method requires pre -
processing of the collected data before training. The preprocessing steps 
include data cleaning, normalization, and reconstruction ( Chen et al., 
2025 ). The purpose of this process is to remove invalid and outlier data 
from the raw data. Normalization is applied to eliminate the impact of 
outlier sample data on model training. Finally, the data are recon -
structed to prepare them for model input. All data used in this paper 
were obtained from the monitoring system, primarily comprising con -
struction parameters related to rings 30 to 471. These data encompass 
complex and diverse geological conditions, which can be broadly cate -
gorized into six geological types. In this section, Geological type 1 to 
Geological type 6 are selected for TFS prediction. The property param -
eters of each Geological type as shown in Table 3 . The construction 
parameters for each Geological type are described in Table 4 . The di -
vision of datasets for each geological type is illustrated in Fig. 12 .
3.3. Knowledge-data driven method based TFS prediction
Based on the reality service layer of the DT platform, the LDSS 
construction process utilizes the knowledge-data driven method, 
including BO-GCN-BiLSTM method, physical data and expert knowl -
edge. First, BO is used for hyperparameter tuning. Then, time steps for 
the BO-GCN-BiLSTM method are set, with each time step being pre -
dicted individually. The optimal time step is selected based on a com -
bination of prediction accuracy and operational efficiency.

prediction accuracy while maintaining stable generalization error across 
six distinct geological data distributions — attributable to the topological 
robustness of GCN and the bidirectional temporal modeling of 
BiLSTM — thereby significantly reducing both retraining overhead and 
inference time.
