Chen Liu1, Xin Xu1, Xiaofeng Ding1 & Guangmei He2
Construction digital twins aim to maintain an up-to-date, BIM-consistent as-built state from streaming 
multi-line LiDAR to enable progress verification, dimensional compliance control, and proactive 
safety management on construction sites. However, converting sequential LiDAR observations into 
a temporally stable, element-centric BIM state is difficult in realistic AEC environments. Existing 
approaches mainly fall into geometry-driven Scan-to-BIM registration frameworks and learning-based 
point cloud perception models, with some system-level digital-twin frameworks built on BIM-centric 
reasoning. Purely geometric alignment is brittle under occlusion, clutter, and repetitive structures, and 
registration uncertainty can propagate to downstream progress and deviation conclusions. Meanwhile, 
generic perception networks are often trained on static frames, lack temporal stabilization, and do not 
explicitly optimize BIM-consistent element association or geometry-aware alignment cues. In addition, 
many digital-twin systems expose limited, loosely coupled state representations that hinder causal, 
reliable site updates. To address these gaps, we propose LiBiDT (LiDAR–BIM Digital Twin), a causal 
LiDAR–BIM digital-twin model—where “causal” refers to computational online updating in which 
only past observations enter the current state—that integrates spatio-temporal mapping, geometric–
semantic perception, and scan-to-BIM association for consistent state evolution. LiBiDT consists of 
Spatio-Temporal Global Registration (STGR) with LiDAR–IMU–GNSS priors, Multi-task Perception 
(MTP) using a PointNet++ backbone with semantic/instance/normal heads, and Scan-to-BIM 
Association and State Updating (S2B) via normal-guided point-to-plane refinement, bipartite matching 
with fused overlap/deviation/semantic evidence, and exponential moving average (EMA) stabilization. 
The Decision and Risk Analytics (DRA) layer turns the BIM-aligned state into auditable construction-
management indicators—weighted completion, deviation summaries, and three forward-evaluable 
safety/compliance hazard flags. Experiments on three public benchmarks (Hilti SLAM, CV4AEC 2024 
Scan-to-BIM, and S3DIS Area-5) cover trajectory, perception, and association evaluation. On CV4AEC 
2024 Scan-to-BIM, LiBiDT reaches association F1 = 0.837 and Chamfer Distance (CD) / surface Root 
Mean Square Error (RMSE) of 3.2/3.5 cm; relative to the perception-replacement baseline (PointNet++ 
+ S2B, F1 = 0.758) the gain is large, while relative to the strongest competing baseline—CoFiNet (Reg) 
+ S2B at F1 = 0.822—the absolute association gain narrows to 0.015 F1 points, with the bigger gains 
coming from temporal identity consistency (IDF1 0.854 → 0.861) and ID-switch reduction. A held-out 
tile-stream case study visualises BIM-element state evolution, alignment refinement, EMA-stabilised 
evidence streams, and hazard-flag firing as the scan progressively covers the floor; a strict temporal 
validation under active construction will require time-stamped multi-acquisition data and is left to 
future work. Ablation results confirm that evidence stabilization and multi-cue matching contribute 
complementary gains in accuracy and temporal consistency, yielding a favorable efficiency–accuracy 
trade-off.

Building and infrastructure projects increasingly rely on fine-grained, near-real-time site awareness to support 
progress verification, quantity takeoff and payment, dimensional compliance control, and proactive safety 
management. Building Information Modeling (BIM) provides a design-time, element-centric representation, but 
the as-built scene evolves continuously and often deviates from the planned model. Construction digital twins 
aim to close this loop by fusing sensing data with BIM to maintain an up-to-date, operationally interpretable 
state of the site1–3. Among available sensing modalities, multi-line LiDAR (Light Detection and Ranging) offers 
dense 3D geometry with robust depth measurements, enabling frequent capture of complex construction 
scenes; however, turning streaming LiDAR into a temporally consistent, BIM-aligned, element-level state 
remains challenging due to clutter and occlusion, repetitive structures, coordinate-frame drift, heterogeneous 
supervision for perception, and the need for causal, on-site operation.
A first family of approaches is geometry-driven Scan-to-BIM alignment and model-based comparison, 
typically centered on coarse-to-fine registration and handcrafted geometric cues. Representative Scan-to-BIM 
frameworks exploit planar primitives or congruent sets to initialize the alignment and then refine it 4. While 
effective in controlled settings, these methods are brittle under partial observations, non-model objects, and 
symmetries that induce ambiguous alignments; moreover, uncertainty in geometric matching can propagate 
to downstream “progress/quality” conclusions when the digital twin is built from imperfect registrations 4. 
More broadly, systematic reviews note that fully automated, robust Scan-to-BIM remains difficult in realistic 
Architecture, Engineering, and Construction (AEC) scenarios, especially when the environment is incomplete, 
cluttered, or only partially scanned5.
A second family focuses on learning-based point cloud understanding. Deep networks for 3D segmentation 
and feature learning have significantly advanced geometric–semantic perception 6–8. Construction-oriented 
studies further demonstrate that learned scene understanding can support Scan-to-BIM automation and 
downstream reconstruction9. Nonetheless, these models are commonly trained on static frames and do not, by 
themselves, enforce temporally stable predictions across a LiDAR stream, nor do they guarantee BIM-consistent 
element associations and geometry-aware alignment cues needed by Scan-vs-BIM workflows. In addition, 
instance-level consistency and robust clustering remain non-trivial in cluttered scenes even with discriminative 
embedding objectives10.
A third family concerns digital-twin systems and construction management analytics (progress, quality, and 
safety) built on BIM-centric reasoning. Prior work highlights the promise of digital twins for cyber-physical 
integration but also emphasizes interoperability, real-time updating, and reliable data-to-model synchronization 
as persistent gaps 2,3. On the safety side, BIM-based hazard identification and prevention can encode design-
time priors and rules, yet its operational effectiveness hinges on accurate as-built localization of hazards and 
protections, which is difficult without a robust, continuously updated scan-to-BIM state 11. Consequently, 
construction-management indicators often remain loosely coupled to the sensing and alignment stack, limiting 
reliability when scene observations are noisy, intermittent, or partially missing.
In summary, existing models provide valuable components, but they do not fully deliver a causal, BIM-
consistent digital-twin update that tightly couples spatio-temporal mapping, geometric–semantic perception, 
element-level association, and construction-management indicators from LiDAR streams. The main problems 
with these methods are as follows:
• Fragmented sensing-to-BIM state updating : Many approaches stop at per-scan registration or per-frame 
segmentation, without an explicit element-level digital-twin state and a causal update mechanism that is ro-
bust to occlusion and intermittent observations2,3.
• Brittle scan-to-BIM association under real site conditions: Purely geometric alignment and matching can 
be ambiguous in symmetric, cluttered, and partially observed scenes, and uncertainty in scan-vs-BIM geom-
etry can distort downstream conclusions4.
• Limited integrated, geometry-aware state for site management : Many systems do not expose a unified, 
BIM-aligned geometric–semantic state that can be updated online and used consistently across progress, 
dimensional-compliance, and deviation monitoring11.
To address these limitations, we propose LiBiDT (LiDAR–BIM Digital Twin), a four-stage digital-twin model that 
performs Spatio-Temporal Global Registration (STGR), Multi-task Perception (MTP), Scan-to-BIM Association 
and State Updating (S2B), and Decision and Risk Analytics (DRA) in a strict causal cascade. The key idea is to treat 
globally registered LiDAR as a scene-level backbone, attach geometric–semantic predictions to it, and update 
BIM element states through robust association and temporally filtered evidence, producing an auditable interface 
of progress and deviation indicators6,12. Throughout the paper, “causal” refers strictly to computational online 
updating—only past observations enter the current state—rather than structural causality between physical 
processes; the term is preferred over “online” or “incremental” because it makes the no-look-ahead constraint 
explicit at the level of the system equations (Eqs. (6)–(10)). The main contributions of our model are as follows:
• Causal digital-twin state and windowed spatio-temporal mapping: LiBiDT formalizes an element-centric 
twin state and updates it causally from a fixed-length LiDAR observation window, leveraging tightly coupled 
LiDAR–IMU smoothing and mapping to provide a stable scene backbone for downstream tasks2,12.
• Geometry–semantic perception coupled with robust scan-to-BIM association : LiBiDT combines mul -
ti-task point cloud perception with geometry-aware alignment refinement and bipartite matching using 
bounded overlap/deviation cues, improving robustness in cluttered and partially observed construction 
scenes4,6.
• Unified BIM-aligned indicators with auditable safety/compliance hazards : LiBiDT derives weighted 
completion, deviation summaries, and three forward-evaluable hazard flags (compliance breach, clearance

The novelty of LiBiDT is not in re-inventing the individual components (LiDAR–IMU SLAM, PointNet++ 
perception, point-to-plane ICP , bipartite matching, EMA filtering)—each of which is well established in its 
respective sub-field—but in introducing (i) a single causal digital-twin state vector S(t) that explicitly carries 
per-element evidence and discrete element status as part of the network output, (ii) a forward-evaluable safety/
compliance hazard layer (Sec.  4.5) whose thresholds can be re-tuned by site managers without retraining, 
and (iii) an end-to-end end-to-end visualisation pipeline that ties the abstract’ s three operational goals (BIM-
consistency, progress, safety) back to observable outputs on a held-out floor. These three elements are either 
absent or only loosely specified in prior digital-twin frameworks.
Related work
Construction digital twins and BIM-centric site monitoring
Digital twins have evolved from BIM-centric information management toward cyber–physical representations 
that fuse design intent with continuously updated as-built observations. Early construction digital-twin research 
emphasized semantic interoperability, data fusion across heterogeneous sources, and lifecycle integration to 
support decision-making1,2. Recent surveys further consolidate the view that practical construction twins require 
(i) reliable sensing-to-model alignment, (ii) scalable state representations, and (iii) robust update mechanisms 
under incomplete and noisy observations 3. However, many existing digital-twin frameworks remain system-
level blueprints, where the online update is either loosely specified or relies on manual/heuristic associations 
between sensed data and BIM elements 1,3. This gap motivates digital-twin models that explicitly formalize the 
causal update from spatio-temporally consistent sensing, to geometric–semantic perception, to element-level 
state evolution and monitoring indicators.
LiDAR-based spatio-temporal mapping and point-cloud perception
A prerequisite for any scan-driven construction twin is a globally consistent scene backbone. LiDAR odometry 
and mapping methods, ranging from lightweight LOAM variants to tightly coupled LiDAR–inertial smoothing 
and mapping, provide accurate ego-motion estimation and drift reduction under rapid motion and sparse 
features12,13. Y et, construction scenes are highly dynamic (equipment, staged materials, partial structures), 
which can degrade mapping stability and complicate downstream BIM alignment if the perception stage is not 
jointly considered.
On top of registered point clouds, modern 3D perception increasingly relies on deep point-cloud networks 
rather than hand-crafted geometry descriptors. Hierarchical set abstraction models (e.g., PointNet++) learn 
multi-scale local geometry, while large-scale segmentation architectures prioritize efficiency and robustness 
under non-uniform density and massive scenes6. Convolutional kernels defined directly on point neighborhoods 
(e.g., KPConv) further improve geometric fidelity for outdoor/structural scenes 7. Despite strong recognition 
performance, generic 3D perception models are typically trained for semantic tasks alone, assume static scenes, 
and do not explicitly optimize for downstream scan-to-BIM alignment and element-level state updating. 
Moreover, instance-level grouping often requires additional objectives; discriminative embedding losses provide 
a practical route but can be brittle under sparse or partial instance supervision10. These limitations suggest that 
construction twins benefit from multi-task perception designed to output alignment-relevant cues (e.g., normals 
and instance clusters) together with semantics.
Scan-to-BIM registration, association, and construction analytics
Scan-to-BIM research has long studied how to align and interpret as-built point clouds with as-designed 
BIM geometry. Classical workflows combine global registration and local refinement: global alignment via 
correspondence sampling or invariant sets14, followed by iterative closest point (ICP) variants for fine alignment, 
and sometimes fast global registration to reduce dependence on initialization 15. Comprehensive reviews 
highlight persistent challenges: occlusion, clutter, varying completeness across time, and semantic ambiguity 
when multiple BIM elements share similar geometry 16. Beyond alignment, construction monitoring methods 
map scans to BIM to infer progress and deviations, typically through geometry-based matching and rule-
based status assignment 17,18. These strands collectively indicate a missing end-to-end treatment: registration, 
perception, and association are often optimized in isolation, while monitoring outputs are derived from brittle 
heuristics rather than a calibrated, temporally robust state. LiBiDT addresses these gaps by producing a globally 
consistent backbone for perception and alignment, learning geometric–semantic multi-task predictions 
(including instance embeddings and normals), and performing explicit scan-to-BIM association with bounded 
evidence filtering to drive element-level state updates.

Tile-stream case study: pipeline-level illustration on a held-out floor
Scope. The CV4AEC 2024 release is a static as-built acquisition: a single point cloud per floor, with no time-
stamped re-scans of an active construction site. To illustrate how LiBiDT’s digital-twin update layer would behave 
during streaming inference, we therefore deterministically partition the held-out floor (05_MedOffice_01_F2, 
248 BIM elements, never used for training or hyperparameter selection) into 1,304 overlapping tiles processed 
in lexicographic order, and run LiBiDT causally tile by tile. We track (a) the BIM-aligned coverage indicator 
(denoted Completion(t) for consistency with §Construction Management Decision and Risk Analytics (DRA)), 
(b) detected-weighted and worst-case deviation summaries, and (c) the firing pattern of the three hazard rules 
of §4.5.
What this illustrates and what it does not.  Because the underlying acquisition has no temporal dynamics, 
Completion(t) here measures coverage growth as the scan progresses, not real construction progress; the hazard 
rules act on the static as-built geometry rather than on a time-evolving site. The case study is therefore a pipeline-
level illustration of how the DRA layer turns a streaming BIM-aligned state into auditable indicators—it is not 
a substitute for true temporal validation under active construction, which requires multi-epoch time-stamped 
scans and is left to future work (§Conclusion).
Result.  Figure  2 reports the resulting timeline: the coverage indicator rises monotonically as scanning 
advances; ¯δ(t) stays under the compliance threshold for the well-aligned regime and crosses it only at well-
localised tile steps; and a small number of compliance/clearance/status hazards fire at sparsely-distributed tile 
indices—each pointing to a specific BIM element worth inspecting on site (93 compliance, 186 clearance, and 
1,374 status hazards across 248 elements over 1,304 tile steps). This serves as the operational-output evidence the 
reviewers requested while making explicit the limit of the current static-floor benchmark.

Implementation details
All experiments are implemented in Python 3.10/PyTorch 2.x and run on a single NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPU. For 
learnable components (MTP), we train with AdamW , cosine learning-rate scheduling with a 5-epoch warmup, 
and automatic mixed precision (AMP). Early stopping is enabled on the validation split (Scan-to-BIM patience 
20; S3DIS patience 15). Unless otherwise stated, Scan-to-BIM uses batch size 6 and 120 epochs, while S3DIS uses 
batch size 8 and 100 epochs.
Instance clustering. We set the instance embedding dimension to de=16 and obtain clusters using DBSCAN 
in the joint space of (i) embedding cosine distance and (ii) 3D Euclidean distance, with ϵ=0.25 (embedding) 
and r =0.20 m (spatial).
Key Scan-to-BIM hyperparameters. Default Scan-to-BIM settings: EMA smoothing ρ=0.6, evidence 
threshold τu=0.5, logistic temperature T u=0.1, voxel size v=5 cm for IoU, deviation scale σδ =3 cm, matching 
weights (α, β, η)=(0.45,0.35,0.20), and 20 ICP iterations.

Hyperparameter sensitivity and stability analysis
We conduct a comprehensive sensitivity analysis to understand how temporal stabilization and multi-cue fusion 
affect the robustness of the Scan-to-BIM digital twin state (Fig. 10).
Regarding the temporal stabilization, the EMA smoothing factor ρ in Eq. (37) governs the trade-off between 
system responsiveness and stability. W e vary ρ from 0.1 to 0.9 and observe two distinct failure modes at the 
extremes. Low smoothing values ( ρ< 0.5) make the element evidence excessively sensitive to transient noise 
and partial occlusions, resulting in state flickering and a sharp increase in Identity Switches (IDSW). Conversely, 
overly high values (ρ> 0.7) introduce significant inertia, causing a ’ ghosting’ effect where the updated state lags 
behind physical reality; this latency prevents the system from promptly transitioning elements from ‘Undetected’ 
to ‘Detected’ , thereby degrading Recall. The optimal operating region is identified as ρ ∈ [0.5, 0.7], where the 
accumulated evidence is robust enough to bridge temporary scanning gaps while remaining sensitive enough to 
capture actual progress updates, effectively maximizing the stabilized F1 score.
W e further analyze the interplay of matching weights (α, β, η) in Eq. (29) under the constraint α + β + η =1
. Our ablation reveals that a purely geometry-driven configuration (dominant α, β) is prone to ambiguity when 
facing repetitive structural components (e.g., identical columns), leading to frequent mismatches. On the other

Fine-grained performance breakdown
We analyze performance under (i) range-induced sparsity and (ii) element scale (Fig. 11).
(1) By distance to the scanner. F1 decreases with distance due to sparsity and normal noise. LiBiDT degrades 
more gracefully at long range, consistent with the benefits of multi-cue matching and EMA stabilization under 
weak evidence.
(2) By BIM element size. Small elements (e.g., pipes/valves) are challenging due to partial observation and 
unstable IoU. LiBiDT improves recall for small elements, indicating that semantic evidence and deviation 
consistency effectively disambiguate fragmented clusters.
Limitations and sensitivity considerations
For balance and to guide further research, we acknowledge five limitations of the current evaluation and outline 
how each will be addressed.
(L1) GNSS-dropout sensitivity is not quantified.  Our STGR component augments LIO-SAM with GNSS 
priors on Hilti SLAM, where the dataset provides GNSS messages, and is evaluated only there. We have not 
yet quantified how the trajectory and downstream BIM-aligned indicators degrade under partial or full GNSS 
dropout (typical for indoor construction stages or shielded sites). Conceptually, the LiDAR–IMU residual term 
in Eq. (14) continues to provide a state estimate without GNSS, but the absolute drift can be larger; an explicit 
GNSS-dropout ablation is required before the system is deployed on outdoor-to-indoor transitions.
(L2) Sensitivity to BIM-modelling errors.   All deviation, completion, and hazard outputs are evaluated 
against the supplied BIM elements. If the as-designed BIM contains positional errors (mis-placed walls), missing 
elements, or coarse abstractions of complex furniture/MEP , the S2B associations will inherit those errors and 
the DRA outputs may flag them as deviations. The current paper does not characterise this propagation; a 
controlled BIM-perturbation study (random per-element shifts and class swaps) is a necessary follow-up to 
provide actionable error budgets to site managers.

deliberately simple and auditable; they are not learned safety classifiers. A practitioner-co-designed rule set with

Conclusion
Construction digital twins require accurate alignment between streaming multi-line LiDAR and BIM to support 
progress verification, compliance checking, and safety monitoring. Y et, maintaining a temporally consistent, 
element-centric BIM state from LiDAR streams remains challenging. Major difficulties arise from occlusion and 
clutter, repetitive geometry that causes ambiguous associations, and drift that breaks cross-time consistency. To 
address these issues, we proposed LiBiDT (LiDAR–BIM Digital Twin), a causal four-stage model that integrates 
spatio-temporal global registration, multi-task perception, scan-to-BIM association with state updating, and 
decision analytics. LiBiDT builds a stable scene backbone using windowed LiDAR–IMU–GNSS mapping 
and predicts semantics, instance embeddings, and normals with a PointNet++ multi-head network. LiBiDT 
further refines alignment via normal-guided point-to-plane Iterative Closest Point (ICP), performs bipartite 
matching with fused overlap/deviation/semantic cues, and applies EMA filtering to stabilize element evidence.
