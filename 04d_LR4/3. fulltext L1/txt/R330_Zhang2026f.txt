ABSTRACT 
It is crucial to assess the structural condition of reinforced concrete (RC) structures to ensure their 
functionality and safety; however, accurate condition assessment remains challenging owing to uncer -
tainties in geometric modelling, finite element analysis and assessment criteria. This study proposes a 
digital twin-driven multi-metrics method for condition assessment of eccentrically loaded RC columns 
with cracks. Three RC columns were tested, with one column used for development and two for valid -
ation specimens. The geometric digital twin (gDT) of the RC column was constructed using point 
cloud data for geometry and rebar scanning for reinforcement layout, thereby reducing geometric 
uncertainties caused by manufacturers. Building on the gDT, a nonlinear finite element model was 
constructed as the virtual entity and calibrated to experimental data using an optimisation algorithm, 
thereby producing a mechanical digital twin (mDT) that mitigates analysis-related inconsistencies. The 
established digital twin was subsequently used to predict the responses of two validation specimens, 
achieving prediction errors within 5% for ultimate load-bearing capacity. Finally, a multi-metrics condi -
tion assessment framework based on DT was proposed, enabling assessment at each loading level for 
all RC columns. The results indicate that the DT-driven approach substantially enhances the accuracy 
of structural condition assessment of the RC columns with cracks.

1. Introduction
Harsh environment and overloading may cause material 
deterioration, cracking, deformation and overstress in rein -
forced concrete (RC) columns of short and medium span 
bridges, which in turn affect the serviceability and safety of 
the bridges (Sun et al., 2023 ; Zaghian et al., 2023 ). 
Furthermore, due to uncertainties involved in design and 
construction, the working conditions of RC columns may 
not be the same as designed. For instance, ideally axially 
loaded RC columns may become eccentrically loaded RC 
columns due to construction defects. It is thus necessary to 
conduct condition assessment of RC columns from time to 
time to ensure their serviceability and safety in their life 
cycles (Jiang et al., 2023 ).
Inspections and digital image processing techniques are 
currently utilised to assess the conditions of RC columns, in 
which the conditions of a cracked RC column are classified 
mainly by comparing inspected crack characteristics with 
allowable crack width or crack density limit (Afzali et al., 
2023 ; Hamidia et al., 2023 ; Ministry of Transport of the 
People’s Republic of China, 2011 ). However, the allowable 
crack width or crack density limit is often set up by rules of 
thumb, and the cracks observed are only an apparent feature 
on the surface of the RC column. Therefore, this kind of 
method is difficult to reproduce the actual response of the 
cracked RC column and provide an accurate condition 
assessment. Nonlinear finite element analysis (NFEA) is 
another common way for the condition assessment of RC 
columns in terms of various parameters such as stiffness, 
crack, stress and bearing capacity (Abbas et al., 2016 ; Lin 
et al., 2017 ). However, since there are geometric, material 
and analytical uncertainties involved in NFEA and difficul -
ties in simulating distributed cracks, NFEA often does not 
produce accurate condition assessments of cracked RC 
columns.
To solve the problem associated with the uncertainties 
involved in NFEA, model updating techniques are utilised 
to reduce uncertainties. Chen et al. ( 2024 ) updated the 
material parameters of both reinforcement and concrete of 
simply supported RC beams using measured concrete 
strains. Zheng et al. ( 2020 ) carried out nonlinear model 
updating to reduce uncertainties in NFEA of a cable-stayed 
bridge using measured natural frequencies and acceleration 
responses. Xiao et al. ( 2023 ) proposed a method to update 
material parameters of simply supported RC beams based

on static deflection and crack width using an artificial

model updating, the geometry of the FE model used in the 
updating process is based on design drawings without 
updating, and the information (e.g. natural frequencies and 
acceleration responses) from the sensors of a structural 
health monitoring system is not integrated with the infor -
mation (cracks and spalling) from the structural inspection 
for model updating.
Benefitting from the rapid development of information 
technology, the digital twin concept (Heng et al., 2024 ; Sang 
et al., 2023 ; Tang et al., 2024 ; Torzoni et al., 2024 ) may pro -
vide an effective framework for more accurate condition 
assessment of cracked RC columns. The digital twin consists 
of four major components: (a) a physical entity and infor -
mation collected from the physical entity; (b) a physical- 
informed virtual entity simulating the physical entity; (c) the 
fusion and interaction between the physical and virtual enti -
ties to form the digital twin; and (d) the service and predic -
tion provided by the digital twin. It is worth noting that the 
digital twin is still in its early stage of development in civil 
engineering, and its definition varies across disciplines 
according to specific application requirements. The defin -
ition adopted in this study does not require real-time inter -
action between the digital twin and the physical entity.
Based on this premise in bridge engineering, Lin et al. 
( 2021 ) proposed a digital twin-based collapse fragility assess -
ment of a long-span cable-stayed bridge under strong earth -
quakes. Zeng et al. ( 2025 ) presented a digital twin- 
empowered performance evaluation of cracked RC beams. 
Lao et al. ( 2024 ) integrated a multiscale crystal plasticity 
model with fatigue tests to establish a digital twin for pro -
viding probabilistic predictions of micro-crack initiation and 
propagation in the weld zone of orthotropic steel decks. 
Liang et al. ( 2025 ) developed a digital twin for the dynamics 
of coupled train-track-bridge systems. However, the digital 
twin-based condition assessment of eccentrically loaded RC 
columns in short and medium span bridges has not yet 
been explored.
There are at least three challenging issues to be addressed 
for digital twin-based condition assessment of eccentrically 
loaded RC columns. The first issue is how to construct the 
geometric digital twin (gDT) of a deteriorated RC column 
using a 3D laser scanner for geometry (Hinks et al., 2013 ; 
Yang et al., 2020 ) and a rebar scanner for reinforcement 
position and then convert it to the nonlinear FE model of 
the column. The second issue is how to convert the nonlin -
ear FE model into the digital twin by mapping the structural 
inspection and health monitoring information to the nonlin -
ear FE model. The third issue is that the currently used 
assessment criteria in terms of allowable crack width or 
crack density limit based on the structural inspection infor -
mation are different from those in terms of deflection and 
loading capacity based on the structural health monitoring 
information. Therefore, the multi-metrics condition assess -
ment by integrating structural health monitoring informa -
tion with structural inspection information is needed (Xu 
et al., 2019 ).
This study thus aims at developing a framework for 
digital twin-based multi-metrics condition assessment of 
eccentrically loaded RC columns. The remainder of this 
paper is organised as follows. Section 2 describes the struc -
tural testing and data collection of the eccentrically loaded 
RC column as a physical entity. Section 3 details the estab -
lishment of a gDT of the physical entity. Section 4 estab -
lishes a NFEA model of the eccentrically loaded RC column 
as a virtual entity. Section 5 develops a digital twin of the 
eccentrically loaded RC column through model updating. 
Section 6 uses the digital twin to predict the structural spal -
ling and response of the three eccentrically loaded RC col -
umns. Section 7 explores the relationship between structural 
health monitoring information and structural inspection 
information and proposes a multi-metrics condition assess -
ment method based on the theory of evidence. Section 8
summarises the research findings and conclusions from this 
study. Figure 1 outlines the proposed framework of this 
study.

loading process was controlled by a hydraulic loading sys -

ure. The ZBL-F103 crack width detector was employed to 
inspect crack widths on the tension side of the column dur -
ing the loading process. The TEST 3826E system and the 
HIKVISION industrial camera were utilised to measure 
strains and deflections of the RC columns.
Before cracking during the loading process, the load was 
increased in increments of 20% of the calculated cracking 
load. When the load approached the calculated cracking 
load, the increments were reduced to 5% of the calculated 
cracking load. After cracking during the loading process, the 
load was increased in 5% increments of the calculated 
ultimate load until failure. The calculated cracking and 
ultimate loads were calculated based on design parameters, 
which are 122 kN and 628 kN for Column C1, respectively. 
The loading test was terminated when either of the follow -
ing conditions was satisfied: (1) structural damage (e.g. the 
collapse of concrete in the compression zone accompanied 
by buckling of the reinforcement); (2) the lateral deflection 
of the column increased rapidly but the loading decreased 
(e.g. the ultimate load carrying capacity reached). After the 
loading process, the geometry of the column was re- 
inspected using the Leica BLK 360 3D laser scanner to 
quantify the concrete spalling volume.

3. Establishment of a geometric digital twin
The establishment of a gDT is the first step in the establish -
ment of a digital twin for Column C1. This is because 
uncertainties during construction lead to discrepancies 
between the fabricated and designed RC columns in the 
geometry and reinforcement position. This section details 
the use of data from the laser scanner and rebar scanner to 
determine the geometry and reinforcement position and 
establish a gDT for Column C1.
3.1. Geometry of Column C1
The geometry of Column C1 is derived from the PCD 
obtained by the 3D laser scanner before the loading test. 
The PCD collected is pre-processed using CloudCompare 
2.6 (Girardeau-Montaut, 2025 ), which includes the manual 
cutting of the initial PCD and the 5 mm voxel down- 
sampling to reduce the data size. The PCD is then rotated 
by the principal component analysis to align with the appro -
priate coordinate directions. Column C1 is seen as a geo -
metric object formed by 16 vertex coordinates, which can be 
considered the gradient of the sample but neglects the flat -
ness of the concrete surface. The vertex coordinates are 
determined from the equations of the three adjacent planes 
(Hinks et al., 2013 ), while the equations of the three adja -
cent planes are determined by the least squares method 
based on the local PCD. The algorithm can be seen in 
Appendix A . The geometry of Column C1 is finally created 
in Rhino 7.0 (Robert McNeel & Associates, 2010) after the 
coordinates of the 16 vertices are determined. The process 
of building the geometry of Column C1 from the PCD is 
illustrated in Figure 8 .

4. Establishment of virtual entity
The gDT of the column is imported into ABAQUS via 
Rhino with IGS format set by the Initial Graphics Exchange 
Specification (IGES) (Nasr & Kamrani, 2007 ). The NFEA 
model of the column is then implemented using ABAQUS 
2024 (Dassault Syst �emes, 2024 ). The NFEA model uses 
C3D8R solid elements for concrete and T3D2 truss elements 
for all reinforcements. C3D8R solid elements and T3D2 
truss elements have only one integration point located in 
the centre of the element. The stirrups and supplementary 
reinforcements are modelled using pre-built reinforcement 
by assuming a complete bond with the concrete. The bond- 
slip between the longitudinal reinforcements and the con -
crete is simulated by nonlinear springs (Liu & Bai, 2019 ).

7.2. Multi-metrics condition assessment method
The Dempster-Shafer (DS) evidence theory, proposed by 
Dempster and further developed by Shafer, has been widely 
used in the field of multi-source information fusion (Zhou 
et al., 2021 ). Sun et al. ( 2021 ) assessed the conditions of stay

Yang et al. ( 2019 ) used the evidence theory and fuzzy ana -
lytic hierarchy process to assess the box girder bridge based 
on inspection and monitoring data. Zhang et al. ( 2023 ) per -
formed multi-resource fusion of test data for bridge deck 
assessment using the discrete wavelet transformation and 
evidence theory. In this section, the evidence theory is used 
to conduct multi-metrics condition assessment of eccentric -
ally loaded RC columns.

7.3. SLS and ULS assessment results
In this study, the ULS assessment of Column C1 under a 
429 kN load is presented as a case study to illustrate the 
proposed computational procedure. The digital twin pro -
vides results for three ULS-related metrics: applied load, 
mid-span deflection and maximum crack width, which are 
429 kN, 6.85 and 0.24 mm, respectively. First, the identifica -
tion frame is defined as H ¼ fL1, L2, L3, L4, L5g, corre -
sponding to the five ULS levels specified in Table 7 . The 
BPA functions for the three metrics are calculated using 
Equation (19) , and the results are provided in Table 8 . The 
distances between the evidence sources are then computed

degree of consistency in the ULS assessment.
Based on Equations (22)–(24) , the absolute credibility val -
ues of the three metrics are calculated as 1.07, 0.94 and 0.49. 
It is worth noting that the absolute reliability of the max -
imum crack width metric is only 0.49, which is lower than 
the other two metrics of 1.07 and 0.94. This means that there 
is a conflict between the three assessment results. The BPA 
values are subsequently adjusted using Equation (26) , with 
the modified results presented in Table 8 . The results show 
that the maximum crack width of BPA is significantly 
reduced, which can alleviate the conflict between the three 
metrics. The total conflict coefficient of the modified evi -
dence is 0.9935. The evidence combination is then performed 
using Equation (27) , resulting in the final ranking probability 
vector: [0.2485, 0.2764, 0.3409, 0.1345, 0.0000]. Among these, 
Level 3 has the highest probability value of 0.3409. Therefore, 
the ULS level of Column C1 under the applied load of 
429 kN is determined to be Level 3.
Figure 22(a) shows the SLS and ULS condition assess -
ment results for Column C1 under different load levels, fol -
lowing the method described in Section 7.2 . The assessment 
metrics are obtained from the digital twin. The SLS level 
reaches 5 when the maximum crack width and deflection 
are 0.18 and 4.81 mm, respectively. Although these values 
do not exceed the defined thresholds, the DS evidence the -
ory accounts for the collective uncertainty, resulting in a 
higher assessment level from a conservative viewpoint.
In contrast, the ULS assessment shows a delayed 
response compared to the SLS. When the ULS level reaches 
5, the corresponding maximum crack width, deflection and 
load are 0.39 mm, 12.59 mm and 600.60 kN, respectively, all 
of which remain within the threshold values. This outcome 
highlights the DS theory’s capacity to incorporate multiple 
metrics and better reflect the true condition of the structure.

8. Conclusions
The digital twin-driven multi-metrics condition assessment 
method has been developed, validated and applied in this 
study for eccentrically loaded RC columns with cracks. The 
main conclusions which can be drawn from this study are 
as follows:
1. The digital twin of eccentrically loaded RC columns 
with cracks can be developed effectively. The interaction 
between the physical and virtual entities is established 
using geometric information, deflections, concrete 
strains and maximum crack width, with implementation 
based on the PSO algorithm.
2. A methodology integrating 3D laser scanning and rebar 
scanning is developed to capture column geometry and 
reinforcement layout and reduce geometric uncertainties, 
enabling high-quality gDTs. This provides a reliable 
foundation for more accurate inference of mechanical 
parameters through model updating.
3. The accuracy of the developed mechanical digital twin 
is validated by comparing its predicted responses with 
experimental data, including concrete spalling, tensile 
and lateral concrete strains and reinforcement strains. 
The identified material parameters can be extended to 
predict the behaviour of other RC columns fabricated 
under similar conditions.
4. An improved condition assessment method based on 
DS evidence theory is proposed, which effectively 
addresses conflicts among multiple metrics. Compared 
to conventional threshold-based approaches, the pro -
posed method demonstrates higher sensitivity and 
enhances the overall reliability of the assessment.
5. The digital twin framework enables more timely condition 
assessment. A functional relationship between deflection 
and maximum crack width is derived from the digital 
twin, allowing crack width estimation from monitored 
deflection data. This demonstrates the digital twin’s reli -
ability and effectiveness in structural condition evaluation.
With recent technological advancements, point cloud and 
rebar scanning techniques provide efficient support for 
structural condition assessment. However, these methods 
face limitations in severely occluded areas or where direct 
detection is difficult. Addressing data gaps and geometric 
occlusion remains a priority. Furthermore, the digital twin 
developed in this study was established and validated with -
out considering environmental degradation. Future work 
should examine the framework under various loading condi -
tions, environmental actions and different cross-sectional 
forms, to enhance its generalisability and predictive capabil -
ity for practice. Its applicability to in-service RC beams with 
initial defects or damage also merits investigation.
