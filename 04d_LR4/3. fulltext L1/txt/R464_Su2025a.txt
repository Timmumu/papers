Purpose – Unstructured and variable construction sites pose significant challenges to earthwork scheduling. 
However, current parameter-tuning strategies fall short in supporting dynamic optimization under such 
complex conditions, and real-time construction data remains underutilized. To address these gaps, this study 
aims to propose a method that enables the automatic and real-time allocation and scheduling of heavy 
construction equipment by fully leveraging real-time construction data and dynamic equipment status 
information. 
Design/methodology/approach – This research proposes a digital twin (DT)-enabled real-time rolling- 
horizon optimization (RHO) method that integrates an online Improved Aquila Optimizer with an adaptive 
parameter adjustment strategy. This method uses real-time resource status and construction progress data 
obtained from high-fidelity DT to dynamically optimize equipment scheduling without manual intervention. 
Findings – The proposed method was implemented for a large hydropower construction project in Southwest 
China. The experimental results showed that this method can achieve the dynamic allocation and scheduling of 
heavy construction equipment with high quality and superior performance, significantly reducing construction 
delays by 38.4%. 
Originality/value – This study presents a DT-enabled real-time RHO method for the automatic and real-time 
allocation and scheduling of heavy construction equipment. It provides an effective solution to the challenges 
posed by unstructured and dynamic construction environments, significantly enhancing operational efficiency 
and reducing scheduling delays.

1. Introduction
Earthwork engineering is an essential component of construction projects and is commonly 
used in infrastructure development and real estate ventures. It plays a pivotal role in the field 
of hydraulic engineering (Moselhi and Alshibani, 2009). Earthwork engineering typically 
involves various types of equipment, such as dump trucks (for transporting materials 
between loading and filling areas), pavers (for the laying and initial compaction of crushed 
stones) and compactors (to increase the density of foundation materials and reinforce 
structural stability). These pieces of equipment ensure the smooth operation of projects 
(Parente et al., 2015).
With the continuous advancement of automated construction technologies, the demand 
for the efficient allocation and scheduling of earthwork construction equipment at 
construction sites has become increasingly prominent during the optimization process of

construction plans ( Liu et al., 2022 ). Efficient allocation and scheduling of earthwork 
construction equipment enhances operational efficiency and ensures orderly progress, 
thereby improving overall construction quality . Hence, the rational and efficient allocation 
and scheduling of earthwork construction equipment has become a hot topic in the field of 
earthwork construction.
Currently , heuristic algorithms are predominantly used in engineering practice for the 
optimization of equipment allocation and scheduling processes. These algorithms aim to 
compute the global optimal solution simultaneously to plan the equipment usage of the entire 
construction process ( Parente et al., 2015 a; Lin et al., 2012 ; Parente et al., 2015 b; Du et al., 
2016 ; Miao et al., 2024 ; Shehadeh et al., 2022 ). However , unexpected events, such as 
adverse weather conditions, equipment failure and traffic congestion, during open-pit 
earthwork construction may impact the construction progress, resulting in deviations 
between the actual construction schedule and the initially optimized construction plan 
( Hasan et al., 2018 ). Leveraging digital twin (DT) to acquire real-time construction 
information, detect dynamic deviations and continuously adjust optimal plans through 
dynamic optimization methods can help ensure controlled project progress and holds 
significant research value through the timely correction of deviations ( Liu et al., 2022 ).
W ith the maturation of the DT technology , real-time construction information can now be 
sensed and traced ( Jiang et al., 2022 a). In current architecture, engineering and construction 
(AEC) practice, DT s are predominantly implemented as virtual representations that mirror 
physical assets, processes and systems ( Ghansah and Lu, 2023 ) and reported applications 
lar gely cluster around monitoring-oriented scenarios (e.g. progress, safety , asset condition; 
Radzi et al., 2023 ). However , such implementations largely emphasize monitoring-oriented 
mirroring of assets, processes and systems ( Ghansah and Lu, 2023 ; Radzi et al., 2023 ; Pour 
Rahimian et al., 2022 ) with comparatively limited attention to decision support for 
scheduling and control. Accordingly , harnessing the visibility and traceability affo rded by 
DT s to tackle the allocation and scheduling of heavy construction equipment on smart sites 
remains an important and underexplored problem warranting systematic investigation.
The rolling-horizon optimization (RHO) strategy ( Sethi and Sor ger , 1991 ) is a dynamic 
optimization and feedback control method applied across various fields, such as operations 
management ( Alimian et al., 2022 ), energy management ( Bertazzi and Maggioni, 2018 ) and 
transportation planning ( Cordeau et al., 2015 ). This strategy operates on a closed-loop 
optimization model that continuously progresses along a timeline. This strategy optimizes 
the control objectives within the current time window based on predictive models, outputting 
the optimal decision for the current time window . This decision is then fed back into the 
actual construction process, thus advancing the time window to determine the actual 
construction status of the next time window for further decision optimization ( Sethi and 
Sor ger , 1991 ). Metaheuristic algorithms are commonly used in optimization processes, 
where the hyperparameter settings of such algorithms directly influence the optimization 
performance.
Therefore, inspired by the aforementioned studies, this study aimed to establish a closed- 
loop model for construction processes based on a DT -enabled RHO strategy to address the 
flexible scheduling problem of multi-objective automated earthwork construction site 
equipment, ensuring construction progress. In the optimization process, considering the 
drawbacks of time-consumption and nonadaptive offline parameter tuning commonly 
associated with algorithmic optimization processes ( Eiben et al., 1999 ), an Improved Aquila 
Optimizer (AO) based on online parameter tuning was adopted. This algorithm uses an 
online parameter control strategy that enables an adaptive adjustment of the AO parameters 
for dynamic optimization problems.

2. Related works
2.1 Applications of digital twin in earthwork construction
DT s are dynamic virtual counterparts of physical entities, processes, services and systems 
( van der Heijden, 2023 ). A canonical DT architecture consists of physical space, virtual 
space and an information connection and processing layer that couples them. Its core 
principle is the bidirectional mapping between physical assets and their virtual models, 
enabling real-time sensing, prediction and optimization of the underlying system ( Glaessgen 
and Star gel, 2012 ). W ithin the AEC sector , DT applications are concentrated in design, 
construction scheduling and operations and maintenance (O&M). In the design phase, DT s 
integrate conceptual, architectural and structural models on a unified platform to support 
holistic computation and simulation, thereby enabling accurate prediction of asset 
performance and informing early design modifications ( Kan and Anumba, 2019 ). Focusing 
on the construction and O&M stages, Assaf et al. (2025) developed a DT -based indoor 
environmental quality monitoring framework that enables real-time monitoring and dynamic 
management of construction environments, improving worker health, safety and 
productivity . Beyond these core AEC scenarios, the fusion of DT s with data-driven methods 
shows substantial benefits in adjacent settings. For example, Muzi et al. (2022) proposed a 
data-driven DT framework for public buildings, residential complexes and port 
infrastructure to optimize construction management, enable predictive maintenance and 
strengthen risk and safety management. Jiang et al. (2022 b) used a real-time, data-driven DT 
to support collaborative scheduling for precast onsite assembly and to enact dynamic 
adjustments during execution.
The allocation and scheduling of construction equipment in earthwork projects are pivotal 
planning decisions for successful project delivery ( Durdyev and Hosseini, 2019 ; W ang et al., 
2020 ). Project managers must determine the fleet composition and temporal sequencing of 
resources across work zones – typically including haul trucks, bulldozers, excavators and 
rollers – to maximize productivity and efficiency . However , conventional approaches often 
rely on practitioner experience ( Salem and Moselhi, 2018 ) or static historical databases ( Lin 
et al., 2012 ), which are ill-suited to the complex operating conditions and stringent 
performance requirements of large-scale earthwork ( Zhang et al., 2022 ). In this context, real- 
time construction information is indispensable for effect ive equipment scheduling. DT s, as

RHO strategies have been widely applied in various feedback control problems across 
differen t domains, demonstrating their adaptability and superiority . In the energy sector , 
Y ang et al. (2023) developed a multi-timescale scheduling framework that integrates robust 
optimization with rolling-horizon updates to enhance microgrid operation efficiency . In 
railway operations, Zhao et al. (2025) proposed a hybrid approach combining a rolling- 
horizon scheme with the Nondominated Sorting Genetic Algorithm II (NSGA-II) to 
reschedule trains under uncertain disruption durations, thereby improving solution quality , 
computational efficiency and operational practicality . For building energy management, Mu 
et al. (2023) used the RHO strategy to dynamically recalibrate weather forecasts, thereby 
mitigating the impact of exogenous uncertainty and significantly reducing system operating 
costs.
RHO strategies, with their inherent capabilities in handling uncertainty , enabling 
dynamic decision-making and supporting continuous optimization, off er a promising 
technical approach for the dynamic scheduling and allocation of earthwork machinery . 
By continuously monitoring real-time data on earthwork progress, remaining stockpile 
volume, dam surface demand, machinery status, traffic conditions and weather , the 
strategies enable the development of an optimization algorithm for machinery scheduling 
and allocation. The optimal solutions derived from the algorithm are implemented on- 
site, with real-time monitoring of their performance. Based on continuous feedback, the 
construction plan is dynamically adjusted and refined until progress deviations are 
ef fectively controlled. In earthwork construction, the application of RHO strategies is 
relatively limited. Among representative studies, Jiang et al. (2024) proposed a data- 
driven RHO scheduling model for modular construction fit-out, thereby supporting 
dynamic, event-responsive managerial decision-making. Bogenber ger et al. (2015) used 
RHO to develop a mathematical model that generates cost-minimal earthwork plans 
under given constraints, thereby enhancing construction efficiency and cost control. Liu

2.3 Aquila optimization algorithm
The allocation and scheduling of construction equipment in earthwork projects are highly 
challenging because of the numerous potential equipment allocation combinations, leading 
to an exceedingly vast search space ( Schaufelberger and Migliaccio, 2019 ). In such cases, 
purely conventional operations research methods (such as linear programming) and blind 
search approaches are infeasible ( Parente et al., 2015 b). Currently , heuristic algorithms are 
used to solve large-scale optimization problems ( Rao, 2019 ). They efficiently explore vast 
search space regions while making reasonable use of computational resources, thereby 
rapidly approximating optimal solutions. Common metaheuristic methods used for 
equipment scheduling include genetic algorithms ( Parente et al., 2015 a), particle swarm 
optimization algorithms ( Song and Xia, 2022 ) ( T an et al., 2021 ) ( Fontes et al., 2023 ), whale 
optimization algorithms ( Y ankai et al., 2021 ), simulated annealing algorithms ( Chiu, 2009 ), 
grey wolf optimization algorithms ( Zhu and Zhou, 2020 ) and tabu search algorithms ( Xu 
et al., 2021 ).
Heuristic algorithms are being increasingly favored to maximize resource 
utilization efficiency and thus address the scheduling and allocation issues of 
construction equipment in earthwork projects. For instance, Du et al. (2016) developed 
a solution model by integrating construction simulation, entropy weighting and genetic 
algorithms, which enables optimal phasing, construction planning and rational 
allocation of equipment such as transport trucks, bulldozers and rollers. Parente et al. 
(2015a ) improved the utilization efficiency of construction equipment by scheduling 
compactors using the NSGA-II and allocating the remaining equipment through linear 
programming. Shehadeh et al. (2022) proposed a new multi-objective, multi-variable 
optimization mathematical model based on genetic algorithms, aiming to optimize the 
time and cost of earthwork activities and determine the optimal combination of 
construction equipment, thus providing strong support for decision-making in 
construction projects.
The AO is a typical heuristic optimization algorithm ( Abualigah et al., 2021 ), which has 
demonstrated strong performance on high-dimensional, nonlinear and mixed-integer 
problems. Its simplicity of implementation facilitates engineering deployment and 
integration with other technical modules ( Fu et al., 2024 ). Evidence from comparative 
studies underscores AO’ s advantages over widely used baselines. A comprehensive survey 
reports that AO and its variants frequently outperform classical metaheuristics – including 
differen tial evolution (DE), firefly algorithm, bat algorithm, GWO, moth-flame optimizer 
and marine predators algorithm – and rank among the top performers across diverse 
benchmark suites and engineering applications ( T aleb et al., 2025 ). Complementing these 
findings, Sharma et al. (2024) systematically evaluated AO on 23 benchmark functions and 
five real-world engineering problems, demonstrating its faster convergence, higher 
efficiency , lower computational cost and superior adaptability over baselines (mountain 
gazelle optimizer , PSO, GWO, ant lion optimizer , social cognitive optimization and WOA). 
Its ef fectiveness has been extensively validated in various domains such as petroleum 
production forecasting ( AlRassas et al., 2021 ) and engineering optimization ( W ang et al., 
2021 ).

3. Methods
3.1 Rolling-horizon optimization
By harnessing the synergy between RHO and DT technology , we can maximize the potential 
of these methodologies, facilitating enhanced precision in decision-making and resource 
allocation. DT s use information technologies, including simulation theory methods, artificial 
intelligence and the IoT , to simulate, predict, optimize and control physical spaces. Our 
research extends DT concepts to intelligent construction simulations by leveraging 
technologies, such as perception, computation and modeling, to analyze, simulate and 
optimize the construction processes of dams. Multiple sensors were used to detect physical 
phenomena, and the collected data were used to digitally recreate the construction site. The 
virtual model directs the state changes and offe rs feedback guidance for construction 
planning and control. Figure 1 illustrates the proposed DT concept for the intelligent 
simulation of the construction process. The intelligent construction simulation DT model 
integrates physical, virtual and data spaces facilitated by a cloud platform service, enabling 
connection and interaction between the DT models.

3.1.1 Construction information per ception. Multimodal data are systematically 
acquired through the integration of diverse sensing devices, enabling the comprehensive 
collection of kinematic, acoustic, visual and meteorological information from 
construction sites. T o transform these heterogeneous data streams into simulation-ready 
inputs, advanced multimodal information processing techniques are used, as shown in 
Figure 3 .
For kinematic and acoustic modalities, data are captured using smartphones embedded 
with GPS modules and accelerometers, as well as external acoustic sensors. An ensemble 
deep learning framework is proposed, incorporating IDeepConvLSTM for kinematic pattern 
recognition and DCNN for acoustic signal classification. This model facilitates the real-time 
identification of compaction machinery activity states. Moreover , a sliding window filtering 
mechanism is applied to enable fine-grained temporal extraction of compaction activity 
durations for simulation input. For the visual modality , construction site imagery is obtained 
via fixed surveillance cameras and mobile imaging platforms. A hybrid method combining 
YOLOv3 object detection, DeepSOR T tracking and rule-based temporal reasoning is 
developed to extract time-stamped unloading operation parameters from the visual stream. 
Regarding the meteorological modality , environmental data are collected through automated 
weather stations monitoring temperature, humidity and precipitation. A PSOMLP-based 
time-series forecasting model is used to estimate work stoppage parameters under inclement 
weather conditions, thereby supporting accurate simulation of weather-induced delays and 
special operational constraints. The extracted parameters from each modality are 
subsequently converted into structured simulation inputs through an expert knowledge-based 
conversion module. This integrated perception and processing framework ensures the 
accurate, timely and automated acquisition of critical simulation parameters, thereby 
enhancing the adaptability , fidelity and real-time responsiveness of construction simulation 
models.

3.1.3 Feedback contr ol based on digital-twin model. Upon triggering the RHO 
process, a closed-loop rolling plan comprising three steps – perception, optimization and 
feedback control – is executed within the time unit of the RHO step. Each iteration of 
this closed loop generates a dynamic optimal solution that is calculated and solved on 
the cloud platform. Once obtained, the construction site command center disseminates 
the solution instructions to on-site construction personnel through the short message 
service and W eb interface. Subsequently , the construction personnel organize the 
construction activities in accordance with the newly provided plan. Throughout this 
process, RHO continuously senses the construction progress, optimizes the construction 
plan and continuously corrects the construction progress until the completion of the 
construction project. Figure 5 shows the implementation process of the dynamic 
feedback control for the construction plan.

30-function CEC2017 benchmark, AO achieved an average Friedman rank of 2.41,

4. Case study
The efficacy of the proposed methodology was assessed by implementing it in a practical 
project in the southwestern region of China. In each construction area, the operational 
workflow proceeds through four stages – unloading, paving, rolling compaction and quality 
inspection. This project involves the construction of the world’ s second-highest earth–rock 
dam, incorporating diverse functionalities such as flood control, power generation and water 
supply . Located in the Y along River Basin, the dam has a maximum height of 295 m, an 
installed capacity of 3,000 MW and a main construction period of nine years.
4.1 Digital-twin cloud platform for earthwork construction
The DT platform developed for this case study was built using the SQL data engine and the 
Unity3D virtual engine. The engine incorporates essential technologies, such as multiscale 
twin-space construction and virtual-real synchronization mapping, to offer model support, 
operation and maintenance assistance. Ultimately , the platform realizes three major 
functions: multi-modal real-time perception of simulation information, simulation analysis 
and prediction and construction plan optimization and feedback control.

4.2 Dynamic allocation and scheduling of heavy construction equipment
4.2.1 Deviation analysis of scheme execution. According to the initial construction 
schedule, activities in Area 969 were planned to commence at 10:50 AM on April 2 of the 
sixth year and conclude by 10:00 PM the same day. The area was to be divided into four 
sequential flow units, supported by the deployment of essential machinery, including 22 
dump trucks, 3 pavers and 6 rollers, to ensure the smooth execution of the construction tasks. 
However, deviations from the planned schedule emerged during the implementation phase. 
At 3:30 PM on April 2, the on-site quality inspector identified that the gravel soil in Flow 
Unit 2 exhibited an excessively high moisture content, rendering it non-compliant with the 
specified quality standards. This necessitated additional drying and re-rolling operations, 
requiring the mobilization of excavators and bulldozers. The drying process further disrupted 
the scheduled paving operations in Flow Unit 3. By 3:30 PM, the drying of Flow Unit 2 had 
been completed and re-rolling was required. According to the original plan, Flow Unit 2 
should have been fully completed by this time, while Flow Units 3 and 4 should have been 
engaged in paving and unloading operations, respectively. In reality, however, paving in 
Flow Unit 3 had not yet commenced, and Flow Unit 2 remained pending re-rolling. The 
deviation between the actual construction progress and the simulation results based on the 
initial schedule exceeded the predefined threshold (i.e. Δcompaction > 100 m
2 or 
Δpaving > 500 m2; Δ = simulated − actual at the corresponding times), thereby triggering the 
activation of RHO strategies.

5. Conclusions
Accurate equipment allocation and scheduling can minimize equipment idle time, enhance 
productivity and facilitate precise control over construction progress. This study introduced a 
DT -based online RHO approach for dynamically allocating and scheduling heavy 
construction equipment. The OIAO is used to optimize the allocation and scheduling of 
construction–equipment schemes:
• 
A RHO framework was established using real-time resource status and construction 
progress information obtained through DT technology . Construction equipment is 
continuously adjusted to ensure that site conditions align with optimized equipment 
allocation and scheduling, thereby achieving ef fective control over the construction 
progress.
• 
During the optimization and solution-seeking process, the OIAO was developed by 
employing an enhanced online parameter- adjustment strategy . This strategy enables 
adaptive adjustments of the algorithm parameters tailored for dynamic optimization 
problems, thereby significantly enhancing the ef fectiveness of the optimization.
• 
The validity of the proposed method was substantiated through engineering case 
studies, demonstrating its capability to dynamically adjust construction plans and 
effectively reduce project delays, resulting in a notable reduction of 38.4% in 
construction delays, thus highlighting its significant potential to support earthwork 
management in complex environments.

6. Discussion
The proposed DT -based RHO approach has strong potential to improve the efficiency 
of construction equipment allocation and scheduling. The framework comprises a 
site-agnostic core and site-specific elements. The site-agnostic core is the 
perception–optimization–feedback loop coupled with the OIAO solver . The site-specific 
elements include (i) activity (process) definitions and constraints – resources, precedence 
relations and time, temperature and quality windows; (ii) sensing infrastructure and 
mappings from modalities to state variables (e.g. GNSS for vehicle localization and 
productivity/progress estimation; vision for indoor asset tracking; sensors for quality 
states); and (iii) boundary and safety constraints. The framework can be deployed without 
modification in analogous large-scale earthmoving contexts (e.g. highway asphalt paving 
( Labban et al., 2013 ) and airfield construction). Extending it to broader civil works – 
including vertical buildings – requires adapting the site-specific elements (i–iii). The core 
perception–optimization–feedback loop with the OIAO solver remains unchanged.
As an illustrative example in vertical construction with tower-crane operations, the 
multimodal perception module described in Section 3.1.1 can capture the parameters 
required to instantiate a discrete-event (DES) model of the hoisting workflow . These 
parameters include the number and type of cranes, service zones, load-radius envelopes, 
component weights, the decomposition and durations of the hoisting cycle (hooking, lifting, 
slewing, trolleying, positioning and releasing), hook travel distances, laydown/receiving 
queue capacities, floor-level takt times and inter-cr ane interference and safety clearances. 
This is consistent with established DES-based tower -crane scheduling research ( Al Hattab 
et al., 2017 ). These perceived variables parameterize the DES of tower -crane-assisted 
assembly and floor -by-floor production. The perception layer then continuously reconciles 
time-aligned observations—crane telemetry , queue lengths and floor progress – with 
synchronized DES predictions (cycle-time distributions, waiting times and resource 
utilization). When deviations exceed predefined thresholds, the OIAO-based RHO is 
triggered to revise crane assignments, hoisting sequences, time-slot allocations, priorities for 
shared resources and crew synchronization. The current best plan is subsequently fed back 
into field execution. For municipal works, the framework can adopt shorter rolling-horizon 
intervals. This further shrinks the decision space and runtime and facilitates online re- 
optimization under tight real-time constraints.
In our case study , the maximum runtime was 712 s, which is operationally acceptable 
within the 3 h rolling interval. Going forward, we will integrate decision-space pruning 
(constraint presolve and activity/resource windowing), hierarchical decomposition with 
short horizons, warm starts with early stopping and parallel or batched (optionally 
surrogate-assisted) evaluation of candidate solutions to further reduce runtime and enable 
scaling to larg er projects. Additionally , the current evaluation benchmarks OIAO 
exclusively against AO under matched rolling-horizon settings. Future work will broaden 
the comparative scope – under identical RHO configurations – to include representative 
metaheuristics (e.g. PSO, DE and SA) and to integrate faster , lightweight optimizers 
within the RHO framework. In addition, we will conduct multi-site validation across 
vertical buildings, transportation infrastructure and small-scale municipal works.
