Abstract: This study proposes a physics-informed neural network 
(PINN)-based edge-cloud collaborative digital twin (DT) for predictive 
maintenance of ageing railway signalling equipment (RSE). The core model, an 
adaptive pigeon-inspired optimised PINN (API-PINN), integrates 
electromechanical degradation physics with operational data to forecast faults 
and estimate remaining useful life (RUL). The architecture enables 
low-latency, real-time anomaly detection at the network edge while leveraging 
the cloud for intensive model training . Experiments on real-world railway 
signalling data sets can show that API-PINN attains the prediction accuracy of 
98.77% and the R 2 of 0.968, which is better than the baseline models. The 
given framework offers a strong performance when the data is noisy and 
imbalanced, allowing for the arrange ment of the maintenance schedule 
proactively, minimising the costs of operations, minimising downtimes, and 
improving safety. 
Keywords: predictive maintenance; digita l twin; edge-cloud computing; 
railway signalling equipment; RSE; ageing infrastructure.

1 Introduction 
Ageing railway signalling equipment (RSE) is defined as inheritance electromechanical 
or electronic control systems that have lost performance, module degradation, and 
reliability as a consequence of long-term stress of use and experience to environmental 
fundamentals. These degrada tions result in disorganisations, operative delays, and 
amplified incident risks. As quantified in Ji et al. (2025) and Wang et al. (2024b), the 
transformation of RSE has developed a necess ity to preserve security and efficiency.

Examples of difficulties are outmoded technologies that are not able to support modern 
operational needs, connotations that are not well modified to real-time traffic 
circumstances, and the wear of the machineries, including microelectronic wear. Railway 
systems are using large-scale asset substitutio n, preventative repairs, and data-based 
dependability techniques to solve these problems. The recent introduction of solid-state 
interlocking (SSI), light-emitting diode (LED) signals that are and constantly track 
circuiting has made things more durable and less likely to break. Data loggers also let you 
keep an eye on how well your equipment is working all the time so you can do predictive 
maintenance. Cybersecurity features are also being added to networks more and more to 
keep them safe from digital attacks (Wang et al., 2024c; García-M éndez et al., 2025; 
Vieira et al., 2024). 
The perpetual modification in RSE struct ure is characterised by technological 
innovation and robotics. Contemporary signalling technology based on electronic 
interlocking (EI) and automatic train control (ATC) is lowering human participation and 
cumulative operating efficiency (Drózd and Rosiński, 2023). Communication-based train 
control (CBTC) is a well-known concept in me tropolitan systems that lets block trains 
through by using wireless interaction all th e time, which increases track capacity and 
operational reliability. In addition, train cont rol systems (TCS) and positive train control 
(PTC) use global positioning system (GPS) and on-board detectors for interconnection 
and collision avoidance (Atik et al., 2025; Sun et al., 2019; Liu et al., 2024). The junction 
of internet of things (IoT) devices, artificia l intelligence (AI), and big data statistical 
analyses empowers real-tim e surveillance, fault forecas t, and adaptive conservation 
development. The arrival of 5G and fibre-optic communication systems could only make 
the data broadcast speed faster, permitting quick er making decisions in real-time traffic 
administration systems. Even with these e xpansions, there are still problems to solve, 
such as the excessive cost of converting infra structure, the difficulty of integrating old 
systems, worries about interoperability, the risk of cyberattacks, and the lack of trained 
staff who know how to use modern digital signalling systems (Fernandes et al., 2025; 
Ibadah et al., 2024; Harrod, 2025; Chacón et al., 2024). This chan ge is a compromise 
between moving to smarter, data-driven operational models and keeping costs and 
integration in mind. Comparative analyses ra nging from electromech anical signaling to 
digital control architectures incorporating IoT sensors and pr edictive dashboards indicate 
a transition toward intelligent, autonomous railway systems. 
To address these issues, a physics-informed neural network (PINN)-based edge-cloud 
collaborative digital twin (D T) architecture (Kazemi Naeini et al., 2025) has been 
developed. Thus, it is a hybrid approach that combines physics-based modelling with 
machine learning (ML) that allows real- time emulation, monitoring, and predictive 
analysis of signalling equipment. The framework can be broken down into three layers: 
physical asset layer (IoT sensors for data collection), edge layer (localised devices like 
FPGAs and microcontrollers running lightweight PINN models for low-latency anomaly 
detection), and a cloud layer (computati onally intensive PINN models for model 
refinement and system-level optimisation) (Yang et al., 2024; Zia et al., 2025). The PINN 
structure encodes physical laws at the neural level and requires less data, and makes 
appropriate predictions even if there is no t much data. The edge-cloud sync mechanism 
ensures a smooth data flow, where the edge performs instantaneous calculations and the 
cloud updates every so often to enhance the precision of predictive computations. 
Applications of this architect ure are predictive maintenance of aircraft systems, electric 
vehicle (EV) batteries, smart grid energy management, energy-efficient robotic control,

2 Literature review 
Current research in autonomous railway pr eservation employs ML, deep learning (DL), 
along with decision trees (DT) to improve the ability to find problems, make predictions, 
and make decisions based on data. This integration has led to more accurate diagnoses, 
less frequent maintenance, and a more reliabl e system. Reinforcement learning (RL) and 
unsupervised detection of anomalies are two important techniques that have problems 
with scalability and real-time flexibility. This shows that a physics-based strategy is 
needed to manage ageing railway signalling systems. The time-series current data-driven 
fault diagnosis (TSCD-FD) technology (Sugiana et al., 2023) improves the maintenance 
of railway point machines (RPMs) by removing features from the time domain. It does 
this by using DT and random forests (RF), which are lucrative and more accurate, but 
they can only be used on convinced types of RPMs. The vibration-based signal-based 
fault detection and identification (VS-FDI) pr ocedure (Sun et al., 2023) merges energy

vibration monitoring (WD-VM) method also finds squat difficulties in railway switches

time, although it has trouble adjusting to different data distributions. The double-scale

3 Methodology 
Ageing RSE is a highly coupled electrom echanical-cyber system consisting of 
programmable logic controllers (PLCs), relays, sensors, and control logic circuits for 
coordinating the movement of trains in a fixed block signalling (FBS) network. Each 
track block is isolated and controlled by a PLC-based block controller that enforces train 
separation through sequential signal actuation governed by inductive sensors, axle 
counters, and timed relay logic. In this set up, electrical excitation of the relays with 
logical feedback loops from track circuits determines the signal occupancy states, such as 
red, yellow, and green, to ensure safe trai n spacing under operatio nal constraints. These 
PLC-based block controllers represent the physical layer of the DT, in which continuous 
mirroring of electrical, thermal, and logical st ates occurs virtually for real-time condition 
assessment of the system. The architecture of the ATC and automatic train protection 
(ATP) subsystem extends to include sensor pairs (n, n + 1), delay timers such as timer 
ON-delay (TON), timer coil holding (TCH), an d Jack Kilby (JK) flip-flop circuits to 
encode the logical transitions of block occupation and clearance. During long operational 
lifecycles, contact wear, oxidation-induced resi stance drift, and coil degradation in the 
signalling components lead to several symptoms, including signal delays, spurious 
triggering, and incomplete relay actuation, which are important indicators for predictive 
maintenance.

3.1 Real-time PLC-based railway signallin g health and fault prediction dataset 
The dataset is collected from a Kaggle (https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/programmer3 
/real-time-railway-signalling-fault-dataset) railway atmosphere to mimic the function of 
an industrial-grade monitoring system for RPMs and FBS networks. Every record has 
timestamped data from PLC parts, track mo nitors, and computational units. This data 
shows mechanical, electrical, environmental, and operational shifts that are important for 
finding faults, analysing anomalies, and estimating remaining useful life (RUL). Some of 
the most important characteris tics are voltage, current, temper ature, vibration, relay, 
clock operations (TON, TCH), JK flip-flop outputs, ATC flags, and occupant states. The 
degradation modelling is helped by other parameters including signal transmission delay, 
train headway, and considerations about the environment. Operational diagnostics use the 
CPU load of the PLC, warning codes from the HMI, and types of failures. Element age, 
service activities, and RUL projections all help with life-cycle management. Before cloud 
synchronisation, procedures like filtering an d standardisation are done to the data. The 
API-PINN combines criteria for physical de terioration with real-time analytics to 
improve failure prediction, restoration scheduling, and infrastructure reliability. 
Identifiers make it possible to trace faults in ageing signalling systems correctly by 
providing context. 
3.2 API-PINN model for intelligent fault prognostics and RUL estimation 
The API-PINN model combines RSE physic s and swarm-based optimisation to make 
sure that the predictions are accurate and physically consistent. It incorporates 
electromechanical partial differential equations of relay circuits, vibration, and thermal 
drift into the neural structure, and the PIO layer simulates the navigation of pigeons to 
balance the weights of the neural network at a global level, avoiding local minima and 
slow convergence. The adaptive control dyna mically adjusts the learning parameters 
from the system feedback, and the learning parameters can converge quickly under 
different conditions. Together, these mechanisms allow for the ability to model 
degradation accurately, predict faults, and estimate the RUL in ageing signalling systems.

restrictions on the loss function such that fault prediction and degradation modelling are
