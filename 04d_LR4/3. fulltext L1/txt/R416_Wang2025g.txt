ABSTRACT
This paper introduces a novel method for assessing the safety of post-construction large-span 
spatial structures. Initially, a brief overview is provided of the operating principles and mea -
surement equipment associated with airborne Light Detection and Ranging (LiDAR) technology. 
Subsequently, a methodology for extracting one-dimensional geometric models from point cloud 
data is described. The proposed safety analysis method is then applied to the retractable roof of 
the Shenzhen Gymnasium, with a comparison made between two approaches: the LiDAR-based 
method and the consistent imperfection mode method, in terms of their consideration of the 
impact of initial geometric imperfections on structural mechanical properties. The research 
findings reveal that the one-dimensional geometric models extracted from airborne LiDAR 
measurements can accurately reflect the real-world structural imperfections and can be regarded 
as geometric digital twins of the structures. In comparison with the consistent imperfection mode 
method, finite element models based on airborne LiDAR measurement technology offer a superior 
representation of the real performance of the structures.
1. Introduction
The use of large-span spatial structures in large-scale public buildings is increasing, due to the advantages of light weight, excellent 
mechanical performance, a variety of structural forms, innovative shapes and substantial available space. Examples of such structures 
include the Shenzhen Gymnasium, Shenzhen Financial and Cultural Centre, Shenzhen Natural History Museum and Shenzhen Reform 
and Opening Exhibition Hall, which are currently under construction and are about to start construction. However, the large-span 
spatial structure mostly adopts steel structure, which is susceptible to instability and failure of components or structures. The 
initial defects of the components during the production and transportation processes, the asymmetrical docking of components, and 
the deviation of joints caused by the on-site installation process can exacerbate the instability and failure of the steel structure. 
Consequently, the impact of initial geometric imperfections must be considered during the stability analysis of large-span spatial 
structures.

Regarding the influence of initial geometric imperfections on the stability performance of large-span spatial structures, Ma et al. [ 1 ] 
investigated the effect of different defect shapes and amplitudes on the stability bearing capacity of a stadium roof structure by 
considering the first three buckling modes and their combinations as the defect distribution. Du [ 2 ] investigated the effect of different 
defect modes on the overall stability of a roof structure by considering the first six buckling modes as the defect distribution. Wang [ 3 ] 
investigated the overall stability performance of a portal frame structure system by taking the first elastic buckling mode as the defect 
distribution. Jiang et al. [ 4 ] investigated the influence of different defect sizes on the overall stability performance of a retractable roof 
structure using the first buckling mode as the defect distribution. Díaz et al. [ 5 ] investigated the overall stability performance of 
large-span metal roofs by considering the first mode of buckling analysis as the defect distribution. Liu et al. [ 6 ] proposed a random 
defect mode superposition method for the stability analysis of single layer reticulated shell structures. This method identifies the most 
unfavourable defect mode for structural instability. Xu et al. [ 7 ] introduced a reliability-based method for calculating the critical 
stability load-bearing capacity of single-layer reticulated shell structures and validated its effectiveness through two case studies. Borri 
et al. [ 8 ] used the random defect mode method to study the influence of the distribution pattern and size of initial geometric im -
perfections on the structural stability bearing capacity. Chen et al. [ 9 ] proposed a modelling method for initial geometric imperfections 
in reticulated shell structures based on random field theory and used it to investigate the effect of these imperfections on the structural 
stability performance. Furthermore, Cui et al. [ 10 ] proposed a simultaneous autoregressive model capable of simulating spatially 
varying initial geometric imperfections, including those in irregular reticulated shell structures. As can be seen from the above studies, 
the main methods currently used to account for initial geometric imperfections are the random defect mode method and the consistent 
defect mode method. However, it should be noted that the defect modes assumed in these methods are random, whereas in constructed 
structures the defect mode is deterministic. Therefore, when performing a safety analysis on an existing structure, calculations should 
be based on the actual defect shape.
Digital twins, which serve as digital replicas of real-world assets, can effectively reflect the actual state of structures, including 
stresses, deformations and geometric imperfections. Many scholars have used three-dimensional (3D) laser scanning technology and 
reverse modelling techniques to capture geometric imperfections of structures and create geometric digital twins. Sangani et al. [ 11 ] 
used 3D digital image correlation equipment to scan tubular steel columns with initial geometric defects, and evaluated the residual 
capacity of tubular steel columns according to the scanned model. Chacon et al. [ 12 ] measured the initial geometric defects of planar 
steel frames using a terrestrial laser scanner. Selvaraj [ 13 ] and Zhang [ 14 ] demonstrated that 3D laser scanning can be used to measure 
the initial geometric defects of steel members. Xu et al. [ 15 ] used 3D laser scanning technology to detect the full-field geometric defects 
of circular steel pipes and investigated the influence of geometric defects on the compressive and flexural strength of circular steel 
pipes. Tzortzinis et al. [ 16 ] used 3D laser scanning technology to measure the corrosion of bridges and combined 3D laser scanning 
with finite element to evaluate the residual capacity of steel beams. Ban et al. [ 17 ] used 3D laser scanning technology to measure the 
size and geometric defects of square hollow section short columns, and investigated the local buckling behaviour of square hollow 
section short columns. Lazzari et al. [ 18 ] proposed a method for measuring geometric defects and dimensional deviations in GFRP 
profiles using a 3D contact coordinate measuring machine. Feng et al. [ 19 ] used 3D laser scanning technology to obtain and evaluate 
initial geometric defects such as prestressed CFRP reinforced columns. Zou et al. [ 20 ] attempted to use 3D laser scanning technology to 
obtain the initial geometric defects of steel structural components, and proposed a method to build a digital model of the components 
based on 3D laser point cloud data. Xu et al. [ 21 ] proposed a method to determine the initial geometric defects of steel members and 
establish a geometric model by using 3D scanning, and proved that this method can better predict the initial geometric defects of steel 
members. Aktepe et al. [ 22 , 23 ] used a 3D laser scanner to measure the geometric defects of cold-formed thin-walled steel in detail to 
propose a predictive expression for the initial geometric defects. The experimental results show that the finite element results based on 
the scanning model are in good agreement with the experimental results. Ma et al. [ 24 ] described a method that combines two types of 
laser scanning technologies (terrestrial laser scanning and unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV)-based laser scanning) for detecting the

dimensional quality of bridges after construction. Lu et al. [ 25 ] proposed an automated approach to create geometric digital twin 
models of bridges based on point cloud data. Drobnyi et al. [ 26 ] presented a method for automatically detecting connectivity re -
lationships in point clouds of buildings, with the aim of improving the efficiency of constructing geometric digital twin models. Both 
Rúa et al. [ 27 ] and Hermida [ 28 ] proposed methods for extracting variable geometric lines from point cloud data, specifically

initial geometric imperfections of components and create geometric digital twins of structures. However, most current research uses 
terrestrial 3D laser scanners to acquire point cloud data of structures, and the geometric digital twins produced are mostly in the form 
of 3D solid models. Terrestrial 3D laser scanners face challenges in measuring spatial structures with large spans, and it is not 
economically feasible to use 3D solid models for computational analysis of such structures.
Therefore, in order to accurately obtain the real geometric defects of the large-span spatial structure and reduce the computational 
effort of the finite element model, this paper proposes a method to obtain the real geometric defects of the structure by using the 
airborne LiDAR measurement technology. At the same time, the method of extracting a one-dimensional geometric model from point 
cloud data is described. Taking the movable roof of Shenzhen Gymnasium as an example, a finite element model based on LiDAR is 
established, and the difference of finite element results under different initial geometric defect modes is explored. The framework of 
digital twin safety analysis of large-span spatial structure based on airborne LiDAR is shown in Fig. 1 .
2. Engineering overview
The Shenzhen Gymnasium has a total of 6 floors, with a total floor area of 57,600 square metres and a building height of 45.00 m. It 
consists of a steel roof structure and a lower grandstand. The gymnasium is shown in Fig. 2 . The lower grandstand adopts the reinforced 
concrete frame-shear wall structural system, and the lower grandstand is the supporting structure of the roof steel structure canopy. 
The roof steel structure canopy is a radial retractable roof composed of one fixed roof and two movable roofs. Each movable roof is 
supported by eight trolleys above the fixed roof. The fixed roof and the movable roof are mainly composed of orthogonal plane truss 
system, where the span of the movable roof is 65 m and the width is 25 m.

3. Airborne LiDAR measurement principle and equipment introduction
3.1. Measurement principle
Airborne LiDAR measurement system is an active measurement system composed of aircraft, dynamic differential global posi -
tioning system, inertial navigation system and laser rangefinder. It takes the aircraft as the carrying platform of the whole mea -
surement system, uses the dynamic differential global positioning system to record the coordinate position of the aircraft, uses the 
inertial navigation system to record the navigation parameters such as the attitude, speed and position of the aircraft, and uses the laser 
ranging system to measure the distance and orientation between the aircraft and the measurement object.

3.2. Equipment
This project uses Zenmuse L1 for scanning, as shown in Fig. 4 . Zenmuse L1 is an integrated industrial laser load that integrates 
LIVOX ™ LiDAR, high-precision inertial navigation and mapping camera, and can quickly generate high-precision point clouds and 
models. The Zenmuse L1 is equipped with a three-axis pan-tilt camera that can be installed on the DJ designated flight platform and 
supports the DJI Pilot App. Together with DJI Terra software, it provides an integrated solution for 3D scene reconstruction and high- 
precision 3D reconstruction of complex scenes in all weather conditions, efficiently and in real time. The main technical parameters of 
the Zenmuse L1 are listed in Table 1 .
The main tasks of using Zenmuse L1 to collect point cloud data outdoors are as follows: 1. Make sure that the L1 load is correctly 
installed on the aircraft, to avoid the L1 load falling in the operation project; 2. Open the power supply of the aircraft and the remote 
control one by one, and make sure that the aircraft and the remote control complete the frequency matching; 3. Enter the DJI Pilot App 
manual flight interface, select the corresponding RTK service type, and make sure that the positioning and orientation state of RTK is 
FIX; 4. The L1 load is preheated for 3 – 5 min after starting, to make sure that the high-precision inertial navigation is in a stable working 
temperature environment; 5. According to the light conditions in the field, set the camera parameters, select the camera focusing 
model, Zenmuse L1 supports MF (manual focusing), AFC (automatic continuous focusing) and AFS (automatic single point focusing); 
6. Select the route task type, Zenmuse L1 supports four types of flight including flight, aerial photography, ground flight and manual 
flight, and set the task parameters; 7. Save the mission, upload the route and execute the mission. During mission execution, the point 
cloud can be paused or started to record at any time, and the point cloud results can be previewed in real time during the scanning 
process; 8. Turn off the aircraft power after the mission.
It should be noted that: 1. During the data collection process, it is imperative that the aircraft is in motion and the flight speed 
exceeds 2 m per second. In the absence of these conditions, DJI Terra will reconstruct errors in data post-processing. 2. Following 100 s 
of uninterrupted flight, it is necessary to manually accelerate or decelerate, or alternatively click ‘Calibration flight ’ to calibrate the 
inertial navigation. 3. Following the conclusion of data acquisition, manual inertial navigation calibration is mandatory. L1 will 
automatically record the inertial navigation data for approximately 2 min after the point cloud acquisition for DJI Terra ’ s posterior 
difference decomposition calculation.
4. Point cloud data processing
In the initial phase, obvious noise points are manually removed. Subsequently, Gaussian filtering and the K-means algorithm are 
applied, respectively, to eliminate noise points between structural members. It is crucial to preserve the geometric feature information 
of the roof structure during the noise reduction process.

5. Safety analysis by FEM
To explore the differences between point cloud model and theoretical model, and to compare the differences between the two 
methods (airborne LiDAR method and consistent defect mode method) in considering the impact of initial geometric defects on 
structural performance, this paper established theoretical model, LiDAR model, and consistent defect model, respectively. The three 
finite element models are shown in Fig. 11 . Among them, the theoretical model is the model used in structural design; the LiDAR model 
is a model that modifies the coordinate values of some nodes according to the LiDAR scanning results; the consistent defect model 
refers to a model where the defect size is 1/300 of the span of the movable roof, and the defect form is the first buckling mode under 
gravity.
5.1. Finite element modelling method
When the finite element model is established, the movable roof adopts BEAM189 element with a length of 1 m. All the members are 
connected by common nodes, and the translational displacement of the node XYZ in three directions at the trolley position is con -
strained. Q355B steel is used in the movable roof, and the steel in the finite element is bilinear elastic-plastic constitutive. The material 
parameters are shown in Table 2 . During the analysis, the node load is applied on the nodes on the top of the roof, and the bearing 
capacity of the movable roof is determined by gradually increasing the size of the node load.

structure after completion and the theoretical design model. The finite element model based on LiDAR can better reflect the influence 
of initial geometric defects on the mechanical properties of the structure than the finite element model based on the consistent defect 
mode method.
6. Conclusion
In order to evaluate the safety of large-span space structures after completion and explore the impact of actual initial geometric 
defects on the mechanical properties of large-span space structures, this paper combines airborne LiDAR measurement technology with 
structural safety analysis. The main research results are as follows.

1. A method of extracting one-dimensional geometric model from point cloud model is proposed. The geometric model based on this

2. Propose a digital twin safety analysis method that combines airborne LiDAR measurement technology with structural safety

Compared to the uniform buckling mode method, the finite element model based on LiDAR can better reflect the influence of initial 
geometric defects on the mechanical properties of the structure.
