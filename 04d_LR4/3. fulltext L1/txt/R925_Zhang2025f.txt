ABSTRACT
Cable-supported bridges are critical civil infrastructure. With increasing traffic volume and extreme weather 
conditions, these bridges face threats to their functional and structural safety during their service life, necessi -
tating timely health monitoring and condition assessment. In recent years, digital twin (DT) has achieved sig -
nificant progress in bridge monitoring and assessment, effectively addressing the shortcomings of traditional 
methods. However, current bridge DTs, especially those for cable-supported bridges, still face a series of issues, 
such as ambiguous concepts and lack of unified standards. To enrich the discussion on this topic, this paper 
explores the current research trends and frameworks of bridge DT through bibliometric analysis and a 
comprehensive review of the latest advancements, providing a systematic overview of relevant research on 
health monitoring and condition assessment of cable-supported bridges based on DT. Firstly, the origin, defi -
nition, and characteristics of DT are introduced. Secondly, a systematic literature review is employed to statis -
tically analyze the current research status and discuss the construction methods of the DT system. Subsequently, 
a novel three-layer framework for DT is proposed, which can be applied to health monitoring and condition 
assessment of cable-supported bridges. Finally, the challenges and future trends of DT in this field are discussed. 
The challenges consist of seven aspects, which are data management, modeling, security and privacy, service 
applications, intelligence, integration, and standardization. Future DT development will focus on advancing 
digitization, integration, and intelligence, while deepening theoretical research and practical applications. The 
establishment of a reliable operational condition assessment framework is also an important trend.
1. Introduction
Cable-supported bridges, as crucial transportation infrastructure, 
face significant threats to their functional and structural safety over their 
service life due to material degradation and various other influencing 
factors, including traffic overloads, vehicle or ship collisions, fatigue 
cracking, steel corrosion, fires, typhoons, earthquakes, and especially 
the ever-increasing traffic demands and changing weather conditions 
[1] . Garg et al. [2] analyzed the failure causes of 2010 bridges from 1977 
to 2017 and found that natural disasters accounted for 80.30 %, material 
degradation for 10.10 %, traffic overloads for 3.28 %, and 
human-induced disasters for 2.19 %. In reality, there are numerous 
specific cases of bridge damage. For example, the Humber Bridge in the 
UK, which experienced excessive wear of its bearings due to extreme 
loads [3] , the Martinus Nijhoff Cable-Stayed Bridge in the Netherlands, 
which showed fatigue damage much earlier than expected during its 
design service life [4] , and the Jiangyin Suspension Bridge in China, 
which suffered from the failure of expansion joints due to traffic loads 
[5] . Therefore, long-term and effective health monitoring and condition 
assessment of cable-supported bridges throughout their service life are 
crucial. Traditional monitoring and assessment methods for 
cable-supported bridges have met certain demands but still face chal -
lenges or issues [6 – 8] .
Currently, digital twin (DT) has become a research hotspot [9 – 11]
due to its unique advantages and is widely used for monitoring, pre -
dictive maintenance, and analysis of various physical assets. DT can be 
considered as a virtual model that represents an object throughout its 
lifecycle, maintaining its state, properties, and operational dynamics. 
Compared to traditional models, DT can provide richer information 
about structural conditions and, by integrating with the latest

2. Background
2.1. Cable-supported bridges
Cable-supported bridges, primarily consisting of suspension bridges 
and cable-stayed bridges, represent one of the most significant in -
vestments in the national transportation network. The main components 
include towers, main cable/stay cables, and the deck. Fig. 1 illustrates 
schematic diagrams of both suspension bridge and cable-stayed bridge. 
Although cable-supported bridges account for only about 1.5 % of all 
bridges, they are an effective structural form for large spans and intri -
cate environments [13 – 15] . In bridge categories with spans exceeding 
200 m, cable-supported bridges account for over 90 % [16] . With a 
service life exceeding 100 years, cable-supported bridges may undergo 
material aging, traffic loads [17] , hurricanes or typhoons [14] , earth -
quakes [18] , fires [16] , etc., during their lifespan, which can signifi -
cantly impact the functionality and structural safety of the bridges.
2.2. Methods of health monitoring and condition assessment
Ensuring the safety and uninterrupted operation of cable-supported 
bridges is a key concern for stakeholders. Health monitoring and con -
dition assessment of these bridges are essential processes for addressing 
the aforementioned issues.
There are various traditional methods for health monitoring and 
condition assessment of cable-supported bridges. The most basic 
method, and one adopted by most countries, is visual inspection for 
bridge condition assessment. However, visual inspection involves 
extensive manual labor and is prone to neglecting defects [6,8] . Bridge 
testing can inspect the overall structure or major components of a 
bridge, providing valuable data support for bridge monitoring and 
assessment. But this method typically requires bridge closures, which 
can have certain traffic and economic impacts. Structural Health 
Monitoring (SHM) is described as the application of sensing technology 
and analysis of structural characteristics to identify structural damage or 
degradation [19] . The development of SHM has facilitated the 
advancement of health monitoring and condition assessment of 
cable-supported bridges [20 – 25] , enabling real-time diagnosis of the 
health status of both local components and the overall structure of

Building Information Modeling (BIM) creates a virtual three-

assessment of bridge performance. However, this method has high re -

overfitting and poor interpretability [14] .
2.3. Digital twin
2.3.1. Origin and definition of digital twin
In recent years, DT has been widely used in various fields [40] for the 
monitoring, analysis, and predictive maintenance of numerous physical 
assets, including buildings [41 – 43] , factories, ships, cranes, aircraft, 
cities [44] , and railways [45,46] . DT allows for personalized de -
scriptions of physical assets in the form of computational models or 
parameters of interest, which evolve over time and maintain synchro -
nization with the physical entities via data collection devices.
The origin of the DT can be traced back to 2002, when Grieves [47, 
48] proposed a conceptual model in a speech and used it in a course at 
the University of Michigan in 2003. Although the term "Digital Twin" did 
not appear in the model, it already encompassed all the essential ele -
ments of a DT, including real space, virtual space, bidirectional 
connection between real space and virtual space via data or information 
flow [49] . Therefore, it is widely recognized as the earliest introduction 
of the concept or idea of DT [50] . In 2010, the term "Digital Twin" was 
first coined by the National Aeronautics and Space Administration 
(NASA) [51] , along with its formal definition.
Although DT has gained increasing popularity in academic research 
and industry practice, there is no universally accepted definition for it. 
Scholars in different fields have proposed various interpretations and 
definitions of DT. By synthesizing these previous definitions, this paper 
proposes the following definition of DT in the context of health moni -
toring and condition assessment of cable-supported bridges: a high- 
fidelity digital virtual representation of a cable-supported bridge, inte -
grated with multiple technologies, capable of real-time data interaction 
with the physical entity and autonomous model updating, providing 
decision support and critical services for the health monitoring and 
condition assessment of the physical entity.
2.3.2. Digital twin characteristics
Fig. 2 illustrates the DT framework, which comprises three essential 
elements [47,52] : (1) the physical entity in the physical environment; 
(2) the DT in the virtual environment; and (3) the bidirectional 
connection between the physical entity and the DT, represented by ar -
rows in the figure, with arrow directions indicating the direction of the 
connection. The "Collect" connection in Fig. 2 denotes the process of 
collecting data from the physical entity. Due to the vast amount of 
collected data and the possibility of some data being invalid or having 
indistinct features, data processing is necessary, corresponding to the 
"Process" connection. The "Feedback" connection indicates that the DT 
model can provide reverse feedback on the data after its establishment. 
The "Service" connection represents various services offered by the DT, 
such as simulation, decision-making, control, health monitoring, con -
dition assessment, and data-driven operations of the physical entity, 
aimed at enhancing system convenience, reliability, and productivity.
DT possesses capabilities such as high integration and intelligence, 
real-time bidirectional data transmission [53] , autonomous and rapid 
model updating [54] , and the ability to reconstruct stress fields in areas 
lacking measurement data [24] . These capabilities effectively comple -
ment the deficiencies of traditional monitoring and assessment methods, 
assisting stakeholders in making reliable and swift decisions. Conse -
quently, this enhances bridge performance, extends service life, and 
ultimately reduces maintenance and operational costs for the 
infrastructure.
2.3.3. Concept clarification
There are multiple concepts similar to the DT, such as Simulation 
(SIM), Building Information Modeling (BIM), Cyber-Physical Systems 
(CPS), and the Internet of Things (IoT). These similar concepts can easily 
be confused with the DT. Therefore, comparing these concepts with the 
DT can help to clarify the distinctions between them.
While the DT may seem similar to SIM, it is far more than that. A DT 
is a real-time, high-fidelity depiction of the operational dynamics of its 
corresponding physical entity. Operational dynamics are a crucial aspect 
of DT because its behavior is grounded in near-real-time data sourced 
from the actual physical entity. SIM emphasizes potential occurrences in 
the real world, rather than what is currently happening. A DT can serve 
multiple purposes such as monitoring, control, assessment, diagnosis, 
and prediction, extending beyond mere simulation.
BIM serves as a digital depiction of the physical and functional at -
tributes of a building object [55] . Although both BIM and DT rely on 
virtual models, BIM emphasizes the existing physical entity it corre -
sponds to, while DT has the capability to depict something that is 
nonexistent or yet to be built. Additionally, DT must reflect the current 
state of the physical entity in a timely manner, which is not a require -
ment for BIM [56] .
A CPS is a multidimensional complex system that integrates 
computation, networking, and physical environments. Although both 
CPS and DT highlight the existence of a physical entity and involve 
timely data transmission between physical entity and virtual model, a 
DT necessitates a virtual model, which is not a necessity for a CPS (as 
CPS focuses more on the "cyber" aspect). Furthermore, a DT mandates a 
twinning relationship between the virtual model and the physical entity, 
which is not a requirement for CPS [56] .
IOT refers to the connection between networks of physical assets, 
enabling data to flow among them. The secure deployment of commu -
nication protocols, the Internet, and computer networks facilitates this 
connection. Nevertheless, while it encompasses connectivity, IOT does 
not encompass the notion of a virtual model within the cyberspace [57] .

3. Research methodology and bibliometric analysis
3.1. Research methodology
The research method employed in this paper is Systematic Literature 
Review (SLR), which is a systematic and comprehensive approach to 
selecting, evaluating, and interpreting existing research within a specific 
field. As such, SLR can synthesize evidence and identify the current 
research status, challenges, and opportunities based on relevant studies 
[58] . The literature review strategy is illustrated in Fig. 3 .
Selecting appropriate search terms as the basis for database searches 
is crucial given the scope and theme of this paper. "Digital twin" is the 
technology of primary focus in this paper, and hence it is chosen as the 
primary search term. It should be noted that although there are terms 
closely related to the concept of DT, such as Digital model, Simulation 
model, and Surrogate model, they are not equivalent to DT [59] . 
Therefore, only "Digital twin" is selected as the primary search term. 
There are diverse types of bridges, such as beam bridges and arch 
bridges, but this paper primarily focuses on cable-supported bridges, 
which mainly include suspension bridges and cable-stayed bridges. 
Therefore, the following two sets of terms are set as search terms, with 
the search fields being Title, Abstract, Keywords or indexing. Consid -
ering that some studies are applicable to all types of bridges, including 
cable-supported bridges, supplementary searches are conducted with 
additional phrases "bridge and health monitoring" and "bridge and 
condition assessment" added to the second set of search terms. 
• Search Term 1: "Digital twin".
• Search Term 2: "cable-supported bridge", "suspension bridge", "cable- 
stayed bridge", "bridge and health monitoring", "bridge and condition 
assessment".
To comprehensively collect existing research, data was gathered 
from four renowned databases: Scopus, ScienceDirect, Web of Science 
(WOS), and IEEE Xplore. All journal articles or conference proceedings 
published in English were included, and considering that 2013 is 
regarded as the nascent stage of DT [60] , the search period spanned from 
January 1, 2013, to August 5, 2024.
Based on the aforementioned criteria, a total of 416 records were 
retrieved from the four databases. Subsequently, these 416 records were 
screened according to the following three principles: (1) elimination of 
duplicate records; (2) exclusion of records that were inconsistent with 
the topic discussed in this paper; (3) selection of papers that included 
specific application cases related to the topic. After this screening 
process, 19 papers [18,24,49,61 – 76] were ultimately obtained. These 
papers will be discussed and analyzed in the subsequent sections of this 
paper. The number of finally obtained papers indicates that the appli -
cation of DT in health monitoring and condition assessment of 
cable-supported bridges is currently in its initial stage, and related 
research is quite limited.

4. Methodologies and technologies in digital twin system
4.1. Data collection methods
Data is a crucial component of the DT system, including data 
generated by both the physical entity and its DT. This subsection mainly 
introduces the data collection methods for the physical entity, which 
play a key role in the establishment of DT model and real-time syn -
chronization between physical entity and its DT.
4.1.1. Historical documentation
Historical documentation for bridges includes design information, 
construction documents, historical records, inspection reports, regula -
tory standards, and expert decision-making information. These data can 
provide support for the establishment of a DT model, such as the di -
mensions, material properties, and age of the physical entity.
4.1.2. Contact sensors
Deploying contact sensors is a commonly used method in bridge 
health monitoring and condition assessment to obtain bridge perfor -
mance indicators. Common contact sensors include stress and strain 
sensors, displacement sensors, vibration sensors, temperature sensors, 
inclination sensors, anchor cable dynamometer, weigh-in-motion (WIM) 
sensors, etc. Installing multiple sensors at different locations of the 
structure according to requirements can obtain data information under 
normal operating conditions or extreme weather events, such as stress 
and strain, displacement, acceleration, velocity, temperature, inclina -
tion angle, cable force, and traffic load.
4.1.3. Laser and radar
Laser scanner and Ground-Penetrating Radar (GPR), as non-contact 
and non-destructive technologies, have been widely recognized and 
applied.
Laser scanner can quickly and accurately collect point cloud data 
from the surface of the physical entity. Based on the position of the laser 
sensor during data collection, it can be classified into three types: 
ground, aerial, and mobile. Terrestrial Laser Scanner (TLS) is capable of 
capturing intricate details of the entire scene along with its color attri -
butes. Due to advantages such as sub-millimeter accuracy and fast 
scanning speed, this technology has been used in numerous studies [81]
to obtain bridge information. Mounting laser scanners on equipment 
such as cars and unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) makes scanning op -
erations more flexible, with a broader scanning coverage and higher 
collection efficiency.
GPR stands as one of the most extensively researched non-contact 
technologies. It determines internal defects and reinforcement layouts 
of bridge decks by quantifying the attenuation of reflected waves [82, 
83] , and also provides important data support for the construction of DT 
system.
4.1.4. Image technology
With the rapid development of UAV technology and photographic 
techniques, UAV photography has become a practical and efficient 
method for data acquisition [84,85] . Images captured by UAVs can be 
processed and used for the construction and updating of the DT model. 
Although UAV photography offers advantages such as rapid, precise, 
and extensive remote data collection, there are still certain issues, such 
as path planning, image matching quality, image orientation accuracy, 
and modeling geometric accuracy. Additionally, in practical applica -
tions, there are blind spots where UAVs cannot capture images, 
requiring manual image capture by humans for areas inaccessible to 
UAVs.
Infrared Thermography (IRT) emits infrared radiation towards the 
physical entity and uses images to convert the thermal distribution data

4.1.5. Multi-method integration
SHM system is used for data collection and analysis of the physical 
entity, where the data collection method involves deploying devices 
(sensors) on the physical entity to monitor any changes. These devices 
can be a combination of contact and non-contact sensors.
4.2. Data transmission methods
Data transmission in DT is bidirectional, which provides a guarantee 
for real-time synchronization of information between DT and the 
physical entity. Moreover, data transmission must possess the capability 
to transfer data with satisfactory latency and cost [66] , in order to 
facilitate the updating of the DT model and its feedback and services to 
the physical entity.
4.2.1. Wired transmission
Wired connections, such as fieldbuses and Ethernet, are among the 
most commonly used methods for data transmission. Due to their low 
cost and sufficient bandwidth, they can achieve fast and stable data 
transmission [93] , and are therefore widely used for data transmission 
between DT and physical entity.
4.2.2. Wireless transmission
Wireless Sensor Networks (WSN) offer high energy efficiency and 
reliability in data transmission. Compared to wired transmission, they 
are more flexible and convenient for communication, making them 
suitable for monitoring bridges or components that are difficult to access 
via wired connections [94] . WSN is highly suitable for real-time data 
collection scenarios and holds a pivotal role in the construction of the DT 
system. Wireless communication technologies can be classified into 
short-range, medium-range, and long-range based on communication 
distance. Short-range wireless communication technologies include 
Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, and Zigbee. Medium-range wireless data transmission 
technologies include commercial cellular networks (such as 3 G, 4 G, 
and 5 G) and low-power wide-area networks (LPWAN, such as NB-IoT 
and LTE-M). Long-range wireless transmission technologies include 
LoRa, Sigfox, and Ingenu, which are ideal for bridge monitoring in 
remote areas with limited resources.
Advancements in wireless transmission technology have effectively 
facilitated the development of DT technology in the field of health 
monitoring and condition assessment of civil infrastructures, which has 
been extensively studied by scholars. Lynch [95] introduced the char -
acteristics, applicable scenarios, and applications of wireless trans -
mission technology, proposing a wireless active sensing unit design 
method. Using the Alamosa Canyon Bridge in New Mexico as an 
example, the accuracy and reliability of the wireless sensing units were 
verified. Zou et al. [96] designed an efficient multihop communication 
service, incorporating a flooding-based routing method and a theoreti -
cally optimal data collection protocol within the proposed protocols, 
achieving optimization of network performance and energy consump -
tion. To address the issue of poor synchronous sensing during data 
collection, Fu et al. [97] proposed a time synchronization method suit -
able for wireless monitoring of civil infrastructures during emergencies, 
improving the efficiency and precision of time synchronization.

4.3. Digital twin modeling methods
As the primary framework of DT, the generation of bridge’s virtual 
model is a crucial and complex stage. The quality of the virtual model 
holds considerable significance in the practical application of the DT 
[100] . The following section outlines four modeling methods for DT.
4.3.1. Geometric-based method
Geometric modeling can be accomplished using 3D modeling soft -
ware such as AutoCAD [71] , Autodesk Civil 3D [49] , Revit [70] , Tekla 
Structures [67] , and Solidworks. Additionally, commercial reverse en -
gineering software can be employed to perform reverse modeling on 
point cloud data [101] , which can originate from laser scanning or 
photogrammetry techniques, ultimately constructing a visual mapping 
of the geometric appearance of the physical entity.
4.3.2. Finite element method
Finite Element Method (FEM) modeling is a widely adopted 
modeling technique [102] , with commonly used software such as Aba -
qus, MSC.Marc [63] , Midas Gen [65,66] , Ansys [73,75] , etc. FEM em -
ploys discretization-derived mathematical approximations, and its 
complexity is positively correlated with the complexity of the physical 
entity. This results in FEM typically requiring excessive computational 
resources, making it difficult for the constructed virtual model to 
maintain real-time synchronization with the physical entity. Moreover, 
limitations in the size and time step of the virtual models make them 
unsuitable for real-time simulation environments in subsequent services 
[103,104] . Additionally, FEM modeling faces challenges in accurate 
visualization as it often relies on certain simplification methods.
4.3.3. Data-driven method
Data-driven modeling is based on data derivation and parameteri -
zation. Specifically, it employs artificial intelligence (AI) and machine 
learning (ML) techniques [105] to extract both implicit and explicit 
knowledge underlying specific phenomena of the physical entity from 
data. Data-driven models can reduce modeling workload and offer 
higher accuracy and broader adaptability. However, data-driven models 
have high requirements for the quantity and quality of data, and their 
black-box nature, due to opaque reasoning mechanisms and lack of 
physical consistency, poses certain obstacles to their use.
4.3.4. Multi-method integration
Combining multiple modeling methods can, to some extent, 
compensate for the deficiencies of each individual method. For example, 
Kang et al. [106] introduced a DT modeling method based on multi -
media knowledge by combining the FEM and data-driven approaches. 
This method balances the advantages of both FEM and data-driven 
methods, facilitating better service for bridge health monitoring. Addi -
tionally, incorporating physical information (such as physical formulas, 
principles, etc.) into data-driven methods can enhance the interpret -
ability of the model.
4.4. Services provided by the digital twin
The application purpose of DT is to provide services to physical en -
tities, such as monitoring, prediction, simulation, decision-making, 
lifecycle management, and condition assessment [24,49] , enhancing 
the system’s reliability, convenience, and overall productivity.
Monitoring service is regarded as a key service for bridge DT, 
allowing real-time understanding of the performance and status of the 
physical bridge [107] . The prediction service aims to forecast the future 
behavior, health status, and structural lifespan of the physical bridge, 
providing a basis for proactive measures and enhancing reliability. The 
simulation service replicates real-world scenarios in a virtual environ -
ment with high fidelity for structural integrity analysis. The 
decision-making service integrates and analyzes data from monitoring, 
prediction, and simulation services to form accurate and effective de -
cisions, while also facilitating strategic planning throughout the bridge’s 
lifecycle [67] . Lifecycle management service can reduce long-term 
repair costs, optimizing operational costs, structural integrity, and 
safety [108] . Condition assessment service utilizes multi-source data 
fusion to evaluate the current health status and structural integrity of the

4.5. Discussion on digital twin system implementation
This section outlines the methodologies for constructing a DT system 
tailored for health monitoring and condition assessment of cable- 
supported bridge. The data acquisition techniques in Section 4.1 em -
phasizes integrating diverse sources, including historical documenta -
tion, contact sensors and advanced non-contact methods such as laser 
scanning, radar, UAV imagery, and hyperspectral imaging. While each 
method offers unique advantages, their combined application enables 
comprehensive data capture encompassing geometric, environmental, 
and structural response parameters. However, challenges remain, 
including sensor durability, precision, UAV blind spots, and the 
complexity of managing heterogeneous, high-volume data.
Section 4.2 addresses data transmission, which is essential for real- 
time synchronization between the physical and virtual entities. Wired 
methods offer high reliability and bandwidth, while wireless solutions 
provide flexibility for remote and inaccessible components. Ensuring 
low latency, high reliability, and robust protocols remains paramount, 
especially given the substantial data volumes generated by continuous 
monitoring systems.
Section 4.3 reviews DT modeling techniques. Geometric modeling 
provides essential visual fidelity but inherently lacks the capability to 
simulate physical behavior. FEM offers valuable physics-based simula -
tion capabilities, yet faces significant computational burdens and limi -
tations in accurate visualization. Data-driven models promise efficiency 
and adaptability but demand high-quality, extensive datasets and suffer 
from interpretability issues due to their "black-box" nature. Thus, hybrid 
approaches, particularly combining FEM with data-driven techniques or 
incorporating physical knowledge into ML models, emerges as a highly 
promising solution to balance accuracy, computational efficiency, and 
interpretability.
Finally, Section 4.4 outlines the core services enabled by DT systems, 
including monitoring, prediction, simulation, decision-making, lifecycle 
management, and condition assessment. These services move beyond 
mere data visualization towards generating actionable insights and 
enabling proactive management. The effectiveness of these services is 
fundamentally dependent on the robustness and synergy of data acqui -
sition, transmission, and modeling.

5.3. Discussion on digital twin applications
This section systematically reviews 19 practical applications of DT in 
health monitoring and condition assessment of cable-supported bridges. 
These applications are categorized into real-world structures ( Section 
5.1 ) and physical scaled models ( Section 5.2 ). The case studies collec -
tively demonstrate the versatility of DT frameworks in integrating 
diverse technologies. These technologies include laser scanning, IoT 
sensors, BIM, machine learning, and finite element modeling. They 
enable real-time monitoring, predictive analytics, and condition evalu -
ation. Key observations are as follows. 
(1) While all studies leverage DT principles, methodologies vary 
significantly in data acquisition (e.g., contact sensors vs. UAV- 
based imaging), modeling techniques (FEM vs. data-driven sur -
rogates), and service outputs (fatigue prediction vs. seismic 
assessment). This reflects the context-specific adaptability of DTs 
but highlights a lack of standardized workflows.
(2) Applications to full-scale bridges (e.g., Clifton Suspension Bridge, 
Yangtze River Bridge) validate DT robustness under operational 
conditions, yet computational demands for high-fidelity models 
remain challenging. In contrast, physical models (e.g., Sutong 
Bridge prototype) facilitate controlled validation but may not 
fully capture complex environmental interactions.
(3) Few studies fully exploit bidirectional DT capabilities; most 
emphasize data-to-model synchronization but seldom use model- 
to-physical feedback for autonomous control or adaptive main -
tenance. Additionally, interoperability between legacy moni -
toring systems and emerging DT architectures requires further 
attention.
These findings underscore DT’s transformative potential in 
enhancing bridge resilience but also reveal critical research needs, i.e., 
standardization of modular DT frameworks, validation of long-term 
autonomy under extreme events, and scalable solutions for legacy 
infrastructure integration.
6. The three-layer digital twin framework
Through compiling and analyzing existing research, this paper pro -
poses a three-layer framework for DT, as shown in Fig. 6 . This frame -
work is applicable to the field of health monitoring and condition 
assessment of cable-supported bridges, clearly presenting the content 
and processes of the DT system. It provides a reference for the research 
and application of DT in cable-supported bridges. Furthermore, the DT 
constructed within this framework incorporates the advantages of both 
the finite element model and the data-driven model, enabling it to better 
serve the physical entity. This framework consists of three layers: the 
data layer, the model layer, and the service layer.
The data layer encompasses data collection, transmission, and pro -
cessing, serving as the most fundamental layer within the three-layer 
framework. Cable-supported bridges requiring health monitoring and 
condition assessment typically possess historical documentation, 
including design, construction, inspection, and decision-making mate -
rials. Furthermore, a SHM system is generally designed and installed on 
the bridge during its construction phase to facilitate subsequent moni -
toring activities during operation. Therefore, data collection can rely on 
both historical documentation and the SHM system. To obtain as much 
bridge data as possible, instruments utilizing technologies such as 
cameras, UVAs, lasers, and radars can be deployed on cable-supported 
bridges as supplementary devices, in addition to traditional sensors. 
Data transmission employs a combination of wired and wireless 
methods, tailored to the actual conditions of the bridge, to achieve the 
most suitable data transmission arrangement. Data processing begins 
with pre-processing large volumes of data, including de-noising, outlier 
detection, cleaning, and normalization. Subsequently, further process -
ing is conducted based on the detailed requirements of operation man -
agers. Finally, the processed data is stored in a database for future use.
The model layer encompasses model construction and model 
updating. The DT model is constructed using a combined approach of 
finite element modeling and data-driven modeling, harnessing the ad -
vantages of both methods. For cable-supported bridges with ample 
historical documentation, such as sizes, positions, and material prop -
erties, the bridge model can be established using finite element software 
Abaqus [109] . For cable-supported bridges with limited historical 
documentation, point cloud and image data from the database can be 
utilized. Specifically, point cloud data is first converted into a mesh 
model using surface reconstruction algorithms, then transformed into 
the INP file format acceptable by Abaqus to obtain the bridge’s Abaqus 
model. Subsequently, feature extraction is performed on the image data 
to obtain key data, which can be used for setting boundary conditions 
and subsequent simulation analysis in the bridge’s Abaqus model. This 
finite element model offers advantages such as good visualization and 
ease of simulation.
In addition to the finite element method, data-driven modeling is 
also employed as a complement to the finite element model. It utilizes 
Python software [110] and some cutting-edge, efficient algorithms and 
programs to establish a data-driven model of the cable-supported bridge. 
When dealing with complex modeling situations or when the service 
provided by the finite element model is unsatisfactory, the data-driven

model can effectively reduce modeling workload and offer higher ac -
curacy and broader adaptability. A key characteristic of DT is the 
real-time connection and data transmission between the virtual model 
and the physical entity, which requires the virtual model to be updated 
in real-time. This framework provides an approach for updating the 
virtual model, which is based on the real-time updated database and 
achieves automated updating of finite element model parameters and 
data-driven model parameters through Python scripts. Specifically, Py -
thon scripts are used to read data from the database in real-time and 
automatically modify the input file (INP file) of the Abaqus model to 
achieve real-time updating of the finite element model. Python scripts 
are also employed for recalculating and optimizing parameters in the 
data-driven model to achieve real-time updating.
The service layer refers to the services provided by the DT to the 
physical entity, including monitoring, prediction, simulation, assess -
ment, decision and management. These six services are all crucial for 
health monitoring and condition assessment of cable-supported bridges. 
They can be categorized into basic services and advanced services. The 
basic services include monitoring, prediction, and simulation, which 
provide essential data and analysis support for understanding the cur -
rent and future behavior of the bridge. The advanced services include 
assessment, decision making, and management, which build on the basic 
services to support complex operational and management activities.

7. Challenges and future trends
7.1. Challenges
The application of DT may encounter some challenges. According to 
the results of the literature review in this paper, the following challenges 
may be faced.
In terms of data management, higher-precision and durable sensors 
are required for data collection and data loss should be minimized. 
When collecting huge amounts of data, low latency and high reliability 
must be ensured during data transmission and processing.
In terms of modeling, the real-time performance and fidelity of 
existing virtual models are not outstanding. Virtual models need to 
reflect physical entities in a more real-time and high-fidelity manner. 
Additionally, model updating and fusion require further enhancement.
In terms of security and privacy, while DT may need to be connected 
to a network to exhibit its excellent characteristics, a large amount of 
data may face the risks of network threats and attacks, as well as data 
theft or tampering, necessitating strengthened supervision and control.
In terms of service applications, the application of DT in cable- 
supported bridges is still limited, and more engineering practice and 
testing are needed to better serve health monitoring, condition assess -
ment, and early warning.
In terms of intelligence, scholars and stakeholders expect DT to be a 
system that can autonomously, adaptively, and self-evolvingly perform 
condition assessment, prediction, and decision-making based on 
collected data. However, current DT still requires human intervention, 
and advanced technologies need to further enhance its intelligence.
In terms of integration, DT involves the integration of multiple 
technologies and disciplines, and how to efficiently and fully integrate 
these systems remains a challenge.
In terms of standardization, there is currently a lack of unified 
standards in the bridge field to guide the construction of DT systems and 
the evaluation of model quality.
7.2. Future trends
Through the review and discussion of relevant research on the 
application of DT, it is found that the ultimate goal is to construct a DT 
system for the physical entity of cable-supported bridge. This system is 
characterized by real-time perception and access to the entire scenario, 
high-fidelity reproduction of the physical entity, and simultaneous 
intelligent provision of multiple services, providing critical assistance 
and problem-solving solutions for decision-makers. However, this goal 
still faces numerous challenges. To this end, this paper proposes several 
suggestions: 
(1) Fusion of multiple technologies in DT. It is worth noting that the 
future development of DT will primarily focus on digitization, 
integration, and intelligence. The combined application of visual 
reality, augmented reality technologies and DT can significantly 
enhance the visualization and interactivity, which in turn 
improves the efficiency and accuracy of bridge monitoring and 
assessment.

standardize the core elements and minimum indicator re -
quirements. This will play a significant role in advancing the 
development of DT in this field.
