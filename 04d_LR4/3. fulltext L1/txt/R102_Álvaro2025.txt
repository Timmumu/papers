 A B S T R A C T
This paper describes the implementation of a digital twin for buildings to enhance the 
emergency response capabilities of first responder teams, including firefighters, police, and 
emergency medical services. The proposed platform improves the planning of preventive evac-
uation strategies and supports real-time operational decisions during emergencies. It integrates 
wireless monitoring beacons, specifically designed for hostile environments, a cloud-based data 
management system, and a predictive model to monitor environmental air quality parameters, 
which are critical during emergency scenarios. The platform uses a digital twin to simulate 
the building’s behavior, incorporating multimedia content and time series graphs to enhance 
situational awareness and decision-making. Real-time building data are seamlessly integrated 
with predictive models generated from smart building sensors, offering a comprehensive 
visualization on a web-based monitoring interface. This approach provides critical insights to 
decision-makers, improving the safety and efficiency of both rescue operations and preventive 
measures.
1. Introduction
A Smart City is an urban area that uses advanced information and communication technologies (ICT) to improve the quality 
of life of its residents, optimize resource usage, improve the efficiency of urban services and promote sustainability. It integrates 
data from various sources, such as IoT (Internet of Things) devices and sensors, to enable real-time monitoring, predictive 
analytics, and informed decision-making. Additionally, Smart Cities foster citizen engagement and collaboration, ensuring that 
technological advancements align with the needs and well-being of the community. These technologies are integrated into key 
sectors, such as transportation, waste management, energy, security, and infrastructure, to create a more sustainable, efficient, and 
livable environment [1]. Smart Cities and smart buildings are closely related, sharing the common goal of improving efficiency, 
sustainability, and quality of life through advanced technologies. Smart buildings are critical components of Smart Cities, playing a 
pivotal role in enhancing efficiency, sustainability, and urban livability [2]. These structures leverage advanced technologies such 
as IoT, AI (Artificial Intelligence), and automation to monitor and optimize energy consumption, improve occupant comfort, and 
ensure operational efficiency. By incorporating real-time data analysis and adaptive management systems, they help reduce the 
environmental footprint of cities while fostering safety and convenience for their users.

Smart buildings are fully integrated with urban infrastructure via communication networks and IoT-enabled sensors. These 
systems collect and share data on various operational aspects, including energy usage, climate control, and security, which is then 
aggregated into the city network. This integration optimizes resource allocation and enhances public services. For instance, building’s 
energy consumption can be dynamically adjusted based on the city’s overall demand, contributing to electrical grid stability and 
efficiency [3,4].
One key area of focus within Smart Cities is safety. Buildings with established safety, evacuation, and environmental monitoring 
plans contribute significantly to urban security [5]. This paper focuses on this crucial aspect, offering supportive tools aimed at 
providing robust, proactive solutions that directly influence the safety and planning efficiency of first responder teams operating in 
emergency or high-risk scenarios. These solutions facilitate early detection of incidents or risk assessment and monitoring, enabling 
a swift return to normalcy and efficient organization of security forces in the event of a disaster.
In this context, virtualization through digital twin technology has emerged as an effective tool for transforming urban emergency 
services, offering faster information and more efficient operations. Integrating monitoring systems to measure relevant environmental 
variables and asset management strategies are key aspects designed and evaluated in this work to enhance operational efficiency. 
Various studies have explored the effectiveness of monitoring in emergency situations [6–11].
According to the report on emergency services and fire-related fatalities in Spain, published by Fundación Mapfre in collaboration 
with the Asociación Profesional de Técnicos de Bomberos (APTB) [12], there were 235 fire and explosion-related fatalities in 2022. Of 
these, 220 were fire-related deaths, with 176 occurring in residential buildings. These fatalities are often caused by direct exposure 
to fire, smoke inhalation, burns, stress, or difficulty escaping enclosed spaces. Other common risks during emergencies include gas 
leaks, such as ammonia, or explosions. Therefore, action and evacuation plans must be highly accurate, emphasizing coordination 
and communication, along with real-time information about the incident.
In recent years, the concept of Smart Cities has expanded to incorporate more advanced strategies for urban safety and 
emergency response, particularly through the integration of digital twin technologies. These platforms enable synchronized multi-
modal emergency monitoring and real-time visualization of urban infrastructure. Recent studies have demonstrated how digital twins 
can be applied in civil and infrastructure emergency management [13] and in fire safety within smart buildings [14]. Moreover, real-
time digital twins are gaining relevance as foundational technologies for the next generation of networked systems, such as 6G and 
beyond, enabling ultra-reliable low-latency communication essential in time-critical scenarios like emergency response [15]. These 
developments highlight the growing trend toward integrated, intelligent platforms for situational awareness and decision-making 
in public safety applications, underscoring the relevance of our proposed platform.
One of the main objectives of the system presented here is to provide accessible tools for intervention teams facing emergency 
scenarios, minimizing response times. This, in turn, reduces the risk of incidents or casualties, both among civilians and emergency 
responders.
Technologies supporting first responder teams have been explored and evaluated in this work, including solutions presented 
in [16,17], which align with our research focus. Both studies highlight the importance of leveraging technology to enhance 
environmental monitoring for strategic oversight, both in emergencies and during normal conditions. Other solutions, such as those 
described in [18], also utilize such resources to provide alternatives.
This work explores the impact of employing Building Information Modeling (BIM) methodologies to facilitate information access 
and operation visualization. BIM enables building virtualization, creating a comprehensive database of assets and installations, 
accessible and updated in real-time [19]. The creation of a building’s digital twin involves establishing information exchange 
standards throughout its lifecycle, with ISO 19650 [20] being the relevant European standard. The Universidad Rey Juan Carlos 
(URJC) has experience in building digitization, both for its campuses and external entities, through collaborative projects [16]. 
This digitization process involves laser scanning and 3D modeling, providing detailed information on numerous objects and assets, 
including their material types, models, etc. This data can be further enhanced with information about the building’s use and condition 
(occupancy, schedules, emergency exits, fire extinguishers, evacuation routes, emergency plans, etc.).
Beyond the digital twin, the integration of real-time data from physical variables, such as temperature or CO2 levels in different 
building areas, is crucial for emergency management. Real-time visualization allows remote area monitoring and segmentation, 
supporting efficient operational responses. Achieving this requires a flexible software platform capable of processing data from 
multiple sources, capturing, storing, and displaying sensor and multimedia content (videos, photos). This approach would help 
better categorize emergencies and establish an environmental and visual media repository.
To implement this, IoT devices equipped with sensors and actuators create an interconnected network using various wireless 
communication technologies such as Wi-Fi, Bluetooth and LoRa. All this information must be efficiently transmitted, managed, 
and stored by the platform, ensuring rapid and intuitive user access through a data integration platform and a web interface that 
visualizes the digital twin, including graphs and other relevant information.
This work introduces GUIDE2FR, a platform designed for data acquisition and visualization, tested at the firefighter training tower 
located at the Unified Security Center (CUS) in Alcorcón (Madrid). Sensor beacons were deployed, and real emergency scenarios 
were simulated during firefighter training exercises, allowing the Advanced Command Post (PMA) to make informed decisions in 
line with emergency service protocols. These sensor beacons can be deployed autonomously, manually or pre-intervention as if they 
were the building’s own monitoring systems. Flexibility and scalability are two of the great advantages of this system, as it adapts 
to existing and new information systems. Besides real-time visualization, the platform offers environmental parameter prediction 
capabilities, such as CO2 levels, using machine learning techniques.
In summary, GUIDE2FR provides significant innovations compared to existing commercial solutions: full integration of real-
time environmental and structural data, predictive capabilities through AI, flexible and scalable architecture, and synchronized

2. Methodology for smart building solutions in emergency scenarios
The GUIDE2FR platform combines hardware and software elements to manage and display information. On the hardware side, 
the system includes sensors and gateways that support various communication protocols. The software tools are organized into 
multiple layers: a data integration layer and a visualization layer, using web tools to display the digital twin and related data. Each 
component is described in the following subsections.
2.1. Sensor layer — Beacon systems
It consists of a distributed system of beacons integrating smart IoT-based sensors. This setup provides data on various physical 
variables that need to be measured, such as temperature, humidity, CO2, and total volatile organic compounds (TVOC), as well as 
other parameters depending on the building’s usage and characteristics. This information can be obtained from various sources, 
such as sensors strategically placed in specific locations, and can utilize different wireless communication protocols. In this case, 
both Wi-Fi and LoRa communication technologies have been considered. Additionally, this array of sensors is complemented by 
strategically placed cameras used for video recording and monitoring critical areas. For instance, cameras with RTSP (Real-Time 
Streaming Protocol [21]) and Wi-Fi connectivity have been implemented in this work. RTSP is an application-level protocol used 
in surveillance systems and IP cameras to control real-time video and audio streaming. It allows communication between media 
servers and client devices. Several types of solutions will be validated in this study:
• Beacons comprising sensors will be pre-positioned across various spaces within the building.
• Beacons will be deployed by first responder teams at the onset of an emergency.
• Beacons will be autonomously distributed by ground or aerial autonomous agents (robots or UAVs) to minimize human 
intervention.
2.1.1. DBEMERGENCY beacons
During the proof of concept, the beacons were designed and implemented in collaboration with Deep-Insight to meet the specific 
requirements of the platform. These beacons are environmental monitoring devices optimized for emergency environments, featuring 
a modern IoT architecture. Under normal conditions, the beacons connect to the building’s power supply and Wi-Fi network, 
transmitting air quality and environmental parameter data. In emergency situations, such as power or communication outages, 
they continue to operate using internal batteries and LoRa communication, facilitated by an EVA (a custom-designed device that 
serves as a remote access unit equipped with LoRa and Internet interface).
The development and implementation of platforms like GUIDE2FR are particularly crucial in environments where pre-existing 
connectivity is either insufficient or entirely unavailable. In many emergency or high-risk scenarios — such as firefighter training 
towers, remote industrial facilities, or large public buildings — the absence of reliable communication networks can significantly 
hinder monitoring and response efforts. Therefore, establishing a dedicated communication and monitoring network in these areas 
is vital for the safety and efficiency of emergency operations.
The beacon system in GUIDE2FR addresses this challenge by creating its own robust communication infrastructure. This is 
achieved through the integration of dual communication protocols: Wi-Fi and LoRa. In areas where traditional Wi-Fi networks are 
unavailable or compromised, the LoRa protocol ensures continuous, long-range, and low-power data transmission. This capability 
allows the beacons to maintain reliable connectivity across extensive areas or in buildings with structural barriers that could impede 
standard wireless signals.
By establishing a self-sustained network, the system ensures that crucial environmental data — such as temperature, humidity, 
and air quality parameters — are continuously monitored and transmitted. This information is essential for first responders and 
emergency teams, as it provides real-time insights into hazardous conditions and informs critical decision-making processes.
The main features of the DBEMERGENCY beacons, designed to support this self-sufficient monitoring network, are summarized 
in Table  1 and detailed below:
1. Integrated sensors: The beacons include sensors for temperature, humidity, atmospheric pressure, real CO2, total volatile 
organic compounds (TVOC), air quality index (IAQ), motion detection, luminosity, and three-axis acceleration. The sensors 
provide essential measurements, with key ranges for human safety as follows:
• Temperature: Typically considered safe for human occupancy between 20 ◦C and 27 ◦C. Values outside this range may 
pose risks such as heat stress or hypothermia.
• CO2: Normal indoor concentrations range from 400 to 1000 ppm. Levels above 1000 ppm can cause discomfort and 
impaired cognitive performance, while concentrations exceeding 5000 ppm are hazardous.
• TVOC: Safe levels are generally below 500 ppb (parts per billion). Higher concentrations may lead to irritation or 
long-term health risks.

2.1.2. Deployment in evaluation environments
Sensor beacons were strategically deployed in two key environments for evaluation: the firefighter training tower in Alcorcón 
and 10 classrooms distributed across Laboratory Building III at the Móstoles campus of URJC. The collected data is securely stored 
in an external database, enabling both real-time monitoring and comprehensive post-event analysis.
Environmental data acquisition occurs through the beacon network, capturing physical variables for storage and visualization. 
As previously mentioned, the beacons primarily use Wi-Fi, switching to LoRa as needed for robust communication. A gateway sends 
sensor data to the manufacturer’s cloud storage, with an API for retrieving data. Python scripts are used to ensure data remains 
updated in the application.
2.2. Video management using OpenVidu
To manage video data captured by the cameras, a module was developed based on the open-source video conferencing platform
OpenVidu.1 OpenVidu is an open-source platform that facilitates the integration and implementation of real-time applications, 
particularly those requiring real-time video and audio streaming. The platform is built on WebRTC 2 technology and provides a 
high-level API, simplifying real-time video integration for web and mobile applications.
WebRTC is an open-source project that enables browsers and mobile applications to communicate in real time via simple APIs. It 
supports video, audio, and generic data transmission of any kind between peers, allowing developers to build robust communication 
solutions. WebRTC employs several underlying protocols and codecs to achieve this.
The most important protocols used for transporting media are:
• Session Description Protocol (SDP): Used for negotiating the media parameters and capabilities. SDP is a format for describing 
the multimedia content of the communication sessions such as codec, format information, network information, and other 
media session details [22].
• Real-time Transport Protocol (RTP): A network protocol used for delivering audio and video over IP networks [23].
• Interactive Connectivity Establishment (ICE): A framework to allow WebRTC to connect peers through NATs and firewalls [24]. 
ICE uses STUN (Session Traversal Utilities for NAT) [25] and TURN (Traversal Using Relays around NAT) [26] protocols to 
find the best path to connect peers.

2.3. Visualization layer — Digital twin
The visualization layer enables users to understand what is happening inside the building. It provides real-time information 
on current events, aiding in decision-making for evacuations or emergencies by visualizing rooms, corridors, and maintenance or 
furniture elements.
Additionally, it grants access to historical data, which is valuable for incident analysis or to create datasets for training models 
used in the data prediction platform. The visualization layer is implemented as a web environment using Angular, creating a portal 
that integrates a geolocated 3D model of the entire building along with historical graphs and sensor data. The 3D model is geolocated 
using ArcGIS, creating a contextual building scene. To visualize both historical and real-time data, this proposal uses Grafana, with
Fiware serving as the data integration platform. The visualization layer synchronizes images from RTSP cameras in the building’s key 
areas with sensor information, using a custom Grafana plugin that aligns the video player timeline with the graphical representation 
of data values. The aligning is done using the video and historical record data recorded from OpenVidu that resides in the server’s 
file system and in the database respectively.

3.1. Sensor data transmission to the digital twin web portal
The system incorporates sensor data for temperature, humidity, CO2, and total volatile organic compounds (TVOC), among other 
variables. These sensors are embedded within DBEMERGENCY beacons, as described previously, allowing deployment in critical 
monitoring zones. The beacons are configurable, and their data transmission rate can be adjusted based on monitoring requirements. 
As a result, the system provides a near real-time solution, with data displayed with only a few seconds or minutes of latency.
Data can be sourced from more than just the sensors deployed in the building. Additional inputs include alarms and processed 
data from other systems, as well as external web sources. The platform is designed to function in both training environments for first 
responders and public spaces, such as universities, to assess routine or emergency situations. For example, if a public building offers 
access to services such as classroom reservations or a Computerized Maintenance Management System (CMMS), this information 
can be integrated into GUIDE2FR to detect anomalies during normal operations and trigger the necessary emergency response.
Sensor data is integrated into the digital twin graphs using Fiware. This system activates subscription services to route information 
to where it is needed, facilitating long-term storage in MySQL and enabling time-series analysis and trend monitoring (Fig.  3).
To enable multi-step forecasting of environmental variables such as CO2 and TVOC, we implemented a fully automated machine 
learning pipeline for training, hyperparameter optimization, and evaluation. The raw time series were first resampled to fixed 
intervals, interpolated to address missing values, and standardized using statistics derived from the training set. Lagged features 
(past values) and lead features (future targets) were generated to provide temporal context for supervised learning.  The data were 
split using a forward-chaining cross-validation scheme based on rolling windows to preserve causality. Candidate models included a 
diverse set of regressors from the scikit-learn library such as Support Vector Regression (SVR), Huber Regressor, Random 
Forest, and Gaussian Process Regression. For each estimator, a tailored hyperparameter grid was automatically constructed by 
combining default values with parameter ranges suggested by a large language model, ensuring valid and model-specific search 
spaces. Sampling distributions (e.g., uniform, logarithmic, categorical) were selected accordingly.  Each model configuration was

Data acquisition and validation for model training
To ensure that the predictive models integrated in GUIDE2FR are valid in realistic emergency contexts, it was necessary 
to construct a dataset that addressed one of the central challenges: the high heterogeneity and unpredictability of emergency 
scenarios. In such contexts, environmental variables (e.g., temperature, humidity, CO2, TVOC) evolve in ways that are tightly 
coupled with human intervention, spatial layout, and the dynamics of fire or ventilation. As no existing dataset offered sufficient 
representativeness under such conditions, we designed a data acquisition process specifically aimed at producing training data that 
is both ecologically valid and structurally rich. This process was carried out in collaboration with the firefighting departments of 
Alcorcón and Fuencarral, leveraging their operational facilities and expertise. The goal was to capture the temporal evolution of 
environmental variables under controlled but realistic interventions, enabling supervised training of models that can generalize to 
unseen but plausible emergency dynamics. The interventions were designed to indirectly perturb the environment, reproducing 
meaningful signal transitions without requiring actual hazardous events.
To ensure traceability and quality, the process followed seven structured steps:
1. Initial coordination: Firefighters provided architectural plans, inventories of standard equipment, and typical movement 
patterns, enabling scenario design grounded in real procedures.
2. Deployment planning: Based on spatial constraints and expected workflows, sensor beacons were positioned to maximize 
coverage of relevant zones while minimizing signal interference.
3. Technical preparation: All devices (beacons, routers, chargers) were validated prior to deployment. Additional materials 
such as labeling, signage, and procedural documentation were prepared to avoid ambiguity during execution.
4. Operational deployment: Sensors were physically installed in pre-selected locations, and tested in situ to verify connectivity 
and real-time data transmission from each zone.
5. Protocol-driven interventions: Firefighters executed planned sequences (e.g., entry, ventilation, movement with equip-
ment), altering the environment in ways that reflect real operations. Each intervention was logged by participants using 
predefined Excel templates.
6. Ongoing monitoring: Throughout the campaign, communication with field staff ensured that data quality remained 
consistent, and technical issues were resolved without compromising the experimental integrity.
7. Post-intervention validation: Data was collected and pre-processed to align signal timestamps with ground-truth anno-
tations. The resulting dataset includes time series annotated with context-specific labels (e.g., ‘‘forced ventilation started’’, 
‘‘thermal load increase due to PPE’’).
By designing the data collection around firefighter workflows rather than artificial lab conditions, we ensured that the models are 
exposed to signal behaviors that reflect actual emergency dynamics, such as abrupt environmental changes or non-linear propagation 
effects. This setup provides not only a robust training base, but also a realistic benchmark to assess model performance in the kinds 
of contexts where their predictions will be used operationally.
3.2. Synchronized video and sensor data visualization
As described earlier, the application allows the visualization of video footage recorded by cameras synchronized with sensor 
data stored as time series. This synchronization is displayed in a Grafana dashboard using a custom plugin specifically developed for 
the platform.
Grafana5 is an open-source data visualization and analysis platform that enables the creation of dashboards and interactive 
graphs. In this context, it is used to display sensor data and video in a synchronized manner. One of Grafana’s main advantages is 
its support for custom plugins that can be integrated seamlessly into the platform.
This custom plugin allows the synchronization of camera-recorded video (via OpenVidu) with sensor data represented as time-
series graphs. This solution enables interactive analysis of data and video, allowing users to navigate moments of interest in near 
real-time or historical information.
Each video segment is timestamped, and metadata is stored in the database by the Video Management Module. The plugin uses this 
data to synchronize RTSP video streams with time-series sensor data stored in MySQL. The plugin cross-references these timestamps 
to display synchronized sensor values and video playback.

and, simultaneously, view environmental parameters collected by the sensors. This capability enables strategic action planning while 
minimizing risks to the intervention team. Historical data analysis is also possible, allowing review of recorded video alongside sensor 
data values at any given moment.

3.3. Data handling in the visualization layer
Environmental data is collected by DBEMERGENCY beacons. As mentioned earlier, these beacons primarily communicate via Wi-
Fi, with LoRaWAN used as a fallback in case of connectivity issues during emergencies. The sensor data is first sent to a proprietary 
cloud API provided by the manufacturer. Periodic data retrieval is handled via Python scripts, which then forward the information 
into the Fiware ecosystem using NGSI interfaces. Fiware enables context-based data integration and distribution to the digital twin, 
where the information is stored in a MySQL database and visualized through Grafana dashboards and the 3D ArcGIS-based scene.
Sensor data processed by Fiware is managed through a web interface that provides real-time visualization of building spaces and 
environmental parameters.
The web page is developed using Angular, offering modularity and easy integration into existing applications as an additional 
component.6 The interface is user-friendly yet powerful, featuring two primary filters (Fig.  6). One filter allows floor selection, and 
the other selects variables to observe. For example, the first floor of the firefighter tower is displayed with color-coded spaces, 
alongside graphical information on temperature, humidity, CO2, and TVOC, shown using Grafana dashboards.
The web interface is deployed using a Docker image, which launches alongside the rest of the Fiware system. It automatically 
connects to the MySQL database to display time-series data, enabling trend analysis and real-time monitoring. The 3D model 
information is divided into layers: static elements like walls and staircases and dynamic elements that update with sensor data, 
using color codes to indicate alarm levels.
The initial filtering of digital twin layers requires creating a 3D model in software such as Revit or AutoCAD and geolocating 
it using ArcGIS. This setup enables the creation of a web scene and interaction with JavaScript tools in ArcGIS to filter by building 
floor or space attributes.

4. Discussion
The evaluation of the GUIDE2FR platform has revealed several key insights, particularly in comparison to existing emergency 
management solutions for smart buildings. One of the main advantages of GUIDE2FR is its comprehensive integration of environ-
mental and structural data, visualized through a digital twin interface. This capability allows intervention teams to make informed 
decisions in real time, improving both safety and response efficiency.

Table  2 provides a comparative analysis of GUIDE2FR against other prominent emergency management systems, including the 
Forest Fire Detection System [6], HelpResponder System [17], and various IoT-based Smart Building Solutions [18]. The comparison 
highlights several distinctive features of GUIDE2FR:
Real-time Data Integration: While other systems focus on specific variables or provide partial data integration, GUIDE2FR 
uniquely combines both environmental and structural data into a cohesive digital twin model. This integration enhances situational 
awareness and allows for a more holistic approach to emergency management.
Machine Learning Capabilities: GUIDE2FR stands out by incorporating advanced machine learning models that predict critical 
environmental parameters, such as CO2 and TVOC levels. These predictive capabilities are crucial for proactive emergency response 
and are not commonly available in other systems.
Scalability and Flexibility: Unlike fixed or limited architectures in other platforms, GUIDE2FR’s highly scalable design supports 
easy integration of new sensors and systems. This flexibility ensures that the platform can be adapted to various emergency scenarios 
and evolving requirements.
Data Visualization: The advanced digital twin visualization in GUIDE2FR, synchronized with real-time video and sensor data 
via a custom Grafana plugin, offers a superior user experience compared to basic graph-based interfaces or unsynchronized data 
visualizations found in other solutions.
Open-source Components: GUIDE2FR leverages mainly open-source technologies, providing a significant advantage in terms of 
customization and adaptability. This contrasts with the proprietary nature of many existing platforms, which often limit flexibility 
and increase dependency on specific vendors.
Emergency Management Support: GUIDE2FR offers comprehensive support for a wide range of emergency scenarios, including 
those in training environments and public spaces. This broad applicability makes it a versatile tool for first responders.
The insights derived from Table  2 underline the innovative features of GUIDE2FR and its potential to transform emergency 
management practices in smart buildings. The platform not only addresses current limitations but also sets a new standard for 
integrating real-time data, predictive analytics, and flexible architectures in urban emergency scenarios.
Despite the promising results, the proposed platform has several limitations that should be acknowledged. First, the current 
implementation relies on ArcGIS for 3D geospatial representation, which may limit portability or require licensing in certain 
deployment scenarios. As future work, the development of digital twin platforms based on open-source libraries and frameworks 
could offer more flexible and cost-effective alternatives, fostering wider adoption in diverse contexts. Second, the platform assumes 
the availability of a reliable wireless network infrastructure within the building, which may not always be the case during an 
emergency or in older facilities. Additionally, sensor calibration and performance in extreme environments (e.g., high temperature, 
smoke, or humidity) can impact data accuracy and model reliability. Finally, adapting the system to different building typologies or 
jurisdictions may require reconfiguration of sensor networks, integration with local emergency protocols, or adaptation to region-
specific cloud and privacy requirements. These limitations point to important directions for future research and development to 
improve the platform’s robustness and generalizability.
5. Conclusions
This work has demonstrated that the GUIDE2FR platform is effective for acquiring, storing, and visualizing real-world information 
using a digital twin. Furthermore, the platform can handle various information sources and effectively combine them to assess 
emergency situations.
GUIDE2FR introduces several significant innovations compared to current commercial solutions for building emergency monitor-
ing and management. Firstly, GUIDE2FR provides comprehensive real-time data integration, combining environmental and structural 
parameters within a single digital twin interface. This integrated visualization capability enables faster and more accurate decision-
making by intervention teams. Secondly, our platform incorporates machine learning techniques for predicting critical parameters, 
such as CO2 levels and total volatile organic compounds (TVOC), an advanced feature not commonly available in commercial 
solutions. Additionally, GUIDE2FR’s architecture is highly flexible and scalable, allowing for the easy integration of new sensors and 
systems, unlike commercial solutions that tend to be less adaptable. Another innovative aspect is the synchronized visualization of 
sensor data and real-time videos using a custom Grafana plugin, facilitating comprehensive emergency situation monitoring. Lastly, 
most GUIDE2FR components are open-source, enabling greater customization and adaptation to specific user needs—a substantial 
advantage over less flexible, proprietary commercial solutions. These features demonstrate that GUIDE2FR not only matches but 
also surpasses the capabilities of existing commercial solutions, offering a more complete and effective tool for urban emergency 
management.
Therefore, this work could be implemented and integrated into the university’s management web system for preventive emergency 
management, opening up the possibility for research and the design of new control systems to manage more complex environments 
in real time.
The modularity and reliance on open-source technologies such as Fiware, Grafana, MySQL, and OpenVidu facilitate the customiza-
tion and deployment of GUIDE2FR in diverse environments, ensuring interoperability with existing emergency response systems. 
While the current GIS visualization layer relies on ArcGIS, future research will explore the adoption of open-source alternatives, 
with the goal of increasing system portability and eliminating dependencies on proprietary software.
Future work will expand this study to integrate the digital twin with more real assets. This will allow for active response and the 
integration of all possible emergency assets to establish effective evacuation mechanisms. The research group is already developing 
related work, focusing on energy efficiency monitoring in public and private building services, given the growing importance of 
IoT-based monitoring in the context of energy efficiency [30]. Furthermore, to overcome current limitations related to platform 
dependencies, future developments will also explore the use of open-source libraries and frameworks for digital twin visualization 
and management.
