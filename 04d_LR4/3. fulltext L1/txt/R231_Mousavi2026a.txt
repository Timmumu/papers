ABSTRACT
The progressive degradation of bridge infrastructure poses significant risks to transportation safety, necessitating 
advanced monitoring and management strategies. Digital Twins (DTs) have emerged as a transformative tech -
nology for real-time structural monitoring, offering a dynamic and data-driven representation of physical assets. 
When combined with Deep Learning (DL), DTs enhance predictive capabilities by leveraging vast amounts of 
sensor data to detect structural anomalies, optimize maintenance strategies, and improve decision-making 
processes. This synergy enables a more accurate, automated, and cost-effective approach to infrastructure 
health monitoring, ensuring timely interventions and prolonged service life. This paper presents a novel data- 
driven DT framework for real-time bridge damage detection, incorporating two key innovations. First, a 
Multi-Domain Fusion Autoencoder (MDFA) that integrates raw acceleration data with feature fusion from time, 
frequency, and wavelet domains. Second, a synthetic anomaly simulation approach for evaluation. The frame -
work employs a transformer-based Generative Adversarial Network (GAN) to generate realistic synthetic signals, 
which are modified to simulate various damage scenarios. Evaluations on Australia ’ s Werrington Bridge 
demonstrate the MDFA ’ s ability to detect subtle anomalies often overlooked by conventional unsupervised DL 
methods. A comparative analysis confirms its superior detection capabilities. Further validation on Austria ’ s 
S101 Bridge using real damage data successfully identifies and localizes all damage scenarios without requiring 
additional optimization. These results highlight the efficiency of feature fusion techniques in improving anomaly 
detection accuracy and suggest that synthetic anomaly simulation offers a cost-effective alternative to laboratory 
testing for evaluating unsupervised damage detection approaches.
1. Introduction
Bridge infrastructures, as a critical component of the transportation 
network, are experiencing significant deterioration due to increasing 
traffic volume and service load, ageing, and the impacts of aggressive 
environmental factors associated with climate change. The progressive 
deterioration of bridge elements results in extensive damage, leading to 
significantly increased repair or replacement costs. If left unaddressed, it 
may ultimately cause catastrophic structural failure, resulting in severe 
economic losses and, tragically, the loss of human lives. Therefore, the 
proper inspection and maintenance of bridge infrastructure through 
Structural Health Monitoring (SHM) approaches have become increas -
ingly important for ensuring structural reliability and safety [ 1 – 3 ].
SHM systems play a crucial role in ensuring the safety and reliability 
of bridge infrastructures by detecting damage and evaluating structural 
conditions. SHM approaches can be broadly categorized into model- 
driven and data-driven methods. Model-driven approaches rely on 
physics-based models and finite element analysis to predict structural 
behaviour, while data-driven methods leverage sensor data and statis -
tical techniques to identify patterns indicative of deterioration [ 4 , 5 ]. 
The increasing complexity of structural systems and the vast amount of 
monitoring data have led to a growing reliance on data-driven methods, 
particularly those incorporating Machine Learning (ML) techniques.
ML techniques have significantly transformed SHM by enhancing the 
ability to analyse large-scale monitoring data and have become a 
cornerstone of modern SHM systems, offering powerful tools for

and detect emerging trends makes it efficient for real-time monitoring 
applications. Several research studies have explored unsupervised 
techniques for the real time analysis of structural monitoring data. 
Despite the extensive progress made in unsupervised damage detection 
in bridges, the extraction of meaningful results from extensive real-time 
measurement data is a significant challenge, which affects the timely 
assessment of structural conditions and the implementation of appro -
priate remedial measures [ 9 ].
Digital Twins (DTs), as a powerful tool for online monitoring and 
predictive maintenance, have gained significant popularity in bridge 
infrastructure management. Various approaches, such as big data 
analysis, ML, and cloud/edge computing can be employed in DT plat -
forms to manage the increasing volume of input data, and facilitate 
continuous, and ideally real-time, monitoring, which is crucial in iden -
tifying defects or problems at an early stage [ 11 ]. By analysing the 
collected data, DTs can identify potential issues within a system, 
enabling bridge management teams to make informed predictive 
maintenance decisions. This enhances reliability, increases efficiency, 
and optimizes the use of available resources [ 12 ]. The development of 
DTs mechanisms within Bridge Management Systems (BMS) in recent 
years has provided valuable opportunities to address real time moni -
toring challenge, particularly through advancements in applying ML 
algorithms within DTs framework [ 13 ].
A DT model serves as a dynamic virtual representation of physical 
infrastructure, providing a real-time reflection of its behaviour, current 
condition, and performance. This synchronization between the virtual 
and physical entities is facilitated by advanced systems with high-speed 
communication capabilities. These intelligent systems, encompassing 
advanced technologies such as Deep Learning (DL) and data analytics, 
enable computers to execute tasks that usually demand human intelli -
gence. They achieve this by collecting and analysing large volumes of 
data from IoT-enabled (Internet of Things) data collection methods and 
other sensors, to identify patterns in data structure and make predictions 
with minimal human oversight [ 14 ].
Data-driven simulation models in DTs are developed based on the 
validated data obtained from experiments/measurements on the actual 
structure and produce a model to analyse the status of the bridge in real 
time. In data-driven models, data from the physical bridge is collected 
using emerging sensor technologies and IoT devices. This data is then 
processed using Big Data analytics and advanced ML techniques, to 
simulate the exact functionality of the bridge [ 15 ]. Consequently, bridge 
DT is equipped with dynamic ML-based data-driven models that 
continuously updates using real-time data analysis enabling the damage 
detection in real time.
While significant advancements have been made in developing of 
different virtual models within bridge DTs, such as geometric modelling 
via unmanned aerial vehicles [ 16 , 17 ] and terrestrial laser scanning [ 18 ,
19 ], as well as Finite Element Method (FEM) models for numerical 
analysis [ 20 , 21 ], the incorporation of robust data-driven DT frameworks 
for long-term monitoring remains a challenge. Current DT imple -
mentations often lack comprehensive data-driven models for processing 
long-term bridge data [ 11 , 22 , 23 ].
Additionally, the use of real measured data and unsupervised DL 
methods for real-time damage detection in bridges still remains under -
explored [ 24 , 25 ]. A key challenge is the lack of historical damage data in 
existing operational bridges. While laboratory and benchmark struc -
tures provide controlled environments for testing, they do not fully 
represent real-world complexities due to differences in scale and 
dynamic behaviour, environmental conditions, and operational 
variability.
Data fusion is a well-known technique in SHM, enabling the inte -
gration of heterogeneous data sources to enhance damage detection and 
structural assessment. By combining multiple data modalities, data 
fusion improves the reliability and accuracy of monitoring systems. 
Feature fusion, as a specific type of data fusion, plays a critical role in 
extracting meaningful insights from complex bridge data using real 
measured data and unsupervised DL techniques. Given the complexity of 
bridge dynamics, relying on a single domain limits the ability to capture 
intricate damage signatures in data-driven models within DTs frame -
works [ 26 , 27 ].This limitation hinders the ability to detect structural 
deterioration promptly, leading to increased maintenance costs and 
potential safety risks.
This study introduces an advanced data-driven DT model for real- 
time bridge damage detection, leveraging feature fusion and novel un -
supervised DL techniques. The proposed AI-enabled DT employs a Multi- 
Domain Fusion Auto encoder (MDFA) to integrate complementary fea -
tures extracted from time, frequency, and wavelet domains to provide 
unique insights into structural responses. This framework enhances the 
DT applications for robust, real-time analysis and damage detection, 
owing to its capabilities to integrate extensive amount of sensor data 
which provide a comprehensive understanding of the structure’s 
behaviour, performance, and status [ 11 , 28 ]. Moreover, the real time 
data processing provides the opportunity for early detection of struc -
tural issues, enabling timely interventions to prevent failures [ 29 ] and 
implement proactive maintenance strategies to extend the structure’s 
life, reduce downtime, and lower maintenance costs [ 30 ].
The primary contributions and novelties of this study include: 
1. Introducing a data-driven DT framework with a real-time data 
analysis and processing modules to prolong service life of bridges, 
advancing BMS toward faster and adaptable performance.
2. Integrating feature fusion techniques with DL within DT framework, 
which leverages complementary strengths to enhance real-time 
damage detection through adaptive unsupervised learning on real- 
world bridge data.
3. Developing a novel MDFA model that integrates raw acceleration 
data with features from time, frequency, and wavelet domains, 
significantly enhancing the detection of subtle structural anomalies 
often missed by conventional methods.
4. Proposing an innovative synthetic anomaly simulation approach 
using transformer-based Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) to 
generate realistic synthetic signals for evaluation, providing a cost- 
effective alternative to laboratory testing for unsupervised damage 
detection methods.
5. Comparison of the performance with the state-of-the-art unsuper -
vised deep learning methods in the literature.
6. Applying real-world bridge case studies for real-time damage 
detection, including simulating new data points and alert generation.
Several experimental tests have been carried out on a real bridge case 
study. Results show that the proposed framework significantly enhances 
early damage detection and predictive maintenance capabilities, 
demonstrating its potential to revolutionize bridge health monitoring 
and ensure long-term infrastructure safety.

2. Related works and motivations
DL models have proven to be efficient in real-time SHM applications, 
enabling to process streaming data from different sensors. DL models 
automatically extract features through their hidden layers, and typically 
demand large volumes of data to perform effectively. Bayane et al. [ 9 ] 
proposed an approach for real-time detection of damage in bridges by 
leveraging unsupervised anomaly detection algorithms applied to strain 
and acceleration measurements. Li et al. [ 31 ] proposed an unsupervised 
damage detection method designed for real-time application during 
vehicle crossings over a bridge. Teng et al. [ 24 ] proposed an unsuper -
vised damage detection method that employs an attention-based con -
volutional auto encoder to detect progressive damage in bridges. Kang 
et al. [ 32 ] proposed a DL-based anomaly detection method has been 
developed for both historical data mining and real-time alerting. Fer -
nandez et al. [ 33 ] also employed a hybrid methodology that in -
corporates Finite Element simulations to enrich the training phase of a 
DL with synthetic damage scenarios for damage identification in bridge 
structures.
The rapid evolution of infrastructure management has led to the 
adoption DL methods within DT framework for efficient bridge health 
monitoring. The integration of DL algorithms has further advanced the 
capabilities of DTs by identifying hidden patterns within the large vol -
umes of data and automating anomaly detection, predictive analytics, 
and model calibration. This capability allows for the application of AI- 
enabled DTs in different tasks including railway maintenance activ -
ities [ 34 , 35 ], building predictive maintenance [ 36 ] and smart 
manufacturing and advanced robotics[ 37 ].
DL methods have also been utilized for synthetic data generation to 
support anomaly detection in bridge DTs, particularly when real-world 
datasets are limited or incomplete. Jim ´enez Rios et al. [ 38 ] proposed a 
synthetic data generation framework to enhance the training of ML 
models for damage detection in bridge structures. In another research 
from the same authors, Jim ´enez Rios et al. [ 39 ] examined the un -
certainties within synthetic data generation frameworks and the po -
tential application of these methodologies as efficient and cost-effective 
alternatives to real monitoring, supporting the rapid development DTs. 
Similarly, Mousavi et al. [ 40 ] proposed a data augmentation approach to 
generate synthetic acceleration data in bridge DTs, aiming to mitigate 
the challenges of substantial training data requirements in DL models, 
which are often costly and time-intensive to collect. However, Limited 
research has explored the integration of DL methods within DTs 
frameworks for the real time analysis of bridge monitoring data. Table 1
presents the comparison of AI-enabled DT solutions (or related frame -
works) for real ‑ time bridge health monitoring as reported in recent 
literature.
The gaps in these methods include real-time performance con -
straints, technical scalability and optimized DL techniques. This study 
aims to address these key challenges through feature fusion, advanced 
pre-processing techniques, and optimized DL architectures to develop 
robust and efficient SHM system capable of managing the complexities 
of real-world environments.
3. Proposed data-driven DT framework
The integration of data analytics and DL within digital twinning 
enhances bridge monitoring and maintenance by enabling real-time 
structural assessment and predictive decision-making. To fully exploit 
these advantages, a data-driven reference framework is essential.
The proposed framework, depicted in Fig. 1 , presents a layered ar -
chitecture for efficient data analytics in bridge DTs. The process begins 
with sensor networks collecting real-time structural data. While this 
study specifically focuses on vibration data, the proposed data-driven 
framework is designed to be adaptable and can be extended to 
incorporate other structural parameters such as strain and temperature. 
This data is then transmitted through communication technologies and 
enters the data analysis layer, where different Artificial Intelligence 
models, statistical analyses, and mathematical approaches construct a 
data-driven digital representation of the bridge. The Artificial Intelli -
gence section of the framework also incorporates other data-driven 
techniques, including synthetic data generation, which is used to 
generate realistic signals for structural health assessment in our work -
flow implementation ( Section 5.1 ). To ensure structural integrity and 
safety, raw sensor data undergoes pre-processing, such as data cleaning, 
filtering, and feature extraction, before analysis using DL and feature 
fusion techniques. This enables bridge behaviour simulation, structural 
health assessment (including damage localization and severity estima -
tion), and visualization to support decision-making. Ultimately, this 
data-driven digital twin framework facilitates proactive maintenance, 
improves operational efficiency, and extends bridge service life by 
providing actionable insights.

4.3. Feature extraction
Feature extraction is a critical step in the proposed methodology, as 
it transforms raw acceleration signals into meaningful representations 
that characterize the structural behaviour. To achieve this, features are 
extracted from three complementary domains, including time, fre -
quency, and wavelet. This multi-domain approach ensures a compre -
hensive analysis by capturing both localized and global behavioural 
changes, which are essential for detecting subtle anomalies and struc -
tural damage and its location in bridge structures [ 47 ]. The process 
begins with a sliding window approach applied to the pre-processed 
signals. The window size and overlap are configurable to balance 
computational efficiency and temporal resolution. The features are 
computed for each window and subsequently interpolated to match the 
original signal length, providing continuous feature patterns.
In the time domain, features such as Root Mean Square (RMS), Crest 
Factor, and Shannon Entropy characterize the vibration signal ’ s in -
tensity, impulsiveness, and complexity, respectively. RMS quantifies the 
overall vibration energy, Crest Factor identifies transient peaks linked to 
localized damage, and Shannon Entropy reflects the randomness of the 
signal, capturing irregularities caused by structural changes. The fre -
quency domain features, including Spectral Centroid, Spectral Spread, 
and Spectral Kurtosis, provide insights into the spectral distribution of 
the signal. These features track shifts in dominant frequencies, energy 
dispersion, and transient events, which are often indicative of changes in 
structural stiffness or localized damage. Lastly, wavelet-domain features 
are derived using a three-level decomposition with Daubechies-4 
wavelets, enabling a multi-resolution analysis of the signal. Energy ra -
tios at different levels capture the distribution of vibrational energy 
across high, mid, and low frequencies, effectively identifying localized 
and global anomalies at varying time scales. By combining features from 
these three domains, the proposed methodology creates a robust and 
diverse representation of the structural response.

4.4. Multi-Domain fusion auto encoder (MDFA) model
The MDFA model provides a new multi-modal data integration 
approach to extract and fuse hidden representations from different 
sources. In this model, as shown in Fig.3 , each data domain is processed 
through a dedicated encoder that transforms the input data into a 
domain-specific representation. This is an important step in order to 
preserve essential domain-specific features and capturing the intrinsic 
characteristics of input data.
Following the encoding stage, a fusion layer combines these indi -
vidual latent representations into a shared latent space. The fusion 
process is designed to capture both general and specific aspects of 
different data domains. Attention mechanisms or constraint-based ag -
gregation techniques are employed to ensure that the shared represen -
tation is both coherent and discriminative. The reconstruction phase 
involves a set of domain-specific decoders tasked with reconstructing 
the original inputs from the fused latent space. The MDFA model is 
trained by minimizing a composite loss function that balances recon -
struction fidelity and fusion consistency.
4.4.1. Input configuration
The pre-processing phase transforms the input data into a structured 
format suitable for training the MDFA network. This phase consists of 
four main steps, including data scaling, sliding window segmentation, 
batch preparation and data structuring.
To normalize the data, a sensor-wise scaling method is used to handle 
potential outliers while preserving the relative relationships between 
features from each accelerometer. Sensor-wise scaling refers to applying 
normalization independently to each accelerometer ’ s data rather than 
globally across all sensors. This approach scales the features based on 
robust statistical measures, ensuring that the influence of outliers is 
minimized. The normalization is performed independently for each 
sensor ’ s features across all domains. The temporal relationships in the 
processed features are preserved through a fine-grained sliding window 
technique. Each feature sequence is further segmented into overlapping 
windows, allowing for comprehensive capture of temporal patterns 
while maintaining sufficient data resolution for training. This window -
ing process is applied uniformly across all feature domains, ensuring 
consistent temporal representation of the structural behaviour. To 
facilitate efficient model training, the pre-processed data is organized 
into a structured format using Tensor Flow ’ s data pipeline. The data is

4.4.2. CNN-BiLSTM-MHA model architecture
The proposed model architecture employs a novel multi-stream 
hybrid approach that processes both raw acceleration signals and their 
domain-transformed representations simultaneously. While raw accel -
eration data contains the original structural response patterns, domain- 
transformed features (time, frequency, and wavelet domains) offer

complementary perspectives of the signal characteristics. The simulta -
neous processing of these complementary features enables the model to 
capture a more comprehensive representation of the structural health 
state. The architecture consists of three main components, including an 
encoder with multi-stream processing, a feature fusion mechanism, and 
a decoder for multi-domain reconstruction, as illustrated in Fig. 3 . In this

Each stream implements a CNN-BiLSTM-MHA (Convolutional Neural 
Network-Bidirectional Long Short-Term Memory-Multi-Head Attention) 
hybrid model [ 48 ]. To address the inherent uncertainties in real-world 
structural data, Gaussian noise layer has been added during the 
training process, which serves as a regularization technique and signif -
icantly enhances the generalization capabilities of the model. The raw 
signal stream processes the original acceleration data through a 
multi-scale Conv1D layer with varying kernel sizes (3, 5, 7, 11) to cap -
ture temporal patterns at different scales, while all other Conv1D op -
erations throughout the streams utilize a kernel size of 3. After each 
convolution, Layer Normalization and an ELU activation are applied to 
stabilize training dynamics and introduce nonlinearity.
4.4.3. Feature fusion
This research focuses on integrating and collectively analysing fea -
tures obtained from different domains using DL techniques to enhance 
real-time damage detection through adaptive, unsupervised learning on 
real-world bridge data. Instead of relying on only raw sensor data, 
combining meaningful information from data and the raw data itself 
provides a more comprehensive understanding of the structure behav -
iour. In this study, the feature fusion was conducted after feature 
extraction from signals.
A crucial component of the architecture is the Feature Fusion 
mechanism, which employs cross-modal attention blocks to intelligently 
combine information from different domains. The cross-modal attention 
operates by learning relationships between domain pairs (Raw-Time, 
Raw-Frequency, Raw-Wavelet), enabling the model to identify correla -
tions and dependencies across different signal representations. This 
fusion approach allows the model to: (1) Learn complex relationships 
between features across different domains, (2) Automatically weight the 
importance of different domain representations, (3) Capture comple -
mentary information that might be missed when domains are processed 
independently and (4) Enhance the model’s sensitivity to subtle anom -
alies that manifest across multiple domains
The global fusion bottleneck serves as a crucial integration point 
where features from all accelerometers are combined and compressed. 
The process begins by concatenating features from all accelerometers. 
This concatenated feature set undergoes progressive dimension reduc -
tion through two 1D convolution layers with kernel sizes of 1, first 
reducing to 128 filters and then to the final bottleneck dimension of 32 
filters. This bottleneck design ensures that the model learns to preserve 
the most essential characteristics of the structural response while 
establishing relationships between different sensor locations, enabling 
the detection of global structural behaviour patterns and anomalies.
4.4.4. Decoder
The decoder architecture implements a reconstruction pathway that 
progressively expands the compressed features back to the original 
domain dimensions. The decoder is a branched architecture, creating 
separate reconstruction pathways for each accelerometer and domain 
combination. Each pathway concludes with a domain-specific projection 
layer that maps the features to the appropriate output dimensions 
through a time distributed dense layer. The time distribution layer in the 
final output ensures proper temporal alignment of the reconstructed 
signals across all domains and accelerometers. This comprehensive 
reconstruction approach enables the model to validate its learned rep -
resentations and provide multiple perspectives for anomaly detection 
through reconstruction error analysis.
5. Evaluation strategy
5.1. Synthetic data generation
To overcome the limitation of insufficient real-world data in bridge 
health monitoring for training and evaluation of unsupervised DL 
models, a transformer-based time-series Wasserstein generative adver -
sarial network with gradient penalty (TTS-WGAN-GP) was employed 
[ 49 ]. This model demonstrated its capacity to generate realistic syn -
thetic acceleration signals representative of the bridge’s structural 
responses.
The synthetic data generation process was structured to create two 
distinct datasets. The first dataset was generated to represent the normal 
operating conditions of the bridge, capturing the typical dynamic re -
sponses under ambient excitation. The second dataset served as a 
foundation for simulating various anomaly scenarios, providing a 
controlled environment for evaluating the proposed methodology’s 
ability to detect structural anomalies.
5.1.1. TTS-WGAN-GP architecture
The TTS-WGAN-GP architecture, illustrated in Fig. 4 , consists of two 
primary components: a generator and a critic, both leveraging trans -
former encoder blocks to effectively capture temporal dependencies in 
acceleration time-series data.
The generator component begins with a random noise vector input 
(z) which undergoes latent expansion to create a foundation for the 
synthetic signal. This expanded representation then receives positional 
encoding, which injects sequential information that helps the model 
understand temporal relationships within the data. The core of the 
generator consists of multiple transformer encoder blocks arranged in 
sequence. Each block contains layer normalization, multi-head attention 
mechanisms, and feed-forward neural networks with residual connec -
tions. These components work in concert to transform the noise input 
into meaningful temporal patterns that mimic real acceleration signals. 
The final output produces synthetic acceleration signals. The Critic 
component evaluates both real acceleration signals and the generator’s 
synthetic outputs. After an initial input projection layer that maps the 
signals to a higher-dimensional space, the data flows through parallel 
transformer encoder blocks. Similar to the generator, these blocks 
contain layer normalization, multi-head attention, and feed-forward 
components with residual connections. The transformer blocks enable 
the critic to analyse the data at multiple temporal scales, capturing both 
local vibration patterns and global structural response characteristics. 
The processed features are then condensed through an attention pooling 
mechanism, which produces a context-aware representation of the input 
signals.
Finally, a Wasserstein score is computed, quantifying the distribu -
tional distance between the real and synthetic data distributions. The 
model’s configuration included several key hyper-parameters: a 
sequence length of 600 time steps, a latent dimension of 128, and a 
hidden dimension of 256 for both generator and critic networks. The 
generator utilized 4 transformer layers with 4 attention heads, while the 
critic employed 3 transformer layers with 8 attention heads, allowing it 
to evaluate signals from multiple perspectives. A consistent dropout rate 
of 0.1 was applied throughout the network to prevent overfitting. Dur -
ing training, the generator used a learning rate of 5e

5.1.2. TTS-WGAN-GP data validation
To ensure the quality and reliability of the synthetically generated 
acceleration signals, a comprehensive validation approach was 
employed using multiple similarity metrics across both time and fre -
quency domains. This validation process was crucial to verify that the 
synthetic data accurately represented the statistical and physical char -
acteristics of real bridge vibration responses [ 50 ].
In the time domain, two primary metrics were utilized to evaluate 
the similarity between real and synthetic signals. The Fr ´echet Inception 
Distance (FID) was adopted to quantify the statistical distance between 
the distributions of real and synthetic data. This metric calculates the 
differences between the means and standard deviations of the feature 
representations, providing a scalar value where lower scores indicate 
greater similarity. Additionally, Average Cosine Similarity was imple -
mented to measure the directional similarity between feature vectors 
extracted from real and synthetic signals. This approach involved 
extracting statistical features (including median, mean, standard devi -
ation, variance, root mean square, maximum, and minimum values) 
from both datasets and computing the normalized dot product between 
these feature vectors.
For frequency domain validation, two complementary methods were 
employed to ensure that the synthetic signals accurately captured the 
spectral characteristics of bridge vibrations. Power Spectral Density 
(PSD) Cosine Similarity compared the frequency content of the signals 
by calculating the cosine similarity between the power spectral density 
estimates of real and synthetic data. This metric specifically evaluates 
how well the synthetic signals replicate the energy distribution across 
different frequencies, which is particularly important for structural vi -
bration analysis. The second frequency domain metric, Magnitude 
Correlation, assessed the linear correlation between the magnitude 
spectra of the Fourier transforms from both datasets, providing insight 
into how well the synthetic signals preserved the dominant frequency 
components characteristic of the bridge ’ s dynamic behaviour.
These four validation metrics collectively provided a robust frame -
work for assessing the TTS-WGAN-GP model ’ s performance in gener -
ating realistic bridge acceleration signals. By validating similarity in 
both time and frequency domains, the approach ensured that the syn -
thetic data captured not only the statistical properties of the signals but 
also the underlying physical dynamics of the structural system.

(typically the 99th percentile) of this distribution. This methodology 
ensures that values exceeding the threshold represent statistically sig -
nificant deviations from the normal pattern.

6.2. Extracted Features from Bridge Acceleration Data
The acceleration signals obtained from the bridge monitoring system 
required pre-processing to ensure data quality before feature extraction. 
As described in Section 4.2 , the raw signals exhibited bias error, which 
was eliminated using OLSR method. Following detrending, a low-pass 
Butterworth filter was applied to remove high-frequency noise compo -
nents while preserving the essential structural response characteristics. 
The selection of an appropriate cut-off frequency for the low-pass filter 
was determined through bandwidth estimation using the Welch method. 
The bandwidth analysis revealed that at least 90% of the signal power 
for all accelerometers was concentrated below 200 Hz, which was sub -
sequently chosen as the cut-off frequency. This filtering approach 
effectively preserved the meaningful structural frequencies while 
attenuating noise components. It is important to note that this filtering 
process was specifically applied to the feature extraction procedure and 
did not affect the detrended raw signals, which retained their original 
frequency content for damage detection analysis. This approach avoids 
losing any potentially valuable information and prevents overfitting 
during model training by allowing the raw data to retain its full dynamic 
range.

state without requiring data from damaged conditions.

the data from each accelerometer independently while preserving the
