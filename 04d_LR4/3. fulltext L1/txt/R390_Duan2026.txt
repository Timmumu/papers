ABSTRACT
As the underground pipelines deteriorate with age, their structural vulnerability increases. Cured-in-place pipe 
(CIPP) lining is an emerging trenchless technology used to rehabilitate deteriorated pipes. However, the buckling 
behavior of CIPP liner-rehabilitated pipes is insufficiently investigated. This study presents an integrated 
framework for real-time structural health monitoring and damage detection for underground pipelines by 
leveraging the knowledge of distributed fiber optic sensors (DFOS), finite element analysis (FEA), deep learning 
(DL) algorithms, and digital twin. The study experimentally and computationally investigates the structural 
behavior of a single steel substrate and a CIPP-rehabilitated steel substrate under eccentric compression con -
ditions. The application of DFOS enables the self-sensing function of the pipelines with high-resolution, 
continuous strain measurement. The finite element model and DL-based strain field correlation are employed 
to complement the function of DFOS from strain field monitoring to structural deformation assessment, facili -
tating the development of virtual replicates of self-sensed pipes. The study further explores the location and 
severity of structural damage through numerical and theoretical strain analysis, enabling the detection of minor 
but critical defects. The developed digital twin framework enables real-time structural visualization and en -
hances structural damage assessment. By accurately identifying the early-stage plastic region of deformation and 
quantifying crack severity, the framework supports predictive maintenance and improves pipeline reliability. 
This proactive approach improves the safety of underground pipelines, extends their service life, and reduces 
maintenance costs, demonstrating its practical potential for long-term pipeline infrastructure management.
1. Introduction
Underground pipeline networks form the backbone of modern urban 
infrastructure, enabling the efficient transportation of water, gas, 
sewage, and various industrial fluids [1] . The underground pipeline 
systems are essential to economic growth, public health, and environ -
mental protection. However, with the progression of time, underground 
pipelines become increasingly susceptible to deterioration due to ma -
terial degradation, external loads, corrosion, and soil-structure in -
teractions [2] . The failure of underground pipelines can result in severe 
economic losses, environmental contamination, service disruptions, and 
even loss of life. Consequently, ensuring the long-term structural 
integrity of underground pipelines has become a critical challenge in 
civil and infrastructure applications. Conventional methods, such as 
replacing deteriorated pipelines, often result in substantial 
inconvenience to local residents and industrial facilities due to service 
interruption. In contrast to conventional methods, various trenchless 
technologies have been developed to restore the function of pipelines 
while minimizing excavation and associated disruptions [3] . Among 
them, the cured-in-place pipe (CIPP) lining method has gained wide -
spread recognition for its adaptability to different pipe materials and 
sizes, cost-effectiveness, and capacity to restore the structural integrity 
of deteriorated pipes [4] .
The CIPP lining method involves inserting a resin-based liner mate -
rial into an underground pipe. After being cured in place appropriately, 
the liner effectively forms a new structural layer within the host pipe 
[5] . Enabled by the enhanced structural integrity provided by the CIPP 
liner, this method enhances pipeline durability, improves hydraulic ef -
ficiency, and extends the service life of the liner-rehabilitated pipe sys -
tem. Despite these advantages, the liner-rehabilitated pipe system can

include cracking, delamination, and deformation due to internal or 
external interferences, with buckling being one of the most critical 
failure modes [6–8] . Buckling in underground pipelines, whether orig -
inal or rehabilitated with a liner, can be caused by soil loads, ground -
water pressure, traffic-induced stresses, or vacuum-induced forces 
[9–12] . Since such failures often occur suddenly and can lead to critical 
structural instability, early detection and mitigation are essential for 
ensuring the pipeline’s reliability. Prior studies have evaluated the 
structural performance of CIPP liners through experimental testing and 
analytical or numerical modeling, including investigations of ovaliza -
tion, local buckling instability, liner–host pipe interaction, and critical 
capacity [13–16] . These studies have improved the understanding of the 
progression of buckling from initial imperfection to post-buckling 
deformation in liner-rehabilitated pipes. However, existing in -
vestigations generally rely on visual inspection or discrete sensors, 
which are unable to capture the distributed strain evolution that governs 
buckling initiation. Therefore, real-time tracking of buckling behavior 
and deterioration in liner-rehabilitated systems remains an open chal -
lenge, due to the inaccessibility of pipelines and the complexity of their 
operating environment.
Structural health monitoring (SHM) is an approach to assess the 
condition of critical infrastructure, providing valuable insights that can 
help detect early signs of damage and prevent catastrophic failures 
[17–19] . However, traditional SHM techniques face significant chal -
lenges when applied to underground pipelines. Conventional 
point-based sensors, such as strain gauges or fiber Bragg grating (FBG) 
sensors, are difficult to deploy in underground environments and offer 
limited spatial coverage. Therefore, these sensors are not suitable for 
monitoring underground pipelines, where the locations of potential 
failures are unpredictable. To address these challenges, distributed fiber 
optic sensing (DFOS) has emerged as a promising solution to monitor the 
structural health condition of underground pipelines in real-time [20] . 
Unlike conventional sensors, DFOS enables spatially continuous strain 
measurements along the entire length of the optic fiber. This continuous 
coverage allows for real-time strain monitoring over a large area. 
Anomalous strain signals are indicative of structural irregularities, 
enabling both damage identification and localization. As a result, DFOS 
facilitates the timely detection of localized damage, enables the assess -
ment of overall strain distribution, and monitors structural performance 
with higher resolution and accuracy. Additionally, DFOS offers several 
advantages, including its ability to work in harsh environments, its high 
sensitivity to strain variations, and its long-term stability, making it an 
ideal choice for underground applications. Recent studies have 
employed DFOS as a strain monitoring tool for various underground 
applications, including fractured steel pipelines subjected to operational 
pressure [21] , polyvinyl chloride liners under grouting pressure [22] , 
and corrugated steel culverts under traffic loading [23] . These previous 
studies demonstrate the capability of DFOS to provide continuous strain 
monitoring in underground structures, establishing a foundation for its 
effective integration into real-time SHM applications. The effectiveness 
of DFOS relies on its underlying principle of analyzing backscattered 
light along the optical fiber. In an optical distributed sensor interrogator, 
a laser source with a continuously varying frequency is injected into the 
fiber, and the backscattered Rayleigh signal is analyzed. Since the 
Rayleigh scattering pattern is unique to each segment of the fiber, any 
mechanical strain changes the local optical path, resulting in a shift in 
the backscattered signal. By comparing these shifts with the reference 
state, highly precise distributed strain measurements can be obtained at 
sub-millimeter-scale spatial resolution (SR). This technique makes 
Rayleigh-based DFOS particularly suitable for detecting fine-scale strain 
variations and tracking minor defect initiation and propagation. As a 
result, it is highly promising for SHM applications of underground pipes, 
where direct visual detection is often infeasible.
DFOS provides a powerful tool for monitoring the structural health 
condition of underground pipelines. However, it is unrealistic to have 
DFOS fully cover the entire pipe due to the high cost of fiber optic 
sensors. Additionally, raw sensing data alone may not fully capture the 
complexities of pipeline behavior under real-world conditions. There -
fore, in practical deployment, DFOS is installed on instrumented seg -
ments that terminate at accessible interrogation points. Since pipeline 
distress typically occurs locally, the sensing data from these instru -
mented segments are used to update finite element models of the 
broader system. By combining localized measurements with numerical 
predictions, the integration of DFOS and finite element analysis (FEA) 
provides a comprehensive assessment of the structural performance of 
the underground pipeline system. FEA-based numerical modeling en -
ables the simulation of various loading conditions, damage mechanisms, 
and failure modes, allowing for the prediction of how a pipeline will 
behave under different scenarios [24,25] . This approach shows a 
promising approach to conduct virtual testing on pipelines and generate 
a dynamic, real-time digital replica through digital twin technology. In 
the context of infrastructure, the digital twin framework can continu -
ously update and provide a virtual representation that offers direct 
insight into the structural condition [26] . Wang et al. [27] applied 
polynomial fitting to process experimental and numerical strain data 
and reconstruct the structural deformation of a pipe-liner system in a 
three-dimensional digital twin model. Wang et al. [28] developed a 
digital twin system for pipeline fatigue crack monitoring through the 
internet of things, advanced analytics, cloud computing, and virtual 
reality. However, there is a lack of efficient and timely digital twin 
modeling methods for SHM in pipeline systems, as current practices 
require substantial computational time for data post-processing. To 
address this issue, the incorporation of machine learning (ML) algo -
rithms represents a further step toward real-time data processing be -
tween the physical twin and the digital twin [29] . Limited studies have 
advanced digital twin-based SHM using ML approaches in infrastructure 
domains, most of which focus on bridge components [30–32] , moni -
toring deflection, moment, and crack propagation. Similar advance -
ments have not yet been fully realized for underground pipeline 
infrastructure applications. Building on these established practices, 
extending the use of ML toward continuous condition monitoring of 
pipeline structures, enabling more effective and preventive SHM stra -
tegies, is necessary.
In order to enhance the effectiveness of SHM for underground 
pipeline infrastructure, it is important to enable preventive damage 
identification and localization. Hoult et al. [33] created artificial dam -
ages in steel plate samples and detected subsequent deterioration using 
DFOS by measuring strain variations. Lan et al. [34] installed DFOS on a 
perforated steel pipeline to measure the longitudinal strain distribution 
under ground movement. They found that while the global behavior of 
the perforated pipeline remained unchanged, the strain variation 
around the perforation was significant, highlighting the capability of 
DFOS for damage identification in underground pipeline applications. 
However, these studies were based on predefined, visually identifiable 
damages. To bridge this gap, DFOS needs to be utilized to continuously 
monitor the full structural damage evolution, from initially intact con -
ditions through progressive deterioration leading to failure, enabling 
comprehensive SHM of damage propagation and facilitating early-stage 
damage assessment. One key component of structural damage originates 
from the plastic deformation of steel, which induces permanent defor -
mation. Once the localized strain exceeds the yield strain, the affected 
region enters plasticity. This plastic region of deformation cannot 
restore its original shape after the external load is removed, resulting in 
permanent deformation. This irreversible deformation compromises the 
strength, bearing capacity, and transmission efficiency of the under -
ground pipe [35] . Another key focus is the investigation of crack initi -
ation at critical structural interfaces, such as the adhesive layer. The 
adhesive layer is the intermediate layer between the pipe and CIPP liner, 
used to bond the liner firmly to the host pipe [36] . This layer has been 
largely overlooked in previous studies despite its impact on the overall 
rehabilitation performance of the CIPP liner. Large structural

deformation, such as buckling, can result in localized defects in the 
adhesive layer, compromising the integrity of the liner-rehabilitated 
pipe system. Therefore, it is essential to identify the conditions and lo -

liner is suggested as a potential solution to monitor the condition of the 
entire liner-rehabilitated pipe. While the installation of DFOS directly on 
the underground pipe remains challenging, embedding DFOS within the 
liner prior to installation offers a promising alternative. Since DFOS is 
not directly attached to the pipe surface, the strain monitoring results 
may be affected, making it critical to investigate the performance of 
DFOS-integrated liners in effectively monitoring pipe conditions.
The objective of this research is to develop a digital twin framework 
for real-time monitoring and damage identification in underground 
pipelines. This work seeks to obtain real-time strain fields from DFOS- 
embedded steel substrate samples, develop corresponding finite 
element models to characterize buckling behavior and structural re -
sponses, correlate DFOS-measured and FEA-predicted strain fields to 
update the virtual pipeline state, and identify structural damage such as 
plastic deformation and adhesive cracks through strain-based analysis. 
Section 2 introduces the methods of this study through five main steps 
for developing the digital twin framework. First, the testbed for samples 
subjected to eccentric compressive loading is introduced, and strain 
distributions on the samples are collected in real-time by DFOS. Second, 
one-dimensional DFOS strain data are converted into two-dimensional 
strain fields across the sensor deployment area. Third, finite element 
models are developed to simulate the experimental setup, and nonlinear 
analysis is performed to track structural buckling deformation and 
variations in strain distribution. Fourth, to connect the strain field with 
the structural response, a deep learning (DL) – based strain field corre -
lation analysis is performed between DFOS measurements and FEA 
predictions, enabling the selection of the best-matching strain fields and 
the employment of corresponding FEA structural response results to 
generate the digital twin framework. Lastly, structural damage, 
including plastic regions of deformation and adhesive cracks, are iden -
tified and localized through numerical and theoretical strain analysis. 
The damage information is incorporated into the digital framework to 
enrich the information presentation and facilitate the virtual visualiza -
tion of the holistic structural health condition. Section 3 presents the 
results and discussion from the developed digital twin framework for the 
smart liner system. The innovative approach presented in this study 
advances the field of underground pipeline infrastructure by enabling 
real-time SHM, structural performance prediction, and automated 
damage identification. Through continuous updates of the pipeline ’ s 
virtual state, the digital twin framework supports proactive mainte -
nance, reduces unplanned service disruptions, and enhances risk man -
agement for aging systems. The research contributes to the development 
of a self-sensed, cured-in-place pipeline system that enhances the safety, 
durability, and operational reliability of underground networks.
2. Digital-twin framework development for buckling
This section aims to establish a digital twin framework for damage 
detection and localization in self-sensed, cured-in-place underground 
pipelines. Fig. 1 shows the procedures to develop the framework for a 
virtual, dynamic, and real-time visual representation of the pipe ’ s 
behavior. A testbed is developed to experimentally investigate the 
eccentric buckling behavior of the CIPP liner-pipe system. With the 
application of distributed fiber optic sensing technology, the pipe be -
comes “ self-sensing ” , with real-time generation of high-resolution strain 
measurements. After the strain data are collected by the DFOS interro -
gator, they are converted into coordinate-based strain results in terms of 
the SR and corresponding geometric location. This conversion enables 
the generation of a two-dimensional experimental strain field. The 
experimental strain field is then systematically compared with the nu -
merical strain field obtained from FEA to validate the finite element 
model. A DL-based method is applied to extract and correlate the fea -
tures from both DFOS and FEA strain fields throughout the entire 
eccentric compression process. This correlation helps real-time identi -
fication of underlying structural mechanical behaviors from strain dis -
tributions and supports the visualization of structural deformation via 
the digital twin framework. To further enhance the versatility and 
diagnostic capacity of the digital twin framework and fully leverage the 
potential of DFOS, the damage regions on the samples are identified and 
localized. The developed digital twin framework enables the visualiza -
tion of two types of commonly invisible damage: plastic deformation in 
the steel substrate and cracking in the adhesive layer. The visualization 
of plastic deformation provides insight into permanent, irreversible 
damage on the steel substrate, and the identification of adhesive cracks 
supports the timely diagnosis of the rehabilitation performance of the 
CIPP liner. The innovative integration of real-time sensing technology, 
finite element modeling, and deep learning algorithms develops this 
enhanced digital twin framework, enabling the capacity to generate a 
real-time, visual representation of structural and mechanical behaviors, 
as well as structural damage in the steel substrate.
The real-time capability of the proposed digital twin framework is 
enabled by a pre-trained inverse workflow. High-resolution DFOS 
continuously monitors spatial strain variations, which are instanta -
neously converted into two-dimensional strain fields based on the pre -
defined sensor spacing and spatial resolution. A comprehensive 
nonlinear FEA database is pre-generated, and deep-learning algorithms 
rapidly correlate the incoming DFOS strain fields with the numerical 
database to reconstruct full-field deformation and identify damage re -
gions without iterative simulations. Damage identification and locali -
zation are performed automatically, as spatial strain gradients and their 
temporal evolution indicate cracks and plastic regions of deformation 
without manual intervention. This framework facilitates real-time, 
comprehensive SHM and predictive maintenance of underground pipe -
lines, providing a clear path to directly visual representation for 
assessing potential failure mechanisms before they propagate into crit -
ical issues.

liner-pipe system
In order to reflect and investigate the feasibility of the self-sensed 
cured-in-place underground pipelines in practice, three layers,

flat steel substrate is used to simulate the underground pipe. Epoxy resin 
adhesive is a thermosetting resin, and is commonly used to form an 
adhesive layer between the pipe and CIPP liner, establishing a robust 
bond to ensure the effective rehabilitation performance. The CIPP liner, 
made of fiber-reinforced polymer composites, typically consists of 
polyester or epoxy resin reinforced with glass or carbon fibers, and 
polyurethane/polyethylene coating. A commercial CIPP liner (Starline ® 
2000), a widely applied product for rehabilitating deteriorated under -
ground pipes in the U.S., is used in this study [37] .
To achieve the function of self-sensing, DFOS is installed on the 
samples for real-time, continuous strain monitoring. Distributed fiber 
optic sensing is a sensing technology that utilizes optical fiber to 
continuously measure strain along the entire fiber length. This allows for 
high SR and accurate strain monitoring of structural elements. The op -
tical fiber, typically made of silica glass, serves as both the sensing 
element and the signal transmission medium [38] . The DFOS system 
used in this study operates based on Rayleigh scattering within the fiber. 
Rayleigh scattering occurs due to the microscopic density and compo -
sitional variation in the fiber material, causing a small fraction of the 
transmitted light to scatter in all directions [39] . When the back -
scattered signal interferes with the reference signal generated by the 
interrogator (Luna ODiSI 6100 in this study), it generates an interference 
pattern containing phase and amplitude information, which correlates 
with strain variations along the optical fiber. By applying a fast Fourier 
transform, the system extracts high-resolution, spatially continuous 
strain data. This processed data is used to generate a high-resolution 
strain profile along the fiber length, providing real-time, visually 
accessible graphical strain feedback on the control platform. The 
self-sensing capability enabled by DFOS significantly enhances the 
awareness of structural issues in underground pipe infrastructure.
As shown in Fig. 2 (a), the DFOS is embedded on the surface of a 
152.4 mm × 304.8 mm × 0.81 mm (W × L × H) steel substrate sample 
in a zig-zag pattern to maximize area coverage. This installation pattern 
enlarges the monitoring area and ensures comprehensive strain mea -
surement of the substrate under buckling conditions. Since the direct 
installation of DFOS on in-service underground pipes is difficult to 
implement, one solution is to pre-embed the DFOS in the CIPP liner 
before its installation into the underground pipe, thereby enabling the 
self-sensing capacity. Additionally, the integrity of the adhesive layer 
directly determines the effectiveness of CIPP rehabilitation; any crack or 
defect is important to be detected in a timely manner. Therefore, a same- 
sized liner-rehabilitated steel substrate sample is developed, which has 
two DFOS embedded at the interface between the steel substrate and 
adhesive layer and the interface between the adhesive layer and CIPP 
liner, as shown in Fig. 2 (b). To fabricate this sample, two DFOS are 
installed on the surfaces of the CIPP liner and steel substrate with thin 
layers of epoxy resin adhesive. The sensors are installed in the same area 
and in the same pattern on these two layers to ensure consistency. After 
curing at room temperature for 6 h, the DFOS-installed CIPP liner and 
steel substrate are bonded together firmly using a thick layer of the 
aforementioned epoxy resin adhesive. The prepared sample is then 
placed at room temperature for a 24-hour curing period before testing. 
The thickness of each layer from top to bottom is 1.94 mm, 1 mm, and 
0.81 mm, respectively. To investigate the behavior of the underground 
pipe under complex loading conditions, an eccentric compressive load is 
applied to the test samples through an MTS Criterion ® electromechan -
ical test machine. The top grip clamps the upper corner of the sample 
over an area of 25.4 mm × 30 mm (W × L) and monotonically moves 
downwards at a constant rate of 0.003 mm/s. The bottom grip fixes the 
bottom corner with the same dimensions to ensure consistency. The total 
displacement is 7 mm at the first stage, simulating a significantly large 
buckling deformation, with the maximum out-of-plane deformation 
approximately 20 mm. Moreover, underground pipes can experience 
complex loading conditions that result in multiple plastic deformations. 
Therefore, a second eccentric compressive test with a 4 mm displace -
ment is conducted on the single steel substrate to investigate its per -
formance after initial buckling damage. This scenario simulates a case 
where the underground pipe undergoes significant compressive loading 
that causes buckling, is subsequently restored by internal pressure, and

2.2. Buckling strain field generation from distributed fiber optic sensing
The measurement performance of DFOS is highly dependent on 
interrogator settings, including sampling rate and SR. An excessively 
high sampling rate can generate redundant data, not only occupying 
excessive storage and computational resources but also containing un -
necessary noise. In previous engineering applications, the DFOS system 
typically operated at sample rates ranging from 2 to 100 Hz [40,41] . 
Therefore, a sampling rate of 10 Hz is selected in this study to balance 
data accuracy and measurement efficiency for validating the framework 
under controlled laboratory conditions. In real-world pipeline moni -
toring, the sampling rate can be flexibly adjusted according to opera -
tional requirements. For example, higher rates may be used during 
hazard events or for critical segments, and lower rates are sufficient for 
routine long-term SHM. SR, also referred to as gauge pitch, defines the 
fixed distance between consecutive measurement points along the op -
tical fiber. Given the relatively small measurement area in this study, as 
shown in Fig. 3 , a large SR is insufficient for capturing fine strain gra -
dients. Therefore, the smallest available SR of 0.65 mm is selected to 
achieve a dense strain measurement across the measurement area.
Fig. 3 illustrates the procedures for converting one-dimensional 
DFOS strain data into a two-dimensional strain field for the test sam -
ple. As illustrated in Fig. 3 (a), the DFOS is installed at the center of the 
sample surface in a zig-zag pattern, forming 14 distinct measurement 
lines, each with a uniform length of 200.2 mm and a spacing of 10 mm. 
No.13 denotes the first line at the far end, and No.0 is the last line 
positioned nearest to the grip area, which experiences the largest 
buckling deformation. The orange diamonds represent the individual 
measurement points along the fiber. As previously discussed, the dis -
tance between two contiguous measurement points corresponds to the 
SR of 0.65 mm. Overall, the DFOS covers an area of approximately 
130 mm × 200.2 mm for strain monitoring purposes. The raw DFOS 
data, continuously collected and transmitted to the interrogator, is 
visualized in real-time on the control platform. However, the raw data is 
continuous in one dimension, a method is required to distinguish the 
strain results along each of the 14 measurement lines distributed across 
the sample.

Following the coordinate conversion method, the one-dimensional

2.3. Development of finite element analysis-based virtual testing for 
buckling behavior
To simulate the mechanical behavior of the test samples under 
eccentric compressive load, FEA is performed using ABAQUS/CAE 2024. 
This simulation-based approach enables accurate prediction of struc -
tural responses and reduces the reliance on physical testing, offering 
both cost-effectiveness and deeper insights into mechanical behavior 
[42] . Two distinct finite element models are developed to represent the 
experimental conditions. Fig. 4 exhibits the finite element models of the 
single steel substrate and the CIPP-liner rehabilitated steel substrate. 
Due to the simple single-layer geometry and thin cross-section of the 
single steel substrate, this sample is modeled using shell elements, spe -
cifically 4-node doubly curved shell elements with reduced integration 
(S4R). The developed finite element model simulates the CIPP 
liner-rehabilitated steel substrate, which includes three layers: the steel 
substrate, an adhesive layer, and a fiber-reinforced polymer CIPP liner.

The buckling analysis in this study is performed through a two-stage 
simulation approach, combining both linear and nonlinear methods to 
comprehensively capture the structural behavior under eccentric 
loading. The first stage involves a linear eigenvalue buckling analysis, 
which provides an estimate of the critical buckling load and identifies 
the associated buckling mode shapes. This analysis is based on the as -
sumptions of small deformations and linear elastic material properties, 
making it computationally efficient. However, it does not account for 
real-world effects such as material plasticity, geometric imperfections, 
or post-buckling response, which can significantly affect the actual 
structural performance. To overcome these limitations and obtain a 
more realistic assessment, a nonlinear buckling analysis is carried out 
using the Riks method. This advanced technique accommodates both 
geometric and material nonlinearities and enables a way of tracing the 
entire load – displacement path, including post-buckling behavior. 
Nonlinear material properties are specified in the model, and geometric 
nonlinearity is activated to simulate large deformation accurately. 
Initial imperfections are introduced into the model to reflect the struc -
tural irregularities that occur in practice. These imperfections are 
derived from the first three buckling mode shapes identified in the linear 
eigenvalue analysis. The amplitude of the first mode shape is varied in a 
parametric sensitivity study, ranging from 1 % to 50 % of the total 
sample thickness, to investigate its influence on the buckling response 
[48] . The second and third mode shapes are scaled to half the amplitude 
of the preceding mode, respectively, following common practices in 
imperfection modeling [49] . Once the nonlinear Riks analysis is com -
plete, the predicted strain distribution is compared with the DFOS strain 
field for validation purposes. The validated strain results are then 
mapped to corresponding deformation shapes, which form the basis of 
the digital twin framework. This integrated approach enables real-time 
visualization and interpretation of complex buckling deformation in the 
underground pipeline structure.
2.4. Buckling deformation reconstruction through deep learning-based 
strain field correlation
Minor discrepancies between experimental and numerical strain re -
sults may occur due to differences in measurement resolution and data 
interpolation, as experimental DFOS strain fields are obtained at discrete 
measurement points, while numerical results represent a continuous 
strain field. To bridge this gap, this research employs a DL-based con -
volutional neural network (CNN) to correlate strain fields and identify 
the most similar pair between the DFOS-measured and FEA-predicted 
strain fields. By correlating DFOS-measured strain fields with FEA pre -
dictions in this study, a digital twin framework can be developed for 
underground pipelines. As illustrated in Fig. 6 , the process begins with 
organizing DFOS and FEA strain fields into image formats, which are 
then input into a CNN model for feature extraction. Cosine similarity is 
computed between the extracted feature vectors to quantify the simi -
larity between each pair of strain fields. By iterating through the entire 
FEA dataset, the pair with the highest similarity to the DFOS result is 
selected. To improve the robustness and accuracy of feature extraction, a 
pre-trained ResNet18 model is employed. ResNet, a widely recognized 
CNN model, is known for its strong performance in feature extraction 
across various engineering applications [50] . ResNet18, one of the 
smallest models of ResNet, is employed for its balance between 
computational efficiency and extraction capacity. To ensure reproduc -
ibility, the pretrained ResNet18 weights provided by PyTorch 2.6.0 and 
trained on the ImageNet-1K dataset [51] are utilized to build the 
data-matching method. ResNet18 processes the transformed strain field 
images and outputs a feature map, which is subsequently flattened into a 
one-dimensional vector for similarity evaluation. Once the best match is 
identified, the corresponding FEA deformation result is used to develop 
and update the digital twin framework, enabling a real-time visualiza -
tion of the structural behavior.
2.5. Damage identification and localization: plastic deformation and 
adhesive crack
Real-time damage detection in the underground pipeline infra -
structure using DFOS requires automated approaches for identifying and 
localizing structural irregularities. This section introduces two ap -
proaches for damage identification and localization: one based on 
detecting peaks in strain rate to indicate crack initiation, and the other 
based on identifying plastic regions of deformation in terms of strain 
distributions. A distinct peak in strain distribution consistently occurs 
when a crack intersects the DFOS pathway, providing a reliable signal 
for identifying its location [52] . To detect these peaks accurately, a finer 
SR of strain data is needed. This requires interpolation techniques to 
estimate strain at points between the discrete measurement points 
imposed by the fixed SR of DFOS. Two commonly adopted interpolation 
strategies in two-dimensional domains are bilinear interpolation and 
triangular interpolation, both of which are useful for reconstructing 
continuous data surfaces from sparse measurements [53] . Bilinear 
interpolation is a method that uses repeated linear interpolation to 
interpolate functions of two variables, typically used on regular grids. 
Given the uniform SR of DFOS, the collected strain data is uniformly 
distributed. Therefore, bilinear interpolation is employed to estimate the 
strain on a finer grid in this study.

3.1.1. Strain field demonstration from distributed fiber optic sensor and 
virtual testing
To enable effective visualization and facilitate comparison, the one- 
dimensional DFOS strain data collected along the steel substrate surface 
are converted into a two-dimensional strain field, using the method 
outlined in Section 2.2 . Fig. 8 presents the strain field evolution as 
vertical displacement increases during loading. Positive tensile strain 
appears near the midpoint of the edge of the grip areas, indicated by red 
regions, and negative compressive strain develops at the side of the grip 
areas, highlighted in blue. Overall, the strain field demonstrates an 
approximately symmetric pattern with respect to the centerline between 
the two grip areas. This symmetry aligns with the expected structural 
response, given the symmetric positioning of the grip areas on the steel 
substrate. Minor deviations from perfect symmetry are observed and 
likely caused by instrumental limitations. Despite the sample being 
securely gripped, minor shifts in the experimental setup may introduce 
minor inconsistencies in boundary conditions, resulting in asymmetric 
strain responses. Due to the advantage of numerical analysis being free 
of external disturbances, the comparison results show that FEA has a 
symmetric strain distribution, which is highly similar to the results from 
DFOS.
To validate the feasibility of the finite element model in virtually 
testing the single steel substrate subjected to eccentric buckling, the 
load-displacement curve is compared, and the differences are quantified 
in strain fields between the experiment and simulation. Fig. 9 shows the 
load-displacement curves obtained from experimental and FEA results.

similarity. Additionally, MAPE results, presented in Fig. 10 (b), provide

In addition to the initial 7 mm vertical displacement that induced 
eccentric buckling, the steel substrate is restored to its original shape 
and subjected to another 4 mm vertical displacement to experimentally 
investigate the structural responses after buckling damage. The buckling 
behavior of the single steel substrate under the second-round eccentric 
load is also analyzed numerically. Fig. 11 presents the comparison of the 
load-displacement curves from the second eccentric compressive test. 
Different from the initial test, the steel substrate is already in a buckled 
state during the second test. As a result, the compressive load increases 
with the vertical displacement, while the rate of load increase decreases. 
The FEA results exhibit a similar trend to the experimental results, 
demonstrating the capability of FEA in capturing the entire buckling 
mechanical performance of the steel substrate. Overall, the comparison 
demonstrates that the FEA results are in good agreement with the DFOS 
results, validating the accuracy of the finite element model. Building on 
this validation, FEA can complement DFOS by extending the capacity 
from strain monitoring to comprehensive mechanical performance 
monitoring and facilitating the development of the digital twin 
framework.
3.1.2. Buckling deformation reconstruction and plastic regions of 
deformation visualization
To reconstruct the shape of buckling-deformed single steel substrate 
for the development of the digital twin framework, the strain field 
correlation is essential for an accurate representation of deformation. In 
terms of the strain field correlation method introduced in Section 2.4 , 
the DFOS strain fields are input into the CNN model and set as the 
ground truth. The FEA strain fields are organized into a database, which 
includes all FEA strain fields at the displacement levels from 0 to 7 mm. 
By extracting the features from DFOS strain fields and iteratively 
comparing the cosine similarities within the FEA strain field database, 
the most similar pairs are identified. Fig. 12 shows the results of strain 
field correlation. The corresponding displacements of the matched strain 
field pairs are compared, showing an average displacement difference of 
0.754 mm. This difference is minor and remains within an acceptable 
sub-millimeter-level range, making it suitable for practical applications 
in the underground pipeline industry [54] .
Based on strain correlation results, this study reconstructs the 
deformation and develops the digital twin framework of the buckled 
single steel substrate. Fig. 13 (a) shows the 3D digital twin framework 
for visualizing the deformation of the single steel substrate subjected to 
eccentric compression. The left figure illustrates that as the vertical 
displacement of the top grip increases to 7 mm, the buckling-induced 
deformation of the steel substrate progressively intensifies. The four 
figures on the right provide separate visual representations of the 
deformation at different displacement levels, further demonstrating that 
the highest deformation is consistently observed at the center of the free 
edge between the grips. Additionally, for typical structural steel, the 
yield strain is commonly around 0.002 [37] . When the estimated strain 
in the steel substrate exceeds the yield strain, the corresponding region is 
classified as the plastic region. Fig. 13 (b) illustrates the developed 3D 
digital-twin framework for visualizing plastic regions of deformation in 
the single steel substrate. These regions originate at the edges of grip 
areas and expand to the high deformation regions as eccentric 
compression increases. The above method leverages the continuous 
measurement capacity of DFOS, enabling the detection and mapping of 
the potential damage. It enhances the efficiency of real-time structural 
damage monitoring and can be employed to identify potential damages 
in liner-rehabilitated underground pipeline infrastructure, ultimately 
extending the structural service life.

3.2. Damage identification from the digital twin of liner-rehabilitated steel 
substrate
Different from the single substrate, the liner-rehabilitated steel sub -
strate has two additional layers: CIPP liner and adhesive layer. The liner 
is attached to the steel substrate through an adhesive layer, simulating 
the application of a CIPP liner in rehabilitating an underground pipeline. 
However, damages on the adhesive layer, such as cracks, can lead to 
poor bonding between the liner and the pipe, compromising the reha -
bilitation performance of the CIPP liner [55] . In addition, the plastic 
deformation that occurs on the underground pipe poses a significant 
risk, as it is an irreversible damage. To date, the plastic deformation on 
the pipe has not been systematically investigated through SHM ap -
proaches [56] . To investigate both adhesive crack and plastic defor -
mation damages, a DFOS-integrated liner is employed. Although the 
DFOS is installed directly on the steel substrate, the installation of DFOS 
on an underground pipeline presents a practical challenge. Therefore, 
the strain measurement results obtained from the DFOS-integrated liner 
are used to identify and localize the cracks and plastic deformation on 
the liner-rehabilitated steel substrate. The findings are validated using 
measurements from the DFOS on the steel substrate and through FEA, 
allowing us to assess the effectiveness and accuracy of the 
DFOS-integrated liner in damage detection. Fig. 14 shows the proced -
ures of the study on the liner-rehabilitated steel substrate.
