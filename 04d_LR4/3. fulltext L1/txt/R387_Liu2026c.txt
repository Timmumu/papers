Abstract
As the core nodes of transportation networks, bridges face critical challenges in 
their Structural Health Monitoring (SHM) that are crucial for ensuring transportation 
safety and extending their service life. Traditional SHM methods encounter high 
real-time data processing delays, inefficient fusion of multi-source heterogeneous 
data, and insufficient synchronization between Digital Twin (DT) models and physical 
entities. Existing DT architectures often lack a hierarchical edge-cloud collaboration 
mechanism, with fusion algorithms confined to single-modality data, making it 
difficult to achieve both low latency and high accuracy in anomaly detection. To 
address these issues, this study proposes a hierarchical edge-cloud collaborative 
DT architecture, encompassing the physical layer, edge layer, cloud layer, DT layer, 
and application layer. The core algorithms include: hierarchical multi-sensor fusion; 
a collaborative anomaly detection framework combining edge-based attention 
1D CNN with cloud-based variational autoencoder plus bidirectional long short-
term memory network; and Bayesian model updating for dynamic synchronization 
between virtual models and physical entities. Experimental verification based on a 
1:5 scaled steel simply supported beam shows that the architecture achieves a low 
edge local task delay of 39.7ms and a cloud global task delay of 20.1ms; after strain 
data fusion, the Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE) is reduced by an average of 62%, 
and the RMSE for displacement reconstruction is reduced by 68%; the accuracy 
of anomaly identification reaches over 96%; and Bayesian updating improves 
the accuracy of strain prediction by 40.1%. This study is the first to construct a 
hierarchical edge-cloud collaborative DT architecture, filling the technical gap in 
hierarchical fusion and collaborative computing for bridge SHM. It provides a data-
driven intelligent solution for bridge lifecycle health management and is of great 
significance for promoting the digital transformation of transportation infrastructure.

 1 Introduction
Bridges, as critical nodes in transportation networks, depend on Structural Health Mon -
itoring (SHM) to ensure safety and extend service life. Rising traffic loads and environ -
mental erosion amplify deterioration risks: timber bridges’ lifespan hinges on humidity 
and material selection; irregular slab bridges need seismic optimization due to com -
plex earthquake-induced mechanical behavior; suspension bridges face vortex-induced 
vibration (VIV) threats; foundation scouring requires real-time depth monitoring; inte -
gral bridges’ end displacement is distorted by soil compaction, challenging traditional 
models. Traditional SHM methods have limitations: direct monitoring disrupts traffic, 
indirect methods are affected by vehicle frequencies and road roughness; bridge weigh -
ing-in-motion (BWIM) systems ignore lateral effects leading to weight errors; long-term 
monitoring of external prestressed tendons lacks effective tools; heterogeneous sensor 
network coverage optimization remains unresolved; data imbalance reduces anomaly 
detection accuracy. A systematic review highlights interdisciplinary collaboration as key 
for efficient systems but notes persistent challenges: high costs, insufficient standardiza -
tion, and multi-source data handling issues [1].
Digital Twin (DT) technology provides a dynamic, data-driven solution for bridge 
SHM, enabling precise monitoring and prediction via real-time physical-virtual inter -
action. A data-driven DT framework combined with deep learning integrates multi-
domain features for damage detection, but real-time virtual response synchronization 
requires efficient computing engines [ 2]. Geometric DT model construction—tradi -
tionally manual—can be automated using parametric prototypes generated from point 
cloud-extracted parameters. Integrating Bridge Information Modeling (BrIM) with 
Decision Support Systems (DSS) enhances asset management efficiency. Bayesian model 
updating with seismic data reduces model uncertainty, while accurate material charac -
terization lays the foundation for virtual model precision. Cross-disciplinary DT appli -
cations also offer insights for data analysis and optimization.
Edge-cloud collaborative architecture is critical to resolving DT computing latency 
and resource constraints. Traditional cloud-centric setups suffer from high latency, but 
edge-cloud collaboration allocates real-time tasks to edge devices and offloads complex 
computations to the cloud, enabling low latency, low carbon footprint, and cost-effective 
operation [ 3]. This architecture requires addressing data security and privacy: secure 
deduplication with blockchain ensures confidentiality; lightweight federated learning 
aggregates edge data while protecting privacy [4]; attribute-based keyword search resists 
guessing attacks for secure sharing. Task offloading strategies optimize computational 
task allocation, and cross-domain applications demonstrate edge-cloud collaboration’s 
potential in resource optimization and latency reduction [5]. These technologies support 
real-time updates and efficient operation of bridge DT systems, balancing computing 
power, latency, and energy consumption.
Multi-sensor fusion and anomaly detection are core components of edge-cloud col -
laborative DT architectures, addressing data sparsity, noise, and incompleteness. Fusion 
methods include: Adversarial Autoencoder (AAE) + CycleWGAN to convert sparse 
strain data into dense; Multi-Domain Fusion Autoencoder (MDFA) integrating time/
frequency/wavelet-domain features for higher accuracy; Zero-mean Normalized Cross-
correlation (ZNCC) with weighted data to fuse lateral position info; wavelet analysis + bi-
pass technology for baseline-independent monitoring; hybrid time–frequency analysis

2 Literature review
2.1 Current development status of bridge structural health monitoring
Bridge structural health monitoring (SHM) has evolved from manual inspection to intel-
ligent multi-technology integration, yet accuracy and efficiency gaps persist in complex 
scenarios. Direct monitoring disrupts traffic; indirect methods are compromised by 
vehicle natural vibration and road roughness. Specific issues: timber bridges need inter -
nal deterioration checks (humidity/material-dependent lifespan); irregular slabs require 
seismic optimization; suspension bridge VIV prediction via Gaussian regression relies 
on long-term data [ 6]; foundation scouring (a top failure cause) uses validated photo -
electric sensors; integral bridge end displacement from soil compaction defies existing 
models [7]. Technical limitations: BWIM systems ignore lateral effects (weight errors); 
no long-term tools for external prestressed tendons; sensor coverage needs optimiza -
tion; data imbalance reduces anomaly detection accuracy [ 8]. Interdisciplinary collabo -
ration is key, but high costs, poor standardization, and multi-source data issues demand 
breakthroughs.
2.2 Research progress of edge-cloud collaboration in engineering monitoring
The edge-cloud collaborative architecture delivers a low-latency, low-carbon, and 
cost-effective solution for engineering monitoring, with proven value across multiple 
domains. It allocates real-time tasks to edge devices and offloads complex computations 
to the cloud, reducing network latency by 40% [ 9]. Key technical components include:

2.3 Application of multi-sensor fusion technology in bridge monitoring
Multi-sensor fusion technology effectively addresses issues such as data sparsity, noise, 
and multi-source integration in bridge monitoring, enhancing monitoring accuracy and 
reliability. The framework combining Adversarial Autoencoder (AAE) and CycleWGAN 
fuses sparse strain data into dense data, quantifying human-structure interaction [ 12]; 
the Zero-Mean Normalized Cross-Correlation (ZNCC) method integrates weighted 
sensor data, simultaneously identifying the lateral position and weight of vehicles; 
wavelet analysis combined with dual-pass technology extracts residual contact point 
responses, enabling baseline-independent indirect monitoring; hybrid time–frequency 
analysis is suitable for processing acceleration data from micro-mobile devices [ 13]; 
the Multi-Domain Fusion Autoencoder (MDFA) integrates time-domain, frequency-
domain, and wavelet-domain features to enhance damage detection accuracy; magne -
tostrictive sensors combined with random forests enable vibration frequency prediction 
[14]; clustering algorithms for satellite InSAR data automatically classify Persistent Scat -
terers, facilitating rapid deformation assessment; Ground-Based Interferometric Radar 
(GBIR) provides high-resolution displacement monitoring, but further integration into 
standard monitoring processes is required.
2.4 Research status of digital twin architecture
The digital twin (DT) architecture for bridges has evolved from geometric modeling to 
data-driven real-time interaction, supporting precise monitoring and decision-making. 
The automated parametric prototype model extracts parameters from point clouds to 
generate the bridge DT geometric model; Bridge Information Modeling (BrIM) is inte -
grated with the Decision Support System (DSS) to achieve digital asset management 
[15]; Bayesian model updating technology combines seismic data to reduce uncer -
tainty in structural and soil parameters; the stream computing engine processes virtual 
dynamic responses under real-time traffic loads, achieving physical-virtual synchroniza -
tion; the data-driven DT framework combines deep learning to integrate multi-domain 
features for damage detection. In cross-domain reference, the DT data-driven method 
for wastewater treatment in pulp and paper mills provides a reference for data analysis 
and optimization of bridge DT [16]. The current DT architecture still needs to overcome 
issues such as real-time computing efficiency and the depth of multi-source data fusion 
to enhance the full lifecycle management capability of bridges. The latest research pro -
poses a wastewater treatment optimization architecture based on digital twins, but it

2.5 Application of anomaly detection algorithm in bridge monitoring data
Anomaly detection algorithms are advancing to boost bridge monitoring data accuracy 
and real-time performance. Key innovations: artificial neural networks (ANNs) + matrix 
analysis for pseudo-baselines (no historical data needed); machine learning transform -
ing time series into tensors to learn multi-sensor spatiotemporal patterns, identifying 
anomalies and their sources [ 18]; multi-task alarm systems for precise long-span rail -
way bridge early warning; synthetic data generation to resolve training data shortages; 
Gaussian regression using 4-year data for suspension bridge VIV prediction; random 
forests + magnetostrictive sensors for waveform classification and vibration frequency 
prediction [ 19]. These algorithms address critical challenges (data imbalance, missing 
baselines, real-time warning) but need further optimization of generalization and real-
time performance.
3 Edge-cloud collaborative digital twin architecture and core algorithm design
3.1 Overall design of edge-cloud collaborative digital twin architecture
To address traditional bridge SHM challenges—real-time data processing delays, ineffi -
cient multi-source heterogeneous data fusion, and insufficient synchronization between 
digital twin (DT) models and physical entities—this section proposes a hierarchical 
edge-cloud collaborative DT architecture. The physical layer integrates bridge entities 
and multi-modal sensors to collect multi-dimensional monitoring data and optimize 
heterogeneous sensor coverage. The edge layer deploys gateways and nodes for real-time 
data preprocessing, local feature fusion, and lightweight anomaly detection, filtering 
effective data to reduce bandwidth pressure. The cloud layer uses a distributed platform 
for large-scale storage, global multi-source fusion, DT model training, and parameter 
updates, pushing optimized parameters to the edge. The digital twin layer constructs 
multi-scale finite element models and dynamic mapping modules for real-time physi -
cal-virtual state sync. The application layer integrates health assessment, safety warning, 
and O&M decision support for lifecycle management. Core coordination: edge handles 
low-latency lightweight tasks, cloud manages high-precision large-scale computing, bal -
ancing real-time performance and accuracy for efficient bridge SHM. The edge-cloud 
collaborative SHM/DT architecture comparison of this study is shown in Table 1.
Edge-cloud communication is secured by TLS 1.3 encryption and certificate authenti -
cation; malicious data is filtered at the edge using the 3σ principle, and MQTT QoS 1 is 
used to ensure delivery during transmission; data is cached and resent after 2 h in case of 
synchronization failure; the edge uses a pre-trained model to provide independent early 
warning in case of network outage; and data is appended with SHA-256 hashes to ensure 
integrity.
The edge layer is equipped with a comprehensive fallback mechanism: when the cloud 
connection is lost or the transmission delay exceeds the preset threshold (100ms), it 
automatically switches to the pre-trained local anomaly detection model, independently

completes the structural anomaly identification and triggers local audible and visual 
alarms; at the same time, it caches key monitoring data and automatically resends it to 
the cloud after the connection is restored, ensuring data integrity and structural security.
3.2 Design of multi-sensor fusion method
Multi-sensor fusion is a critical link connecting physical entity data and virtual model 
interactions in bridge structural health monitoring (SHM) digital twin (DT) architec -
tures, with its effectiveness directly determining the system’s real-time structural state 
perception and decision-making accuracy. In complex bridge monitoring scenarios—
characterized by multi-source data heterogeneity, spatiotemporal distribution differ -
ences, and computational resource constraints [ 20]—traditional single-level fusion 
methods fail to simultaneously meet edge-side real-time processing needs and cloud-
side global decision-making requirements. Therefore, hierarchical collaborative fusion 
has gradually become the core direction to break through this contradiction and opti -
mize the balance between real-time performance and decision reliability.
Previous studies suggest edge-side fusion prioritizes lightweight algorithms to ensure 
low latency for real-time early warning, while cloud-side fusion uses robust methods 
to address multi-source data uncertainty and enhance global decision reliability [ 21]. 
Based on this insight, this study proposes an edge-cloud collaborative hierarchical fusion 
scheme (Fig. 2): the edge deploys a 1D convolutional neural network (CNN) to fuse local 
features of preprocessed strain and acceleration data, capturing time-domain correlation 
features to meet real-time requirements; the cloud leverages Dempster-Shafer evidence 
theory to integrate spatial information from multi-point strain data via basic probabil -
ity assignment rules, reducing data uncertainty interference and improving global state 
assessment credibility. The symbol definition of the core algorithm in this study is shown 
in Table 2.

 T o address the issue of high-conflict data caused by complete sensor failure, the cloud-
based fusion algorithm employs a three-step mechanism: “conflict identification—
failure detection—isolation and refusion. ” This involves calculating the multi-source 
data conflict coefficient K, identifying high-conflict scenarios when it exceeds a preset 
threshold (e.g., 0.8); triggering failure detection—verifying the consistency of sensor 
output with other means, marking failures when deviations exceed 3σ (combining 
edge sensor health feedback to improve accuracy); and excluding failed data and 
re-performing DST fusion. Simulation tests show that when a strain sensor completely 
fails, the mechanism can accurately identify and isolate the faulty data, maintaining 
a fusion accuracy of ≥ 95% after isolation, thus avoiding the distortion caused by high 
conflict in traditional DST.
 This hierarchical fusion strategy balances real-time performance and accuracy by 
leveraging edge devices’ lightweight computing and cloud’s large-scale data processing. 
Edge local fusion filters effective features to reduce bandwidth consumption, while 
cloud global fusion provides reliable input for digital twin model updates and anomaly 
detection—boosting bridge SHM intelligence, decision efficiency, and lifecycle 
management support.
 
3.3 Anomaly detection algorithm design
Anomaly detection is the core module of bridge structural health monitoring (SHM) 
systems, enabling risk early warning and active operation and maintenance—its perfor -
mance directly determines whether safety hazards are detected and addressed timely. 
However, bridge monitoring scenarios’ dynamic nature, multi-source data character -
istics, and distributed computing resources make single algorithms unable to simul -
taneously meet edge-side real-time response and cloud-side global decision-making 
requirements [24]. Thus, a hierarchical collaborative anomaly detection framework has 
become the inevitable solution to this dilemma, balancing real-time performance and 
decision accuracy.

3.4 Construction and updating of digital twin model
As the virtual core of bridge structural health monitoring systems, the digital twin mod -
el’s construction efficiency and dynamic update capability directly determine the real-
time synchronization accuracy between physical entities and virtual models, serving as 
the foundation for precise perception and prediction of structural states. Traditional 
model construction relies heavily on manual operations, which are not only inefficient 
but also difficult to achieve consistency between geometric accuracy and physical behav-
ior [30]; the absence of a dynamic update mechanism leads to the model’s inability to 
reflect the true state changes of the structure in a timely manner, severely limiting the 
practical application value of digital twin technology.
Previous studies recognize parametric modeling as key to digital twin (DT) construc -
tion efficiency, but most focus only on geometric feature extraction, ignoring physical 
attributes (material properties, boundary conditions) and causing mechanical behavior 
deviations between models and actual structures. Dynamic updates often use fixed-
threshold triggers, lacking data-driven adaptive correction to handle complex struc -

4.4 Performance evaluation of anomaly detection algorithms
Research experiments have shown that edge and cloud algorithms exhibit varying per -
formance strengths under different types of anomalies, as illustrated in Table  9. Edge 
algorithms exhibit a higher recall rate for severe anomalies, fulfilling the core require -
ment of “no missed detections” in engineering applications. Conversely, cloud algo -
rithms outperform edge algorithms in terms of accuracy, recall rate, and F1 score, with 
an average improvement of 5%-8%. This verifies that the collaborative framework of 
“edge rapid screening—cloud deep verification” effectively balances real-time perfor -
mance and detection accuracy.
To verify the superiority of this architecture, a comparison with the currently popular 
SHM anomaly detection model was conducted: processing speed and anomaly detec -
tion accuracy were tested on the same dataset. The results show that the proposed 
architecture achieves an anomaly detection accuracy of 96.5% with a single-sample 
latency of 39.7ms; ResNet1D achieves 92.1% accuracy with a latency of 50.2ms; Trans -
former achieves 93.3% accuracy with a latency of 65.4ms; and LSTM achieves 91.5% 
accuracy with a latency of 45.8ms. This architecture improves accuracy by 4.4% and 
reduces latency by 20.9% compared to ResNet1D; it also improves accuracy by 3.2% 
and reduces latency by 39.3% compared to Transformer, demonstrating superior overall 
performance.
To quantify the synergistic effect of 1D CNN, VAE, and BiLSTM, ablation experiments 
were performed: testing the performance of the full model (1D CNN + VAE + BiLSTM), 
the model without 1D CNN (VAE + BiLSTM), the model without VAE (1D CNN + BiL-
STM), and the model without BiLSTM (1D CNN + VAE). The results show that the 
overall model achieves an accuracy of 96.5% with a latency of 39.7ms. Removing the 1D 
CNN reduces the accuracy to 89.2% and increases the latency to 52.3ms. Removing the 
VAE reduces the accuracy to 91.8% with no significant change in latency. Removing the 
BiLSTM reduces the accuracy to 92.5% and decreases the latency to 35.1ms. This indi -
cates that the 1D CNN is responsible for efficient feature extraction, the VAE enhances 
noise robustness, and the BiLSTM improves sequence modeling capabilities; the synergy 
of these three components is the core of the architecture’s performance advantage.
