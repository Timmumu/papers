ABSTRACT
This study introduces an innovative data-BIM integrated digital twin framework for the quality 
monitoring residential developments. The framework amalgamates residential building infor -
mation modeling (RBIM), surveying sensors and Internet of Things (SSIoT) technologies to 
establish a multi-faceted and multi-scale monitoring system. Within this framework, RBIM serves 
as a residential information twin model that aggregates comprehensive life cycle data of resi -
dential projects, encompassing design, building, operation, and maintenance. The physical things 
are linked to cyberspace via SSIoT technology, significantly improving data exchange and pro -
cessing capabilities. To address the limitations of singular monitoring technology, a BIM 
collaborative multi-source data monitoring method for residential engineering quality is pro -
posed, utilizing the BIM model alongside the advancement of real-time transmitting monitoring 
LiDAR, automated monitoring robots, GNSS, and various other sensors. This framework seeks to 
create a novel paradigm for intelligent, comprehensive data management and synthesizing het -
erogeneous data from several sources, focusing on the quality monitoring of residential projects to 
guarantee quality, safety, and operational efficiency.
1. Introduction
With the increase in the volume of the building and the complexity of the structure [ 1 , 2 ], the quality of residential engineering 
problems is becoming more and more prominent, and once an accident occurs, all of them are very likely to lead to significant loss of 
people ’ s lives and property. At the present stage of China ’ s residential engineering, quality monitoring methods generally still rely on 
traditional methods [ 3 , 4 ], mostly using a single instrument. However, now that intelligent construction has become the development 
trend of building construction [ 5 – 7 ], traditional monitoring methods have problems such as low efficiency, poor timeliness, infor -
mation silos, insufficient data utilization, and high cost [ 8 – 10 ], which have been difficult to meet the needs of quality monitoring of 
residential projects. There is an urgent need to seek skill innovation to realize the intelligence of residential engineering quality 
monitoring, reduce building energy consumption and promote the sustainability of building materials [ 11 , 12 ].

Consequently, academia and industry have investigated sophisticated approaches for monitoring the quality of residential projects 
to address the intricate demands of contemporary residential developments [ 13 – 16 ]. The integrated remote sensing monitoring 
technique, including sky, space, and ground, utilizes various sensors through Surveying Sensors and Internet of Things (SSIoT) 
technology [ 17 – 19 ] to facilitate the real-time and complete assessment of residential project quality. Advancements in technology 
have elevated 3D LiDAR technology for its capacity to deliver high-precision and high-resolution 3D spatial data [ 20 ]. Compared to 
conventional measuring methods, Lidar technology offers rapid measurement speed, less reliance on external conditions, and the 
capacity for automatic processing of extensive data sets [ 21 – 23 ]. Nevertheless, conventional LiDAR systems sometimes need more

In order to realize the all-round monitoring of the quality of residential projects, this paper proposes a sky-space-ground integrated

multi-sensor-integrated cloud platform for monitoring the quality of residential projects, so as to realize sky-space-ground integrated 
monitoring of residential projects. This framework provides a new research paradigm for intelligent big data management and multi- 
source heterogeneous data fusion for residential project quality monitoring.
2. Methodology
2.1. Digital twin architecture and framework
2.1.1. SSIoT-RBIM system architecture
This research introduces an SSIoT-RBIM architecture for digital twins to oversee the quality of residential construction. The ar -
chitecture comprises data acquisition, transmission, digital modeling, data/model integration, and service layers (see Fig. 1 ).
The data acquisition layer serves as the cornerstone of the digital twin and is responsible for collecting the necessary information 
from various data sources. This layer deal with the challenges of heterogeneity and large amounts of data from the building level, 
including data of different types, formats, and sources. In particular, each sub-asset has its unique sub-data identifiers that can provide 
the required data, information, or models when the corresponding query is received. Various data collection technologies are used in 
this layer, including contactless technologies (e.g., RFID, image-based technologies), distributed sensor networks, wireless commu -
nications, and mobile access. The selection and implementation of these technologies need to be customized based on the asset type 
and expected data processing needs to ensure real-time data collection and efficient management.
The principal duty of the data transmission layer is to guarantee the secure and efficient transfer of gathered data to a higher level 
for subsequent modeling and analysis. This layer accommodates several communication technologies, encompassing short-range 
access networks (including WiFi, Zigbee, NFC, M2M, and Z-Wave) and broader-range communication technologies (such as 3G, 4G, 
LTE, 5G, and LP-WAN). As technology progresses, although WiFi remains the predominant wireless LAN technology, alternatives such 
as Li-Fi and LP-WAN are becoming acknowledged as viable options for creating digital twins at the city and building levels, particularly 
regarding security and unlicensed frequency bands. Critical concerns at the transport layer are network energy efficiency, transmission 
velocity, data security, and the imperative to maintain data integrity and privacy protection throughout transmission.
The data modeling layer is essential for creating and managing digital representations of physical assets. This layer utilizes Building 
Information Modeling (BIM) and City Information Modeling (CIM) to store intricate data regarding buildings and urban assets, 
incorporating external factors that influence these assets, including meteorological and socio-cultural contexts. The digital modeling 
layer facilitates several applications, including real-time condition monitoring, asset management, urban planning, and scenario

The Data/Model Integration Layer is essential to the digital twin architecture and effectively integrates and processes all data

engagement.
2.1.2. SSIoT-RBIM framework
The framework is founded on the SSIOT-RBIM architecture and comprises a physical layer, a data storage layer, a data integration 
layer, and an application layer (see Fig. 2 ).
The physical layer collects raw data about the building structure and environmental conditions through multiple sensor devices 
deployed in the building environment. These sensors transmit the data to the upper layer for processing through a local gateway, which 
not only acts as a data relay but also provides initial data filtering and pre-processing. The core of the physical layer design lies in the 
selection and deployment of sensors. Sensors not only need to meet the requirements of high accuracy and low energy consumption but 
also must operate stably in the building environment over a long period. The selection of appropriate sensor types and deployment 
locations is fundamental to ensuring that comprehensive and accurate data can be collected. In addition, the communication protocol 
and security of data transmission between the sensors and the local gateway are also elements that must be considered in the design.
In the data storage layer, dynamic building data are stored within the SSIoT sensor database specially set up by the RBIM-SSIoT 
framework. Considering the time-varying nature of sensor data, this layer adopts an efficient time-series database to manage his -
torical and real-time observation data and uses knowledge graphs to organize and manage static data such as attributes and structural 
information. By constructing relationships between entities, the knowledge graph not only stores static structural information but also 
combines this information with dynamic data to form a more comprehensive view of the data. Regular data backup and recovery 
mechanisms are implemented, as well as encryption of sensitive data, taking into account the security and integrity of the data.

Residential Building Information Modeling (RBIM) data is sent in the data integration layer utilizing the Industrial Foundation

integration of RBIM and SSIoT multi-source heterogeneous data through object mapping tables. The mapping table internally cor -

framework enables the retrieval of the associated local identifier from the mapping table by querying the sensor ’ s GUID when SSIoT

establishing a dependable basis for the system ’ s sophisticated analysis and decision assistance.
The application layer provides various application services and interfaces directly to users. In this layer, by leveraging the inte -
grated data, various applications can be run to provide real-time data views, historical data analysis, early warning systems and 
maintenance recommendations to help users effectively manage and maintain building facilities. In addition, the integration of 
advanced data analytics and machine learning models can further enhance the intelligence of the decision support system, enabling it 
to anticipate building maintenance needs and potential problems based on historical and real-time data.
The established digital twin framework employs a modular data architecture in which the data from each monitoring point is 
treated as a separate unit. For a residential complex with hundreds of monitoring points, the modular design allows the system to 
perform effective data isolation and parallel processing across different monitoring points, thus reducing data processing bottlenecks. 
The study employs advanced data stream processing techniques to support high throughput and low latency data processing. With real- 
time data stream processing, the system is able to instantly receive and analyze data streams from hundreds of monitoring points, 
ensuring real-time and accurate data analysis in order to enhance further the scalability and flexibility of the system, a cloud-based 
resource management strategy is implemented, which utilizes the elastic scaling function of cloud computing to dynamically adjust 
resource allocation.

2.2.1. Automated robotic residential quality monitoring

target tracking, and automated data recording. Automatic target identification may compute the laser beam ’ s deviation from the 
prism ’ s center and direct the operation to precisely target the prism ’ s center utilizing the CCD array sensor ’ s automatic motor.

monitor moving or altered points in real-time. The automated data recording feature transfers measurement data to the monitoring 
center or cloud platform in real time via the integrated or external data transmission module, enabling prompt analysis and long-term 
data storage.
The monitoring system routinely activates monitoring robots to conduct scheduled inspections of designated monitoring stations at

comprehensive structural monitoring. The coordinates and elevation data of each measurement point are systematically gathered to

2.2.4. Environmental sensing monitoring systems
Environmental monitoring sensors play a critical role in residential construction quality monitoring, collecting environmental data 
from construction sites in real-time to support project managers in assessing environmental conditions, developing construction 
strategies, and ensuring the safety and health of construction workers. These sensors include temperature and humidity meters, wind 
speed and direction meters, noise, PM2.5/10 sensors, etc., each performing a specific monitoring function. The placement strategy of 
the sensors is critical to ensure that effective monitoring data is obtained.
Temperature and humidity sensors should be placed away from the effects of direct sunlight and precipitation to avoid the direct 
effects of these environmental factors on temperature and humidity readings. At the same time, these sensors should be placed in well- 
ventilated areas to ensure that the temperature and humidity data measured are a true reflection of the environmental conditions on 
the construction site.
Wind speed and direction sensors should be placed in a manner that takes into account the ability of the building itself to block and 
direct the wind flow. In order to obtain accurate wind data, sensors should normally be mounted on top of the building or in an open 
area, above surrounding buildings and other obstacles, to minimize the effect of these obstacles on wind speed and direction.
Noise sensors should be deployed in locations representative of typical noise levels from construction activities while avoiding 
proximity to overly noisy mechanical equipment or major traffic thoroughfares so that excessive background noise does not affect the 
accuracy of the data.
PM2.5/10 sensors, on the other hand, should take into account the possible contribution of dust from wind direction and other 
sources. They should be placed downwind from major dust sources so that changes in air quality caused by construction activities can 
be accurately captured.
To mitigate the interference of environmental factors with sensor readings, research into the use of sensors with high immunity to 
interference and enhanced signal processing algorithms can significantly reduce data errors caused by electromagnetic and physical 
interference. Regular calibration and maintenance are necessary to ensure the long-term stable operation of the sensors. The system 
performs regular performance testing and calibration of all sensors to correct deviations that may be caused by long-term use and 
environmental changes.

2.3.2. Comparison of BIM and point cloud analysis methods
The dynamic change detection system utilizing BIM and point cloud data is a crucial novel method for monitoring building safety, 
with detection outcomes predominantly conveyed through reports. Real-time online dynamic comparison and analysis of BIM and 
point cloud data is a technical difficulty. A method for organizing data within the BIM model and point cloud is provided, employing a 
‘three-step ’ strategy of digital-module decoupling, geometric conversion, and attribute mapping to address issues of inadequate data 
interoperability and integration. This study investigates the dynamic change detection approach for BIM models and point cloud data 
to address the challenge of comparing and evaluating BIM and point cloud data in three-dimensional space, facilitating the efficient 
integration of both data types (see Fig. 4 ).
A dynamic change detection analysis and visualization method combining BIM and point cloud data is proposed to obtain 3D 
deformation results of buildings in real time. Distinguishing from the existing research, a BIM and point cloud data organization 
method is proposed to construct a 3DTiles tile structure of point cloud and BIM models to achieve effective integration of BIM and point 
cloud data. The IFC file is separated and reconstructed by component level through digital-modular decoupling to achieve error-free 
linking of geometric and semantic information of components; for the storage and processing problems of large-scale point cloud data, 
an octree segmentation algorithm is adopted. The octree refines the field point cloud data into a multilevel hash file by recursively 
partitioning the three-dimensional space. This data structure effectively reduces the redundancy of data storage and greatly improves 
the efficiency during spatial queries. The octree is able to partition the point cloud data into multiple small spatial units, which can be 
loaded and processed as needed. Through the spatial division of point cloud data, the system is able to quickly locate part of the data 
related to the BIM model, thus greatly improving the efficiency of comparison and analysis between point cloud data and the BIM 
model. The use of Octree not only improves the speed of data loading and rendering but also effectively optimizes memory man -
agement, especially when facing large building models containing millions of point cloud data, and still maintains high performance.

3. Experiments and analyses
3.1. Multi-source remote sensing monitoring experiments
3.1.1. Monitoring of frame buildings
A monitoring LIDAR measurement system was employed to observe a frame construction (see Fig. 5 ). Two distinct target spheres 
were positioned at varying elevations within the building for an eight-phase point cloud data acquisition, and the alterations in the 
centers of the individual table target spheres were examined to assess the modifications in the building.
The point cloud data obtained from the acquisition were processed and analyzed (see Fig. 6 ). The particular target ball was 
extracted (see Fig. 7 ), and the data were processed to calculate the spherical center coordinates using the scanline-expanded local 
interpolation smoothing algorithm (LSIIS). The coordinates of the two unique target balls for the eight phases are shown in Table 1 .
This study employs a comprehensive analytical approach to illustrate the building ’ s deformation trend over time precisely. Uti -
lizing the coordinate data of the marker sphere collected during the initial period as a reference baseline, we monitor the displacement 
variations of the sphere ’ s center for each marker sphere across the x, y, and z axes in successive periods (refer to Table 2 ). By analyzing 
the coordinate offsets of each period relative to the reference period, we can meticulously measure the motion and deformation of the 
building structure in all directions. Furthermore, the axial change data for each marker sphere is represented as a time series of line 
graphs (see Fig. 8 ), enhancing the intuitiveness of the results and aiding in the detection and analysis of change trends.
Through the examination of the positional alterations of the two primary target spheres during the eight phases of data collection, 
we discern the following phenomena: The positional variation of target sphere ① remains below 1 cm, indicating a degree of stability, 
but the positional variation of target sphere ② extends to within 2 cm, demonstrating comparatively less stability. Target ball ① is 
situated at the base of the building, and its minimal positional variation signifies the structural stability of the foundation; conversely, 
target ball ② , positioned at the apex of the building, exhibits more pronounced fluctuations, suggesting a more extraordinary 
displacement phenomenon in the upper section of the structure. Subsequent investigation indicates that the fundamental cause of the 
disparity in displacement between the upper and lower sections is the instability of the beam connections in the steel frame structure.
This discovery is crucial for evaluating the structural integrity and stability of edifices. In steel-framed structures, the stability of 
beam connections is directly linked to the total structural integrity. This study elucidates the disparities in building stability across 
varying heights. It underscores the necessity of enhancing the resilience of structural joints, offering critical insights for future 
maintenance and reinforcement efforts.

3.1.2. Tower crane monitoring
This study developed a dynamic scanning protocol for ongoing data acquisition during the operation of a tower crane at a building 
site (see Fig. 9 ). The objective was to document the crane ’ s detailed condition at various intervals.
Two sets of 100,000-point cloud data were obtained at distinct scanning intervals (see Fig. 10 ). The tower crane ’ s geographical 
position and structural modifications at these successive time intervals were meticulously analyzed and contrasted utilizing advanced 
data techniques, thereby precisely illustrating its operational dynamics.
The results of the comparative analyses revealed that the average offset of the tower crane during operation exhibited a positive 
displacement of 0.77 cm and a negative displacement of   0.78 cm (see Fig. 11 ). This result implies that during the monitoring period, 
the trajectory of the tower crane moved forward in the north direction by an average of about 0.77 cm and backward in the other 
direction by an average of about 0.78 cm. This slight bi-directional offset reveals the stability characteristics of the tower crane during 
operation and, at the same time, hints at the possible existence of cyclic oscillations or structural offsetting behaviors.
Further analyses suggest that the determination of this offset not only reflects the elastic response of the crane structure during 
stresses but may also indicate the effects on the crane positional accuracy due to factors such as wind loads, load variations, and 
operational practices. In addition, this data is of great value in assessing the operational safety and structural health of the tower crane, 
providing a quantitative means to monitor the real-time operational condition of the crane and providing data support for taking 
necessary maintenance or adjustment measures.

3.2. Digital twins for real-time monitoring of multi-source sensor data
This study takes the cloud platform as the core and relies on the cloud server to construct a cloud database for receiving, processing, 
storing and forwarding multi-source data generated in residential engineering quality monitoring, such as environmental monitoring 
data, building structural safety monitoring data, and point cloud data. Using the elasticity and scalability of the resources of the cloud 
platform, a digital twin system based on RBIM for monitoring the quality of residential works is developed and designed with the server 
as the support and the SSIoT system architecture. The system is able to call the information table and data table stored in the cloud 
database through the back-end service to achieve dynamic management and scheduling of data. The end devices in the field are 
configured with a local server and an edge control unit designed to collect data from the end sensors while storing this data and 
forwarding it to the cloud database. In order to ensure that data are not lost or corrupted during transmission between the local server 
and the cloud database, this system is designed with data integrity and security safeguards. Firstly, to prevent loss or damage during 
data transmission, the system adopts a distributed data backup mechanism. The local server caches sensor data in real time and 
synchronizes it with the cloud database during network recovery. Each data transmission is accompanied by an integrity check to 
ensure that the data is not damaged during transmission. If the data is corrupted during transmission, the local server will auto -
matically re-transmit the data through the retransmission mechanism until the cloud database successfully receives and verifies the 
data. Secondly, in order to improve the fault tolerance of the system, the local database will be used as a backup source for the cloud 
database in the event of network interruption or security attack on the cloud server. This backup function not only ensures high data 
availability but also ensures data security through encrypted storage. To prevent data loss or tampering, the data synchronization 
between the local database and the cloud database adopts a two-way confirmation mechanism, i.e., before and after each data syn -
chronization, the system will carry out a data consistency check to ensure the consistency of the data during synchronization through 
timestamp control. The local server and the edge control node are connected through various communication modules (e.g., WiFi, 
Bluetooth) and interfaces based on the Windows operating system to efficiently summarise and process data from various types of 
sensors (see Fig. 14 ).

3.2.1. Design of a spatial database for integrated remote sensing of space-air-ground
An object-relational spatial database is proposed to address the challenges of efficient storage and management of multi-source 
monitoring data in long-term monitoring activities. The conceptual and logical models of the space-air-ground integrated remote 
sensing monitoring spatial database are designed, and the model ’ s entities, attributes, and connections are discussed. In this paper, 
following the standardized design methodology and targeting the critical needs of quality monitoring of residential construction 
projects, a spatial database management system with the functions of data storage, management, analysis, and visualization is 
established based on the conceptual and logical models by adopting a mainstream backend framework and combining the develop -
ment with common database and programming language tools as well as BIM technology. The system can process monitoring data 
efficiently and stably, has the advantages of fast response and low error rate, effectively improves the storage and management ef -
ficiency of space-air-ground integrated remote sensing monitoring data, and achieves comprehensive analysis and management of sky- 
space-ground integrated remote sensing monitoring data (see Fig. 15 ).
3.2.2. Analyses of integrated remote sensing monitoring by space-air-ground
This work established a generic family model to efficiently describe sensor devices within the RBIM, encapsulating all pertinent 
sensor devices. The sensor families in this model are positioned appropriately within the BIM model to represent their precise and 
accurate placements in the physical structure. To systematically manage the information of these devices, two-parameter groups are 
established for the sensor families, categorized as ‘Identity Data ’ and ‘Building Attributes. ’ Constructing Properties. Identity Data refers 
to the section of the Type Properties box that retains identifying parameters, like the sensor ’ s name and position. The Building 
Properties section in Revit ’ s Type Properties window primarily documents the environmental parameters recorded by the sensor. The 
sensors included in the RBIM model establish GUID that facilitate the precise and unique mapping of the sensors. Fig. 16 illustrates the 
integration of sensor data with the RBIM model, enabling the RBIM model to serve as the data ’ s conduit, creating a digital twin of the 
physical and virtual realms of engineering.
Contemporary home engineering monitoring initiatives depend on integrating diverse data from many sources for precise spatial 
positioning and structural stability assessment. Automated monitoring robots deliver precise coordinate information and altitude 
measurements. Simultaneously, GNSS is tasked with delivering worldwide positioning services. SBAS-InSAR technology observes the 
subsidence of building structures by examining the phase discrepancies between radar images captured at various intervals. The data 
integration layer of the SSIoT-RBIM system effectively integrates these multi-source data. The time-series data collected by the 
automated monitoring robot is utilized to compute building settlement, and the resultant settlement data is compared and merged with 
the time-series settlements obtained by the SBAS-InSAR technique. In the data integration process, settlement data are linked to GUID 
specified in the BIM via an object mapping table, ensuring that data from each measurement point accurately corresponds to the 
precise location of the physical structure. Fig. 17 illustrates that the settlement monitoring outcomes from the autonomous monitoring 
robot and the SBAS-InSAR technique at the same monitoring location exhibit identical settlement patterns, achieving millimeter and 
sub-millimeter precision. This high-precision monitoring dataset offers robust scientific support for project management and decision- 
making, improving the capacity to anticipate and avert structural issues.
To ensure data consistency and deal with potential data conflicts, the system uses a weighted average method to integrate

4. Conclusion
(1) This paper proposes a digital twin framework based on sky-space-ground integrated remote sensing monitoring, which is 
capable of automatically integrating multi-source sensing data and achieving unified data processing and storage through a 
cloud platform. It not only improves the efficiency of data processing but also avoids the problem of information islands existing 
in the existing system, thus providing a new paradigm of data fusion and information integration for the operation and 
maintenance management of residential projects. Through automated and intelligent monitoring, this framework significantly 
reduces the investment of manpower and material resources and drastically lowers the maintenance cost of the project.
(2) A multi-sensor-integrated digital twin system for monitoring the quality of residential projects is developed and designed 
relying on the SSIoT-RBIM system architecture. In practice, the SSIoT-RBIM framework involves the integration of a large 
number of data from different sources and formats, especially when dealing with heterogeneous data from multiple sources; 
there are problems with standardization and data format conversion. The BIM model and the point cloud data come from 
different devices and platforms, while the point cloud data may come from a laser scanner, a drone, or other sensors with

(3) The current system is limited to the integration and visual presentation of data from multiple sources, and future research needs 
to be further extended in terms of prediction and assessment by integrating advanced data analytics and machine learning 
models, making it possible to predict the maintenance needs and potential problems of a building based on historical and real- 
time data.
