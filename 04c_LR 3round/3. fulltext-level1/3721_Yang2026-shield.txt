ABSTRACT
Optimizing shield tunneling parameters is critical for ensuring both safety and efficiency in tunnel construction. 
However, existing optimization approaches often underutilize operational data and are prone to entrapment in 
local optima. This study proposes a digital twin (DT) -based framework for multi-objective optimization (MOO) 
of shield tunneling parameters. The framework integrates data acquisition, preprocessing, modeling, and opti -
mization within a layered architecture. Key parameters are identified using the shapley additive explanations 
(SHAP) method, while a hybrid optimization algorithm — artificial bee colony (ABC) – non-dominated sorting 
genetic algorithm III (NSGA-III) — combines the strengths of both algorithms and is applied across three opera -
tional scenarios. Optimized parameters are iteratively fed back into the DT to guide parameter adjustment. The 
framework is validated using data from the Shanghai Airport Link Line project. Full-parameter optimization 
yields the best performance, achieving an overall optimization rate of 32.02%, with particularly notable im -
provements in controlling the vertical deviation of shield head. Comparative analyses show that the proposed 
framework surpasses benchmark methods in convergence speed and solution quality, reducing shield attitude 
deviation by 2.21% – 17.13%. These results underscore the framework ’ s potential as an effective decision-support 
tool for shield tunneling operations.
1. Introduction
With the rapid expansion of urban underground infrastructure, the 
demand for tunnel construction has increased substantially ( Guo et al., 
2025 ). Among the available tunneling methods, shield tunneling has 
been widely adopted due to its advantages in construction efficiency, 
operational safety, and minimal disturbance to surrounding environ -
ments ( Lei et al., 2024 ). Nevertheless, shield tunneling control remains 
inherently complex, and inappropriate tunneling parameter settings can 
induce deviations in shield attitude, leading to trajectory misalignment, 
segment dislocation, or even structural damage ( Li et al., 2024 ). 
Therefore, optimizing shield tunneling parameters is essential to main -
tain alignment with the designed tunnel axis and ensure construction 
safety ( Shen et al., 2022 ).
Optimization of shield tunneling parameters involves multiple 
interdependent variables — such as thrust force, cutterhead torque, and 
advance speed — that exhibit nonlinear relationships ( Fu et al., 2026 ). As 
such, the problem is typically formulated as a multi-objective optimi -
zation (MOO) task ( Chen et al., 2024 ). Existing approaches to addressing 
this problem include statistical analysis, numerical simulation, and 
intelligent algorithms ( Chang et al., 2024 ). Statistical analysis methods 
can reveal empirical relationships among variables ( Sugimoto and Sra -
moon, 2002 ), but often fail to capture nonlinear interactions under 
heterogeneous geological conditions ( Huang et al., 2022 ). Fu et al. 
(2025) introduced automated stratum identification techniques to 
address challenges arising from geological uncertainties. Numerical 
simulations, such as those based on finite element methods, provide 
valuable insights into mechanical behavior and deformation control 
( Yin et al., 2018 ), yet their applicability is limited by computational cost 
( Huang et al., 2024 ). More recently, intelligent optimization algorithms

Several MOO algorithms such as non-dominated sorting genetic al -
gorithm II (NSGA-II) ( Deb et al., 2002 ), non-dominated sorting genetic 
algorithm III (NSGA-III) ( Deb and Jain, 2014 ), multi-objective evolu -
tionary algorithm based on decomposition (MOEA/D) ( Fan et al., 2022; 
Wang et al., 2021 ), and multi-objective marine predator algorithm 
(MOMPA) ( Zhong et al., 2021 ), have been developed and applied across 
engineering domains. These algorithms are well-suited for problems 
characterized by nonlinear relationships, high dimensionality, and 
conflicting objectives. However, when dealing with six or more objec -
tives, MOEA/D struggles to maintain a uniform distribution of weight 
vectors, whereas MOMPA tends to converge prematurely to local optima 
due to limited mechanisms for preserving population diversity ( Lai 
et al., 2025 ). By contrast, NSGA-III employs a reference-point-based 
selection strategy that effectively preserves solution diversity in high- 
dimensional objective spaces, rendering it suitable for complex MOO 
problems. In the context of shield tunneling, NSGA-III has been applied 
to optimize critical tunneling parameters. For instance, Liu et al. (2021)
utilized it to determine optimal cutterhead rotation speeds. Neverthe -
less, NSGA-III exhibits slow convergence, which may hinder its ability to 
approximate the true Pareto front ( Cui et al., 2019 ). Thus, a research gap 
remains in achieving both convergence performance and solution 
quality in high-dimensional optimization for tunneling parameters.
To address these limitations, meta-heuristic algorithms such as par -
ticle swarm optimization ( Tharwat and Schenck, 2021 ), ant colony 
optimization ( Priyadarshi and Kumar, 2025 ), and artificial bee colony 
(ABC) optimization ( Ibrahim et al., 2025 ) have been used to navigate 
complex search spaces and avoid entrapment in local optima ( Hussain 
et al., 2020 ). Among them, the ABC algorithm is particularly notable for 
its strong global search capability, inspired by the collective foraging 
behavior of honeybees ( Lin et al., 2025 ). Accordingly, integrating the 
ABC algorithm with NSGA-III offers the potential to improve the di -
versity and quality of initial candidate solutions, thereby enhancing the 
overall optimization performance. However, current research on shield 
tunneling parameter optimization remains predominantly algorithm- 
centric, with limited attention given to integrating optimization 
models with the underlying physical processes of tunneling ( Li et al., 
2019 ).
In response, the emergence of digital twin (DT) technology has 
attracted growing attention as a promising pathway toward bridging 
this gap ( Kritzinger et al., 2018 ). First conceptualized by Grieves (2005) , 
DT technology establishes high-fidelity virtual representations of phys -
ical assets, enabling interactions among simulation, optimization, and 
feedback. This capability creates new opportunities for intelligent con -
trol in tunnel construction ( Barata and Kayser, 2024 ). For example, Ye 
et al. (2023) developed a DT-based safety management system for 
simulating tunneling processes and monitoring risks; and Feng et al. 
(2024) applied DT to simulate ground deformation. Despite these ad -
vances, most DT applications focused on simulation and monitoring, 
with limited functionality for high-level optimization decisions ( Zheng 
et al., 2024 ).
This study proposes a dynamic optimization framework for shield 
tunnelling parameters by integrating DT with an improved NSGA-III 
algorithm. Leveraging the continuous synchronization capabilities of 
DT, data streams are used to update constraint conditions and objective 
functions, which overcome the shortcomings of conventional static 
decision-making approaches. The main contributions of this study are as 
follows: (1) A dynamic optimization framework that integrates DT 
technology with an enhanced evolutionary algorithm is developed, 
expanding the application scope of DT in tunnel construction and 
advancing its functional evolution from monitoring and simulation to 
intelligent decision-making; (2) A human–machine collaborative closed- 
loop architecture is introduced, which enhances the framework’s 
adaptability under complex tunneling conditions by integrating algo -
rithmic optimization with expert operational knowledge.
In the remainder of this paper, Section 2 presents the DT framework 
and the MOO approach for shield tunneling parameters. Section 3
evaluates the performance of the proposed framework through a real- 
world case study. Section 4 discusses the case study results. Section 5
concludes this study.
2. Framework for tunneling parameter optimization
2.1. Overview of the framework
As illustrated in Fig. 1 , the proposed DT-based tunneling parameter 
optimization framework comprises four interrelated layers: the physical 
layer, responsible for monitoring of the tunneling process; the virtual 
layer, dedicated to constructing a high-fidelity DT model; the data 
interaction layer, which ensures seamless synchronization between the 
physical and virtual layers; and the application service layer, which 
executes dynamic optimizations and generates operational feedback. 
Grounded in continuously synchronized data, the integration of these 
layers forms a closed-loop architecture enabling parameter perception, 
intelligent optimization, and dynamic feedback. This integrated design 
enables a paradigm shift from passive monitoring to data-driven, 
human–machine collaborative shield tunneling operations.
Effective optimization of shield tunneling parameters necessitates a 
robust mapping mechanism that connects physical measurements with 
virtual modeling. The physical layer comprises the shield machine and 
an array of data acquisition devices, which together provide the primary 
data source for parameter optimization. After data preprocessing and 
analysis, tunneling parameters and geological information are extracted 
as input variables for the MOO algorithm. The multi-dimensional nature 
of these variables directly affects the interdependencies among the 
objective functions that must be balanced during optimization. Table 1
summarizes the types of sensors used to quantify tunneling performance.
The virtual layer establishes a digital environment for parameter 
optimization through the coordinated integration of three models. The 
geometric model provides spatial constraints for shield machine. The 
behavior model uses the shapley additive explanations(SHAP) method 
( Qiao et al., 2024 ) to construct a dynamic response mechanism between 
tunneling parameters and optimization objectives. The rule model de -
fines permissible parameter ranges and operational constraints, thereby 
establishing a feasible decision space for the MOO problem. Interaction 
among these models allows the optimization algorithm to periodically 
evaluate the impact of different parameter combinations on conflicting 
objectives within the virtual environment.
The application service layer integrates monitoring data from the 
physical layer with virtual modeling to execute tunneling parameter 
optimization. By using an advanced MOO algorithm, this layer identifies 
optimal parameter combinations and established recommended control 
ranges aimed at minimizing shield attitude deviations. Once the opti -
mization results are obtained, the geometric model within the virtual 
layer maps the optimized parameters to corresponding components or 
locations of the shield machine. These validated adjustments are sub -
sequently transmitted back to the physical layer, ensuring adaptive 
control of shield advancement.
The data interaction layer serves as the information hub of the 
framework, enabling seamless communication and integration across all 
layers. It manages data flows among the physical layer, virtual models, 
application service layer, and embedded knowledge bases, including 
industry standards, optimization algorithms, and historical parameter 
libraries. Through multi-source data integration and transmission, this 
layer establishes a comprehensive data integration framework to pro -
vide data support and feedback for parameter optimization, and en -
hances the level of intelligence in shield tunneling operations.

2.2.3. Data imputation
Removing outliers can introduce missing values that must be 
handled to preserve dataset integrity. Simply discarding incomplete 
records may compromise representativeness. To address this issue, 
random forest (RF) imputation is applied ( Luo et al., 2025 ). As a non- 
parametric, supervised learning-based approach, RF imputation lever -
ages inter-variable correlations to estimate missing values, making it 
effective for high-dimensional engineering datasets characterized by 
nonlinear dependencies.
2.2.4. Wavelet-based denoising
Operational data collected from shield machines are often affected 
by environmental noise and sensor inaccuracies. To enhance signal fi -
delity, denoising is performed after missing-value imputation. In this 
study, the discrete wavelet transform (DWT) is adopted, with the Dau -
bechies wavelet family selected as the basis function due to its strong 
time – frequency localization characteristics and suitability for process -
ing non-stationary signals ( Barjasteh et al., 2024 ).
2.3. Optimization of tunneling parameters
To address the high-dimensionality and non-linearity inherent in 
tunneling parameter optimization, the ABC-NSGA-III algorithm was 
developed within the application service layer of the proposed frame -
work. This hybrid algorithm incorporates the global search capability of 
the ABC algorithm into the initialization and evolutionary processes of 
NSGA-III, thereby mitigating the tendency of standard NSGA-III to 
converge prematurely to local optima. By maintaining population di -
versity, ABC-NSGA-III enables a more efficient and stable search of the 
Pareto front. Once the Pareto-optimal solutions are obtained, the ideal 
point method ( Xu et al., 2018 ) is applied to determine the most suitable 
combination of tunneling parameters. Finally, the optimized parameters 
are transmitted to the physical layer, establishing a closed-loop archi -
tecture of perception, optimization, and feedback.

2.3.2. Hyparameter setting
Before conducting MOO, it is necessary to reasonably configure the 
algorithm to ensure both convergence efficiency and global search 
capability. The parameters considered include the number of objective 
functions in NSGA-III, population size, crossover probability, mutation 
probability, maximum number of iterations, the number of ABC itera -
tions, and the limit on honey collection attempts for abandoning a nectar 
source. Common approaches to hyperparameter optimization include 
random search, grid search, and Bayesian optimization ( Zhao et al., 
2024 ). Among these, grid search can systematically explore the 
parameter space through exhaustive traversal. Therefore, the grid 
search method is employed to configure the hyperparameters and 
determine the optimal parameter ranges.
For the optimization of the six objective functions considered in this 
study, parameter settings were determined following the recommen -
dations of Deb and Jain. (2014) . The population size was varied within 
the range of 200 – 500, the number of iterations between 100 and 500, 
the crossover probability from 0.3 to 0.7 in increments of 0.1, and the 
mutation probability from 0.001 to 0.02 in increments of 0.001. For 
each parameter combination, multiple independent runs were per -
formed, and the configuration that achieved the best average perfor -
mance across these trials was selected as the optimal parameter setting.

3. Case study
3.1. Project background
The DT-based tunneling parameter optimization framework was 
evaluated using a real-world case study from the western section of the 
Shanghai Airport Link Line project. A large slurry pressure balance 
shield machine was employed for tunnel construction, with lining seg -
ments of 13.6 m outer diameter and 12.5 m inner diameter. As shown in 
Fig. 3 , a continuous stretch of 100 rings (Rings 2275 – 2375) — located 
between Air Shaft No. 2 and Air Shaft No. 3 — was selected for detailed 
analysis. This subsection features a tight horizontal curve radius of 520 
m and lies in close proximity to existing metro lines. Additionally, the 
geological profile in this area increases the complexity of tunnel con -
struction, which traverses interbedded strata composed of highly 
permeable layers of clay, silty clay, clayey silt, and silty sand. The het -
erogeneous distribution of these strata creates a challenging excavation 
environment, making the selected subsection an ideal testbed for eval -
uating the performance of the proposed framework.
A set of sensory devices, as detailed in Table 1 , continuously recor -
ded key operational parameters at 10-second intervals along the tunnel 
alignment, enabling high-resolution monitoring of tunneling operations. 
To mitigate multicollinearity arising from nonlinear coupling between 
cutterhead penetration rate and parameters such as total thrust and 
cutterhead torque ( Lin et al., 2025 ), the penetration rate was excluded 
during model construction to enhance generalization capability. The 
ranges of the final input and output parameters are presented in Table 2 .

3.3. Data transmission and visualization
The dataset from data preprocessing enables DT with perception and 
optimization capabilities. Within the data interaction layer of the 
framework, data dynamically collected from the physical layer are 
managed using a time-series database. Simultaneously, the virtual layer 
employs distributed storage systems to host geometric, behavior, and 
rule models — each characterized by large data volumes, complex 
structures, and high-frequency access requirements.
Geometric models are developed to represent the shape, dimensions, 
and spatial configuration of physical elements. As shown in Fig. 7(a) , a 
high-fidelity geometric model of the slurry pressure balance shield 
machine was constructed using SolidWorks. In current practice, DT are 
predominantly developed by integrating Building Information Modeling 
(BIM) with sensor data, serving to provide updated representations of 
physical elements. The proposed framework retains these monitoring 
capabilities while incorporating an advanced optimization algorithm, 
thereby establishing a closed-loop architecture that integrates percep -
tion, optimization, and feedback. The rule model encodes constraints 
and control logic to ensure that the shield machine can adjust its posture 
under different geological conditions, while the behavior model — driven 
by the ABC-NSGA-III algorithm — enables adaptive control by dynami -
cally tuning key operational parameters. Consequently, the proposed 
framework enables a transition from passive monitoring to proactive 
decision support.

3.4.1. Feature importance analysis based on SHAP
The SHAP method was employed to assess the contribution of 20 
tunneling parameters to shield attitude outcomes. This method quan -
tifies the marginal impact of each parameter on shield attitude, as 
illustrated in Fig. 8 . The parameters were ranked based on their average 
SHAP values.
The pressures from the six sets of thrust cylinder pressure exert the 
most significant influence on shield attitude. As primary actuators, these 
pressures directly determine the thrust force and operational stability of 
the shield machine. Their precise control is crucial for maintaining and 
adjusting shield machine's attitude. Parameters such as cutterhead 
speed, total thrust, thrust speed, cutterhead torque, air cushion pressure, 
inlet slurry density, outlet slurry density, inlet slurry flow rate, outlet 
slurry flow rate, and grouting pressure also play a vital role. Cutterhead 
speed and cutterhead torque are directly associated with cutting per -
formance and equipment loading, while total thrust and thrust speed 
influence the advance trajectory. Parameters like air cushion pressure, 
inlet slurry density, outlet slurry density, inlet slurry flow rate, outlet 
slurry flow rate, and grouting pressure affect shield attitude indirectly 
through their impact on soil disturbance, cutting resistance, and slurry 
rheology.
The importance of different parameter categories follows the order: 
structural > geometric > geological. Owing to the limited variation of 
geometric and geological parameters in the dataset, factors such as 
slurry pressure, burial depth, soil cohesion, and internal friction angle 
exhibit relatively minor influence, indicating their limited effect on 
attitude control. Considering that geometric and geological parameters 
are typically predetermined during the design phase and remain fixed 
during operation, the study ultimately identified 16 key adjustable

After selecting the 16 most relevant parameters, further analysis was

3.4.3. Comparative analysis of optimization results
To evaluate the performance of ABC-NSGA-III across three distinct 
operational scenarios, an optimization rate index was used to quantify 
improvements in shield attitude-related objectives before and after 
optimization. Each evaluation is based on the average results of 10 in -
dependent runs. As shown in Table 5 , ABC-NSGA-III delivers strong 
improvements across most deviation parameters, particularly in con -
trolling VDSH, where the optimization rate reaches over 28.01%. 
Moreover, the optimal attitudes obtained under each scenario illustrate 
the correlation between the dimensionality of parameter optimization 
and overall performance. As the number of optimized parameters in -
creases, the overall optimization rate rises substantially, reaching 
32.02% in Scenario 3. This indicates that a higher-dimensional param -
eter space allows the algorithm to better exploit solution possibilities 
and improve shield attitude control. Comparison of optimal solutions in 
the Pareto sets shows that Scenario 3 outperforms Scenarios 1 and 2 
across all objectives. This confirms that regulating all 16 tunneling pa -
rameters simultaneously achieves the best attitude control. It also vali -
dates the reliability of the parameter importance ranking from SHAP 
analysis and highlights the feasibility of ABC-NSGA-III for shield attitude 
control.
Based on the results of Scenario 3, a high-performance set of Pareto- 
optimal solutions was obtained (see Table 6 ). These solutions represent 
various optimal combinations of shield tunneling parameters that bal -
ance the multiple attitude-related objectives. Through analysis of solu -
tion distributions, recommended control ranges for each parameter were 
established. The optimal parameter combinations derived from the 
MOO was then fed back through the data interaction layer to initialize 
the control rules. The DT-based framework facilitated closed-loop 
feedback, dynamically adjusting parameters based on the evolving 
relationship between machine behavior and control settings. This en -
ables continuous refinement of key parameters throughout the entire 
tunneling process.
4. Discussions
4.1. Comparative performance analysis
The performance of ABC-NSGA-III was further benchmarked against 
the standard NSGA-III and PSO-NSGA-III. The evaluation was conducted 
using six identical optimization models, which tasked with minimizing 
six attitude deviations. The results, summarized in Table 7 , demonstrate 
the superiority of ABC-NSGA-III. Compared with the standard NSGA-III, 
ABC-NSGA-III exhibited enhanced global exploration and local exploi -
tation capabilities. The average optimization magnitudes of HDSH and 
VDSH improved by 6.12% and 17.13%, respectively, and the best-found 
optimal values for HDSH and VDSH were enhanced by 4.74% and 
14.98%. Compared with PSO-NSGA-III, ABC-NSGA-III yielded a 2.5% 
and 8.14% increase in the average performance for HDSH and VDSH, 
and a 2.21% and 7.34% improvement in their respective optimal values.
To compare the convergence performance of the three algorithms 
under the 100-ring operational dataset, a sliding-window approach was 
employed. Convergence was defined as the condition in which fluctua -
tions of the HV value over the most recent 20 generations fell below 1%, 
and the slope of the fitted linear regression approximated zero. The re -
sults are reported in Table 8 . From the perspective of near real-time 
performance, ABC-NSGA-III demonstrates a distinct advantage in 
convergence speed. The algorithm converges in approximately 900.5 s, 
yielding a stable Pareto-optimal solution set, with the convergence 
phase accounting for only 64% of the total computational time. By 
contrast, the standard NSGA-III requires 993.6 s, with convergence 
occupying 92% of its iteration time. Moreover, ABC-NSGA-III achieves 
an HV value of 0.532, representing a 35% improvement over that of the 
standard NSGA-III (0.394), thereby confirming its capability to produce 
high-quality solutions within constrained timeframes. The intermediate 
performance of PSO-NSGA-III, with a convergence time of 945.0 s and 
an HV value of 0.482, highlights the effectiveness of the ABC search

4.2. Prospection of DT in tunnel construction
Using the Shanghai Airport Link Line project as a case study, this 
study illustrates the efficacy of the proposed framework for optimizing 
shield tunneling parameters. Unlike conventional approaches that rely 
on operator experience ( Liu et al., 2021 ) and current DT applications 
that remain largely limited to monitoring ( Zheng et al., 2024 ), the 
proposed framework represents a step forward by integrating DT tech -
nology with advanced optimization algorithms.
The framework algorithmically generates optimal parameter 
combinations in an interpretable form, providing a quantitative basis to 
support operator decisions. Within this human – machine collaborative 
mechanism, the framework performs MOO, while operators — drawing 
on their judgment of complex conditions and expert knowledge -
— evaluate the optimization results. This mechanism treats operator 
expertise as a safeguard, filtering out abnormal outputs that may arise 
from field uncertainties. In doing so, the framework fuses the compu -
tational efficiency of data-driven models with the adaptive intelligence 
of human expertise, which ensures its applicability under real-world 
tunneling conditions.
The hierarchical structure of the proposed framework provides a for 
scalability and broader applicability. Future work may extend this 
framework to more diverse geological conditions. In hard-rock strata, 
for instance, the optimization objectives could be reformulated to bal -
ance cutter wear and advance rate through integration with appropriate 
geomechanical models; When passing through aquifer-bearing layers, 
the framework could instead prioritize tunnel-face stability and surface- 
settlement control. These extensions underscore the framework ’ s po -
tential to incorporate context-specific objectives to meet the needs of 
different tunneling projects.
5. Conclusion
This paper proposes a framework for optimizing shield tunneling 
parameters by integrating DT technology with an improved NSGA-III. 
The framework facilitates the transmission of data from the physical 
layer to the data interaction layer, thereby supporting digital modeling 
and optimization decision-making. Within the application service layer, 
key tunneling parameters are identified using SHAP method. These 
parameters are then grouped into multiple optimization scenarios based 
on their influence on tunneling performance. A MOO process is carried 
out using ABC-NSGA-III to improve global search diversity and solution 
quality. The optimized parameter sets are subsequently transmitted 
back through the data interaction layer to guide physical shield 
tunneling operations.
The feasibility and effectiveness of the proposed framework were 
validated using real-world project data. Six shield attitude indicators 
were optimized across three defined parameter scenarios. Through 
SHAP analysis, 16 critical tunneling parameters were identified. In the 
full-parameter optimization scenario, the framework achieved an 
overall optimization rate of 32.02%. Compared with the standard NSGA- 
III and PSO-NSGA-III algorithms, ABC-NSGA-III demonstrated faster
