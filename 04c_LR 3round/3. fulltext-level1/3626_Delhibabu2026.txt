This article examines a digital twin (DT) for complex infrastructure assets using the subway system as a case 
study. The proposed solution includes twins for each station, which are interconnected to interact during various 
operational scenarios. Existing digital twins for subway systems are examined, and their shortcomings—primarily  
the latency in operator information retrieval due to slow model rebuilding—are  identiﬁed. The study addresses 
decision support during medium- and large-scale incidents requiring the synchronization of multiple depart-
ments. The authors propose a digital twin architecture based on data collected from cameras, microphones, 
and sensors, processed within a fog computing environment. A separate digital twin is developed for each sta-
tion, capable of utilizing templates from previously built assets. An accompanying mobile application with an 
intelligent assistant displays detected events and answers queries via voice interaction. Implementation results 
indicate that personnel response time to emerging events improved by 17.4% compared to the REPEAT platform. 
The proposed architecture is applicable to subway systems and other public transportation networks requiring 
coordinated personnel actions during hazardous situations.
1.  Introduction
A digital twin (DT) is a digital model of a real-world object. Modern 
DTs are not only maps, they also support usage of situational modeling, 
simulation, and other tools that allow for the display and evaluation 
of the states of simulated objects in the present, past, and future. Fire 
alarm systems are especially in demand during hazardous situations, 
where decisions must be made appropriately within the available time 
and resources.
Particularly in hazardous situations (ﬁre or explosion threat), a ﬁre 
alarm system can help coordinate personnel and activate a remote ﬁre 
suppression system to eliminate the emergency without direct human in-
tervention, especially in cases where the danger to humans is extremely 
high (e.g., risk of death for a ﬁreﬁghter or chemical engineer).
A ﬁre causes signiﬁcant economic and human losses, but ﬂooding 
from a ﬁre can also cause signiﬁcant damage to a building, and in some 
cases, lead to its partial destruction or the destruction of specialized 
equipment, machinery, etc. In each case, activating an automatic ﬁre 
suppression system and ﬂooding the premises with carbon dioxide will 
lead to high expense, and in some cases, the use of automatic ﬁre sup-

Precise and well-considered personnel actions and the use of intel-

limitations. Currently, digital twins are used in many subject areas, in 
particular, in the petrochemical industry, subways, railways and tun-
nels, where even a minor error can cost lives and lead to signiﬁcant 
ﬁnancial losses.
However, existing digital twins have a number of signiﬁcant limita-
tions, in particular:
1. there is no ability to process video images, sounds and data from 
sensors installed at facilities and there is no ability to promptly detect 
events at facilities, including dangerous ones, which does not allow 
for their prompt elimination;

2. there is no mobile platform (smartphone application) with support

response to events;
3. there is no support for cloud distributed computing and fog comput-

4. a web interface for work through a browser and from mobile devices

5. integration with intelligent video surveillance systems (IVSS) is not

7. the ability to view a map of facilities and connect to video cameras

In the subway sector, these limitations hinder prompt decision-
making and reduce the response time of personnel and support services 
at subway facilities. The required subway twin must possess new capa-
bilities and be built on new principles. The new digital display for the 
subway must display the actual simulated facility. It must also allow 
events to be displayed on a map (2D) and in three-dimensional space 
(3D). The displayed model of the facility must have multiple nesting 
levels, be navigable using a mouse, keys, or other pointers, and display 
various fragments of the observed facility, including small fragments, in 
detail. It must also be possible to zoom in and out of the image in vector 
format.
The digital twin should be capable of playing video clips and audio 
ﬁles, giving operators a clearer understanding of situations occurring at 
the facility. Typical examples of digital twin use include the following: 
The system detects a “scream”, the operator selects the “play” function 
and listens to the clip. The system identiﬁes the event at the facility as 
a “ﬁre”, and the operator watches the video clip and reclassiﬁes it as 
“smoke”.
To conveniently work with the digital twin, operators need an intelli-
gent assistant with functions for displaying objects and situations arising 
within them, as well as speech recognition, which can answer operator 
questions about events occurring in the subway. Furthermore, the as-
sistant’s speech recognition capabilities will allow navigation through 
the digital twin objects displayed on the screen using voice, which is 
convenient for all operators in dark and poorly lit subway areas.
The main challenges in creating the considered digital twins are de-
termined by the complexity of the structure and behavior of subway ob-
jects, the highly dynamic changes occurring within the subway, and the 
large volume of data collected by cameras, various sensors, and micro-
phones installed in stations and other subway facilities. Furthermore, 
the development of intelligent assistants for operators of complex in-
frastructure facilities, where hazardous situations can occur that require 
rapid personnel coordination, is poorly worked out. Another signiﬁcant 
problem is the high cost of creating twins and upgrading them.
The authors propose creating a digital twin with a two-level struc-
ture: the upper level will house the digital twin of the subway, while 
the lower level will house the digital twins of individual stations. The 
proposed digital twin is scalable, with intermediate levels being cre-
ated where stations are grouped together. For example, groupings could 
be based on districts, lines, and station types: indoor/outdoor, single-
platform, transfer, multiple-exit, with or without escalators. It is possible 
to use existing digital twins of individual stations if they were previously 
built.
For new stations, new digital twins are proposed to be built using a 
specially developed template, which is a standard digital twin. When a 
new digital twin is built using this template, the standard digital twin 
is modiﬁed taking into account the station’s speciﬁc features, as well 
as previously developed digital twins for other stations. In particular, 
when constructing a subway station’s digital twin, the template varies 
depending on the architectural plan of the speciﬁc station: non-standard 
exits, passages, multi-level escalators, etc.
This study addresses the identiﬁed gaps by making the following 
primary contributions:
• The proposition of an interconnected digital twin architecture link-
ing neighboring stations for preemptive response.
• Integration of a fog-computing environment to process multi-modal 
data, signiﬁcantly reducing latency.
• Development of a mobile intelligent assistant for real-time 2𝐷∕3𝐷
visualization and voice-navigated management.

2.  Popular DTs
While commercial platforms dominate industrial applications, recent 
academic literature has heavily focused on the theoretical frameworks of 
digital twins in transportation and infrastructure. Broader applications 
of DTs have been proposed for building construction frameworks [5], 
condition assessment of railway infrastructures [6], arch cover method 
monitoring [7], rail operation safety assessment [8], and maintaining 
resilient ports [9]. Speciﬁc to subway environments, Kaewunruen et al. 
[10] explored sustainability audits for subway stations, and Shevlyugin 
et al. [11] modeled traction power systems. More recently, Zhao et al. 
[12] and Faraji et al. [13] highlighted the critical role of DTs in sub-
way ﬂood risk management and intelligent tunnel monitoring. Despite 
these advancements, a gap remains in integrating real-time, fog-assisted 
multi-modal sensor fusion [14] into a uniﬁed, mobile-accessible frame-
work for station operators. The vast majority of existing commercial 
tools for developing digital twins.
A popular tool for creating interactive diagrams and digital twins is 
the REPEAT platform. REPEAT’s advantages are the ability to develop 
highly complex models of technological objects (nuclear reactors, air-
craft, electric vehicles); the ability to create and conﬁgure a digital twin 
control system; the ability to conduct multiple virtual tests; and the abil-
ity to generate control algorithm code for the target device. The platform 
is intended for use in design institutes, engineering bureaus, and educa-
tional institutions. The REPEAT platform supports cloud infrastructure 
with ﬂexible access via a web browser from any device; data integra-
tion; model integration; and a built-in parallel computing library. The 
REPEAT platform is used by Rosatom and many other technology giants 
for building digital twins.
One widely used system for displaying monitored objects on maps 
is Milestone XProtect. The system is used for video surveillance and al-
lows viewing event logs and pattern recognition. It also features a map 
designer for designing maps of monitored objects. Maps are managed 
in the Management Client 2020 R1 subprogram from the XProtect pro-
gram’s main menu. Smart maps in XProtect Smart Client do not reﬂect 
the actual behavior of the monitored object and do not include simula-
tion tools.
Other popular tools for developing digital twins in the world are: 
Ansys Twin Builder, COMSOL Multiphysics, CML-Bench, Unreal Engine, 
SOLIDWORKS Simulation, FlowVision, Rocky DEM, AMBER, SimMan-
ager, Altair: One Total Twin platform, Microsoft: Azure Digital Twins, 
AnyLogic, iTwin, PlantSight, and Merius Oy Smart Mill.
Ansys is a multimedia platform that provides sets of elements and 
connections for connecting to sensors. The initially large element li-
brary can be expanded, and models can be built and displayed in de-
tailed layer-by-layer manner. Ansys supports the creation of prototypes

diagrams, COMSOL, allows to build process diagrams and analyze indi-

CML-Bench supports mathematical modeling and digital design ca-

comparison of modeling results with models created using 2D modeling

Unreal Engine works well with planning functions and supports im-
porting models from all popular CAD packages. SOLIDWORKS Simula-
tion breaks the model into ﬁnite nested models. Typical models of the 
program include: biological cell growth and heat transfer. It is possi-
ble to quantitatively evaluate physical phenomena occurring in liquids 
and solids, as well as radio wave propagation, using the ﬁnite element 
method and other numerical methods.
FlowVision is used to study ﬂow processes in ﬂuid dynamics. Meth-
ods of the physical impact of ﬂuid ﬂow and heat transfer on the modeled 
object can be used. The model generated by the program can be veri-
ﬁed taking into account chemical processes occurring in a real object 
(ﬂasks, simulation rig) using the simulation model. Physical prototyp-
ing of a soil environment with soil (aggregate) destruction is possible on 
a simulation rig (physical object) and comparison with a digital model 
based on numerical methods.
Unlike FlowVision, Rocky DEM builds a model based on discrete el-
ements and allows for the simulation of soil environment destruction. 
A model template for the early stage of solid deformation is provided, 
which is necessary when modeling rocks and ice (ﬂuid mechanics). The 
topology used includes material particles and rock blocks. The motion 
of individual model elements and their links with the deformated ob-
ject can be determined. Together, the deformated object and the model 
elements form a comprehensive soil environment model with a ﬁxed 
starting point and conditions for the motion of the model elements.
AMBER is designed for modeling chemical processes at the molecu-
lar level. The SimManager program simulates biological processes and 
object interactions: models of living organs (kidneys, liver, spleen, etc.) 
with the environment as a set of discrete particles, with the ability to 
specify a smoothing length (return to the initial state before impacting 
the organ). Operation in liquid environments is possible, with the use 
of kernel smoothing and kernel approximation functions.
The Altair software platform allows the development and launch of 
digital twins in any environment: in a browser, on a smartphone, or on a 
computer. All data is stored in the cloud. The platform provides machine 
learning tools and neural networks used to build models of real-world 
objects. The Azure Digital Twins cloud platform also provides the abil-
ity to host applications in various data centers. The Azure environment 
includes models of complex environments, including subways, railways, 
and entire cities. The platform is positioned as a platform for building a 
business in a way that reduces costs and optimizes operating expenses. 
The platform enables companies to analyze models, improve customer 
service, and enhance their products.
AnyLogic is a simple, fast, and popular simulation environment suit-
able for all industries. Models built in AnyLogic are highly ﬂexible, ap-
plicable to a variety of tasks, and can be reconﬁgured based on input 
data. iTwin allows for modeling and visualization of infrastructure assets 
with the ability to convert them to a BIM model. The program supports a 
hybrid 2D/3D design environment with timeline visualization and data 
completeness veriﬁcation. iTwin allows for the creation of interactive 
monitoring models. The program’s primary purpose is to implement en-
gineering projects.
PlantSight allows for the construction of economic assets in 2D/3D 
space and the description of various interactions between objects to 
develop problem-solving strategies, regardless of the physical loca-
tion of the assets. The program supports Industrial Internet of Things 
(IIoT) technologies with augmented reality elements. The ﬁrst version 
of PlantSight’s digital twin provided the ability to build enterprise mod-
els, and later, models of smaller physical assets were developed.
Merius Oy Smart Mill uses the ContextCapture engine, which creates 
3D models. A distinctive feature of the platform is its ability to utilize 
drone images, laser scanning data, and visualize information in real time 
in a 3D environment.
The vast majority of existing tools for developing digital twins in-
clude graphic elements that are both part of the digital twin develop-
ment environment and elements of the digital twin itself (people, build-
ings, workshops, entrances, parking lots, etc.). It should be noted that 
many digital twins operate only within the digital twin development en-
vironment and do not have the ability to be launched separately (*.ﬂv, 
*.exe, *.class, *.app, etc.).
Currently, in the situation center of the State Unitary Enterprise “Pe-
tersburg Subway” Milestone XProtect video surveillance system is used 
as a monitoring and decision support system. Events that may occur in 
the subway and should be monitored by staﬀ are structured into diﬀer-
ent categories. An example of such events is the discovery of abandoned 
items by passengers. Video clips showing these events are copied to a 
separate secure server. Video streams from all cameras are stored in the 
city’s shared storage for 30 days. Servers optimized for working with 
large volumes of data are located at the City Monitoring Center and are 
accessible through the “Safe City” program, where Milestone XProtect, 
VideoNet, and other programs are used.

3.  Overview of intelligent assistants
Currently, the most widely used intelligent assistants include the fol-
lowing: Alisa, Google Assistant, ClickUp AI, Copy.ai, Amazon Alexa, Siri, 
Notion AI, Zapier AI, Jasper Generative AI Chatbot, SaneBox, MindPal, 
and other programs that are virtual assistants with artiﬁcial intelligence. 
Some intelligent assistants have highly accurate voice recognition mod-
els in national languages (English, Russian, etc.) and can be integrated 
into smart devices.
One such assistant is the Alisa assistant from Yandex. This intelligent 
assistant not only includes a smart speaker but also provides function-
ality that requires interaction with other devices such as purchasing ad-
ditional services via a smartphone or PC, sending emails. Alice supports 
integration with Smart Home sensors, can turn lights on and oﬀ, open 
and close windows, operate an intercom, recognize faces, act as a search 
engine, and provide other services.
The voice assistant from Google (Google Assistant) can maintain a 
conversation, search for information online and return search results, 
provide weather information, create vehicle routes, perform simple 
mathematical operations, and convert various values, such as exchange 
rates and measurements. The Assistant app can translate phrases into 
another languages (from English to Spanish, from Chinese to Japanese, 
etc.), search for recipes for homemakers, provide sports scores, ex-
plain word meanings, and display exchange rates. The app can work 
in standby mode and runs on all popular operating systems: iOS/iPa-
dOS/macOS, Android, Linux, and Windows.
ClickUp AI is not only a voice assistant but also a project man-
agement service based on the Kanban methodology, allowing to ad-
just workﬂows, visualize all project stages, and prioritize their comple-
tion. Compared to Google Assistant, which is more focused on home 
use, ClickUp’s voice assistant is oriented on solving business problems. 
ClickUp’s modules allow to manage tasks and assign roles, while its ar-
tiﬁcial intelligence (AI) module generates technical speciﬁcations, re-
views checklists, and creates subtasks.
Amazon’s Alexa voice assistant is designed primarily for home use 
and works best with Amazon Echo and Amazon Echo Dot speakers. 
Alexa supports virtual assistant functions, including music playback 
and audiobooks reading. Alexa can create to-do lists, set alarms, leave 
voice messages, and provide news updates, stock quotes, sports scores, 
weather, and traﬃc  information. Like Yandex’s Alisa, Alexa can control 
smart home devices (opening doors, windows, turning on lights, etc.). 
Alexa supports multilingual dialogue in Japanese, Spanish, German, and 
other languages—not  just English.
Siri is the voice assistant built into Apple operating systems. Siri can 
make calls (on iPhone or iPad) and launch apps on all devices: iPhone, 
iPad, Mac, Apple Watch, and Apple TV. Siri can send messages in chats, 
manage alarms, calendars, and to-do lists, build routes, search the inter-
net, get news and weather forecasts, play music, and search for movies 
based on user requests. In the latest version of macOS 15.2 Sequoia, 
Siri can generate images using artiﬁcial intelligence algorithms and re-
member conversations. Siri can run in standby mode on MacBooks using 
Apple Intelligence technology.
Notion AI is designed to organize information in a uniﬁed workspace, 
oﬀering both business and home use. Notion AI stores information in the 
form of notes, abstract documents, and ﬁles in the cloud. Notion AI al-
lows to organize data in tables and dashboards, create calendar events, 
and manage projects. The key feature of Notion AI is its modularity. It 
allows to add modules for working with tables, lists, databases, mes-
sage boards, and galleries. Notion AI can be accessed through a browser 
or through a desktop app, both of which can be installed on popular 
operating systems: MacOS, Windows, Android, Linux.
Zapier AI, an AI-powered automated assistant, combines the func-
tionality of Slack, Trello, and Gmail so the user never misses important 
events. Zapier’s core elements are: Zap (automated workﬂow), Trigger 
(process launch), and Action (actions that occur after the trigger is trig-
gered). Notiﬁcations in Zapier are sent to the Slack module, which is 
designed for user interaction. Data in Zapier is stored in Smartsheet ta-
bles, which must be created before using the assistant. Zapier oﬀers in-
tegration with email and social media. Zapier also allows to automate 
business processes.
The Jasper Generative AI Chatbot is an alternative to ChatGPT with 
text generation capabilities. Jasper can work with blogs, social me-
dia, and email. Jasper is suitable for business and can generate im-
ages. Jasper uses the same Generative Pre-trained Transformer language 
(GPT) model as GPT-3.5. Jasper AI supports integration with Chrome 
and other programs. Jasper AI can write both books and informational 
guides.
SaneBox provides email as a service, manages accounts, and func-
tions as an intelligent assistant. SaneBox works with all email services. 
Its main function is to calculate email statistics for several days and 
delete unnecessary emails over time, using artiﬁcial intelligence algo-
rithms. SaneBox supports IMAP and Exchange Web Services.
The MindPal assistant is focused on education and business. It en-
ables the creation of artiﬁcial intelligence agents to improve company 
productivity and automate business tasks. MindPal oﬀers analytics, task 
management, statistics visualization, advertising auditing, business plan 
generation, and production improvement proposals.

4.  Statement of the problem
The conducted analysis has shown that there are currently no dig-
ital twins that ensure high speed of receiving data on events as they 
occur in subway systems and ensure coordination of personnel actions 
aimed at eliminating hazardous and emergency situations and their con-
sequences. It is necessary to create a new digital twin with signiﬁcantly 
broader capabilities: the ability to process video data, audio data and 
data from various sensors that is continuously received from subway 
facilities, the ability to take into account the relationships between sta-
tions and the impact of events occurring at one or more stations on other 
stations.
For end users, it is necessary to create an intelligent assistant that is 
installed on user devices and provides visualization of events on 2D/3D 
models with varying degrees of detail, has speech recognition capabili-
ties, which allows for the provision of information on recognized objects, 
receiving information on emerging situations, asking questions verbally, 
sending text messages to the operator; that is able to navigate in the dig-
ital twin’s graphical objects, and also has the ability to integrate with 
the IVSS.
To ensure the required capabilities, the digital twin must include 
the digital twins of individual stations, and the digital twins of adja-
cent stations must be interconnected. It must be possible to use existing 
digital twins and create new twins using a template. When creating dig-
ital twins based on a template, the template-based digital twins must 
be adapted to the speciﬁc characteristics of the speciﬁc station. Further-
more, knowledge gained from the construction and use of digital twins 
of other stations must be taken into account.
Creation of digital twins of the stations should allow fast identiﬁ-
cation of emerging events (ﬁre, ﬂooding, littering, etc.) based on data 
collected from subway facilities, predict their progression, assess the 
potential impact on adjacent upstream and downstream stations, evalu-
ate potential consequences, and quickly inform station personnel of any 
subway incidents.
The digital twin should include two components: a basic component 
capable of solving problems requiring the use of AI, and a graphical 
component providing 2D/3D models of subway stations used to display 
events on user devices. The digital twin should be built using sensor 
data, video information, and audio data processed using neural networks 
and should be also capable of solving tasks such as event detection, fore-
casting, and other tasks that require intellectual capabilities. Neural net-
works are used since they allow accurately identify objects and events 
thanks to more complex algorithms, though they are more demanding 
on GPUs [15].
Digital twin computationally complex processes should be executed 
in a fog computing environment on a server (as they are resource-
intensive) adapted for processing data using neural network methods. 
Digital twins can be designed using ready-made platforms with adap-
tive elements developed for a speciﬁc subject area (domain) and sup-
porting the connection of signaling elements (sensors, video cameras, 
audio, etc.). However, creating custom digital twins and platforms for 
their design will signiﬁcantly improve the performance of systems that 
use these custom digital twins, compared to systems that use digital 
twins built using existing large platforms.
Custom digital twins and platforms operate several times faster be-
cause they don’t have a large number of universal functions and ele-
ments that are unnecessary when creating subway digital twins. Creat-
ing a custom digital twin also allows for more precise customization of 
the digital twin based on data sources. Furthermore, using almost all 
standard digital twins poses installation and maintenance challenges, 
and they also have limitations in supported elements, objects, move-
ments, and model dimensions.
When developing platforms for building digital twins, many devel-
opment environments can be used, providing ready-made libraries and 
components for drawing graphics, drawings, diagrams, and so on. All el-
ements or objects of digital twin (people, cars, trains, etc.) can be drawn 
using these libraries. It is also possible to create diagrams in 2D space, 
program the reception of signals (data from sensors, audio, video, etc.), 
and create your own digital twin in a programming language. The cre-
ation process may slow down by 30–100 times, but the result digital twin 
will be faster than digital twins that are built using a standard platform.
Developing a digital twin for the subway should involve developing 
components for processing data received from various sources installed 
at subway facilities, which are used to build the digital twin, as well as 
developing a mobile application for subway employees, which imple-
ments the functions of an intelligent assistant and uses the developed 
digital twin as a model. The data processing component should process 
continuously incoming data streams using neural networks.
The component should be hosted on a server in a fog computing 
environment, and data on detected events should be sent to intelligent 
assistants installed on staﬀ mobile phones. It should be possible to in-
stall the intelligent assistant on various Android smartphone versions, 
as most devices worldwide run this operating system. It is necessary to 
create an APK application for a stable version of Android to avoid the 
need for employees to change devices.
The Android application can reside permanently in the smartphone’s 
memory and display events in the status bar. After the initial connec-
tion to the fog server via HTTP(s), a persistent connection is established 
(TCP/IP route), which is very fast and can transmit event data in frac-
tions of a second (10–200 ms). If the fog server is located in a local 
Ethernet network without internet access, a high-speed connection can 
be maintained for years.

5.  Solution method
5.1.  General description of the proposed solution
The proposed digital twin of the subway system includes multiple 
interconnected twins of diﬀerent stations (Fig. 2). In its simplest form, 
the structure of the subway system’s digital twin is two-level: on the 
ﬁrst level the digital twins of individual stations are placed, and on the 
second, higher level, the subway system’s digital twin, which includes 
the digital twins of individual stations, is located.
Events occurring at one station (e.g., a ﬁre) are reﬂected in the digital 
twin of the subway system. The digital twins of the preceding station 
and the following station receive information about events (e.g., a ﬁre), 
allowing subway staﬀ and support services that works at other stations 
to quickly receive information about events occurring at neighboring 
stations.
Currently, many passenger collisions on station tracks are prevented 
only due to the attentiveness of the train driver. Failure of switch equip-
ment and track deformation caused by wear can lead to disaster. Crowd-
ing on platforms can lead to crushes when entering train compartments, 
escalators, and exit stairs. In many of these cases, tragedies can be 
avoided by using the proposed interconnected digital twins, that take 
into account the states of neighboring stations, collect real-time data on 
subway facilities from various sources, and include an image recognition 
system that ensures high-accuracy event recognition.
When constructing the subway’s digital twin and its station’s digital 
twins, audio and sensor data are collected and processed in addition to 
video data, enabling the creation of datasets that enable highly accu-
rate event detection. In new Tesla vehicles, sensors have been excluded 
from the autonomous driving system, and the road navigation system re-
lies solely on information from surveillance cameras. Tesla’s tests have 
shown that in some cases, the image recognition system is inaccurate, 
particularly in cases of deliberate sabotage (painting a road on a wall, 
etc.).
In the process of building the subway’s digital twin for processing 
data received from diﬀerent sources neural networks are used. Using 
neural networks helps prevent erroneous and inaccurate event detec-
tion at subway locations as by now neural networks have shown high 
eﬃciency  in data processing, including image recognition.
The subway’s digital twin contains the following objects: people, 
train cars, and robots (AI-powered car washers, which are planned for 
future implementation and use in the subway). The digital twin also con-
tains stationary objects: kiosks, stairs, railway tracks, and utility rooms. 
The digital twin displays the movement of people, train arrivals, car 
door openings, vestibule closings, etc. based on pattern recognition us-
ing images from video cameras, as well as interactions between objects 
(the door’s stuck, a breakdown, etc.) based on processing sensor data.
The digital twin also displays events such as ﬁres, ﬂoods, escalator 
or car door failures, etc. The digital twin allows employees to conduct a 
chronological analysis of events with the additional ability to view video 
clips, sensor data, and listen to audio recordings, leveraging automated 
video analysis techniques [16].

5.2.  Architecture of the decision support system
The architecture is presented in blocks with detailed information. 
Decision-making based on events requires an architecture (Fig. 4) con-
sisting of two modules.
The ﬁrst module is a “small digital twin” of a single station that is 
intended for the operator. The operator monitors safety at the subway 
station. Information for the operator is obtained from the digital twin. 
The digital twin analyzes incoming and previous information, and an 
event is generated for the operator. In Module 1 of the digital twin the 
situation at various stations is assessed. When new data is obtained a 
digital twin is rebuilt and an event is generated on the digital twin. 
All these steps are interconnected. Then, the event history is analyzed, 
and the best response to the event is determined. All these steps are 
inseparably linked.
Module 2 houses a “large digital twin” of the entire subway. The 
server processes data generating summary information on the events. 
The quality of the decision-making process depends on interconnected 
modules 1-2, exchanging information on the events and decisions made 
in response to them. Computational costs are minimal, since all the most 
complex operations are processed on the fog server.
The proposed architecture is distinguished by deep detailed pro-
cessing of occurring events in real-time in combination with general 
processing of events that occur across all subway stations. Decision-
making is based on the information received from monitored and at 
other neighboring stations. The architecture of the system is modular 
and block-based. Modules are decomposed into individual blocks and 
interconnections are deﬁned for the blocks. Overall, the architecture is 
designed to generate well founded decisions and ultimately respond to
events.
5.3.  Platform for constructing a digital twin for the subway
Existing digital twin platforms generally don’t require programming; 
it is suﬃcient  to have design skills. To construct a digital twin using 
these platforms it is required to place objects, build chains, and deﬁne 
connections with digital twin. This approach allows for the rapid devel-
opment of digital twins.
However, existing platforms are designed for modeling speciﬁc ob-
jects in a speciﬁc domain. Not all platforms support image recognition 
from video cameras and the Internet of Things (smartphones, etc.), and 
many are very slow. Existing digital twin platforms also have limitations 
on the types of modeled objects and other design features of the models. 
They operate quite slowly when the number of objects is large.
Customized digital twins developed as binary applications (*.apk, 
*.exe, *.elf, etc.) operate several times faster, but require programming 
skills. Building a customized digital twin can take weeks, sometimes 
months, but it doesn’t have the limitations of digital twins created using 
oﬀ-the-shelf platforms.
To build digital twins the authors developed their own platform. The 
platform provides sets of standard elements (conﬁgurations for the sub-
ject area). The digital twins are developed as part of the digital twin plat-
form. The digital twin platform allows for the deﬁnition of object move-
ments and the customization of data acquisition from various sources 
(video cameras, sensors) to determine the movement of objects and their 
type.
When developing the platform, the core was programmed and ready-
made standard components were used, eliminating the need for the de-
veloper to spend time drawing each component. A professionally design 
of a component and its integration into the platform can take 1-2 weeks. 
If the digital twin platform developer lacks graphic design skills and rel-
evant education, the components will look unpresentable and out of 
place. The appearance of the elements is quite important.

the location of the event, and a video is played. This is only possible with 
certain access rights. Event data is sent to users at the station where the 
event occurred and at neighboring stations. The operator also has the 
ability to navigate through the subway facilities in 2D/3D space and 
connect to video cameras to view the current situation at the facilities.
When using existing solutions, manual rebuilding of the digital twin 
is required when station architecture changes, new elements are added, 
or new stations are opened (commissioned). The proposed solution im-
poses no restrictions on the composition and parameters of subway ob-
jects, nor does it limit modeling to speciﬁc subway architecture and 
station objects. New elements can be added from a set of standard ele-

new solution supports the use of not only speciﬁc camera types (AXIS 
P13/P33 series with DC-Iris and P-Iris) and sensors for data collection, 
but also other camera and sensor models.
5.4.  Standard template for a digital twin of a station
New digital twins are built using a specially developed constructor 
and template, which represents a typical station digital twin. The digi-
tal twin constructor is a set of elements and relationships from the sub-
ject area. The elements are grouped based on their relevance: basic, 
advanced, additional, other, and selected. Elements are further divided 
into complex and simple.
Relationships between elements are conﬁgured depending of the 
connected elements. For example, a railway track can be connected to 
a tunnel, while people and traﬃc  lights cannot, as they are elements of 
diﬀerent entity types. The workspace for constructing digital twins is 
divided into the following main areas: the elements panel, the settings 
panel, the properties panel for the active (selected) element, and the 
main application menu for selecting panels, loading, and saving digi-
tal twins. Relationships between elements (and behavior for simulating 
events) are deﬁned in a separate panel with detailed settings.
The digital twin template for a station includes a standard set of 
elements and links and, as a rule, requires minor modiﬁcations when

5.5.  Implementation of the digital twin of the subway
The following components were implemented for decision support 
systems based on digital twins of the subway:
• An intelligent assistant with the ability to visualize events on 2D/3D 
models and speech recognition capabilities;
• A server component for processing data about subway assets ob-
tained from video cameras, microphones, and various sensors.
The mobile application, codenamed “Dt2DtSub-0.4.98a.apk,” was 
developed using the GIGA IDE Community Edition 2024.1.1 develop-
ment environment. The development environment supports Java (Level 
4) and Kotlin (Level 5), and can compile and run programs on a high-
performance cloud server hosted by GIGA. Kotlin Novator is suitable for 
development on Android and Linux. Using Kotlin, a level 5 language, 
greatly simpliﬁes development for the mobile platform. The GigaIDE 
development environment is in no way inferior to IDEA/PyCharm from 
IntelliJ, but surpasses it in the number of additional plugins.
The ready-made APK application for Android-v.15 is located in the 
smartphone’s ﬁle system and runs on the smartphone itself with the “de-
veloper mode” option enabled in the settings. The “Dt2DtSub” applica-
tion connects to the fog server on which the twin is built depending 
on geolocation (GPRS) and is in the mode of receiving events from the 
server.
The Cisco UCS C480 ML M5 is used as a server in the developed sys-
tem. The server processes video sequences. SubScript, which is adapted 
for intelligent video surveillance systems, in particular, subway systems, 
was used to develop the server component of the system. SubScript en-
ables the storage of settings and statistics in a database, as well as the vi-
sualization of events on graphical models of objects. Event visualization 
is performed in applications installed on the employees’ mobile phones. 
The HUAWEI Mate X6 is compatible with the mobile app.
The server and smartphone models used are adapted for machine 
learning and neural networks, which increases the speed of data pro-
cessing when identifying objects and events. Data objects in the app are 
presented to users as 2D/3D models with zoom capabilities and the abil-
ity to view ﬁne details, displaying data on the results of object and event 
recognition.
The intelligent assistant features voice assistant functions and speech 
synthesis capabilities, building upon robust multimodal interaction 
frameworks [17]. VOSK is used for speech recognition, SAPI5 is used for 
speech generation, and NAudio is used for working with audio streams. 
The Vosk solution is a ready-made solution written in Python. Unlike 
SpeechRecognition, the Vosk library does not require an internet con-
nection and can operate without third-party services. Vosk supports over 
25 languages, is lightweight, easily integrates with programming lan-
guages C#, JavaScript, Java, etc.), and runs on all popular operating 
systems (Windows, Linux, and macOS).

7.  Discussion
While the interconnected twin architecture demonstrates a 17.4% re-
duction in latency, this study relies heavily on simulated emergency 
scenarios. A primary limitation is the system’s dependency on contin-
uous TCP/IP connectivity; network degradation in deep subterranean 
environments could impede the fog-to-mobile data pipeline, requiring 
robust fallback protocols for physical, safety-critical deployment. De-
spite these constraints, the modular nature of the Dt2DtSub architecture 
oﬀers high generalizability, allowing it to be scaled to other complex in-
frastructure networks such as airport terminals or underground mining
facilities.
8.  Conclusion
The authors proposed a digital twin of the subway system, built on 
the basis of interconnected digital twins of individual stations for deci-
sion support systems. We also developed a platform for building digital 
twins and an application for operators’ mobile phones with intelligent 
assistant functions.
The developed mobile application functions as an intelligent assis-
tant, displaying data about subway stations on smartphones while ac-
counting for situations occurring at neighboring stations, and supports 
voice data processing. A key advantage is the high operating speed, en-
sured by the lightweight nature of the application and the oﬄoading  of 
heavy computation to the fog computing environment.
Experimental assessments demonstrated that the implementation of 
the Dt2DtSub architecture signiﬁcantly accelerated emergency response 
protocols. Personnel response times improved by an average of 17.4% 
compared to the popular REPEAT platform. Speciﬁcally, the time re-
quired to contain ﬁre and smoke incidents was reduced by 52 seconds 
compared to the current XProtect-based solution, and by roughly 47 
seconds compared to the industry average. The absolute response times 
varied between a minimum of 267 seconds and a maximum of 307 sec-
onds, validating the eﬃciency  of oﬄoading  heavy computational tasks 
to the fog environment.
The proposed solution is applicable not only to subway systems but 
also to building models of other public transport assets: bus depots, trol-
leybuses, trams, and assets related to other network infrastructure (river 
transport and general rail service), where the performance of passenger 
company personnel needs to be improved.
Further developments include deeper integration with signaling and 
control systems to automatically stop mechanisms (trains, escalators, 
disconnecting live cables, etc.). Plans include enhancing the assistant’s 
intelligence and support the intelligent analysis of complex informa-
tion received from “Safe City” video cameras and the creation of event 
chronologies.
Future plans also include assessing the coordination of personnel ac-
tions, which assumes delegating responses to events from one service to 
another and can take considerable time, as well as the coordination of 
emergency response actions. The assistant is expected to include the fol-
