In recent years, the emergence of advanced information technology and artificial intelligence has significantly 
advanced the construction industry, leading to a new era characterized by intelligent construction. Prefabricated build-
ings, distinguished by their standard components, have emerged as a crucial factor in this evolution, demonstrating 
rapid growth. Intelligent construction embodies a groundbreaking methodology integrating advanced information 
technology with engineering construction techniques. An essential technical challenge involves efficiently integrating 
the ‘physical’ and ‘
digital’ domains within prefabricated structures. This paper explores the integration of digital twin 
(DT) technology within the intelligent construction of prefabricated buildings. DTs offer real-time connectivity between 
digital and physical construction environments, supporting adaptive decision making and enhanced collaboration. The 
state-of-the-art literature is reviewed, a structured DT framework is proposed and applications in hoisting manage -
ment, safety and predictive analytics are examined. The study provides strategic insights into advancing Construction 
4.0 by addressing scientific challenges that hinder the transition to intelligent construction and establishing a theoreti -
cal foundation for this transformation.
Keywords: artificial intelligence/building information modelling (BIM)/buildings, structures & design/digital twin/intelligent construction/ 
prefabricated building
1. Introduction
Before widespread industrialization, China’s construction sector 
grappled with considerable challenges concerning information man-
agement and digital transformation, yet simultaneously opportunities 
were presented for improved quality development. Prefabricated con
-
struction, distinguished by its standardized design, industrialized 
manufacturing, modular assembly and comprehensive finishing, is a 
crucial mechanism and primary avenue for advancing new construc
-
tion industrialization (Hao, 2022; Li et al., 2020). Achieving intellec-
tualization within the prefabrication process necessitates real-time 
data exchange and multidisciplinary collaboration among all con -
struction stakeholders, a feat made possible by advanced information 
technology (IT) systems (Zhong et al., 2017
).
Recently, technologies including the Internet of Things (IoT), big 
data analytics, cloud computing and artificial intelligence (AI) have 
seen increasing adoption in the construction sector (Haris et al., 
2024; Jagatheesaperumal et al., 2022; Zhong et al., 2017). However, 
their application has largely remained confined to solving isolated 
engineering tasks such as equipment tracking, energy monitoring and 
scheduling optimization (Ibrahim et al., 2024; Mabrouka and Younis, 
2022; Wen, 2021). As a result, the industry still lacks a unified, inte-
grated platform to bridge design, production, assembly and post- 
construction stages under an intelligent and data-centric umbrella.
1.1 Related works 
Intelligent construction has emerged as a transformative 
approach that merges digital technologies with the entire 
construction life cycle, from planning and design to on-site man -
agement and post-occupancy evaluation ( Meshref et al ., 2023). 
It emphasizes dynamic decision making, real-time feedback and 
cross-disciplinary collaboration. Here, intelligent construction is 
not limited to automation alone, but extends to cognitive deci -
sion support, proactive risk control and autonomous optimization 
across the construction value chain (
Sacks et al ., 2020
; 
Sepasgozar et al., 2023).
The digital twin (DT) – an interactive virtual replica of a physical 
asset or system – plays a pivotal role in enabling intelligent con -
struction. By continuously synchronizing real-time data from sen -
sors, devices and construction equipment with its digital 
counterpart, DT allows stakeholders to simulate, predict and con
-
trol the physical environment in real time ( Boje et al., 2020
; Tao 
et al., 2022). While DT is widely adopted in aerospace and manu -
facturing, its integration in the architecture, engineering, construc-
tion and facility management (AEC-FM) sectors is relatively 
nascent (
Hosamo et al., 2022
; Madubuike et al., 2022).
Recent research, such as that by Liu et al. (2022b), has explored 
the use of DT in risk control for prefabricated hoisting operations. 
Similarly, Jiang et al. (2022)
proposed a DT-enabled modular inte-
grated construction system for on-site assembly. Although these 
studies illustrate the feasibility of DT, they also highlight chal
-
lenges in interoperability, stakeholder alignment and scalability. In 
addition, 
Pan and Zhang (2021) presented a data-driven DT model 
incorporating IoT and building information modelling (BIM) for

1.2 Intelligent construction services 
Integrating DT technology with intelligent construction services 
represents a paradigm shift in how buildings and infrastructure are 
designed, assembled and managed ( Madubuike et al., 2022 ; 
Sepasgozar et al., 2023 ) DT s facilitate real-time monitoring, pre -
dictive maintenance, adaptive resource allocation and enhanced 
safety protocols ( Liu et al., 2022a ; T ao et al., 2022 ) W ithin this 
synergy , intelligent services emerge from the ability to sense, ana -
lyse and respond to conditions across various construction phases. 
Smart scheduling and logistics are enabled through real- time 
updates of site conditions ( Jiang et al., 2022 ), while safety man -
agement is reinforced by way of risk modelling and early-warning 
systems derived from sensor data ( Kor et al., 2023 ; Liu et al., 
2021 ). V irtual prototyping and simulations empower designers 
and engineers to validate performance before implementation, 
minimising errors and reworking ( Pan and Zhang, 2021 ). These 
capabilities promote proactive decision making and dynamic 
adaptability , aligning the construction process with the goals of 
Construction 4.0 ( Hu et al., 2022 ; Kosse et al., 2022 ). In addition, 
integrating BIM, geographic information system (GIS), IoT and 
enterprise systems allows for comprehensive data exchange and 
inter-platform collaboration ( Boje et al., 2020 ), forming the back -
bone of intelligent construction ecosystems.
The need to evolve prefabricated construction from isolated and 
manually coordinated processes into digitally synchronized and 
autonomous systems is more urgent than ever . T raditional con -
struction methods suff er from fragmentation, inefficiencies and 
inconsistent quality control ( Moshood et al., 2024 ). DT technol -
ogy offers a robust framework to overcome these limitations by 
enabling real-time bidirectional interaction between the physical 
and virtual worlds ( Sacks et al., 2020 ). However , several chal -
lenges remain unresolved, including the lack of scalable DT 
frameworks across project life cycles, the integration of intelligent 
services with heterogeneous construction systems, and the devel -
opment of predictive models capable of learning from diverse 
operational data ( Feng et al., 2021 ; Su et al., 2023 ). These 
knowledge gaps motivate this study to systematically explore how 
DT s can be applied not only as monitoring tools but as active ena -
blers of intelligent, adaptive and automated construction environ -
ments. Subsequently , through consolidated interdisciplinary 
insights and proposing structured methodologies, the aim of this 
work is to lay the groundwork for future innovation in intelligent 
prefabricated construction.

2.2 Intelligent construction 
Implications of intelligent construction. Intelligent construction 
involves a comprehensive framework of theories, methodologies, 
techniques and technologies that synergize sensing, communica
-
tion, digital tools, construction practices and project management. 
The aim of this integration is to monitor, analyse, control and opti-
mize aspects such as safety, quality, environmental impact, progress 
and cost associated with construction projects (Zhang et al
., 2024). 
The notion of intelligent construction is continually evolving, particu-
larly with the incorporation of AI and other cutting-edge technolo-
gies, resulting in increasingly innovative applications and a shifting 
definition (Jagatheesaperumal et al
., 2023a).
Key characteristics of intelligent construction
. The core of intelli-
gent construction is rooted in a thorough understanding of 
resource allocation in construction activities, process flow, busi -
ness operations dynamics, structural health integrity, project time -
line advancement and construction expense management 
(Jagatheesaperumal et al
., 2023b ). The process entails gathering 
various and heterogeneous data, automatic analysis and learning 
from these data using particular strategies and algorithms, ulti -
mately resulting in intelligent control of the construction process 
(Jagatheesaperumal et al
., 2023a).
The smart construction management platform uses sensing tech -
nology and the IoT to connect buildings, construction activities, 
controlled equipment, people and services ( Jagatheesaperumal 
et al
., 2023c). This allows information to be sent and received 
interactively and in real time (Zhao et al., 2021).
Closed-loop mechanisms encompass the precise detection, imme -
diate evaluation and responsive regulation of construction opera -
tions and machinery ( Liu et al
., 2023 ). Intelligent sensors can 
detect environmental changes, and AI technologies facilitate the 
analysis of extensive and varied data in real time. This process 
yields decision-supporting information and allows for the auto -
matic control of production processes, accompanied by real-time 
feedback, as illustrated in Figure 2.
Technologies including cloud computing, fuzzy processing, opti -
mization analysis and AI can potentially enhance decision making 
within the construction process. These advancements facilitate 
error correction, optimization, iteration and the implementation of 
agile warning and control systems ( Mohammadi and Mukhtar, 
2013). This enables ongoing enhancement and optimization of all 
construction processes within a closed-loop framework.
The intelligent construction system must exhibit capabilities for 
self-perception, adaptation, learning, decision making and execu -
tion, thereby effectively addressing complex construction chal -
lenges ( Du et al
., 2023 ). The system must understand, find and 
change important management and resources by carefully analy -
sing and evaluating them. It should then work as an intelligent 
entity that helps people and construction work together and 
improves the building process (Zhang et al
., 2024).

and optimization empowers all construction stakeholders to

2.3 Digital twins 
Implications of DTs. DT technology, often called digital imaging 
or mapping, encompasses the virtual representation of physical 
products within the digital domain (Liu et al., 2023). It mimics 
digital space by combining phenomena from different fields, sizes 
and types of matter, accurately capturing the whole life cycle of 
real things (Liu et al., 2023).
Key characteristics of DTs. DTs create representations of the real 
world or models within a virtual environment, facilitating accurate 
and precise reconstructions and allowing for dynamic interactions 
between reality and these models.
DTs leverage extensive datasets, employing comprehensive analy-
sis to classify, condense and actively oversee information ( Kumar 
and Jagatheesaperumal, 2025). They derive insights throughout 
the life cycle and evaluate the worth of the data.
DTs emphasize service provision by comprehensively monitoring 
‘human, machine, material, method and environment’. Their aim is to 
integrate energy, supply chain and resource management to enhance 
safety, progress, quality and cost efficiency (Jagatheesaperumal et al
., 
2023c).
DTs highlight the importance of data– model interoperability and 
the ability to perform transformations across multiple ports, proto-
cols, platforms and interfaces. Such capability enhances informa -
tion exchange across various industries and services, prioritizing 
real time and widespread connectivity.
DTs are conceptualized as representations of physical entities, 
highlighting the relationship between physical mapping and the 
digital realm. However, relying too heavily on virtual digital envi -
ronments for simulations and problem solving could make it more 
challenging to use DT technology in real life, decreasing the like -
lihood that more people will accept and study it.

Several research studies have underscored the role of DT technol -

Zhao et al. (2023) introduced a detailed framework that combines 
DT technology with BIM and the IoT to enhance the eff iciency of 
hoisting management systems. The framework employs Dijkstra’ s 
algorithm to gather real-time data, strategize hoisting routes and 
accurately position components throughout hoisting operations. 
An example case study showed that this method could work in a 
prefabricated building project by making intelligent management 
of hoisting operations much better and providing a faster way to 
reach intelligent construction goals.
Safety remains a critical consideration in hoisting operations, as 
these activities can introduce considerable hazards stemming from 
dynamic variables encountered during the lifting and assembly of 
prefabricated components. Liu et al. (2021) explored the necessity 
for enhanced safety risk management by developing a DT -based 
safety risk coupling model. Here, the model synthesizes IoT , BIM 
and a complex network approach to elucidate the correlations 
among hoisting risk factors. The study’ s findings illustrate the pro -
posed model’ s efficacy in improving the visualization and man -
agement of real-time safety risks associated with hoisting 
operations.
According to research by Liu et al. (2022b) , developing a real- 
time risk prediction framework has recently improved hoisting 
safety . Support vector machine (SVM) algorithms added to the 
decision tree model make it easier for the system to predict risks 
related to hoisting. This facilitates the early identification of 
potential hazards, thereby enhancing safety protocols during the 
hoisting processes associated with prefabricated construction.
The findings of Liu et al. (2022b) emphasize the significance of 
DT technology in risk management during hoisting operations. 
The authors highlighted the capabilities of DT systems for real- 
time interaction, facilitating dynamic monitoring of safety risks. 
Adding a Bayesian network model to the DT framework creates a 
decision-making tool that makes risk mitigation strategies much 
more effective in lar ge-scale prefabricated building projects.
Beyond prefabricated construction, various sectors, including min -
ing, have experienced advantages from implementing DT -based 
hoisting management. Kalinowski et al. (2021) investigated the 
utilization of digital technologies in managing shaft infrastructure 
diagnostics and hoisting operations in the mining sector . The 
results show that DT systems can improve monitoring and diag -
nostics, which suggests that similar approaches could be used to 
improve the management of construction hoisting.
More research has been done on crane hoisting operations. For 
example, Jiang et al. (2022) created a DT -based framework to test 
tower cranes’ stability when hoisted dif ferently . Here, the frame -
work provides significant insights into the safety of crane opera -
tions, highlighting essential areas for sensor deployment to 
mitigate unsafe behaviours during hoisting. In another similar 
work by Jiang et al. (2021) , they suggested the incorporation of 
DT with knowledge graphs to develop a real-time early-warning 
system aimed at enhancing safety in hoisting operations. Here, the 
methodology facilitates the prompt identification of anomalies 
and breaches, thereby minimizing the occurrence of safety viola -
tions in construction settings.
The management of hoisting operations within industrial contexts, 
including mining and maritime sectors, has experienced advance -
ments through digital technologies. Liang et al. (2023) introduced 
a DT technology system for mine hoisting, facilitating real-time 
monitoring and temperature forecasting of hoist motors. Such 
application presents significant implications for prefabricated con -
struction, emphasizing the potential of DT technology for enhanc -
ing predictive maintenance and ensuring operational safety . In 
addition, Zhao et al. (2023) showed a DT system specially made 
for rockshaft hoists, showing that DT technology can be used in 
many fields. For example, Lipeiyong et al. (2024) used DT tech -
nology to improve berth hoisting planning in shipbuilding, show -
ing how this method can improve hoisting operations in many 
fields.
Such recent advancements highlight the essential function of digi -
tal technologies in tackling the intricate challenges associated with 
contemporary construction and hoisting operations, thereby facili -
tating the development of more secure and efficient workflows.

3.3 Risk monitoring and safety management 
Implementing DT technology presents significant risk monitoring 
and safety management advancements within construction proj -
ects. It enables the utilization of real-time data alongside predic -
tive analysis functionalities. The growing complexity of 
construction processes necessitates the adoption of DT frame -
works, which are crucial for improving safety management prac -
tices. The progression of DT technology within construction 
safety management highlights its increasing influence on risk 
assessment and safety protocols. DT and building information 
modelling technologies show promise for keeping an eye on and 
predicting possible safety risks during the building process of pre -
fabricated buildings ( Lee and Moon, 2021 ). The provision of real- 
time data and visual management significantly enhances the anal -
ysis of safety risks and informs decision-making processes.
Liu et al. (2020) established a foundational framework for indoor 
safety management by applying DT s. It combines IoT sensors, 
BIM and SVMs to facilitate the automatic identification and clas -
sification of hazards within indoor settings. The framework signi -
fies a preliminary advancement in intelligent safety management, 
and showcased the capability for real-time monitoring and hazard 
classification within a stadium context during the Beijing W inter 
Olympics. While concentrating on indoor safety , this study estab -
lishes a foundational framework for applying safety management 
practices in the construction industry . T eizer et al. (2022) devel -
oped a more structured approach for a DT for construction safety 
(DTCS), expanding upon these concepts. A workflow was 
delineated to improve safety by focusing on hazard prevention, 
risk monitoring and ongoing performance enhancement. The 
DTCS system prioritizes data-driven safety management through -
out all project phases, offering a framework for developing future 
DT -based safety monitoring systems. Their vision integrates digi -
tal and physical twins, facilitating proactive hazard detection and 
enabling real-time feedback.
Liu et al. (2022a) created an intelligent safety risk prediction 
model based on DT technology to improve the use of DT s and risk 
prediction. Such a model employs a DT -SVM algorithm to fore -
cast risks associated with hoisting in prefabricated construction. 
The system examines the spatial – temporal evolution of risks and 
delivers real-time risk alerts. Here, the advancement represents a 
notable progression in safety management, facilitating the antici -
pation of safety hazards before their manifestation and allowing 
for more efficient interventions. Similarly , the same authors ( Liu 
et al., 2022a ) introduced a more sophisticated risk control method 
focused on the hoisting operations of prefabricated buildings. A 
Bayesian network model was integrated into the decision tree 
framework to facilitate the visualization and analysis of the corre -
lations among dif ferent risk factors. The model delivers immediate 
insights regarding safety risks, enabling construction managers to 
implement preventive measures effec tively . Such an approach 
enhances decision making by establishing a hierarchy of safety 
risks and pinpointing the most significant risk factors. Here, the 
framework’ s ef fectiveness was evidenced in a large-scale prefabri -
cated building project.
Alongside predicting safety risks, Ogunseiju et al. (2021) investi -
gated the potential of digital technologies to enhance the manage -
ment of ergonomic risks. The authors introduced a framework 
based on DT technology that uses wearable sensors to track 
employees’ postures and an long short-term memory neural net -
work to provide real-time feedback on er gonomic risks. The 
framework improves the capacity of workers to effec tively man -
age musculoskeletal risks, leading to a decrease in injuries and an 
enhancement of overall well-being. Such an approach illustrates 
the potential for DT to transcend traditional safety management 
practices, incorporating tailored health and safety solutions specif -
ically designed for construction workers.
T orrecilla García et al. (2021) highlighted the significance of com -
bining BIM with DT to improve decision-making processes in 
occupational health and safety management. Their research 
emphasized the importance of collaboration in safety planning and 
risk awareness in construction projects, advocating for predictive 
risk analysis and intelligent asset optimization. Here, the integra -
tion enables a proactive safety strategy , promoting the early detec -
tion of potential hazards and enhancing communication 
throughout all operational tiers ( Bibri et al., 2024 ). The evolution 
from fundamental monitoring systems to sophisticated predictive 
models and tailored health management illustrates how DT facili -
tates a more dynamic and real-time methodology in construction 
safety . The ongoing advancements indicate that the evolution of 
DT technology will significantly enhance the safety of construc -
tion environments.

4.1 Comprehensive element information 
The integration of data derived from tangible entities and digital 
models is crucial for thoroughly monitoring factors influencing 
construction production. The foundation of the DT system is built 
upon real-time data collection and transmission. For the virtual 
model to accurately reflect the operational state of physical enti
-
ties, sensors must work at their best. The virtual model acts as a 
real-time dynamic representation of these entities. The current 
technical limitations affect sensors’ variety, accuracy, dependabil
-
ity and operational context, influencing the data acquisition pro -
cess. Real-time performance and security measures are at the heart 
of data transmission. Considering the existing technological limi -
tations, the attainment of increased transmission speeds while 
maintaining network security presents significant challenges (

or modules with existing systems, or their compatibility, is

bottleneck that impedes data analysis, utilization and the advance -
ment of DT technology both now and in the future.
4.2 Operational mechanism 
The intricate mechanism involves creating a virtual model within 
the digital environment, ensuring precise mapping from the 
physical domain to the virtual one, and achieving real-time feed -
back control during the construction process in the physical 
space.
Multi-domain modelling encompasses integrating physical sys -
tems across various domains, addressing typical and atypical con -
ditions. Such a process spans from the initial design stage to an 
in-depth exploration of mechanisms, facilitating a thorough under
-
standing and modelling of the design. Most contemporary mod -
elling approaches are tailored to specific domains, with the 
integration and data fusion processes taking place subsequently 
to amalgamate separate models into a unified framework. 
Nonetheless, this fusion approach frequently proves inadequate 
and lacks a clear rationale, constraining integration potential.

4.3 Establishment of intelligent construction 
The intelligent construction system platform, which benefits from 
DT technology , makes making decisions and controlling the con -
struction engineering DT’ s functionality easier . It could be 
achieved by analysing the actual needs of physical space, utilizing 
the virtual space algorithm base and leveraging the robust data 
processing capabilities of the information layer .
The intelligent construction system, as a platform that integrates 
various intelligent technologies, must have a technical architecture 
that is both interoperable and scalable. Implementing a service bus 
utilizes the principles of service-oriented architecture, integrating 
conventional middleware, extensible mark-up language and web 
service technologies. The construction service bus (CSB) is the 
essential connection point within the intelligent construction 
system network, enabling seamless interoperability among various 
services. The software subsystem in the intelligent construction 
system is structured as web services to obscure its internal com -
plexity . The services offered are articulated in WSDL (web serv -
ices description language) and made available through UDDI 
(universal description, discovery and integration). The resources 
involved in physical space construction, including labour and 
advanced construction machinery , are transformed into virtual 
agents and incorporated into the CSB. Such integration facilitates 
the distributed control capabilities of the intelligent construction 
system, as illustrated in Figure 5 .
