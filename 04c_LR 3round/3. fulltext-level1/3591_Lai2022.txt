Undetected fatigue and overload damages at the key locations of
the crane boom are among the biggest threats in construction,
leading to structural failure. Thus, the structural health of the
crane boom should be monitored in real time to ensure that it
works under the designed load capacity. In this study, we developed
a lightweight digital twin by the multi ﬁdelity surrogate (MFS)
model to improve the real-time monitoring and prediction accuracy
of the structural safety of a crane boom. Digital twin technology,
which can establish real-time mapping between the physical
space and the digital space, has a promising potential for online
monitoring and analysis of structures, equipment, and even
human bodies. By combining the MFS model and sensor data, the
lightweight digital twin can dynamically mirror the crane boom
postures and predict its structural performance in real time. In
this study, the structural analysis of the crane boom is limited to
the linear elastic stage of materials. Numerical experiments
showed that the accuracy of the lightweight digital twin was
enhanced compared with that established by the single- ﬁdelity sur-
rogate model, and the computational cost of the lightweight digital
twin was decreased with respect to the digital twin built by the
numerical method. Meanwhile, the uncertainty from the physical
space was analyzed to enhance the reliability of the lightweight
digital twin. Thus, the lightweight digital twin developed in our
work can ensure accurate safety prediction and design optimization
for crane booms. [DOI: 10.1115/1.4053606]
Keywords: digital twin, safety monitoring, multi ﬁdelity surrogate
model, crane boom, design automation
1 Introduction
Cranes are one of the most widely used heavy equipment in fac-
tories, construction sites, and ports. As a key component, the crane
boom deserves great attention in terms of structural safety as acci-
dents caused by the structural damage of the crane boom may
cause disastrous consequences [ 1,2]. By monitoring, analyzing,
and predicting the structural status of the crane boom in real time,
the occurrence of such accidents can be decreased to a certain
extent. Therefore, an effective way is required to quickly and reli-
ably predict the structural performance of any key location on the
crane boom and improve its design. As a promising solution, the
digital twin is widely used for online monitoring, analysis, and pre-
diction of various industrial devices including aircraft, vessels, and
automobiles. Therefore, the digital twin was adopted in this study
for the safety monitoring and prediction of the crane boom.
As initially deﬁned by Grieves [3], the digital twin contains phys-
ical products, virtual products, and connections between them.
Since its introduction, the concept, its implementation, and applica-
tions have been heavily studied [ 4–8]. NASA and the US Air Force
have applied digital twin technology to the condition monitoring,
fault diagnosis, life prediction, and design optimization of various
types of air vehicles by integrating diverse heterogeneous informa-
tion sources [ 9–11]. In addition, numerous studies have demon-
strated its applications in diverse equipment, including mobile
cranes [ 12], wind turbines [ 13], and computer numerical control
machine tools [14]. The digital twin has transformed from a descrip-
tive concept to an actionable technique.
In particular, structural safety monitoring is one of the most
promising applications of the digital twin as it can obtain the real-
time reﬂection of the physical world and facilitate decision-making
based on fast analytical algorithms. By taking the real forces or dis-
placements obtained from sensors as inputs, Haag and Andrel
developed a digital twin that employed the ﬁnite element method
(FEM) to perform the structural analysis of a simple bending
beam [ 15]. Guivarch et al. proposed an approach to building a
digital twin for a swashplate rotor assembly of a helicopter using
multibody simulations [16]. Fotland et al. performed multiple simu-
lation methods and ef ﬁciently built a digital twin for a crane pulley
and cable [ 17]. Moi et al. implemented a digital twin for the condi-
tion monitoring of a knuckle boom crane based on an inverse

method using strain gauges as load sensors, which provided real-
time determinations of stresses, strains, and loads at an unlimited
number of spots [ 18]. These studies focused on combining mecha-
nistic models, such as structural statics and dynamics with numeri-
cal methods to establish digital twins. The essential bene ﬁt of using
this way to build the digital twin is that the model parameters have
an actual physical meaning, which facilitates the scienti ﬁc interpre-
tation of the results.
However, numerical methods are typically dif ﬁcult to be used
in real-time applications due to their high computational con-
sumption. To overcome this limitation, Rasheed et al. suggested
using arti ﬁcial intelligence (AI) and machine learning (ML) to
build the digital twin [ 19]. Von Rueden et al. described a
hybrid modeling approach by combining AI/ML algorithms and
simulations, aimed at bridging the gap between data-based and
knowledge-based modeling [ 20]. Magargle et al. introduced a
simulation-based digital twin to support heat monitoring and pre-
dictive maintenance for an automotive braking system [ 21]. Wang
et al. presented a digital twin for rotating machinery fault diagno-
sis based on rotor dynamics and the FEM [ 22]. Li et al. employed
a dynamic Bayesian network to build a digital twin for monitor-
ing the crack growth on the leading edge of an aircraft wing [ 23].
Essentially, the currently popular AI and ML methods utilize sta-
tistics to predict trends and patterns. To use digital twins in struc-
tural safety monitoring, a common way to introduce AI/ML is to
use the results of numerical analysis as the training data to build
the AI/ML model. In this way, AI/ML model equations are
derived by developing a probabilistic model that best describes
the relationship between dependent and independent variables.
However, since the statistical model is based on correlation
rather than causality, it cannot provide an interpretation of the
mechanistic process.
Both mechanistic and statistical models have their advantages
and shortcomings. Therefore, a more reasonable way to combine
simulation with AI/ML should be adopted to build the digital
twin for the structural analysis. One promising approach was pre-
sented by Willcox and her coworkers by combining a library of
component-based reduced-order models with Bayesian state esti-
mation or optimal tree algorithm to create data-driven physics-based
digital twins [ 24,25]. Compared with an optimal tree, the Bayesian
state estimation could quantify the uncertainty of the digital twin.
Furthermore, they proposed a probabilistic graphical model as a
formal mathematical representation of the digital twin and its asso-
ciated physical asset, which helped to move the digital twin from a
one-off model to an accessible robust model at scale [ 26]. Another
way to combine simulations with AI/ML was realized simply by
training the simulation results based on several data points of
samples, while other data points in the solution space were interpo-
lated or ﬁtted [ 27]. However, a reliable model built by this way
generally requires substantial samples, which will consume consid-
erable computational resources. The two combination-based
approaches are limited by high computational costs. Therefore, if
the computational effort is reduced and the prediction accuracy is
improved, the digital twin composed of mechanistic and statistical
models will be more lightweight, ef ﬁcient, and reliable.
The multi ﬁdelity surrogate (MFS) model widely used in engi-
neering design and optimization is adopted to achieve this goal.
A high- ﬁdelity (HF) model is generally accurate but also more
time consuming than the corresponding low- ﬁdelity (LF) model
[28]. A general trend of the outputs of interest can be provided by
a large number of inexpensive LF samples, and the accuracy of
outputs can be improved by a few expensive HF samples [ 29].
Thus, the MFS model can not only greatly reduce the computational
effort of the numerical methods but also improve the accuracy of the
single-ﬁdelity surrogate model. In this study, a lightweight digital
twin for structural safety monitoring based on the MFS model
was proposed by adopting both statistical and mechanics-based
models, and the data obtained from the models and multiple
sensors were combined to provide more accurate results and
faster solutions. Notably, the simulation of the crane boom was
limited to the linear elastic stage of materials. The MFS model-
based lightweight digital twin focuses on the key locations of equip-
ment, is more accurate, ef ﬁcient, and lightweight, and uses fewer
computing resources to achieve higher accuracy compared with
the digital twin proposed in our previous work [ 27].
The remainder of this article is organized as follows. In Sec. 2,
the method used to establish the MFS model-based lightweight
digital twin for structural safety monitoring is introduced.
Section 3 presents the implementation process of the digital twin
in detail by taking a crane boom as an example. Compared with
the results obtained from the HF computer simulation, the effective-
ness of the proposed digital twin is veri ﬁed in Sec. 4, as well as the
uncertainty analysis of the lightweight digital twin is performed.
Discussion and conclusions are presented in Sec. 5.
2 Composition of the Lightweight Digital Twin
2.1 Computer Simulation Model. When taking into account
the structural performance information, the current digital twin is
generally expressed by various mechanistic models and calculated
by multiple numerical methods, including the FEM, ﬁnite volume
method, ﬁnite difference method, etc. In this study, the FEM was
adopted to execute the simulation for the structural performance
of the crane boom. However, running HF ﬁnite element analysis
might be extremely time consuming, especially for systems with
high-dimensional inputs. Therefore, it is signi ﬁcant to conduct the
discretization of input variables and the simpli ﬁcation of the
system for building a digital twin. The input variables of engineer-
ing problems mainly contain shape, posture, velocity, load, and so
on. The combination of these input variables is different in various
states of the same system, and it is dif ﬁcult to simulate a system that
contains all these different states simultaneously. Discretization can
signiﬁcantly simplify this problem and break the limitation. When
simulating a system, the input variables are divided into several
dimensions, and each dimension is further discretized based on
which the entire solution space can be represented through sam-
pling. In this study, two types of computer simulations are executed:
the LF ﬁnite element (LFFE) model and the HF ﬁnite element
(HFFE) model. In this study, the LFFE model is built by three-
dimensional (3D) beam element, while the HFFE model is built
by the tetrahedral element. The key to building the digital twin is
to take advantage of both the LFFE and HFFE models to improve
computational ef ﬁciency while meeting certain accuracy
requirements.
2.2 Multi ﬁdelity Surrogate Model. Surrogate models can be
classiﬁed into four types: single-ﬁdelity, hybrid, adaptive sampling-
based, and the MFS models. For the construction of a lightweight
digital twin, the MFS models with three features, lightweight,
high accuracy, and high robustness, are more suitable. Here, “light-
weight” means computationally inexpensive, which takes up less
storage space. Among the three features, lightweight is the most
important one as it can guarantee the real-time calculation of the
digital twin. Besides, both accuracy and robustness are considered.
To ﬁnd an MFS model appropriate to build the lightweight digital
twin, several commonly used MFS models have been studied,
including co-Kriging [ 30], MFS-radial basic function (MFS-RBF)
[31], MFS-moving least-square (MFS-MLS) [32], and linear regres-
sion MFS (LR-MFS) models [ 33]. The lightweight, accuracy, and
robustness of these models were compared with each other compre-
hensively (see Sec. 3.1).
2.3 Sensor Data. With multiple sensors, the states of physical
entities can be dynamically transformed into the digital space, indi-
cating that the sensor data are used as the inputs of the MFS model.
Taking these sensor data as dynamic inputs in this way, the struc-
tural performance of the system can be predicted in real time
once the MFS model is built. The locations where the sensors

3 Implementation of the Lightweight Digital Twin of
the Crane Boom
The framework of the MFS model-based lightweight digital twin
for structural safety monitoring is depicted in Fig. 1. In the physical
space, the main object is a small-scale boom crane with two types of
sensors. The two-dimensional inclination sensor is used to obtain
the degree of the luf ﬁng angle of the crane boom. The load cells
installed on the footplates are employed to check the mass of the
lifting load. In the digital space, the combination of the ﬁnite
element model and the MFS model is adopted to analyze and
predict the structural performance of the crane boom. The other
components of the boom crane are regarded as the rigid body. To
connect the physical space with the digital space, the controller
area network and WebSocket are used to build the communication
module of the digital twin.

MFS model used for building the digital twin. Table 1 presents the

the MFS-RBF model is less than that of the other three MFS
models. The difference in the occupation of storage space will be
more obvious when the models become complex. The duration of

However, the elapsed time during the training process does not inﬂu-
ence the computational efﬁciency since only the prediction process is
performed in the lightweight digital twin. Thus, a model, which com-
pletes its prediction process in a short time (<0.05 s), is more suitable
for the lightweight digital twin. Therefore, the MFS-RBF model is
relatively the most suitable model for the construction of a light-
weight digital twin. The detailed building process of MFS-RBF
models can be found in our previous work [ 31].

4 Results and Analysis
4.1 Accuracy Veri ﬁcation. The 18 test points shown in
Fig. 2(b) are used to evaluate the accuracy of three models, includ-
ing the single- ﬁdelity RBF model trained by HF and LF samples,
respectively, and the MFS-RBF model. Table 2 shows that the
MFS-RBF model presents a higher accuracy than the single- ﬁdelity
RBF models. “Imp.” denotes the accuracy improvement of the
MFS-RBF model over the RBF model trained by HF samples. It
can be seen that most R2s of the LF RBF model are lower than
that of the HF RBF model because the prediction of the LF RBF
model is typically not numerically equivalent to the real value but
only provides a trend, while the HF RBF model can provide more
accurate prediction near the HF samples. Thus, the purpose of the
MFS-RBF model is to add the information provided by the LF
RBF to the HF RBF to improve the accuracy in the area far away
from HF samples.
4.2 Uncertainty Analysis. Note that the MFS-RBF model and
FEM used in the lightweight digital twin are set to be deterministic.
Thus, only the uncertainty from the physical space (i.e., sensors and
other factors) is analyzed. The stress and deformation can be pre-
dicted by leveraging the measured data as inputs; thus, the uncer-
tainty in the prediction can be obtained. The uncertainty from the
physical space needs to be quanti ﬁed during the operation
process. Thus, we consider evaluating the prior estimate of the
sensor data based on the data obtained during the running stage.

A speci ﬁc operation process shown in Fig. 3(a) is repeated 140
times in almost the same trajectory during which seven loads of dif-
ferent masses are lifted, and each type of load is lifted 20 times
repeatedly. Correspondingly, 140 groups of stress and displacement
data are generated, respectively. At each sampling point, we model
the uncertainty using the Gaussian distribution and estimate the
parameters in the distribution by the sensor data and predicted
value (i.e., stress or deformation). Consequently, two RBF
models are built to obtain the relationship between means and stan-
dard deviations of the load and the luf ﬁng angle, respectively. In
addition, two RBF models are used to establish the relationship
between the means of sensors (a two-dimensional data composed
of load and luf ﬁng angle) and the standard deviations of the stress
and the deformation, respectively. The 95% credible intervals
(light gray band) shown in Figs. 3(b)–3(d ) achieve good uncertainty
quantiﬁcation in both the static and operating stages of the boom.
By considering the unknown uncertainty caused by the operation,
the credible interval can be adaptable so that a more reasonable esti-
mation of the uncertainty can be provided.
5 Discussion and Conclusions
This article presents an efﬁcient way to build a lightweight digital
twin of a crane boom for structural safety monitoring based on the
MFS model. Different from the previous method, which only uses
numerical methods or single- ﬁdelity surrogate models to build
digital twins, the MFS model-based lightweight digital twin com-
bines their advantages while eliminating their shortcomings.
Taking the MFS-RBF model as an example, we proved that the pro-
posed digital twin can improve the accuracy of digital twins built by
single-ﬁdelity surrogate models and reduce the computational costs
of digital twins built by numerical methods. In addition, the uncer-
tainty from the physical space (i.e., sensors and other factors) was ana-
lyzed to quantify the uncertainty of the lightweight digital twin. The
effectiveness of the proposed digital twin was validated by applying it
on a lab crane boom. In the MFS model-based digital twin, the single-
ﬁdelity LF surrogate model trained by considerable LF data are
approximately regarded as the mechanistic model over the entire solu-
tion space, while the single- ﬁdelity HF surrogate model built by a
small amount of HF data is employed as the statistical model. There-
fore, the MFS model can integrate the advantages of the mechanistic
model that can provide a causal relationship in structural analysis
and the characteristics of statistical models that are easy to build
and less costly. Thus, the MFS model-based lightweight digital
twin is reliable and computationally affordable, enabling real-time
structural safety monitoring. Moreover, the MFS model-based light-
weight digital twin is not only suitable for mechanical structures,
equipment, and systems but also can be extended to other ﬁelds and
industries, such as biomechanics and ﬂuid mechanics.
This work was limited to predicting the structural performance of
a linear elastic material, and the structural analysis is only restricted
to the elastic stage. Future work should, therefore, be designed to
explore more complex physical processes, such as nonlinear mate-
rials, plastic deformation, and multiphysics coupling because a
more complex model can more closely re ﬂect complex physical
processes in real life and can provide a more realistic digital twin.
