ABSTRACT
As the service life of some buildings increases, some safety hazards may seriously endanger public safety and
cause economic losses. Defect detection and management are necessary steps to eliminate potential safety
hazards. At present, the inspection of building exterior walls mostly relies on manual inspection, which is highly
subjective, risky and has low inspection accuracy. To this end, this study developed an algorithm based on the
infrared and point cloud data fusion model of the Point Cloud Reconstruction Network (PCRNet) to increase the
depth information of the model and improve its understanding of complex scenes. For the anomaly detection of
regular and irregular geometric envelope structures, an automatic anomaly detection algorithm model of geo-
metric envelope structures based on convolutional neural network (CNN) is proposed. By comparing the
extracted temperature information with the labeled data in the real abnormal data set, the detection accuracy,
recall rate and accuracy of the evaluation model have been improved. Finally, a reverse digital twin mapping
method based on the abnormal information of spatial geometric envelope structure of infrared point clouds is
proposed, which is convenient for systematic and object-oriented management. The experimental results show
that among 299 experimental data points, single-data anomaly detection can be completed quickly. The average
accuracy rate of various hazard detection is 92%, the Precision is 96.3 %, the Recall Rate is 94 %, the F1-Score is
95.1 %, and the average detection speed is 5.21 s. It only takes 26 min to complete the building anomaly
detection.
1. Introduction
With the rapid development of the social economy, the construction
industry is also booming, and people ’ s demand for the functionality and
aesthetics of buildings is growing day by day. However, with the in-
crease in the service life of the building, hidden security risks are
gradually exposed, such as cracks and external walls falling off, and
other problems may seriously threaten public safety and cause huge
economic losses[ 1 , 2 ]. Therefore, it is crucial to conduct safety in-
spections and manage external wall defects promptly to prevent po-
tential safety hazards, casualties, and economic losses. Currently, the
majority of building envelope inspections are manual, requiring in-
spectors to work at heights and manually identify defects [ 3 ]. This
manual inspection method has high subjectivity, high risk, and low
precision, resulting in low efficiency in building inspection and main-
tenance. Using visible spectrum images to create 3D models may not
detect all damage. In addition, certain building areas are difficult to
reach, such as the roofs and facades of irregular high-rise buildings. To
make manual inspection safer and cheaper, to look into and learn more
about more things, and to solve the problem of finding and managing
defects in buildings with odd conditions, we need to create a highly
automated method based on spatial geometry envelope. The possibility
of combining heat loss detection with unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs)
simplifies the inspection of hard-to-access or dangerous areas [ 4 ]. It is a
common detection method to use a thermal imager to capture data
remotely.
At present, infrared thermography (IRT) has been employed in
diagnostic applications across various scientific domains, such as

2. Background and related studies
2.1. Infrared thermal imaging detection
IRT is a non-contact, nondestructive evaluation NDE technology that
allows the measurement of the energy emitted by the object under test
(OUT) in the infrared spectrum. Balaras et al. [ 20 ] demonstrated that the
surface emissivity of the OUT can produce an infrared image known as a
heat map, highlighting anomalies in the temperature distribution that
identify faulty parts and damaged structural parts. Kylili et al.[ 8 ]intro-
duce pre-nondestructive testing (NDT) for building diagnosis using
passive, active pulse, and active locking thermal imagers, emphasizing
that while IRT is a useful tool for characterizing defects in the con-
struction field, there is great promise for developing more advanced,
efficient, and accurate methods that would employ a combination of
thermal imaging methods. Feng et al.[ 21 ] propose a portable automatic
detection system for quantitative corrosion detection of the replaced
steel wire. Based on the discovery of the surface temperature charac-
teristics of the corroded steel wire after heating, the automatic detection
device can be upgraded to achieve quantitative detection of in-situ
corrosion of the bridge cable.

2.2. Three-dimensional point cloud technology
In the technical processing of 3D point cloud, current mainstream
research combines the point cloud with other technologies, such as BIM,
infrared imaging, and 3DMAX, to complete the 3D reconstruction of the
target building. At the same time, a large part of the point cloud pro-
cessing methods, such as point cloud noise reduction filtering[ 26 ], point
cloud feature extraction[ 27 ], point cloud registration [ 28 ], and other
algorithms, are also studied. In order to improve the processing effi-
ciency of point cloud data. Tim Kaiser et al.[ 29 ] extracted 3D line seg-
ments from image data and associated them with boundary surfaces of
architectural models to propose a highly automated point-cloud regis-
tration algorithm for photographic measurement that does not require
the use of control points. Truong-Hong et al.[ 30 ] studied the extraction
of structural components of concrete buildings from laser scanning point
clouds at construction sites and proposed a method for automatically
extracting surface point clouds of structural components of concrete
buildings, which could be used for construction quality detection and
defect inspection based on surface geometry information. Min-Koo Kim
et al.[ 31 ] developed a technique based on laser scanning to automati-
cally evaluate reinforced concrete units, including steel bar spacing and
concrete cover relative to formwork, by studying the automatic assess-
ment of reinforced concrete member formwork and steel bar size qual-
ity. Dong et al.[ 32 ] a three-dimensional point cloud enhancement and
segmentation system for potholes is proposed. The system is made up of
a 3DGAN transformer fused with a 3D convolution network called Conv-
Trans-3DGAN and a 3D segmentation network fused with a 3DGAN
transformer named Conv-Trans-3DSeg. Through ablation comparison
experiments, accuracy and F1 scores went up by 4.733 % and 3.03 %,
respectively.

tion, building monitoring, object surface defect detection and other
fields[ 33 – 35 ]. The core principle is to obtain the heat distribution map
of the object surface by infrared sensor, and then combine the three-
dimensional point cloud data for defect analysis and positioning.

2.4. Research based on deep learning and BIM-based management
In recent years, deep learning has achieved outstanding results in
many fields of research, such as healthcare, machinery, and finance.
[ 42 – 44 ] Moreover, deep learning has the advantages of strong learning
ability, wide coverage, strong adaptability, and good portability in
construction engineering image detection, especially in the automatic
inspection of buildings, combining 3D point cloud and infrared thermal
imaging technology, and training data based on deep learning methods
to establish an automated inspection system. It has great application
prospects in the management of building operations and maintenance
stages. Omar et al.[ 12 ] proposed that the application of the IRT and K-
means clustering algorithms showed good results in the automatic
detection and classification of layered defects of concrete bridge floors.
Sang et al.[ 45 ] Deep learning and convolutional neural network
methods for damage identification. Some advantages of these methods
include noise elasticity while extracting features that represent damage
during training without the need for pre-processing and post-processing
steps[ 46 ]. For example, some scholars have proposed that deep learning
and neural networks in IRT have been used to classify damage by
analyzing the cooling curves of two stainless steel plates with air, oil,
and water penetration[ 47 , 48 ]. For engineering applications, Ali et al.
[ 49 ] proposed a deep learning-based method for detecting underground
damage of steel truss bridges from infrared images and showed an
overall accuracy of about 98 % in detecting defects compared to ultra-
sonic pulse velocity testing.

3. Methodology
In this study, the infrared thermal image and point cloud data of the
building were obtained by UAV in the data collection stage, and pre-
liminary positioning was carried out. Then, in the construction stage of
detection framework, infrared image resolution is improved by fusion
with RGB image, anomaly detection based on CNN is developed, and
point cloud data is fused to obtain more accurate detection results. Next,
the digital twin reverse mapping technology is used to improve the
abnormal diagnosis, maintenance and management of building struc-
tures. The overall technical flow chart of this study is shown in Fig. 1 .
This section will explain the proposed data fusion model based on
PCRNet, anomaly detection model based on CNN and digital twin
reverse mapping technology respectively.
3.1. Construct data fusion model based on PCRNet
PCRNet network is a kind of fully connected neural network struc-
ture with strong nonlinear mapping ability. As a deep learning model
integrating point cloud data and RGB image data, it shows superior
performance in object recognition and scene understanding, and reveals
the possibility of building a fusion model of infrared data and point
cloud data. In 3D reconstruction tasks, network structures such as
PointNet are used to process point cloud data, enabling the alignment
and blending of prediction results with the original image. In addition,
more complex network structures and loss functions can be used to
further enhance the fusion effect. PCRNet is divided into two parts, the
first part is basically equivalent to the PointNet feature extraction
structure, which is composed of several Multilayer Perceptron (MLP),
and the data dimension is transformed into (64,64,64,128,1024). In the
end, each point is transformed into a feature vector of 1024 dimensions,
and then the maximum pool is done, and the maximum value is taken for
each dimension, so that the point cloud of n*3 becomes a feature vector
of 1*1024. The registration input is two point clouds, so you get two
vectors of 1024 dimensions, and when you combine the two, you get a
vector of 2048 dimensions. At this point, the PointNet-based coding
process is complete.
In this study, image data is used to assist the processing of point
cloud data, and local feature integration module (LFA) is added to the
subsequent point cloud feature extraction for local feature extraction.
The following sections describe the PCRNet model in detail.
3.1.1. Pcrnet data fusion model
In this study, an infrared camera was used as a source of raw infrared
images and point cloud data. Prior to this, this study first converted
infrared images into point cloud data, which was then input into the
PCRNet network along with the point cloud data. Fig. 2 shows the basic
flow of infrared point cloud data fusion based on PCRNet proposed by
this research.
The overall framework of the PCRNet network model is shown in
Fig. 3 . The overall structure of this network model consists of a feature
extraction module, a feature fusion module and a pose regression
module. In the feature extraction module, this model uses PointNet to
extract global information from the input target point cloud and source
point cloud respectively. That is, it uses 5 layers of MLP to perform
feature abstraction and extraction on the input point cloud, and then
performs dimensionality reduction operations on the point cloud fea-
tures abstracted to the high-dimensional space through 1 layer of Max
pooling, thereby ensuring that the order of the input point cloud remains
unchanged. In the feature fusion module, two 1 × 1024-dimensional
global features are concatenated into one 1 × 2048-dimensional feature.
In the pose regression module, this model uses fully connected layers of
different dimensions to predict relative poses and obtain rotation and
translation amounts. The fully connected layer has a total of 5 hidden
layers, with dimensions of 1024, 1024, 512, 512, and 256 respectively.

In this study, the model adopts two convolution layers, two pooling
layers, and one fully connected layer to realize the fusion of infrared
image and point cloud data. Fig. 5 illustrates the construction of the
module ’ s registration process using the PCRNet model. The model
registered the transformed point cloud data with the field data collected
through several iterations.
In the proposed PCRNet fusion model, infrared and point cloud data
are defined as (64,64,3) RGB images as input data. There are 64 filters in
the Infrared Data Convolution layer (Conv2D), whose convolution
kernel size is defined as 3 × 3, and the ReLU activation function is used.
The output of the first convolution layer is processed by the second
convolution layer. The point cloud data processing layer is the same as
the infrared data processing layer, and the point cloud data is processed
through two convolution layers to extract the features of the point cloud
data. Table 1 is PCRNet data fusion model brief code.
Two pooling layers are used to downsample the input infrared point
cloud feature map to reduce the resolution of each feature map and
effectively reduce the computation and storage requirements. Finally,
the features of infrared and point cloud data are combined to form a
larger feature vector. Through a fully connected layer, the number of
neurons in this study was set to 128 and the ReLU function was used. In
the data output phase, the sigmoid function is used to perform binary
classification tasks. Finally, the PCRNet model performs data fusion by
combining infrared and point cloud data features. Fig. 6 shows the 3D
thermal image output after fusion of infrared thermal image and point
cloud data.
3.2. Anomaly DetectionAlgorithm based on CNN
The anomaly automatic detection model based on CNN proposed in
this study is used for anomaly detection of regular and irregular geo-
metric envelope structures. By extracting temperature information and
comparing it with the labels in the real abnormal data set, the detection
accuracy, recall rate and precision of the evaluation model can be
improved. The network architecture consists of five parts: input layer,
convolutional layer, pooling layer, activation function layer, and full
connection layer. Fig. 7 illustrates the specific network architecture.
When analyzing image and video data, the simple pre-processed image
and video data can be directly input into the convolutional neural
network, and then the required features are extracted through the
convolutional pooling process. The feature extraction results pass
through the fully connected layer to obtain the final classification re-
sults. The essence of this process lies in the automatic learning of hier-
archical features within images through convolution operations,
progressing from low-level edge and texture characteristics to high-level
semantic attributes (such as “ hot spots ” and “ regions with abnormal
temperature gradients ” ). Ultimately, it aims to classify each region as
either “ normal ” or “ abnormal. ” .
The first layer of convolution in the model will learn simple tem-
perature gradient edges, while deeper convolutions will combine these
basic features into more complex patterns, such as “ a small high-
temperature rectangular area ” (corresponding to the outdoor unit of
an air conditioner) or “ a linear high-temperature band ” (corresponding
to a structural thermal bridge). The input data is compressed into low
dimensional space by convolutional layer and pooling layer, and the key
features of the data are extracted. Input data is reconstructed through
up-sampling and convolution layers. The goal is to reconstruct normal
data as closely as possible to the input. For normal data, the model can
reconstruct the input image well. For abnormal data, the reconstruction
error is large. Therefore, the image recognition result is classified by
Softmax function to determine whether it is abnormal. Eq. (5) , (6) is the

3.3. Reverse digital twin mapping
This study adopted a deep learning-based defect detection method to
identify defects and extract their features from UAV images. The region
convolutional neural network (R-CNN) developed based on an object
instance segmentation framework is called Mask R-CNN. The abnormal
defect marker pickup is mapped into the Digital Twin model using Mask
R-CNN, including defect type, location, and quantity. Fig. 8 illustrates
the workflow of Mask R-CNN. The workflow of mask R-CNN is similar to
Faster R-CNN. It first extracts image features through multiple CNN
layers, then trains a region proposal network (RPN) to generate a region
proposal, and then generates a region of interest (RoI). The primary
distinction lies in the addition of a branch to the Mask R-CNN, which
predicts the split mask for each RoI in a manner similar to the current
classification and bounding box regression processes. The mask R-CNN
identifies each defect detection result with a bounding box that indicates
its type and general location, reflecting its characteristics. Finally, the
Digital Twin model integrates the identified defect data after coordinate
transformation.

4. Case study
4.1. UAV data acquisition
In this study, Yinwu Building of Huaqiao University in Xiamen City,
Fujian Province was selected as an experimental case to verify the
anomaly detection algorithm of this study. The building envelope
structure in this case includes: spherical, curved and other irregular
structures, and vertical rectangular regular structures, as shown in
Fig. 10 . To verify whether the PCRNet infrared point cloud data fusion
model and CNN anomaly detection model proposed in this study have
good detection efficiency and robustness.
In this case, DJI M600 UAV was used for point cloud data acquisition,
and the acquisition time was 14:00 – 18:00 with sufficient sunshine. The
DJI Pilot set the flight path of the drone and flew five routes from
different angles. As shown in Fig. 11 , the drone fires vertically down and
tilts at an Angle (left or right) toward the center of the measured area.
The flight time of each route is about 7 min and 30 s. After flying 5
routes, all the construction site information is collected and the real
three-dimensional point cloud model is generated, which takes about 37
min.
At the same time, the M600 is equipped with an infrared thermal
imager ZENMUSE XT2 to fully scan the building to obtain its infrared
thermal imaging data, and ensure that the data covers all parts of the
building, including the exterior walls, roofs, Windows, etc., and is
collected at different times in different weather conditions to obtain data
under different light and temperature. Fig. 12 shows DJI UAV M600 and

4.2. UAV data processing and fusion
4.2.1. Transformation and processing of point cloud data
The processed oblique photography point cloud data is imported into
DJI Terra software. To perform the import operation, navigate to the
“File” menu bar, select “Import Data”, and then select the folder con-
taining the imported tilt photography point cloud data. After the data is
imported, it is preprocessed by DJI Terra, including data correction,
registration, and ghosting removal. DJI Terra then carries out feature
extraction and matching, extracting feature points from oblique photo-
graphic images and using these points for image registration and
matching. Following feature matching and registration, we convert the
matched pixel points into three-dimensional points based on the geo-
metric information of the image and camera parameters. The converted
point cloud data is shown in Fig. 14.
After building the point cloud model, CloudCompare software
removes point clouds irrelevant to the case and reduces the number of
points in the point cloud model through uniform filtering. The point
cloud simplification is quickly completed by CloudCompare, as shown in
Fig. 15.
4.2.2. Infrared image Mass processing
Noise reduction of infrared data is carried out by Gaussian filtering
method. Gaussian filter can effectively eliminate Gaussian noise ac-
cording to the features of the image, and retain the edge features of the
image. The infrared image data after processing in this way can more
clearly show the details and features of the image, and provide a better
basis for the subsequent data processing. Fig. 16 shows the infrared
image after Gaussian filtering.
4.2.3. Data fusion
The PCRNet data fusion model proposed in this study fuses infrared
image with point cloud model. First, the features in the original infrared
image are extracted, and the features in the point cloud data are
extracted and fused by combining the features of the infrared and point
cloud. The fused data is shown in Fig. 17.

5. Conclusion and future studies
In this study, the application of infrared point cloud fusion tech-
nology, deep learning, and BIM management in the defect detection of
spatial geometric envelope structure is discussed in depth. In this paper,
we propose a data fusion model based on PCRNet and introduce the PCA
module of the Adam activation function, which makes the model
training speed up and has stronger robustness.
An algorithm model based on CNN was established to detect the
anomalies of regular and irregular geometric enclosures. UAV collected
the infrared heat map and point cloud data of the building envelope,
then processed the data through noise reduction, segmentation, and
three-dimensional reconstruction. This allowed for the extraction of
temperature information and its comparison with the marks in the real
abnormal data set, thereby reducing human resource consumption and
enhancing detection efficiency. After experimental verification, the
developed anomaly detection algorithm can quickly complete the
anomaly detection for a single set of data; the average accuracy rate of
other hidden danger detection is 0.92, and the average detection speed is
5.21 s. Additionally, this study conducted anomaly detection by
comparing the surface temperature of the outer wall envelope structure,
ensuring that the data collection time remained consistent and unaf-
fected by weather, orientation, or time constraints.
This research proposed a general framework for defect detection and
management of building envelope structures based on the combination
of infrared thermal imaging and point cloud, which can carry out sys-
tematic and intuitive data management in a digital twin environment.
Enable users to visualize, query, and analyze temperature with reference
to 3D spatial information, thereby speeding up the building diagnosis
and modeling process. Provides visual interaction for data users.
However, the dataset of this study is relatively simple, with only the
content of the dataset designed for a single case, which has certain
limitations for research in other industries. Future research can collect
building data from different climate zones, various building types (such
as stadiums and airport terminals), different materials, and different
degrees of degradation to construct a large-scale and diverse benchmark
dataset, in order to train more universal and powerful models. In addi-
tion, current research mainly focuses on the detection of thermal defects
(such as thermal Bridges and damage to insulation layers), and has
limited recognition capabilities for other types of defects (such as fine
cracks, surface weathering, and mold growth). Future research can
conduct multi-modal fusion of various sensing data such as visible light,
infrared, and hyperspectral data. Realize the leap from “ ingle thermal
anomaly detection ” to “ comprehensive building pathological
diagnosis ” .
Finally, the current data processing, analysis and mapping processes
still require certain manual intervention and have not yet formed a fully
end-to-end automated closed loop. We hope to deploy the algorithm on
the cloud platform in future research. The data collected by the drones
can be transmitted to the cloud in real time for processing and analysis,
and the results can be fed back to users in near real time through the 5G
network. This can greatly shorten the cycle from detection to decision-
making. In the future, it is possible to explore the introduction of large
language models to develop operation and maintenance decision-
making agents. These agents can not only detect and label defects but
also automatically analyze the causes of defects, assess risk levels, and
even automatically estimate maintenance costs and construction pe-
riods, providing managers with deeper decision support.
