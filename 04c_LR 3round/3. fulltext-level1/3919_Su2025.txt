The construction phases of bridges involve a wide range of elements and tasks, generating a vast amount of data in 
terms of type and volume. Traditional manual and document-based methods are no longer sufficient for effective and 
accurate project management. Consequently, more comprehensive and systematic management approaches are 
required. This study proposes a three-dimensional digital twin information display platform that links with the actual 
state of the structure, facilitating bridge construction monitoring and safety warning through the integration of digital 
twin technology with multi-source monitoring information. A dynamic multi-resolution digital twin model for bridge 
construction has been established with a bridge digital model and structural finite element model. Then a structural 
evaluation and warning method focusing on key stress and deformation indicators has been comprehensively formu-
lated. A comprehensive digital twin information monitoring and management system platform has been developed for 
bridge construction, complete with a feedback mechanism for warning information. The experimental case application 
in the Zhanlin Yellow River Bridge construction process demonstrates that this method enables flexible management of 
the construction process and dynamic evaluation of safety status. These advancements contribute to ensuring bridge 
construction safety and provide administrators with a digital, real-time online management platform.  
Keywords: bridges/construction/digital twin/health & safety/monitoring 
1. Introduction  
With the continuous development of the socioeconomic, the total 
volume of road transportation is increasingly being climbed, and 
the issue of overloading vehicles is becoming more prominent 
(Gomez and Vassallo, 2015; Li et al., 2020; Lv et al., 2014). In 
addition to 
design and construction defects, improper operation 
and maintenance, as well as material performance deterioration 
and many other factors, a large number of bridges in service are in 
a state of “working with disease,” which poses serious safety haz -
ards (Feng et al., 2023). The increasingly critical situation of the 
operational safety 
of bridges has led the international community 
to recognize the importance of adopting more systematic and tar -
geted technical measures during the service life of bridges in order 
to respond more effectively to the growing challenges of bridge 
safety (Han et al ., 2018
 ; Jeon et al., 2023; Somodikova et al.,
2016
 
; Zacchei et al., 2022; Deng et al., 2020). Therefore, the safe 
service of 
the existing bridges will be the main direction of bridge 
engineering in the future, and at the same time, it is urgent to vigo-
rously develop new bridge intelligent operation and maintenance 
technologies such as intelligent detection, monitoring, evaluation, 
and maintenance. 
At present, researchers at domestic and overseas have established
bridge construction 
monitoring and operational maintenance
 
 
systems, which have initially realized the digital collection and

Given this context, digital twin technology emerges as a promising

3. Bridge  digital  twin  model  construction  and  
simultaneous  simulation  
3.1 Bridge digital twin model construction 
To establish a bridge digital twin system platform, it is essential to 
construct a digital bridge model to provide virtual digital assets 
that serve as the central carrier for data integration, presentation, 
and interaction. 
This study utilizes BIM and 3D modeling soft -
ware to initially create the digital bridge model based on funda-
mental bridge design data. The established model encompasses 
the main structure of the bridge, deck slabs, piers, abutments, and 
other components. The modeling logic for the primary bridge 
structures and components is as follows: (1) Overall modeling 
sequence: a) bridge deck system; b) superstructure; c) substruc -
ture; (2) Modeling sequence of bridge deck system parts: a) bridge 
deck pavement, expansion joints; b) parapets, crash walls; c) light-
ing, signs; (3) Modeling sequence of superstructure parts: a) main 
beam; b) hinge joints, wet joints; c) beams, diaphragms; d) bear-
ings; (4) Modeling sequence of substructure parts: a) cover beams, 
table caps; b) pier body or columns, platform and other abutment 
appurtenances; c) pier foundations, bearing platforms and pile 
foundations. 
After completing the creation of all structure and component mod-
els, web-BIM 
model conversion technology is used to convert the 
bridge and its associated models into a standard 3D model format 
(e.g. .glb, .gltf) and model asset files (e.g. JSON/XML data files). 
This process enables the separation of the model from its numeri -
cal data and ensures lightweight processing. Based on this, a web-
based 3D model rendering framework (e.g. three.js, babyon.js) is 
utilized to load and display the bridge model on the web. The 
result of loading the established bridge model on the web is shown 
in Figures 2 and 3.

4. Construction  structure  safety  analysis  and  
warning  method  based  on  digital  twins  
4.1 Structural safety evaluation indicators 
To ensure timely and effective structural safety warnings during 
bridge construction, representative factors must be selected as 
early warning indicators. According to bridge construction stand-
ards, the constructed bridge structure must possess sufficient 
strength, stiffness, and stability. Strength and stiffness are typically 
reflected through indicators such as internal forces and deforma-
tion, which are critical points of focus for monitoring during con-
struction. While stability is generally addressed in the design 
phase with stability checks, in the case of cable-stayed bridge con-
struction, internal forces and deformation indicators should be pri-
oritized for continuous monitoring and evaluation. 
4. 1. 1 Internal  force  indicators  
Internal forces are indicators of the physical and mechanical state 
of a bridge 
structure, relating directly to the strength and stability 
of the structure. In the construction of cable-stayed bridges, inter -
nal force indicators include stresses in the main tower and the 
main girder, with key sections selected for monitoring these 
parameters. 
4. 1.2 Deformation  indicators  
Deformation indicators are crucial for ensuring the safety of 
cable-stayed bridge construction, 
reflecting the stiffness and sta -
bility of the bridge structure. These indicators primarily include: 
the deformation of the main girder and the deformation of the 
main tower. 
For the main girder, deformation must be measured at 
each construction stage and under each working condition to 
ensure that the girder’s elevation remains within controlled limits. 
The deformation of the main tower involves measuring the cross-
sectional dimensions during the construction phase to ensure that 
the tower’s profile remains smooth and within allowable limits. 
After identifying the warning indicators, standardization of these indi-
cators is necessary 
to facilitate the analysis and assessment of the 
bridge’s safety status. In this study, stress and deformation are selected 
as the primary warning indicators. The stress indicator is processed by 
calculating the percentage difference between the theoretical and 
measured values, while the deformation indicator is handled by deter-
mining the difference between the theoretical and measured values.

the quality and reliability of the warning system. This study, in 
conjunction with bridge construction control standards, proposes a 
set of warning criteria that integrates mechanical principles, com-
putational models, and relevant bridge construction standards. The 
criteria are introduced as follows:

5.2 System framework 
This system adopts a service-oriented architecture based on the B/S 
architecture framework, utilizing JA V A programming language and 
incorporating mobile terminal technology. The platform’s overall 
architecture follows a three-tier system, comprising the decision anal-
ysis layer, integrated supervision layer, and field application layer in 
a top-down manner. The comprehensive framework diagram is pre-
sented in Figure 8. 
5.2. 1 Decision  analysis  layer  
The decision analysis layer is built upon the dataset of integrated 
supervision and 
field application, enabling comprehensive data 
analysis across multiple dimensions including objectives, safety, 
risks, schedule, and resources. Meanwhile, the platform generates 
decision analysis content in accordance with relevant technical 
requirements and means through automated processes. The current 
project’s operation status, security status, and progress information 
can be directly queried through this layer, which facilitates the 
provision of a solid decision-making foundation for project 
managers. 
5.2.2 Integrated  supervisory  layer  
The integrated supervisory layer aims to establish a mature data 
standard and 
uniform data interface for the project. The data infor-
mation is structured based on various business modules within the 
application layer, and it is uploaded through the pre-defined inter-
face before being aggregated into the supervision layer. 
Simultaneously, serving as the intermediary layer, this level also 
transmits data information to the decision analysis level through a 
standardized data interface.

5.3 Functional module design 
The platform is primarily designed to effectively manage, visu-
ally present, comprehensively analyze, and accurately calculate 
the data and structure during the bridge construction phase. 
Digital twin technology is used to connect different objects in 
different periods, virtual data and physical structures, so as to 
meet the series requirements of digital management, visual dis-
play, comprehensive utilization of data, and structural assessment 
and early warning, as shown in Figure 9 . The main structure as 
well as 
the components of the “Human-Machine-Materials-
Methods-Environment” are subject to real-time monitoring, ena -

equipment status (Bao et al., 2018; He et al., 2017; Wang, Li and

1). By integrating comprehensive monitoring informa-
tion, inspection 
data, specialized testing results, environmental 
data, and other multi-source datasets, it achieves real-time three- 
dimensional visualization of bridge structures, surrounding envi -
ronments, components, mechanical and electrical equipment, 
personnel activities, materials usage and other elements, and ena-
bles early warning for structural safety concerns while facilitat-
ing asset management of structures and equipment. 
In the aforementioned process, BIM technology relies on the digital 
model throughout 
the entire cycle to seamlessly integrate information 
from various stages, ensuring comprehensive data preservation. 
Simultaneously, it possesses the temporal label associated with a spe-
cific stage and the spatial label linked to a particular compo-
nent, thereby facilitating vertical data interoperability. The 
digital twin technology ensures real-time feedback of the actual 
state of 
the solid model within the platform, transforming the 
BIM model from a mere isolated display tool into an integrated 
solution that facilitates more accurate structural analysis and 
potential feedback control. 
6. Case  studies  and  results  analysis  
6.1 Case project introduction  
This study uses the construction monitoring of the Zhanhua to 
Linzi Highway Yellow River Bridge in China as a case project. 
The bridge has a total length of 4630 m. The main bridge con-
sists of a cable-stayed steel-mixed composite beam with two 
towers and a double cable plane. The main bridge uses the push-
ing construction method, which poses significant challenges, 
necessitating comprehensive construction monitoring. The mon-
itoring process entails overseeing a multitude of parameters and 
a substantial volume of data. However, conventional methods 
for construction monitoring fail to ensure real-time data acquisi -
tion, continuity, and timeliness. Using the digital twin model for 
construction process monitoring facilitates the visualization of 
on-site conditions. In the established bridge digital twin system, 
the digital twin model of the bridge is provided along with vis -
ual representations of the project progress and beam segment 
classifications, as shown in Figures 10 and 11. Figure 10 illus-
trates the 
interface and digital twin model of the established 
bridge digital twin system, showcasing its interactive functional-
ities and real-time data integration. In Figure 11, the varying 
colors of 
the bridge segments in the model represent different 
structural types, providing a clear visualization of the structural 
composition and facilitating the interpretation of model-specific 
information.

7 . Discussion  and  conclusion  
This study investigates a structural safety early warning assess-
ment and monitoring system for bridge construction processes 
based on digital twin technology. Leveraging digital twin technol-
ogy and considering the specific requirements of the Zhanlin 
Yellow River Bridge project, this research establishes a three-
dimensional digital twin monitoring platform for the bridge. By 
integrating multi-source 
information, the platform links bridge 
data with its actual state, facilitating construction monitoring and 
safety early warnings. Built on the digital twin platform, this 
research ensures seamless integration of data management, safety 
assurance, and operational control throughout the bridge construc-
tion process. It promotes the digitalization of activities such as 
construction, inspection, evaluation, and maintenance, harnessing 
the potential of big data to enhance the informatization and
