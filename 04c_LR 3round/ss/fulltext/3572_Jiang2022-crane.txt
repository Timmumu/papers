ABSTRACT  
Unsafe hoisting behaviors are the fuse of tower crane overturning safety accident. However, the hoisting oper -
ation has a certain degree of randomness and invisibility, and it is extremely dangerous to carry out the 
experiment on the construction site, which makes the research on the relevant regularity very scarce. To simulate 
different hoisting behaviors as realistically as possible and analyze their dynamic effects on the tower crane, a 
digital twin (DT) framework of human – machine interaction construction activities is constructed based on the 
scale model in this study. Through experimental study, the stability change law of the tower crane with the 
incremental dynamic load is obtained based on the collapse probability level function. Experiment results show 
that tilt hoisting is the most dangerous behavior causing the tower crane instability. Tower crane foundation and 
masts are the most vulnerable components to be disturbed by unsafe hoisting behaviors, which can be used as 
key areas for sensor deployment. This paper can support the detection of the dangerous parts of the tower crane 
structure, and the monitoring system design for unsafe hoisting behaviors will be carried out in the future.   
1. Introduction 
The tower crane is a high-rise structure, and its collapse risk is 
extremely significant caused by hoisting error (e.g., tilt hoisting, sudden 
braking, and sudden unloading). Wiethorn conducted a detailed analysis 
of 701 lifting and hoisting accidents that occurred in the past 30 years, 
92.9% of all crane accidents had some type of human intervention. 
Among them, the accident responsibilities of the tower crane driver and 
the rigger both accounted for more than 20% [ 1 ]. In South Korea, Hong 
Kong China and other regions, researchers have found that workers who 
do not comply with the operation procedures are the main cause of 
tower crane accidents [ 2 – 4 ]. 
Analyzing the dynamic effects of tower cranes under unsafe hoisting 
behaviors is a prerequisite for on-site safety management, which means 
that managers can focus more on the weakest structural parts and deploy 
sensors at the most sensitive locations. However, these unsafe hoisting 
behaviors and habits are especially tricky to detect because of their 
indistinct feature in the actual construction. The hoisting excitation ef -
fects on the stability of the machinery itself also cannot be compre -
hensively analyzed. At present, a mainstream solution is to use the 
simulation method, where the external excitation is applied to the model 
as a sequential load by expertise [ 5 ]. However, the simulation results 
always have difficulty reflecting the real operation status accurately 
because of too much boundary hypothesis condition and the randomness 
of driving behaviors. Another way is to design a scale model in which to 
test the real running state. Some studies have proposed the hoisting 
sway control method of tower cranes by the scale model [ 6 ]. In the field 
of manufacturing, the experimentable DT based on the scale model have 
been established to obtain the small sample data and conduct cross-over 
analysis of systems in different fields and extreme condition simulation 
(e.g., working on mars) [ 7 , 8 ]. The experimental method of the scale 
model DT provides a way to analyze the safety risk problem of human- 
machine interaction construction, such as hoisting. 
For the construction process, DT has produced a lot of practical 
value. Boje ’ s research suggested that DT can play an important role in 
construction site monitoring, quality control, safety management, 
scheduling, and visual communication [ 9 ]. Meanwhile, the establish -
ment of a construction DT is even more challenging because most con -
struction activities are the process of human-machine interaction. 
Among them, workers ’ control, command behaviors, and habits will 
greatly affect the safety state of machinery, and the vibration and noise 
generated by machinery will also affect workers ’ emotions and health

2. Related work 
2.1. DT for construction machinery 
DT for construction machinery is to establish the virtual machinery 
prototype (e.g., structural, mechanism, and working condition), where 
real-time operating data will be integrated and analyzed to achieve 
lifecycle optimal management of production design, manufacturing, 
operation, and maintenance [14]. In the design phase, DT can greatly 
reduce the cost of trial and error. As early as 1998, DiMaio proposed the 
concept of excavator virtualization by using virtual environments to 
evaluate and improve excavator performance [15]. In addition, DT- 
driven construction machinery design can integrate expert knowledge, 
real-time working data, and end-user feedback to maximize design co- 
optimization [16]. 
In the manufacturing phase, DT has been used as a management tool 
for flexible production and customized production. At this point, the 
mechanical DT also needs context-related elements such as production 
lines and operators in the factory. For instance, Lim realized the rapid 
reconfiguration of the tower crane product family (Liebherr) through DT 
[17]. Thomas used DT for the upgrade of the intelligent product line of 
excavators [18]. In the operation and maintenance phase, DT provides 
support for preventive maintenance and fault diagnosis of the con -
struction machinery. Zhidchenko analyzed the hydraulic cylinder pres -
sure data of mobile construction machinery based on DT to estimate 
engine fatigue life [19,20]. Moi established a DT of the small-scale 
knuckle boom crane to analyze the partial pressure data for predictive 
maintenance and product life cycle management [21]. Some research 
has also embedded the sequential Markov chain Monte Carlo simulation 
model in DT to optimize the maintenance cycle of construction ma -
chinery [22]. In general, DT has become a part of the smart factory, and 
its research path mainly includes modeling, experiment, analytics, and 
learning [23]. However, a research gap still needs to be filled, that is, DT 
for machinery in the construction site. 
Compared with DT in the design and manufacturing phase, that in 
the construction site is more complicated because of the complexity and 
various uncertain factors. Currently, an increasing number of re -
searchers are studying the data acquisition and analysis method of 
construction machinery. On the one hand, some of them use machine 
vision and inertial measurement unit technology to estimate the posture 
of excavators, bulldozers, and cranes [24,25]. Some mechanical and 
electrical sensors are used to collect the data of mechanical operation 
performance. On the other hand, data analysis is a process in which DT 
generates extended value. Sepasgozar combined AR technology and DT 
data of excavators, tower cranes, and shield machines for driving 
training [26]. Deryabina discussed the application of DT in the optimi -
zation of the whole mine mining process of the excavator digging, crane 
hoisting, and loader transportation [27]. However, DT and other infor -
mation technology applications are scarce for construction safety anal -
ysis [28,29], especially for establishing the DT model and conducting 
the data analysis of human-machine interaction operation on the tower 
crane. 
2.2. Tower crane safety monitoring 
Tower cranes are the most common machinery in the construction 
site, and hoisting activities are also a construction link with frequent 
safety accidents and extremely high safety risks. Researchers have 
conducted considerable research on tower crane safety monitoring.  
2.2.1. Anti-collision with surrounding objects. The reason for the collision 
risk is that tower cranes and surrounding obstacles (building structure 
and workers on site) are mutually exclusive in construction space. 
Collision accidents can be avoided if the operation attitude of the tower 
crane and the position of the obstacle can be collected in real time. On 
the one hand, researchers have used ultra-wide band, ZigBee, visual 
tracking, and other methods to locate the three motion trajectories of the 
tower crane, when lifting, rotation, and luffing [30–33]. The distance 
threshold is used to avoid obstacles in different operations of the tower 
crane. On the other hand, ultrasonic sensors, 3D scanners, and multi- 
sensor fusion methods have been used to rapidly reconstruct the work -
ing space of the tower cranes to obtain real-time on-site objects [34–36], 
especially static and dynamic objects on the ground. 
2.2.2. Tower crane hoisting monitoring 
Research on hoisting monitoring is mainly concerned with the health 
status of the tower crane to avoid some structural damage and the risk of 
buckling instability. For instance, Nadar designed a monitoring system 
of the tower crane based on wireless sensor network, which can collect 
the torque data of tower crane jib and conduct early warning of the 
overrun risk [37]. Li proposed a data-driven remote monitoring and 
early-warning system for tower cranes, which embeds a 3D kinematics 
model to provide threshold warning for the critical moment state of the 
structure [38]. Lalik realized the rapid measurement of the mast pres -
sure state by the partial tilt angle of the tower crane body and jib [39]. 
Sayed analyzed the structure health state of the tower crane by estab -
lishing the relationship between its dynamic displacement and vibration 
characteristics [40]. 
2.2.3. Blind driving assistance and training 
A large area of blind observation exists in the driver’s line of sight for 
the larger-scale working space of the tower crane, while signalmen on- 
site are also unable to provide accurate information, thereby causing a 
great safety risk. Thus, researchers have built a global visual driving 
assistance system by various sensors and BIM. Drivers could obtain the 
hook motion and load [41], on-site invisible areas [42,43], and safety

damage, instability, and collapse of tower cranes. At present, effective 
monitoring means are lacking, and the influence of different driving 
behaviors on the stability of tower crane structure needs to be further 
explored. 
2.3. Tower crane modeling and simulation 
Modeling is an effective means to analyze the internal mechanism 
and operation process of construction machinery. The finite element 
method (FEM) is still considered to be the most effective method to 
analyze the structural mechanics and dynamics of the tower crane. 
Rauscher conducted the elastoplastic analysis of tower crane structures 
by using FEM in consideration of the dynamic couplings among steel 
structure, load motion, system drives, and rope [ 50 ]. Wang described 
the damage state and normal state model of the tower crane, which was 
distinguished by regression calculation of displacement data [ 51 ]. The 
scale model is another research method for structural analysis. 
Compared with the numerical model, the physical scale model can test 
the operation and control process with experiment, and it can easily find 
the problems and deviations in different working conditions. Kenan put 
forward the construction method of the tower crane scale model based 
on similarity theory and compared the structural modal analysis of the 
model under the scale factors of 30 and 40 [ 52 ].

pendulum rule and the adaptive swing strategy for tower cranes [ 6 , 54 ]. 
All the abovementioned studies provide a foundation for the DT of the 
tower crane. 
Tower crane simulation is another major research area. Ushio 
analyzed the failure modes of different mast joint types under the 
seismic load based on FEM [ 55 ]. Chen studied the dynamic effects on 
different parts of the tower crane (e.g., cat head, jib, counter-jib, and 
mast) under different total wind loadings from different angles, and the 
evaluation scheme of the total safety of the tower crane was proposed 
[ 56 ]. Voisin modeled the tower crane and its surrounding buildings and 
analyzed the transient effect of the tower crane foundation and auxiliary 
arm under strong wind condition [ 57 ]. Zrinic concluded based on FEM 
mechanical experiments that the failure of the angle brace plate of the 
counter-jib and the low quality of the weld are the main reasons for the 
structural failure of the tower crane [ 58 ]. The simulation results of Cho 
showed that the maximum deformation of tower crane hoisting is likely 
to occur at the jib-end, which needs to be enhanced during design [ 59 ]. 
Obviously, simulation helps engineers understand the performance of 
different parts of the tower crane under different load-up conditions. 
However, the current research is mainly used to support the design 
optimization, and few methods for safety risk performance analysis with 
different hoisting behaviors are available. Milazzo analyzed the 
complexity and risk of human – machine interaction in the construction 
process and believed that intelligent means would be an effective way to 
ensure man – machine interaction through investigation [ 60 ]. 
Overall, the research of tower crane modeling and simulation focuses 
on the structural steady state under environmental load, including wind, 
earthquake, and snow. Nearly no discussion on the stability performance 
of tower cranes under unsafe driving behaviors is available. However, 
construction practice has indicated that human driving behaviors are an 
important cause of safety risk accidents, and related research is

3. DT of tower crane 
Any DT requires two components: the physical and virtual worlds. 
Data flow between them, transform, and generate service value. In the 
construction phase, DT for human – machine interaction activities 
consists of the driver, machinery, and environment. In this system, the 
state of machinery is the core because it directly determines the con -
struction efficiency, safety, and progress. From the perspective of safety, 
this study describes the DT of human-machine interaction construction 
activities, as shown in Fig. 1 . Among them, the construction machinery 
will be built as a virtual model, and the driving and environment data 
collected by sensors will be analyzed on the virtual model as the external 
excitation effect. This data interactive process will provide more accu -
rate analysis services of operational efficiency, energy consumption, and 
operational safety. To comprehensively illustrate the framework, a 
tower crane is taken as the research object to propose the method and 
experiment of the DT for human – machine interaction activities, 
including three parts: tower crane scale model, virtual model, and data 
collection and flow based on IoT.

3.2. Tower crane virtual model 
The virtual model is a virtual testbed for structural dynamic analysis, 
and operation safety analysis of the tower crane, as shown in Fig. 3 . 
From the perspective of the hoisting process, the virtual model is a 
comprehensive synchronization of the actual hoisting conditions of the 
tower crane. Among them, hoisting behaviors (load size and direction), 
mechanical attitude (initial hoisting height, rotation angle, and radius 
change distance), and environment conditions (wind pulsation) act as 
real-time external incentive time series data on the virtual model. The 
virtual model will analyze the structure dynamic response, stability, and 
damage of the tower crane to realize the experiment simulation under 
different hoisting conditions.

model the complex mechanical components. The FEM model is the core 
of dynamic analysis in DT. At this point, the structural components are

3.3. DT dataflow in hoisting operations 
IoT is the foundation of the virtual and real synchronization in the 
DT. According to the hoisting characteristics of the tower crane, col -
lecting mechanical, posture, and environmental data during the opera -
tion is necessary. Among them, the mechanical data reflect the load 
state, local stress, and vibration of the structure. The posture data reflect 
the operation state of the tower crane and indirectly reflect the structure 
form of the tower crane when hoisting, including the position of cir -
cumrotating and radius-changing. Environmental data specifically refer 
to wind load, which is the most easily disturbed load for tower cranes 
and other tall structures. 
In the process of human – machine interaction, the data go through a 
completed closed-loop flow from personnel hoisting, sensor collection, 
dynamic analysis, and danger warning, as shown in Fig. 4 . First, the data 
from different sensors are numbered according to the serial port and 
asked to read data. Then, the parsing command will read analog and 
digital signals, and the key information of different sensors, such as ID, 
data acquisition time, and value, are all stored in the database. Next, the 
interface program of the finite element input file of ABAQUS converts 
the sensor data in the database into effective excitation and loads it on 
the virtual model to realize the dynamic analysis. Finally, the results are 
synchronized to the database, and a structural dynamic threshold under 
conventional operations is set as the warning benchmark. Notably, once 
the real-time analysis result exceeds the threshold, the hoisting behavior 
will be identified as dangerous, and the warning information will be 
displayed on the web interface to remind personnel to correct it. 
4. Tower crane hoisting experiment 
4.1. DT experiment platform 
The tower crane scale model is made and assembled before the 
hoisting experiment based on the similarity theory. Meanwhile, the 
sensor and monitoring system is installed on the scale model simulta -
neously, which covers each component of the tower crane, as shown in 
Fig. 5 . This experiment mainly focuses on the dynamic effect analysis of 
the tower crane under different hoisting behaviors and temporarily ig -
nores the influence of wind load. Therefore, the sensors are mainly 
composed of rotation, radius-changing, jib vibration, hoisting rope load, 
mast body tilt-angle, and foundation stress. Foundation stress data are 
used as the main observation data to compare with the simulation data 
in the experiment. Table 3 shows the sensor parameters in detail. In the 
terms of software, C ++ is used for system development deployed in 
Windows 10 to support real-time data collection and online display on 
the web. Each sensor is programmed according to the industrial 485 
communication protocol. The database is developed by the Navicat 
platform and MYSQL. 
The hoisting operation is monitored in real time based on the DT 
experimental platform. The human – machine interaction interface is 
shown in Fig. 6 , including control platform, monitoring platform, and 
tower crane. Among them, the control platform has two modes of 
manual and automatic. The three-degree-of-freedom control of lifting, 
turning, and luffing can be realized through the touch screen. At the

Taking the data of observation points as an example ( Fig. 5 ), the

time series trend but differ in the data magnitude. This phenomenon is 
due to the difference in the material properties of the structure, but it 
does not affect the analysis of the dynamic effects under different 
hoisting behaviors in this experiment. 
The trend of the data shows that the significant difference in time 
series data is caused by the four different hoisting behaviors. The dy -
namic response caused by the tilt hoisting is larger and the reduction 
cycle is longer than those by the vertical hoisting. Given the obvious 
change in acceleration, the sudden braking produces the obvious tran -
sient effect, which enlarges the original dynamic response amplitude. 
During the process of sudden unloading to loading, the dynamic 
response data show a significant downward and upward trend with the 
changes in hoisting load. Overall, the time series data of dynamic 
response under different unsafe behaviors are obviously different, and 
the influence on the structural stability will also be different. At the same 
time, different data trends also provide the basis for classifying and 
identifying unsafe hoisting behaviors. 
In addition, the equivalent stress cloud of the virtual model can 
intuitively reflect the dynamic response effect of different hoisting be -
haviors when they happened. When tilt hoisting and braking occur, the 
stress of the cross foundation increases significantly, and the effect of the 
tilt hoisting is stronger. When sudden unloading occurs, the stress of the 
foundation decreases significantly.

5.2. Stability discussion of the tower crane 
5.2.1. Stability changes in load incremental steps 
The first thing we can discuss is the stability changes caused by 
different hosting behaviors during the load incremental step process, as 
shown in Table 5 and Fig. 11 . For the TCL, the collapse risk of the tower 
crane gradually slows down after a rapid increase as the load increases. 
This result is caused by plastic deformation in local parts. In the process 
of elastic-plastic deformation, the effect of tilt hoisting and sudden 
braking on the stability is increasingly becoming obvious, and the 
growth and declining rate are also faster. Meanwhile, tilt hoisting is the 
first hoisting condition that produces plastic deformation, which means 
that it is most likely to cause local component instability. The rigidity of 
FCL has been significantly degraded during the incremental process. 
Among them, tilt hoisting has also the greatest effect on the stability of 
the foundation. In addition, the increased amplitude in MCL has also 
changed, which indicates that slight plastic deformation has also 
occurred in the mast part of the tower body. Although JCL has not un -
dergone a change in growth rate, it maintains at a relatively high level. 
By contrast, CCL and RCL are maintained in a relatively stable state, and 
the effect of instability is low. In summary, all unsafe hoisting behaviors, 
especially tilt hoisting and sudden braking will bring obvious influence

5.2.2. Foundation instability evolution under hoisting conditions 
Aiming at the cross foundation where the instability occurs first, we 
can further observe the separation process of the foundation unit under 
different hoisting conditions, as shown in Fig. 12 (stress-section view, 
plastic-total view, and deformation magnification for 50 times). The tilt 
hoisting and sudden braking have already undergone more obvious 
plastic deformation at the incremental Step 135 (Plastic) . From the 
perspective of the instability process, the damage for the tilt hoisting, 
which is the most severely damaged, first appears on the connection 
node of the foundation, especially the node where the side of the jib is 
located. Then, the damage gradually spreads to the two axes of the cross 
foundation. Finally (at step 135), obvious plastic deformation appears at 
the intersection of the foundation, and the deformation on the same side 
of the jib will also be more obvious. The abovementioned phenomenon 
can represent the instability propagation process of a typical hoisting 
behavior. If the same type of instability evolution process (e.g., crack) is 
found in the actual tower crane inspection process, then the engineer 
can concentrate on checking the driver ’ s hoisting behavior during the 
hoisting process.

6. Conclusion 
Human-machine interaction construction activities, especially for 
tower crane hoisting, have a very high safety risk, where unsafe hoisting 
behaviors can easily lead to accidents such as collapse and instability. 
Thus, this study proposes a general framework based on DT for the 
human – machine interaction construction activities. This framework is 
used to analyze the influence of the unsafe hoisting behaviors on the 
stability of the tower crane. In our research, a refined scale model is 
assembled based on the similarity principle, including cross foundation, 
mast, jib, counter-jib, and rope. The virtual model of the tower crane is 
constructed synchronously to cover the components, FEM, and building 
information. The component elements are divided by a reasonable 
strategy to ensure calculation efficiency and accuracy. Meanwhile, a 
data flow for real-time dynamic analysis of the tower crane is proposed 
based on IoT, which realizes the interaction between the virtual and 
physical worlds. 
In the hoisting experiment, the time series effect of load excitation 
with vertical hoisting, tilt hoisting, sudden braking, and sudden 
unloading are summarized. The dynamic response characteristics are 
described under the four hoisting behaviors through the comparison of 
the tower crane scale model sensor data, simulation data, and the real 
tower crane model simulation data. Meanwhile, this study thoroughly 
discusses the effect of unsafe hoisting behaviors on the total and local 
stabilities of the tower crane, constructs the collapse probability func -
tion, and proposes the description methods by TCL, FCL, MCL, JCL, CCL, 
and RCL. The elastoplastic changes of each component under different 
working conditions is discussed based on the IDA method. The results 
show that tilt hoisting is the unsafe behavior that is most likely to cause 
tower crane instability, and the effect gradually becomes larger as the 
load increases. The foundation is the most sensitive part to unsafe 
hoisting behaviors, which can reflect instability characteristics in the 
tower crane monitoring. The research results provide a basic theoretical 
basis for the monitoring of unsafe hoisting behaviors of tower cranes in 
the future. The dynamic response effects of tower cranes under more 
complex environmental and behavioral coupling can be further explored 
in the future research.
