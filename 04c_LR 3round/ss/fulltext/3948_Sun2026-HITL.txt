An Enhanced Digital Twin-Driven Fault
Diagnosis Approach Based on Deep Multimodal
Information Fusion for Marine Machinery
Jiawen Sun
 , Hongxiang Ren
 , Y axin Dong, Boxiang Zhang, Shaoyang Qiu, and Yi Zhou
Abstract—Condition monitoring and fault diagnosis of
marine machinery are essential for ensuring the safety
and stability of offshore operations. Traditional approaches,
which typically rely solely on either physical mechanisms
or large volumes of data, often struggle to achieve both
high accuracy and adaptability to component degradation.
To address this issue, this article proposes a novel digital
twin framework based on deep multimodal information fusion
(DMIF-DT) that integrates the complementary strengths of a
physics-based model (PBM) and a data-driven model (DDM).
An attention-enhanced multiscale convolutional network is
developed to extract features from sensor measurements and calibrated simulation data. A low-rank fusion strategy is
then employed to map the two modalities into a unified high-dimensional space for effective integration between the PBM
and the DDM. Furthermore, a performance-degradation adaptive correction module, based on sensor measurements
and probabilistic distribution features, is used to map health factors and feed them back to the PBM for component-
level correction, thereby supporting periodic model updates. Experimental results show that the proposed DMIF-DT
framework significantly reduces parameter prediction errors and achieves an average fault diagnosis accuracy of 98.02%
under diverse operating conditions. By uniquely integrating a performance-degradation adaptive correction module with
attention-enhanced multimodal fusion, the framework bridges physics-based and data-driven insights, providing a new
approach for the intelligent health management of marine machinery.
Index Terms— Adaptive correction, digital twin (DT), fault diagnosis, marine machinery, multimodal fusion.

Fault detection and diagnosis technology for marine
machinery refers to the capability to identify and localize
faults down to replaceable components or specific subsys-
tems [2]. Timely and reliable diagnosis provides strong
support for optimizing maintenance decisions and extending
the service life of machinery, thereby reducing maintenance
costs and improving system operational efficiency [3]. The
two main techniques for marine machinery fault diagno-
sis are model-based methods and data-driven methods [4].
Model-based methods rely on prior knowledge of the struc-
ture or internal mechanisms of the target system to enable
the representation and analysis of its operating states [5].
Milioulis et al. [6] developed a functional model of a marine
liquefied natural gas (LNG) fuel supply system and identified
critical faults by integrating failure mode, effects, and critical-
ity analysis (FMECA) with fault tree analysis (FTA). In gen-
eral, function-based modeling methods emphasize fault path
reasoning and are well suited for system-level failure analysis.
However, they depend on a predefined fault mode library and
exhibit limitations in identifying performance degradation and

coupled faults. Physics-based models (PBMs) constructed
from first principles are typically more accurate and capable
of effectively capturing the nonlinear operational behavior
of machinery [7]. For instance, Wang et al. [8] developed
a PBM of a two-stroke low-speed marine diesel engine
using MATLAB/Simulink to simulate typical fault scenarios,
thereby supporting fault identification and health assessment.
Benefiting from modular simulation strategies, PBMs offer
strong interpretability. However, one key prerequisite for
implementing PBMs is access to precise component character-
istics, which are often derived under ideal design conditions.
When the actual system deviates from the design point, PBM
reliability and accuracy may decline. Moreover, as the opera-
tional life of ship systems increases, component performance
inevitably degrades, making it difficult for PBMs to maintain
high-fidelity simulation of real-world behavior. Therefore, the
dynamic mismatch between PBMs and the physical sys-
tem remains a significant challenge for current model-based
approaches [9].
On the other hand, data-driven models (DDMs) use col-
lected data and prior experience to extract high-dimensional
features representing machinery health and construct fault
identification models [10]. Numerous studies on DDMs
have focused on modeling marine systems using machine
learning techniques, including ensemble learning [11], sup-
port vector machines [12], extreme learning machines [13],
and various neural network architectures featuring efficient
nonlinear multilayer modeling and adaptability [14], [15].
Although these methods do not require an in-depth under-
standing of the underlying physical mechanisms, they also
present certain limitations. In practice, the imbalance and
incompleteness of measurement data, together with the
scarcity of labeled samples, make it challenging to obtain
high-quality training datasets [16]. Moreover, inevitable noise
and redundancy in the acquired data may distort feature
representations and thus degrade the diagnostic performance
of DDMs. To address these limitations, recent studies have
proposed a variety of more advanced DDMs. For example,
Huang et al. [17] developed a deep adversarial capsule network
(DACN) that combines capsule networks with adversarial
domain generalization for compound fault diagnosis under
unseen conditions. Zhang et al. [18] proposed a self-supervised
graph feature enhancement and scale attention-driven frame-
work (EG-SAGCN) to improve diagnosis with limited labeled
data. In addition, to address the challenges of nonstation-
ary and imbalanced conditions in machinery fault diagnosis,
Xu et al. [19] designed a domain-constrained cascadic multire-
ceptive learning network (DC-CMLN) integrating multiscale
feature extraction and domain-constrained label adjustment.
Li et al. [20] presented a sensor-aware capsule neural net-
work that integrates interpretable multisensory fusion with
uncertainty-aware estimation for reliable remaining useful life
prediction. The above advanced DDMs have demonstrated
promising results in industrial prognostics and health manage-
ment (PHM) tasks, providing valuable insights for developing
more robust and generalizable diagnostic frameworks. How-
ever, their application in marine machinery fault diagnosis still
faces challenges, including the limited exploitation of physical
knowledge and inadequate adaptability to performance degra-
dation.
Since the concept of the digital twin (DT) was proposed,
it has attracted widespread interest [21]. As an innovative
approach, the DT enables high-fidelity system modeling and
supports condition awareness and management throughout the
entire equipment lifecycle, which provides a more effective
solution for fault prediction and health management of marine
machinery. The core focus of the DT lies in the interaction
between the physical and virtual spaces [22]. Multisource
information from complex systems—such as sensor data and
simulation model outputs—is integrated and transmitted to
the virtual space to construct the DT model. Subsequently,
the DT model enables functions such as condition monitoring
and fault diagnosis. Finally, feedback information is sent back
to the physical space to support optimization and decision-
making.
As mentioned above, PBMs and DDMs represent two dis-
tinct paradigms in marine machinery modeling—mechanism-
based and data-driven models, respectively. One of the key
challenges in constructing DT systems is the effective integra-
tion of these two approaches [23]. Currently, DT applications
in marine machinery remain in an early stage of development,
and research on model construction is still relatively limited.
Hautala et al. [24] developed a DT system for marine engines
based on a 1-D thermodynamic model, enhancing real-time
simulation capability through optimization and simplification
strategies. However, this study did not consider degradation
adaptive modeling, suggesting that the DT model may have
difficulty adapting when component performance declines due
to wear, corrosion, or other aging effects during vessel opera-
tion. To address this issue, Jeon and Theotokatos [9] proposed
a trustworthiness assurance framework for marine engine DTs
that incorporates residual analysis and credibility evaluation to
mitigate model mismatches arising from degradation, thereby
improving the engineering adaptability of the DT system.
Li and Brennan [25] proposed a DT framework for virtual
maintenance of complex marine equipment, integrating struc-
tural modeling with operational data monitoring for predictive
maintenance and fault warning. Similarly, Mousavi et al. [26]
presented a DT framework combining finite element-based
physical modeling with Bayesian uncertainty reasoning to
detect damage and update system states under uncertain
loading conditions. These methods further underscore the
important role of DDMs in DT model development. Notably,
DDMs are proficient in learning patterns and trends from
data, making them particularly advantageous for modeling
component degradation [27]. However, for complex marine
machinery such as ship engines and propeller propulsion
systems, DT models that effectively integrate PBMs and
DDMs remain relatively underexplored in fault diagnosis and
health management applications, especially when performance
degradation is considered.
Similar to how humans perceive the external environment
through multiple sensory channels, such as vision and hearing,
multimodal learning can integrate multisource heterogeneous
information from complex systems by constructing joint repre-
sentations [28]. In DT systems, the PBM typically offers high

Given the above, a novel DT approach based on deep
multimodal information fusion (DMIF-DT) is proposed for
fault diagnosis of marine machinery. Compared with tradi-

the DDM utilizes an attention-enhanced multiscale feature

two modalities are then integrated using a low-rank fusion
strategy to form a joint representation. Furthermore, to allow
the DT model to adapt to component performance degra-
dation, a performance-degradation correction (PDC) module
is introduced. This module adjusts the model using adaptive
factors computed from sensor data and probability distribution
outcomes, enabling better alignment with real-world measure-
ments. Overall, the main contributions of this article are as
follows.
1) A deep multimodal information fusion DT framework
is developed, featuring a feedback-enabled two-way
interaction between the PBM and DDM to achieve more
effective integration.
2) A PDC mechanism, driven by Kullback–Leibler (KL)-

to adaptively refine PBM parameters and maintain
long-term consistency with real operating conditions.
3) An attention-enhanced multiscale feature (MSF) extrac-

effective integration scheme to improve feature dis-
criminability and capture high-order correlations across
modalities.
4) Compared with conventional model-based and data-
driven methods, the proposed DMIF-DT framework
demonstrates higher diagnostic accuracy and robustness
across diverse operating conditions.

A. Establishment of the Marine Engine Simulation Model
As a core system of marine machinery, the operational status
of the marine engine directly affects the vessel’s propulsion
performance and overall efficiency. Therefore, a simulation
model of a marine engine is developed in this study to
serve as the original PBM within the DT framework. The
model is based on a Wartsila 9L34DF dual-fuel, medium-
speed, turbocharged diesel engine, which functions as the
generator prime mover in an electrically propelled ship. The
detailed specifications of the engine are listed in Table I.
Based on these technical specifications, the simulation model
is constructed using GT-SUITE, as shown in Fig. 1. For a
detailed description of the modeling process using GT-SUITE
for marine engines, refer to [30].
As shown in Fig. 1, the original PBM is developed using the
standard component library available in GT-SUITE. Specif-
ically, the heat transfer and combustion processes in the
cylinder (CYL) are modeled using the Woschni equation
and the Wiebe function, respectively. The intake and exhaust
piping systems are simulated using 1-D methods to capture
gas flow characteristics. In addition, the turbocharger (TC)
unit consists of a compressor and a turbine. The compressor is
positioned between the ambient environment and the charge air
cooler (CAC), while the turbine is located between the exhaust
manifold and the exhaust outlet. The input and output ports of
each component are connected according to mass flow balance
principles and the ideal gas law, allowing serial coupling

C. Attention Mechanism
The attention mechanism in deep learning is inspired by the
ability of the human visual system to selectively focus on key
targets or local regions during information processing [32].
In sequence modeling tasks, attention enables the model to
capture the sensitivity of features across multiple dimensions.
Its core idea is to assign importance weights to input elements
so that the model focuses on the most relevant information
for the task. This mechanism not only improves the model’s
ability to process complex data but also enhances its inter-
pretability and computational efficiency [33]. In this study,
the channel-attention module (CAM) and the squeeze-and-
excitation (SE) module are introduced as fundamental building
blocks.

III. M ETHODOLOGY
A. Overall Framework of the Proposed DT
The proposed DT framework for marine machinery com-
prises the PBM, DDM, multimodal fusion, and functional
modules, as illustrated in Fig. 4. This framework aims
to construct a virtual DT model that closely reflects the
actual operating behavior of a marine engine by integrat-
ing mechanism-based modeling with data-driven approaches,
thereby supporting accurate fault diagnosis and degradation
prediction.
The PBM performs simulation modeling by describing
the physical and thermodynamic relationships among inter-
nal engine components, with its output serving as the
mechanism-based modality. To address the inevitable perfor-
mance degradation of engine components, a PDC module is
employed to construct HIs that characterize degradation trends.
These indicators are used to periodically refine key parameters
of the PBM, maintaining the long-term accuracy and reliability
of the simulation model.
The DDM is constructed using sensor data collected during
actual engine operation to uncover latent patterns in the engine
state data. Specifically, an AMSF method is designed to facil-
itate deep feature extraction and interaction. By enhancing the
representation of degradation-related features under varying
operating conditions, the AMSF improves the adaptability and
robustness of the DT framework. Details of the AMSF method
are provided in Section III-C.
A low-rank multimodal fusion (LMF) model is adopted
to integrate PBM and DDM modalities, generating a joint
representation in a high-dimensional feature space. Compared
with single-modality information, the fused representation
provides greater expressive capacity and improved diagnostic
sensitivity. Based on this integrated representation, the DT
performs condition monitoring and fault diagnosis for the
marine engine. Diagnostic results and degradation information
are periodically fed back through the PDC mechanism to refine
PBM parameters.
B. Adaptive PDC for PBM
Performance degradation of marine machinery components
overextended service periods is inevitable. This degradation
causes key parameters to drift from their initial baselines and
reduces PBM accuracy within the DT system. To address this
issue, a PDC module is developed by integrating historical
monitoring data and model knowledge. The module employs
KL divergence to capture distributional differences in repre-
sentative operating conditions across historical voyages. Using
these differences, HIs are constructed to track degradation
trends and enable periodic correction of key PBM components,
thereby maintaining long-term model accuracy.

C. Attention-Enhanced MSF Fusion
In this section, AMSF is developed to enhance the capability
of the DDM in extracting critical fault features. It comprises
an MSF extraction module, an improved CAM (ICAM), and
an SE module based on sliding-window pooling (Sliding-SE).
1) MSF Extraction and Fusion: Effective feature extraction
from diverse fault signals is essential for accurate diagno-
sis [36]. To overcome the limitations of single-scale CNNs,
this study designs an MSF extraction and fusion module.
Specifically, two improvements are introduced to the 1-D
CNN. First, multiple convolutional kernels with different
scales are applied in parallel to extract features from the
input data, providing stronger generalization capabilities com-
pared to single-scale convolution. Second, a cross-level feature
fusion mechanism is incorporated to reduce potential infor-
mation loss in deep networks. The fused features retain more
detailed degradation information of mechanical components
and offer a more comprehensive representation of system
condition variations.

to extract fault-related features at multiple scales, and the
fused results enhance shared representations while preserv-
ing scale-specific characteristics for accurate and stable fault
identification. The above process can be expressed as:

3) Overall Process of the AMSF: The overall process of
AMSF is illustrated in Fig. 9 and includes feature extraction,
multiscale fusion with attention enhancement, and final output
prediction. First, the input multivariate time-series data pass
through three stacked multiscale convolution modules. Each
module applies multiple 1-D convolutional kernels of different
sizes to capture local patterns across varying receptive fields.
An ICAM is inserted after the first multiscale convolution
module to enhance the response of key feature channels,
thereby improving the perception of shallow features toward
potential fault patterns. Then, the fused features from the three
convolutional modules are reduced in dimension and concate-

hierarchical levels. The Sliding-SE module is applied to the
fused feature maps to further improve the network capacity to
capture local abnormal features while preserving global trend
information. Finally, the enhanced feature representations are
passed through an FC layer to complete feature extraction.
AMSF progressively enhances feature discriminability and
temporal modeling through the joint effect of local convolution
modeling and channel-attention mechanisms, providing richer
and more distinctive representations for subsequent multi-
modal fusion.

markedly reduce redundancy among cross-modal information

decreases by 0.60% and 0.86%. These findings indicate that
the ICAM and Sliding-SE modules make positive contri-
butions to the overall diagnostic performance by enhancing
channel-wise feature representation and improving temporal
feature capture, respectively.

V. C ONCLUSION
To enable intelligent fault diagnosis for marine machinery,
this study proposes a deep multimodal information fusion DT
framework. Serving as two complementary modalities, the
PBM and DDM describe the operating state of marine machin-
ery from the mechanistic and data perspectives, respectively.
An AMSF module performs deep extraction and enhancement
of multimodal features, while a low-rank fusion strategy
constructs a unified high-dimensional representation for high-
accuracy fault diagnosis. To mitigate model mismatch caused
by performance degradation, a PDC module leverages mea-
sured data and probability distribution analysis to build a
feedback-enabled interaction between the PBM and DDM,
thereby improving model adaptability. The effectiveness of the
proposed framework is demonstrated through case studies, and
the principal findings are as follows.
1) With PDC correction, the PBM improves the simula-
tion accuracy of key components by 21.76%–61.51%,
achieving a closer alignment with measured engine
performance across different operating conditions and
demonstrating potential for reliable long-term applica-
tion.
2) Through deep fusion, the PBM and DDM form a
dual-direction information path. Compared with the sin-
gle modality, the multimodal fusion approach increases
average fault diagnosis accuracy by 9.30%, confirming
the effectiveness of the fusion strategy in enhancing
diagnostic robustness.
3) Under the tested load conditions, DMIF-DT effectively
identifies fault types and severities across multiple sce-
narios, demonstrating strong generalization within the
experimental scope. Moreover, DMIF-DT shows a clear
accuracy advantage over other comparative models.
4) Ablation studies indicate that AMSF—via the synergy
of multiscale convolutional modeling and an improved
channel-attention mechanism—effectively boosts diag-
nostic performance by reinforcing fault feature repre-
sentations.
In summary, the proposed DT framework offers a valuable
reference for predictive maintenance of marine machin-
ery. Despite its high diagnostic performance, this study
assumes that the PBM accurately captures the main thermo-
dynamic behavior and that sensor data remain within valid
ranges. Therefore, faults that violate these assumptions (e.g.,
mechanical breakage or severe sensor failure) may limit the
applicability of the framework. In addition, the proposed
framework has been validated under the tested load conditions,
and future work will focus on extending it toward engineering
deployment to demonstrate its potential for practical applica-
tion in shipboard monitoring systems.
