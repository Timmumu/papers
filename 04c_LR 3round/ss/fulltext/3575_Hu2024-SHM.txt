An intelligent BIM-enabled digital twin framework for real-time structural 
health monitoring using wireless IoT sensing, digital signal processing, and 
structural analysis

*

Technology, Newark, NJ 07102, USA

ABSTRACT  
Structural health monitoring (SHM) of civil infrastructure is critically important due to its direct influence on 
public safety and economic activities. Exiting Building Information Modeling (BIM)-based SHM systems often use 
offline data processing techniques to analyze and visualize structural health data. Despite some of them adopting 
Internet of Things (IoT) to enable real-time sensor data collection, sensor data quality still remains uncertain due 
to a lack of sensor signal preprocessing in those existing systems. Additionally, the IoT-based SHM systems often 
disregard structural analysis domain knowledge, which is important for accurate and precise SHM. Therefore, 
there is still a need to improve existing systems and practices by enabling more efficient and reliable data 
collection and processing as well as providing more representative SHM data visualization in BIM. As such, this 
paper proposes an intelligent BIM-enabled digital twin (DT) framework that integrates wireless IoT sensing and 
communication, digital signal processing (DSP), and structural analysis domain knowledge. The proposed system 
(1) leverages IoT sensing and wireless communication to enable autonomous and real-time SHM sensor data 
collection and transmission, (2) applies and compares multiple DSP techniques to preprocess the sensor data/ 
signals, and (3) innovatively embraces structural analysis expertise into structural behavior visualization in BIM 
by performing sensor data interpolation for enabling the visualization of structural behaviors at different loca -
tions of a structural element/component. The proposed BIM-enabled DT framework was demonstrated and tested 
for monitoring and visualizing the structural deformations of critical structural components using a prototyped 
structural frame subject to bending forces. The developed framework could be used and extended for any 
structural elements (such as beams, columns, trusses, slabs, arches, bracings, walls, footings, foundations, and 
girders, among others) and could be applied to any kind of structure. Experimental results showed that the 
proposed framework could effectively monitor and intuitively visualize the structural deformations under 
different load configurations with a high DT updating frequency of 5 Hz. The innovation of this study is reflected 
by integrating structural analysis expertise with IoT-enabling sensing data analytics in order to improve the 
representativeness of real-time structural behavior visualization in BIM and to advance the DT-based SHM 
systems in a faster and more adaptive direction. Ultimately, this paper contributes to the body of knowledge by 
developing a generic and easily extendable BIM-enabled DT framework for SHM with high sensor data quality 
and improved visualization to advance the existing practices of BIM-based SHM for civil infrastructure asset 
management.   
1. Introduction 
Civil infrastructure encompasses a wide range of physical structures 
and facilities (such as buildings and bridges) that play a crucial role in 
the development and functioning of societies, such as providing the 
foundation for economic growth, public safety, and overall well-being 
( Hu et al., 2024 ). Thus, the health condition of civil infrastructure is 
critically important to society due to its direct impact on public safety, 
economic activities, quality of life, resilience to natural disasters, and 
even public trust ( Osei-Kyei et al., 2021 ). For example, a deteriorating

bridge in poor condition has high risks of collapse when there is no

lead to disruptions in transportation, injuries and potential loss of life,

lenges due to disruption to transportation network, cascading damages 
to other surrounding infrastructure such as pavements, and erosion of 
public confidence ( FHWA, 2023 ). Therefore, it is significantly necessary 
to dynamically monitor the conditions of the civil infrastructure or its 
critical components by providing real-time data on the structural 
integrity, performance, and safety. 
Capitalizing on the advancement of the Internet of Things (IoT) 
technology, plenty of IoT-based structural health monitoring (SHM) 
systems have been developed for a variety of applications in the civil 
infrastructure sector ( Tong et al., 2019; Peng et al., 2023; Hu and 
Assaad, 2023 ). More specifically, IoT-based SHM uses an interconnected 
network of sensors (e.g., strain gauges) and other devices such as 4G 
modem embedded in the infrastructure. It collects data on many pa -
rameters such as strain and vibration frequency and then transmits the 
data to a centralized platform using different communication protocols 
(e.g., Bluetooth, LoRaWAN, and Zigbee) ( Gao et al., 2018; Assaad et al., 
2023 ). Afterwards, the data is processed, analyzed, and made accessible 
to infrastructure asset owners or other stakeholders for decision-making 
purposes. 
Empowered by more advanced communication technology and 
stronger computational capability of machines, digital twin-enabled 
IoT-based SHM systems have been developed recently for real-time 
data transmission and processing. A digital twin (DT) is a virtual 
replica of a physical object that mimics its real-world counterpart by 
incorporating data from various sources such as sensors, simulations, 
and historical information ( Botín-Sanabria et al., 2022 ). 
Building information modeling (BIM), on the other hand, provides 
many added benefits and advantages. In fact, BIM has been widely used 
to supplement IoT-based SHM by (1) creating a digital model of a 
structure with incorporated geometric information, materials, and 
components, (2) providing a user-friendly visualization environment to 
display the structural behavior and performance, and (3) offering high 
customizability to multiple items such as digital model ’ s property, level 
of detail, and view interface ( O ’ Shea et al., 2020; Gragnaniello et al., 
2024 ). Therefore, it is beneficial to integrate IoT-based SHM with BIM 
because the research on BIM-IoT integration is still considered to be in 
the initial phases; hence, requiring substantial efforts to gain a deeper 
understanding of the current situation ( Tang et al., 2019 ). 
In addition, most of the existing studies on IoT-based SHM barely 
addressed the sensor data quality issue that could be caused by noises or 
missing values before transmitting the data for subsequent analysis 
( Sakr and Sadhu, 2023 ). Lack of adequate SHM data pre-processing 
unavoidably reduces the reliability of IoT-based SHM systems due to 
uncertain sensor data quality. 
While some studies have attempted to integrate BIM and IoT for SHM 
( Scianna et al., 2022 ), they commonly have a lack of demonstrating the 
interoperability of their BIM models and do not justify the rationale for 
guiding sensor installation on the structure or structural components 
either. 
Therefore, there is still a need to enhance the existing practices of 
BIM-enabled and IoT-based SHM by handling the sensor data quality 
issue, enabling BIM model interoperability, as well as explaining the 
logic followed for guiding sensor installation. To address this need, the 
goal of this paper is to develop an intelligent BIM-enabled digital twin 
framework that embraces IoT sensors, wireless communication, digital 
signal processing, and structural analysis domain knowledge for 
autonomously sensing and visualizing the structural deformation of the 
critical components of a structure in a real-time manner. 
More specifically, an intelligent BIM-enabled digital twin framework 
represents a sophisticated integration of BIM, digital twin technology, 
and intelligent systems ( Hu and Assaad 2024b ). BIM provides a digital 
representation of physical and functional characteristics (e.g., geometry, 
and material properties) of an infrastructure and/or its critical compo -
nents. This digital representation will be used as the base to develop a 
digital twin model of the physical structure. A digital twin model is a 
virtual representation of the physical infrastructure, which works as a 
dynamic digital counterpart that mirrors the behaviors, status, and 
performance of its real-world counterpart in real-time. The real-time 
correspondence between the virtual and physical counterparts are 
enabled by intelligent systems with high-speed communication capa -
bility. An intelligent system refers to technologies such as artificial in -
telligence, machine learning, and data analytics allowing machines to 
perform tasks that typically require human intelligence. Such a system 
can collect and analyze vast amounts of data from IoT sensing networks 
or other sources to identify patterns and perform predictions with 
minimal human intervention. Therefore, BIM-enabled digital twins for 
SHM hold great significance because of its capability of allowing (1) 
comprehensive data integration to enable a holistic understanding of the 
structure ’ s behaviors, performance, and condition ( Sharma et al., 2022 ), 
(2) early detection of structural issues for supporting timely in -
terventions to prevent structural failures or accidents, and (3) imple -
mentation of preventative maintenance strategies to extend the lifespan 
of the structure, minimize downtime, and reduce maintenance costs 
( Errandonea et al., 2020 ). 
The contributions of this study are summarized as follows:  
• Proposing a BIM-enabled digital twin framework with much higher 
updating frequency to advance the development of real-time SHM 
systems toward faster and more adaptive direction  
• Incorporating digital twins with BIM interoperability to allow 
seamless real-time SHM data exchange for supporting structural 
analysis, smart sensing, and structural behavior visualization 
• Innovatively integrating the rich structural analysis domain knowl -
edge with sensing data analytics to unleash the potential of existing 
BIM software for more intuitive structural behavior representation 
and visualization 
2. Literature review 
The existing body of knowledge on SHM is huge and includes various 
studies with multiple applications and methods. Since it is not feasible or 
possible to review all existing studies in the broader SHM field, this 
section focuses on relevant studies that have considered the use of BIM 
for SHM-related applications and approaches. This particular focus 
helps in offering better insights on the existing relevant literature and 
hence better identifying and highlighting the knowledge gaps that this 
paper specifically aims to address. 
2.1. BIM-based SHM: General work mechanism and applications 
BIM functions as a comprehensive and digital representation of the 
physical and operational attributes of a structure. BIM emerges as an 
indispensable tool to optimize and document the performance of a 
structure across its lifespan. Based on its strong capability of information 
modeling and visualization, BIM enables a move toward intelligent 
management-related practices of structures with the use of smart 
sensing ( Kensek, 2014 ). Using facility management (FM) as an example, 
previous research endeavors have sought to merge BIM with a variety of 
sensing technologies and devices across multiple domains within 
building automation ( Abideen et al., 2022; Altohami et al., 2021 ). These 
studies focus on energy management in residential buildings, and 
employing sensors for measuring temperature, humidity, and energy 
consumption ( Abdelalim et al., 2017; Chang et al., 2018; Costa et al., 
2013; Ozturk et al., 2012; Xie et al., 2020 ). Meanwhile, some other 
studies particularly concentrated on structural monitoring and assess -
ment, either within the framework of operations and maintenance 
(O & M) or under the umbrella of SHM ( Panah & Kioumarsi, 2021 ).

The integration of BIM and SHM aims to incorporate SHM data into 
the information stored and presented within the BIM model for updating 
the model’s attributes or properties to assist in evaluating the structure’s 
performance and condition. Numerous conceptual frameworks have 
previously focused on fusing sensor data into BIM elements by gener -
ating customized BIM families and establishing links with sensor data for 
interpretation and storage purposes (Banfi et al., 2017; Davila Delgado 
et al., 2017; O’Shea & Murphy, 2020; Theiler et al., 2017; Valinejad -
shoubi et al., 2017; Zhang et al., 2015). For instance, identifying 
structural anomalies and damages is also a significant application of 
BIM-SHM integration (Panah & Kioumarsi, 2021). In summary, re -
searchers commonly utilize BIM-based SHM for monitoring and assess -
ing changes in structural properties based on the data obtained from 
sensors (Delgado et al., 2018; Hess et al., 2018; Wang et al., 2017). 
2.2. BIM-based SHM: Structural behavior visualization 
Previous studies also revealed that analyzing sensor data alone does 
not substantially contribute to understanding the facility’s condition, as 
the data makes far more sense when analyzed within a relevant 
“context” (Liu and Akinci, 2009). This context can be supplied by spatial 
information derived from BIM models, which enables a visualized and 
unified approach to data analysis. 
In order to enhance the visual aspects of the monitoring process, 
researchers have experimented with various software and visual pro -
gramming environments. Efforts have been made to link sensor data 
with BIM for plenty of applications such as building maintenance 
management to attain comprehensive control over project and structural 
monitoring (Kensek, 2014). Kazado et al. (2019) aimed to visualize and 
analyze real-time building information by developing a user-friendly 
add-in program that streams sensor data into the BIM process using 
Navisworks software. Chang et al. (2018) introduced a platform 
focusing on translating sensor data into visual representations by 
employing a color-coded visualization in a BIM model to present mul -
tiple sensor data sets visually. Valinejadshoubi et al. (2019) developed a 
framework to transfer the real-world sensor data into virtual one and 
then detect structural damages and visualize them in BIM through color 
coding. Delgado et al. (2018) integrated fiber optic sensor data into BIM, 
visualizing structural deformations through color coding and dynamic 
charts. Additionally, Boddupalli et al. (2019) proposed a framework for 
digitally representing the collected large volumes of vibration sensor 
data from bridges, analyzing the sensor data for bridge SHM, and linking 
results in a tabular form to sensor parameters defined in the BIM 
environment. 
2.3. Knowledge gap 
Despite the importance of existing studies about BIM-based SHM, 
three primary knowledge gaps still exist. 
First, majority of existing BIM-based SHM systems used offline pro -
cessing techniques to analyze the heterogenous data (e.g., vibration 
sensor data) and visualize the structural behaviors in BIM software for 
supporting maintenance decision making. An offline processing tech -
nique refers to the handling or analysis of data without real-time 
interaction, which often results in a significant delay between data 
collection and data analysis (Ed-daoudy and Kaalmi, 2019). However, 
early warning of the health conditions of a civil infrastructure often 
requires more efficient data processing to evaluate structural safety and 
reliability in time. Nowadays, real-time/online processing has become 
more and more popular for SHM due to more affordable sensing tech -
nologies and faster communication capability, which provides a great 
opportunity to develop DT-based intelligent SHM systems. In fact, a DT- 
based SHM system relies on wired or wireless IoT connectivity, real-time 
data processing, and graphical representation of physical civil struc -
tures. Despite that many studies proposing different frameworks to 
integrate sensor data into BIM for structural behavior visualization in 
the context of DT, they commonly used historical sensor data collected 
without IoT connectivity. Meanwhile, based on IoT, a few studies 
claimed real-time BIM-based SHM, but they suffer from either high la -
tency due to long response time or low transparency caused by unre -
ported response time. For instance, Wang et al. (2022) developed a real- 
time BIM-based SHM and early warning system for timber heritage 
structures with a response frequency at a daily base. In fact, a response 
frequency of one day is associated with high latency, resulting in 
delayed decision making, low capability of reacting to rapid events (e.g., 
earthquakes), and low system flexibility (Pons et al., 2023). Another 
study by Fawad et al. (2023) proposed a real-time bridge SHM system 
based on IoT, BIM, finite element analysis, and field loading test. 
However, there is missing information of how fast that system can 
collect and process the data from the field using IoT devices. It further 
hinders the transparency and reliability of the BIM-based bridge SHM 
system. Thus, there is still a need to develop a BIM-enabled DT system 
for SHM using smart sensing and wireless communication for high-speed 
real-time data transmission, processing, and visualization in support of 
early warning of poor structural conditions, which is the focus of this 
paper. 
Second, little-to-no studies have emphasized sensor data quality is -
sues such as noises and missing values for IoT-based SHM. As a result, 
the reliability and trustworthiness of those works would remain unsure 
due to a lack of sensor data/signal preprocessing for addressing the data 
quality issues. Therefore, this paper applies and compares different 
digital signal processing techniques to preprocess the sensor signals in 
order to increase the trustworthiness of IoT-based SHM systems based on 
sensor data. 
Third, most of the existing BIM-based SHM systems commonly apply 
color coding methods to visualize the structural behaviors (e.g., defor -
mation) using one sensor reading to colorize an entire element of a 
structure. In other words, when there is a sensor installed on one 
structural element, the entire element will be visualized using a uniform 
color based on the sensor reading. While such visualization method 
might reflect the overall performance level of the element in a global 
way, it has a lack of differentiating the structural responses at different 
locations of the structural element. Therefore, it may suffer from low 
representativeness when there is a need to visualize the heterogenous 
behaviors of a structural element. Thus, this paper innovatively proposes 
the use of structural analysis domain knowledge to perform sensor data 
interpolation for enabling the visualization of different structural be -
haviors in BIM using measurements from one single sensor. In this way, 
it would not only increase the SHM visualization representativeness in 
BIM but also have great potential to improve the cost-effectiveness of the 
IoT-based SHM systems due to less sensors being required.

3.2. Module 1: Digital asset development 
Digital asset development aims to generate a 3D virtual structural 
model of a physical structure/asset and then conduct structural analysis 
on the model for identifying the asset’s critical components and the

potential damage locations under both external and internal forces. 
More specifically, it is a process of 3D modeling that involves the crea -
tion of virtual representations of physical structures or components 
using computer-aided design software. These digital assets serve as ac -
curate and detailed models that capture the geometry, material prop -
erties, and other relevant characteristics of the structure. Through 
meticulous modeling, engineers can replicate real-world structures in a 
virtual environment, enabling comprehensive analysis and simulation of 
their behavior under various conditions. 
The Robot Structural Analysis Professional 2023 (a structural anal -
ysis computer software tool) was used to develop the digital assess and 
perform structural analysis; hence, generating a range of results that 
provide insights into the behaviors of the structure under different 
loading conditions. Fig. 2 shows the created digital model for a building 
structural frame prototype, which is comprised of 12 elements made of 
6063-T52 aluminum. The 12 structural elements include four strip 
footings (3.5 mm × 31.8 mm × 350.0 mm) on the base, four columns 
(6.2 mm × 25.4 mm × 300.0 mm), two type-A beams (1.6 mm × 25.4 
mm × 300.0 mm), and two type-B beams (3.2 mm × 12.7 mm × 300.0 
mm). The beam and column elements are numbered separately using 
green and blue colors, respectively, as shown in Fig. 2 . 
This study specifically focuses on two types of structural deformation 
(i.e., deflection and strain) due to their popularity in performance-based 
design and the assessment of structural safety ( Wei et al., 2021 ). To 
ensure the validity of the structural analysis results, self-checking-based 
hand calculations are implemented to check the potential errors in the 
computer results ( Hanson, 2022 ). The self-checking-based hand calcu -
lations refer to the use of standard formulations to perform manual 
calculation of the responses (e.g., stress, and stain) at a given location of 
a structural element of interest under pre-defined load conditions. 
Satisfactory structural analysis results occur when computer-generated 
outcomes closely match hand calculations. In cases where there is a 
discrepancy, revising the digital structural model becomes necessary, 
followed by repeating the structural analysis and conducting error 
checks. 
As a result, the digital model development module in this paper 
generates two major outputs to support physical asset development, 
sensing unit development, and BIM-enabled digital twin creation and 
visualization. One output contains the bending moment diagrams for all 
components of the structure to guide the installation of SHM sensors (e. 
g., strain gauges) at the places where the maximum bending moment(s) 
occur. The other output is an interoperable digital model that can be 
used in different BIM software tools seamlessly. The interoperability 
refers to the characteristic of the digital model to work with different 
BIM software tools. More specifically, the digital model of the structural 
frame was developed using the BIM software named Robot Structural 
Analysis Professional 2023. This software was also used to perform 
structural analysis on the created digital model. Meanwhile, due to the 
interoperability of the digital model, it was later imported into another 
BIM software (Autodesk Revit 2024) for structural behavior visualiza -
tion based on streaming sensor data and color coding. The interopera -
bility of the digital structural model enabled by Industry Foundation 
Classes (IFCs) allows for an efficient data exchange between software 
without information loss, elimination of reworks and errors, and a 
smooth transition from construction to facility management ( Ghaffar -
ianhoseini et al., 2017; Sibenik and Kovacic, 2020 ).

3.3.2. Smart sensing and processing unit development 
Since the proposed framework focuses on monitoring structural 
deflection and strain, an IoT sensing unit with a single board computer 
(SBC) and two types of sensors was developed to enable the smart 
sensing of desired structural deformations. The used hardware included 
Raspberry Pi 4 as the SBC, BF350-3AA as a strain gauge sensor, Teyleten 
Y3 as a strain gauge amplifier, and TOF10120 as a laser distance sensor 
to collect and preprocess the strain values and distance measurements. 
The sensors are installed on the structure based on the optimal sensor 
location information obtained from the structural analysis conducted in 
the previous module (i.e., Module 1). The involved software tools 
included the use of Python 3 to develop and run programs on Raspberry 
Pi 4 for sensor data collection and automatic data preprocessing. More 
details are provided in the next subsections. 
3.3.3. Digital signal preprocessing 
Preprocessing the raw digital signals are critical steps to improve and

Yet, laser sensor data often carry inevitable noises due to multiple

Therefore, to preprocess the collected laser distance sensor data in this

convolution with a set of wavelet functions and then employs thresh -
olding on these coefficients; hence, eliminating noise components while 
preserving the signal components ( Silva et al., 2006 ). On the other hand, 
FFT converts a signal from the time domain to a frequency domain, and 
thus revealing diverse frequency spectrums. To filter out noisy spec -
trums, a power spectrum threshold is applied. Finally, the Inverse FFT 
converts the filtered frequency domain signal back to its time domain 
( Zhang et al., 2013 ).

signal prior to its processing because SNR is often a reliable metric to 
assess the quality of the signal, where a signal with high SRN is desirable 
for better signal quality ( Fiete and Tantalo, 2001; Liu et al., 2015 ). This 
study applies the reciprocal of the coefficient of variation (which is the

Different parameters of each denoising method are investigated in 
order to seek an optimal denoising method to handle the noises in the 
laser distance signals, as summarized in Table 2 . 
Table 2 shows three parameters, including the decomposition level, 
wavelet coefficient threshold, and wavelet kernel type, which are 
investigated and fine-tuned for the WT method. Decomposition level 
refers to the number of times the distance signal is decomposed using a 
wavelet transform. Higher decomposition levels provide more detailed 
information about the signal at different scales but also result in more 
coefficients and a more complex representation ( Jang et al., 2021 ). 
Wavelet coefficient threshold is boundary value used to suppress or 
eliminate wavelet coefficients that are identified with noises. Wavelet 
kernel type is a specific wavelet function or filter that splits the signals 
into components with different frequency and then studies each 
component with a resolution that fits its scale ( Graps, 1995 ). This paper 
focuses on the use and comparison of Symlets 4 (sym4) wavelet and 
Daubechies 1 (db1) wavelet due to their wide applications in WT-based 
signal preprocessing ( Song et al., 2021; Li et al., 2022 ). 
One parameter (i.e., power spectral density threshold) of FFT is fine- 
tuned in this study. Power spectral density is a measure that describes 
how the power of a signal is distributed across different frequencies. 
When a signal has been transformed from time domain to frequency 
domain, its frequencies with power spectral density less than a threshold 
are identified with noises, and vice versa. 
The investigated values for the parameters of two signal denoising 
methods are empirically determined through a trial-and-error process by 
beginning with a large range of values. Then, the value range for each 
parameter was further narrowed down to a smaller range with a finer 
step size when the denoised signals were observed to be close to the 
desired signals without noise. 
3.3.4. Wireless communication unit development and sensor data

A TL-WDR5620 router was used to connect the sensors to other 
computer networks and establish wireless communication between 
multiple devices. To facilitate the smooth transmission of data from the 
smart sensing unit to BIM software, a wireless communication unit is 
developed using HTTP webserver and wireless local area network 
(WLAN). The HTTP webserver, operating on the Raspberry Pi with 
internet connectivity, functions as a software application. The web -
server ’ s role involves receiving and managing periodical HTTP requests 
(e.g., at a requesting rate of 5 Hz) from clients, such as personal com -
puters running BIM software. This server can deliver various resources, 
including web pages, files, and data, to clients within the WLAN through 
a request-response mechanism ( Cardellini et al., 2002 ). In the proposed 
framework, one HTTP server is established for high-speed wireless 
communication to enable sensor data transmission. 
3.4. Module 3: BIM-enabled SHM digital twin creation and visualization

The proposed framework reuses the digital structural model devel -

Autodesk Revit 2024 was used as the BIM-enabled visualization envi -

To visualize deformations, a visual programming program is devel -

model. 
The structural deformations under various loadings are monitored by 
innovatively integrating the sensor data and the existing domain

mation values are color coded based on pre-defined limit states. Two

concentrated load P applied at the midspan, and a distributed load w

4.3. Optimal method for laser distance signal preprocessing 
Fig. 9 shows a sample of raw distance signal collected by the TOF 
10120 laser distance sensor, in support of developing an optimal signal 
denoising method for laser distance signal preprocessing. 
As shown in Fig. 9 , the raw distance signal is comprised of distance 
measurements collected during three sub-periods: 0 ~ 90 s, 91 ~ 315 s, 
and 316 ~ 420 s. In particular, the signals from 0 ~ 90 s and 316 ~ 420 s 
contain static distance measurements, which means that the distance 
information is collected when both the physical structure and laser 
distance sensor remain stationary. However, the signals from these two 
periods fluctuate up and down and show great variance of the distance 
measurements, ranging from 270 mm up to 290 mm. Compared to the 
actual fixed distance of 278 mm, the absolute distance measurement 
error is about 8 ~ 10 mm, causing a low distance measurement precision 
if a millimeter-level deflection is desired. Regarding the signal during 
91 ~ 315 s, it shows an obvious bell-shape pattern when lateral load is 
applied and released periodically, demonstrating the effectiveness of the 
sensor for distance measurement. Meanwhile, the signal segments for 
the static distance measurements have SNR of 67.19 and 71.53, which 
are much greater than that of the dynamic distance signal (i.e., 29.58). 
This further indicates that the quality of the static distance signal seg -
ments is higher than that of the dynamic distance signal segment. The 
entire signal has low smoothness with an overall low SNR of 29.56 due 
to the noises caused by ambient light, object reflectivity, and interfer -
ence from other electronics. This further shows and validates that an 
optimal signal processing method is required to clean the laser distance 
signal by removing the noises so that the signal becomes smooth to 
better represent the actual distance measurements.

Fig. 11 shows two major observations. First, regardless of the signal 
decomposition level (DL), a WT with ‘db1 ′ kernel generates a pre -
processed signal with ladder-like patterns. The insight is that ‘db1 ′ (i.e.,

has a compact support and is particularly effective at representing and 
detecting abrupt changes or step-like features in signals ( Daubechies and 
Sweldens, 1998 ). Due to this unique characteristic, for all preprocessed 
signals, WT with ‘db1 ′ kernel well differentiates the signals from static 
distance measurements to dynamic distance measurements, especially 
when the DL is greater than 3. Second, a higher DL enables the pre -
processed data by ‘db1 ′ kernel to be smoother with less fluctuations. For 
example, when the DL is at 3, the preprocessed signals for both static and 
dynamic distance measurements still contain plenty of step-ups and 
step-downs. Once the DL reaches 6, the preprocessed signal tends to be 
stable, which indicates that continuously increasing the signal DL may 
not be able to improve the performance of WT with ‘db1 ′ kernel. 
Therefore, the optimal parameter for WT with ‘db1 ′ kernel includes a 
WCT of 25 and a DL of 6.

4.4. Dynamo graphical programming for structural behavior visualization 
Given a measured strain value, the proposed domain knowledge- 
guided structural behavior visualization method (detailed in the Meth -
odology section) is able to interpolate a strain value for any cross-section 
that is in parallel with applied concentrated load, and then color-coding 
scheme can be further applied to visualize both the measured and 
interpolated strain. However, in Revit, it is impractical to split a beam 
object into unlimited cross-sections due to the limitation of the soft -
ware ’ s functionality and efficiency. Instead, it is possible to divide the 
beam into finite parts/cells through spitting elements. Spitting elements 
is one available functionality in Revit to allow a BIM modeler to cut a 
model element into individual parts, ultimately resulting in a set of 
smaller cells ( Autodesk, 2024 ). Therefore, the digital model of beam #2

5. Discussion, contributions, and limitations 
5.1. Importance of high updating frequency for digital twin-based SHM 
systems 
First of all, this study developed a digital twin-based real-time SHM 
system with a much higher system updating frequency than the existing 
similar systems. In fact, updating frequency of a real-time SHM system is 
a standard metric to evaluate the system ’ s performance regarding its

capability of real-time responses as it is a packed assessment on the 
applied technologies (e.g., sensors, computational performance, and 
hardware), types and volume of data, and inference methods ( Bado 
et al., 2022 ). It is worth noting that a digital twin-based SHM system is 
equipped with the capability of real-time data acquisition and process -
ing, while a real-time SHM system may not be in the context of digital 
twins when the digital/virtual replicas of the physical objects (e.g., a 
bridge) are not involved. Table 3 summaries the most recent studies 
directed to digital twin-based and/or real-time SHM system, which are 
used as references to assess the proposed framework in this study based 
on updating frequency. 
As shown in Table 3 , a large proportion of the developed systems [e. 
g., Fawad et al (2023) , and Teng et al. (2023) ] for real-time SHM do not 
report their updating frequency. As a result, the real-time characteristic 
of those systems remains unknown, resulting in (1) a low trustworthi -
ness of those systems for practical implementation in SHM, and (2) low 
applicability because unknown updating frequency cannot properly and 
optimally inform the scheduling of structural maintenance and reha -
bilitation activities. The larger the updating frequency (shown in 
Table 3 ) the better since higher frequency reflects that the data is 
collected, processed, and visualized at a shorted period of time (i.e., 
more frequently). Although a few studies presented the updating fre -
quency for their real-time SHM systems, the updating frequency values 
are still small, which means observable lags still exist in those systems. 
For example, the system by Wang et al. (2022) has a high latency due to 
an updating frequency of each day (1.15 × 10
-5 
Hz). This study specif -
ically reaches a high updating frequency of once per 0.2 s (i.e., 5 Hz) to 
complete an entire process of sensing and preprocessing the sensor data, 
handling data requests, processing the data, and updating the digital 
model in BIM to visualize the structural behaviors, which is considerably 
higher from existing reported frequencies in the literature. 
From a practical implementation perspective, this high digital twin 
updating frequency of 5 Hz can enhance the effectiveness of real-time 
monitoring and decision making in two main ways. First, it provides 
real-time insights into the structural assessment, offering both engineers 
and stakeholders access to the current structural conditions with a delay 
as small as 0.2 s. As a result, more efficient maintenance decision can be 
made to save time by avoiding prolonged deliberations and indecision, 
optimize the allocation of resources (e.g., materials, and manpower), 
and improve productivity of structural maintenance-related activities. 
Second, capitalizing on the high updating frequency, it enables early 
detection of any subtle changes or anomalies in the structural behaviors, 
thus further reducing the risks of a structural health issue escalating 
from minor to major problems. Therefore, the proposed digital twin- 
based SHM framework has not only the technical advancement based 
on a higher updating frequency but also greater impact on the practi -
cality of digital twins for SHM.

This paper contributes to the body of knowledge in three main ways. 
First, it proposes a BIM-enabled digital twin framework with much 
higher updating frequency than the existing similar BIM-based real-time 
SHM systems, advancing the development of digital twins-based SHM 
toward faster and more adaptive direction. The proposed framework can 
retrieve, analyze, and visualize the real-time data on the performance

5.3. Limitations 
Three limitations are acknowledged in this study. First, the proposed 
structural analysis domain knowledge-based strain sensor data inter -
polation method has limitation on interpolating the data for a structural 
element when live load with varying locations are applied. Given the 
information of the loading conditions and boundary conditions, it is 
straightforward to formulate the mathematical representations [i.e., Eq. 
(6) and Eq. (10) for enabling automated sensor data interpolation]. In 
fact, the mathematical representations need to be pre-defined by system 
developers based on structural analysis domain knowledge. However, 
when live loads with changing locations occur, it will result in the 
changes in the mathematical representations, leading to a challenge of 
dynamically defining the mathematical representations by precisely 
locating the live loads. Thus, it is impractical to update those mathe -
matical representations to fit the scenarios in which the live loads have 
changing locations. Then, it exposes another research need to address 
the strain sensor data interpolation challenge caused by the varying 
locations of live loads. Second, since the scope of this study is to perform 
tensile strain sensor data interpolation and visualization, it currently 
does not consider other structural behaviors such as shear strain induced 
by torsional loads. As a result, the monitored health conditions of a 
structural element by the proposed SHM system may not comprehen -
sively reflect the overall health conditions of the structural elements. It 
further points out that future work can focus on expanding the smart 
sensing unit by adding more sensors to sense heterogenous structural 
behaviors under more complex loading conditions. Third, the proposed 
BIM-enabled digital framework system was tested on a small-scale 
structural frame in a controlled environment. The applicability of the 
proposed system for real-time scenarios still remains uncertain. To 
ensure the applicability of the proposed system for real-world structures, 
in the future, the authors will conduct additional validations of the 
system ’ s applicability through full-scale structural test in the laboratory 
or in the field by considering more types of structural geometries and 
material properties. 
6. Conclusions 
This study proposed an innovative BIM-enabled digital twin frame -
work that embeds IoT, wireless communication, digital signal process -
ing, and structural analysis domain knowledge for SHM. The proposed 
framework was tested by monitoring and visualizing the deformation of 
critical structural elements (i.e., beam, and column) of a prototyped 
structural frame using strain gauge and laser displacement sensor 
measurements. Different load configurations are applied on the frame 
protype to validate the proposed framework. The results showed that the 
developed digital model of the structural frame prototype has high 
interoperability as it can be used across different BIM software tools (i.e., 
Robot Structural Analysis Professional and Revit). This model was used 
for (1) performing structural analysis to identify the most vulnerable 
locations on a structure in order to inform optimal locations for sensor 
installation, and (2) guiding the creating of a DT model for real-time 
structural behavior visualization. By comparing two different digital 
signal processing techniques (FFT, and WT), this study found that WT 
with ‘sym4 ′ kernel (WCT = 25; DL = 6) was the optimal technique to 
handle the noises in the laser sensor signals. Thus, it further proves the 
significance of signal preprocessing to address the potential sensor data 
quality issues in IoT-based SHM. With the use of visual programming, 
BIM, and structural analysis domain knowledge, this paper creatively 
proposed a sensor data interpolation method in Revit to visualize the 
diverse structural behaviors of a structural element using measurements 
from one single sensor only. In Revit, the real-time visualization of the 
beam deformation and column displacement under different loads was 
smooth as the digital twin model could refresh at a frequency of 5 Hz. 
Meanwhile, the knowledge-based sensor interpolation enabled an intu -
itive beam deformation visualization as the deformations at different
