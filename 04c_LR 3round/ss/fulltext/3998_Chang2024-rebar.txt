ABSTRACT  
Accurate and efficient inspection of rebar dimensions has proven to be a persistent challenge for researchers and 
practitioners. This paper introduces a semantically enriched 3D model-based system that employs computer 
vision and deep learning for location-aware identification and tracking of rebar issues. The system comprises four 
modules: (A) digital twin generation, (B) segmentation, (C) inspection, and (D) issue identification and tracking. 
The generation module constructs 3D models from rebar structures. The segmentation and inspection modules 
analyze the 3D models, enriching them with semantic information. The issue identification and tracking module 
exchanges information between the semantically enriched 3D models and the building information models across 
time. An experiment on a column rebar cage is conducted. A precision of over 90% and a recall of over 97% are 
reported in 3D instance segmentation. Diameter inspection achieves an accuracy of 95.5% for large-size rebars. 
Spacing inspection achieves a mean relative error of 0.98%. The defective spacing is identified and tracked.   
1. Introduction 
Reinforced concrete structures are the most used structures in con -
struction as a result of their strength and economy. Furthermore, rebars 
play a significant role in reinforced concrete structures since they pro -
vide most of the tensile strength. To successfully execute rebar con -
struction, it is imperative to consider key aspects, such as scheduling, 
quality, and cost. In terms of scheduling, enhancements of supply chain 
and installation planning have been undertaken [ 1 – 3 ]. In terms of 
quality, rigorous inspections must be implemented across material, 
processing, and installation phases. Among these inspections, rebar 
dimensional quality control (DQC), which aims to inspect the dimen -
sional properties of rebars (e.g., the spacing of tie rebars and the 
diameter of each rebar), is probably the most critical part since bearing 
capacity of a structure is subjected to proper arrangement of rebars. 
Rebar DQC varies from project to project and is performed on con -
struction sites, thereby posing a challenge for further investigation. With 
the advanced and rapid development of machine learning (ML), deep 
learning (DL), and computer vision (CV), these technologies offer 
promising solutions to traditional engineering problems [ 4 – 8 ]. These 
successful breakthroughs inspire us to develop a novel rebar dimen -
sional quality control based on semantically enriched 3D models and 
building information modeling (BIM), proposing a solution to the 
aforementioned challenge. 
The traditional approach to perform rebar DQC requires qualified 
inspectors and tap measures, which is time-consuming, labor-intensive, 
and error-prone. In recent years, several studies [ 7 , 9 , 10 ] have been 
conducted to improve the quality and efficiency of rebar DQC. Accord -
ing to them, a 3D-based system is preferred and can be divided into four 
major processes: (A) 3D reconstruction of rebar structures, (B) rebar 
geometric extraction from 3D models, (C) measurement of rebar 
dimensional properties, and (D) examination of measured dimensional 
properties. Although research on each process has been developed, 
certain difficulties remain when connecting the processes, such as full 
automation, acquisition of semantically enriched rebar 3D models, and 
rebar issue tracking. Below is a summary of related work:  
(A) Terrestrial laser scanning (TLS) and the combination of structure 
from motion (SfM) and multi-view stereo (MVS) are commonly 
utilized to build 3D models from real assets in construction. First, 
since TLS is capable of scanning large structures rapidly and 
provides highly accurate data, it has been adopted in precast 
element inspection [ 5 , 7 , 11 – 14 ]; common structural component 
inspection [ 15 – 18 ]; rebar inspection [ 19 – 21 ]; mechanical, elec -
trical, and plumbing inspection [ 9 , 22 , 23 ]; and industrial equip -
ment inspection [ 24 , 25 ]. However, TLS has been found to be

spatial demand, and the requirement of operators. Second, since 
the SfM-to-MVS pipeline shows great potential for generating 3D 
scenes from common 2D images, it has been applied in large-scale 
reconstruction from Internet photos [26–28], reinforced concrete 
structure inspection [6], and rebar inspection [10,29–31]. How -
ever, the relatively low accuracy of point clouds poses a challenge 
in dimensional inspections. Furthermore, SfM's up-to-scale point- 
cloud results cause a limitation in automated processes.  
(B) To separate individual structural components, pipes, or rebars 
from a point cloud of as-built facilities, computer vision (CV) 
algorithms and deep learning (DL)-based approaches are possible 
solutions. First, random sample consensus (RANSAC) [32] and 
Hough transform [33] are frequently adopted in rebar inspection 
[20,21], pipe inspection [9,22,23], and structural component 
inspection [5,11,34,35]. However, RANSAC is hard to scale to 
large-size point clouds [16,36] and performing a Hough trans -
form on a large dataset is computationally expensive [16]. Sec -
ond, although DL-based 2D instance segmentation models such as 
Mask R-CNN [37] have been successfully applied in 2D rebar 
inspection [38,39], the segmentation results lack global spatial 
information. As a result, 3D segmentation models (e.g., Point 
Transformer [40]) may directly tackle the issue, but annotating 
rebar data in a 3D space is too costly. Hence, a combination of 2D 
segmentation and 3D fusion may be the solution. Chen et al. [31] 
demonstrated a prototype of a hybrid method. Nonetheless, the 
integration of 2D and 3D information remains an open problem.  
(C) Diameter inspections of cylindrical point-cloud instances (e.g., 
rebars and pipes) are usually transformed into projections and 2D 
circle fitting. Although the geometric fitting and algebraic fitting 
have been applied in the literature [17,18,20,21,24,25] and show 
advantages in accuracy and efficiency, respectively, they both 
suffer from noise and outliers. Hence, the robust fitting method of 
the Hough transform is preferred, which has been implemented in 
several studies [9,22,23,34]. In spacing inspection, studies 
[38,41] have demonstrated automated approaches by using CV 
algorithms and DL. However, these studies were limited to 2D 
images, where information on the rebar's location is missing. 
Other studies [12,21,30,39] presented 3D rebar spacing inspec -
tion. Nonetheless, they were limited to rebar structures of simple 
arrangement.  
(D) Recently, advancements in digital technology have facilitated 
issue identification in construction-related inspections. Studies 
[11,42] have utilized TLS to obtain point-cloud data of precast 
elements and perform dimensional measurement by CV algo -
rithms. Then, measurements were matched and compared with 
the tolerances extracted from BIM models. Finally, the inspection 
results were stored in an Industry Foundation Classes (IFC) 
format. One study [9] demonstrated a similar Scan-vs-BIM 
method, but on pipes and conduits. Matching Scan-and-BIM is 
necessary to extract corresponding tolerances of as-built struc -
tures from BIM models. Maalek [15] investigated the registration 
of common structural components. They leveraged global regis -
tration when as-built information was provided without con -
struction error, while global registration and subsequent local 
registration were integrated when construction error was con -
tained in the data. 
Despite that previous studies have shown advancements in the 3D- 
based rebar DQC framework, the following research gaps exist: (1) in -
spection algorithms are not fully automated or limited to strong as -
sumptions (2) on-site rebar structures are hard to transform into 
semantic information, and (3) efficient rebar issue tracking is not 
explored. First, the algorithm in SfM only computes relative rotation and 
translation between images. The need for manual scale calibration of the 
reconstruction [30] hinders the 3D reconstruction from being fully 
automated. Also, existing studies mostly simplified the configurations of 
rebar installation. Specifically, they have assumed that rebars are ar -
ranged orthogonally, constrained to 2D surfaces, and positioned within 
well-illuminated surroundings. These stringent assumptions limit the 
inspection to relatively simple rebar structures, such as slabs and walls. 
Second, current methods include analyzing the complex rebar structures 
in either a 2D or 3D way. On the one hand, the 2D methods are relative 
mature and capable of extracting semantic attributes. However, the 
spatial information is lost inherently. On the other hand, the 3D methods 
come with a higher cost and can only be performed on well-arranged 
rebar structures. Third, implementing a tracking system in quality 
control has always been a challenging task. Despite there have been 
studies [8] proposing BIM-based issue tracking systems, critical issue 
identification and tracking still require manual handling and integra -
tion. Regarding rebar DQC, the issues to be confirmed often involve 
errors in the diameter of a single rebar or the spacing between two 
specific rebars not meeting the specifications. Linking such small-scale 
rebar dimensional defects with their location poses significant 
challenges. 
Furthermore, to facilitate rebar issue tracking, digital twins (DTs), 
which are defined as physical products, virtual products, and connections 
between them [43], are incorporated in this study. DTs are expected to 
resolve problems regarding semantic enrichment and interoperable 
building representations, which are identified as the primary focuses in 
construction technologies [44]. Sacks et al. [45] conceptually explored 
digital twin construction, which can be characterized by interactions 
between four essential dimensions: physical–virtual, product–process, 
intent–status, and data–information–knowledge–decisions. Based on the 
inspection pipeline summarized from previous research and the concept 
of DTs shown in Fig. 1 (b), an ideal rebar DQC system using semantically 
enriched 3D model is formulated and achieves location-aware rebar issue 
identification. By adding the time dimension into the digital twin con -
struction proposed by Sacks et al. [45], an efficient rebar issue tracking 
can be realized to connect issue identification results at different inspec -
tion checkpoints. To be clear, the connection is essentially based on 
consistent BIM models and registration of 3D reconstructions into BIM 
models. 
In summary, a novel rebar DQC system is proposed to overcome the 
aforementioned obstacles. First, this study focuses on developing a 
method that fuses DL and CV techniques to construct semantic 3D 
models of on-site rebar structures. Second, rebar issue identification and 
issue tracking are fulfilled through the incorporation of BIM and 
semantically enriched 3D models. Third, algorithms utilized in the 
processes are integrated to ensure that data processing works without 
manual interference. Furthermore, this study adopts realistic configu -
rations of rebar installation. Specifically, rebars include arbitrary di -
ameters, orientations, ties, couplers, lap splicing, and are positioned in 
outdoor environments. This facilitates the implementation of in -
spections on complex rebar structures, such as columns. 
This paper is organized in the following order. Section 1 introduced 
the background, summarized past research, and explained the objec -
tives. Section 2 elaborates on the methodology. Section 3 details the 
experimental validation. Section 4 highlights and discusses the experi -
mental results. Section 5 concludes the study and provides recommen -
dations for future work. 
2. Methodology 
2.1. System workflow 
Fig. 1(a) shows the system workflow, which includes four modules: 
(A) DT generation, (B) segmentation, (C) inspection, and (D) issue 
identification and tracking. First, the DT generation module constructs a 
3D model from a rebar structure. Then, the segmentation module and 
inspection module perform analytics on the 3D model. Finally, the issue 
identification and tracking module exchanges information between the

semantically enriched 3D model and the BIM model over time. 
The system inputs a structural component (e.g., column and slab) 
and works with LOD 400 BIM models (i.e., BIM models with rebar de -
tailing). The workflow can be characterized as (1) an autonomous pro -
cess, (2) as-built vs. as-designed, and (3) a structural-component-to- 
rebar flow. First, the system inputs images and a BIM model, processes 
them with CV, DL, and BIM techniques, and outputs an issue report. The 
system is autonomous since no manual interference is required in the 
algorithms. Second, the information is extracted from the as-built 
semantically enriched 3D model and the as-designed BIM model. 
Accordingly, identifying underlying issues is accomplished by 
comparing the as-built and the as-designed parts. Third, a structural- 
component-level model is constructed and decomposed into a rebar- 
level model. Ultimately, the semantically enriched 3D model and the 
BIM model comparison is at the rebar level. 
Fig. 1 (b) shows that the proposed system is fundamentally a DT- 
based framework that includes three conceptual dimensions: (1) phys -
ical – virtual, (2) past – future, and (3) status – intent (adapted from [ 45 ]). 
The DT generation and analytical processes (i.e., segmentation and in -
spection) are performed at different checkpoints. In this study, different 
checkpoints indicate whether the inspection is conducted when the 
rebar structure is defective or rectified. The semantic as-built 3D model 
is then compared with the as-designed BIM model to identify underlying 
issues. To incorporate results from different checkpoints, the issue- 
tracking process is utilized. We note that the aforementioned “ as-built 
3D model ” is actually the “ status DT ” and the “ as-designed BIM model ” 
is actually the “ intent DT. ” Hence, the proposed system is based on two 
types of DT. 
2.2. Digital twin generation 
Generating 3D models from 2D images is preferred since the equip -
ment required to collect 2D data is more convenient and applicable to a 
construction site than that used to collect 3D data, such as TLS and Lidar. 
The process is divided into data collection and 3D reconstruction, which 
collects 2D images and reconstructs 3D point clouds from 2D images. 
2.2.1. Data collection 
A sports camera, GoPro Hero 9 Black, is utilized to collect 2D images 
on a construction site, thanks to its high mobility, flexible shooting 
mode, and high resolution. A worker can walk on a construction site 
while holding the camera without restrictions. Either interval shooting 
or video recording is suitable for collecting the required data. The 
maximum 5K resolution is expected to meet the requirement of 
inspecting small-size objects. 
During the shooting, the camera's movement, the movement speed, 
and the distance between the camera and the rebar structure are care -
fully chosen. Images are collected in spatial order to retrieve more 
precise camera poses when sequential matching is utilized during SfM. 
The camera is moved slowly at around 5 cm to 10 cm per frame to ensure 
a sufficient area of overlap in subsequent photos. In addition, the camera 
is kept close at around 30 cm to 50 cm to the rebar structure to ensure 
adequate pixels of rebars. 
2.2.2. 3D reconstruction 
The generation of a 3D point cloud from 2D images is accomplished 
by the SfM-to-MVS pipeline and a proposed scale calibration algorithm. 
First, SfM is utilized to reconstruct the intrinsic and extrinsic parameters

of the camera for each image. A pixel-wise correspondence search with 
known camera poses generates depth maps, a point cloud, and a mesh. 
In this study, COLMAP [ 46 , 47 ] is utilized to perform the reconstruction. 
Exhaustive feature matching is adopted rather than sequential feature 
matching since it provides more accurate results at the rebar level. 
Although photometric and geometric consistency are utilized in COL -
MAP, the depth maps are complete at the structural-component level but 
not the rebar level. Hence, generating depth maps from a mesh elimi -
nates the discontinuity of rebars in depth maps. 
Commonly, scale ambiguity in the SfM coordinate system is over -
come by selecting artificial objects with known sizes, such as predefined 
surveying points. Although this measure can be easily implemented, it 
requires manual interference. Hence, this study proposes an automated 
scale calibration algorithm using AprilTag [ 48 , 49 ], which is an efficient 
and robust fiducial system. Fig. 2 presents the proposed scale calibration 
approach. A single AprilTag of a known family is placed on the structural 
component at an arbitrary position. During one recording process, it is 
sufficient to capture a AprilTag only once. For example, when recording 
a slab and the surrounding columns and walls, only one AprilTag is 
needed. Furthermore, the AprilTag can appear only once in several 
frames. Then, the images containing AprilTags are collected and pro -
cessed by the proposed algorithm. Finally, the scale factor is calculated 
to calibrate the SfM-based point cloud. 
2.3. Segmentation 
The rebar structure 3D models should be segmented into smaller 
rebar instances to perform a further inspection. Considering the accu -
racy and feasibility of acquiring 3D annotations, a hybrid model 
combining 2D DL and CV is proposed and illustrated in Fig. 3 . The 
hybrid model is divided into (1) a DL-based 2D instance segmentation 
model to obtain 2D segmentation masks of rebar spacings and (2) a 3D 
clustering algorithm to integrate 2D masks of the same rebar from 
different viewpoints into a unique 3D instance. 
2.3.1. 2D instance segmentation 
Mask R-CNN [ 37 ] is adopted to conduct 2D instance segmentation. 
Furthermore, a rebar segmentation model [ 38 ] that is trained on an on- 
site rebar dataset is utilized as a pre-trained model. Fine-tuning is per -
formed, owing to the difference in the targeted domain. 
Datasets of 155, 27, and 10 images are used in the training, valida -
tion, and testing. While the images include columns, slabs, and walls, 
this study focuses on inspecting columns. The labeling categories are 
formulated as spacing and intersection, which are shown in the middle 
of Fig. 3 . This is designed to conform with the nature of rebar arrange -
ment and to facilitate spacing inspection. First, in common structural 
components, vertical and horizontal rebars naturally form grids. In 
consideration of the occlusion due to rebar arrangement, recognizing a 
spacing segment as the first step is more intuitive than recognizing 
complete rebar. Second, a spacing instance provides information about 
the relationship between its ends, which is useful in spacing inspection. 
2.3.2. 3D clustering 
A 3D clustering algorithm using CV techniques is developed to 
cluster 2D masks into 3D line instances automatically. The process is 
divided into (1) preprocessing of 2D masks, (2) clustering of raw in -
stances, and (3) clustering of spacing instances. First, 2D masks were 
back-projected to 3D and are named “ raw instances. ” After the raw in -
stances were down-sampled, their orientations and center lines were 
estimated by 3D line RANSAC and 2D circle Hough transform, 
respectively. 
Second, Density-Based Spatial Clustering of Applications with Noise 
(DBSCAN) [ 50 ] was used to cluster raw instances into non-overlapping 
instances named “ spacing instances. ” The end coordinates of the center 
lines were utilized during the clustering. After spacing instances were 
extracted, outliers were removed. 
Third, DBSCAN was also used to cluster spacing instances into 
complete linear instances named “ line instances. ” The segmentation 
method for linear objects presented in [ 16 ] was adopted. The orientation 
vector and the coordinate of the projected point from an arbitrary 
reference point were utilized during the clustering. Finally, lap splices 
were separated through repeated clustering on the aforementioned 
grouped line instances with a lowered distance threshold in DBSCAN. 
Fig. 4 summarizes the differences between the aforementioned raw 
instance, spacing instance, line instance, and rebar. 
2.3.3. Semantically enriched 3D model 
As a result, this module enriches the complicated 3D rebar model 
with substantial semantic information, thereby enabling thorough and 
practical inspections. After the point cloud was segmented into line in -
stances, as depicted in Fig. 3 , essential rebar attributes can be efficiently 
acquired. Subsequently, these attributes facilitate a range of inspections. 
For instance, by measuring the cross section of a line instance's points, 
the diameter can be obtained and checked; by measuring the center lines 
between two line instances, the spacing can be obtained and checked; by 
measuring the overlapping distance along the longitudinal axis of two 
line instances, it is possible to determine and validate the lap splicing 
length and lap splicing location. 
2.4. Inspection 
To ensure structural integrity, the dimensions of rebars should be 
checked, where diameter and spacing are the most important items to be 
inspected. Thus, a diameter inspection algorithm utilizing robust CV 
algorithms and a spacing inspection algorithm integrating DL and CV 
algorithms are developed. 
2.4.1. Diameter inspection 
A diameter inspection algorithm using CV algorithms is developed to 
automatically measure each line instance's diameter. The process is 
demonstrated in Fig. 5 and can be divided into (1) preprocessing, (2) 
spacing-instance-level diameter classification, and (3) line-instance- 
level diameter classification. 
The preprocessing consists of inpainting, down-sampling, and

projection from 3D to 2D. Since only the outer faces of rebars are 
recognized by the 2D instance segmentation model, spacing instances 
are incomplete and should be inpainted. The rough center line of the 
spacing instance was first estimated. Then, points within an extended 
radius from the center line were resampled from the overall point cloud. 
After inpainting, the resampled instances were down-sampled and pro -

The measurement of diameter is formulated as a classification 
problem that categorizes each line instance into the best-fitting diam -
eter. This is accomplished by circle fitting, and a 2D circular Hough 
transform is utilized because of its robustness. Diameters of D10 to D32 
rebars, i.e., 9.53, 12.7, 15.9, 19.1, 22.2, 25.4, 28.7, and 32.2 mm, are 
adopted since they are the most commonly used sizes in Taiwan's con -
struction industry. A Hough transform was conducted for each pre -
defined diameter, outputting the number of inliers and the estimated 
center. Then, the spacing instance was classified into the diameter with 
the maximum number of inliers. Finally, the diameter of the line 
instance was determined by a majority voting on the contained spacing 
instances. 
2.4.2. Spacing inspection 
A spacing inspection algorithm using CV algorithms and DL is 
developed to automatically detect spacings on arbitrary axes and mea -
sure the spacing distances. The process is demonstrated in Fig. 6 and can 
be divided into (1) clustering of line instances and (2) spacing detection 
and measurement. First, to fulfill the automated spacing detection, the 
meaning of spacing is revisited, and it can be geometrically defined as 
the relationship between two line instances in the same orientation. 
Intuitively, line instances with nearly identical orientations were clus -
tered into groups by using DBSCAN ( Fig. 6 (b)). 
Second, whether each pair of line instances in a group should be 
linked was determined by checking if any spacing instance lies in the 
space between the pair ( Fig. 6 (c)). In other words, a spacing instance 
that lies between a pair indicates the pair is related. To implement the 
strategy, the line instances in a group were projected onto 2D. Then, the 
following criteria were examined to determine if the relationship of a 
pair exists:  
1. 2D overlapping ratio of the pair and the spacing instances;  
2. Included angle between the pair and the spacing instances;  
3. Nearest neighbors of the spacing instances; and  
4. Height difference (the z-value after projection) between the pair and 
the spacing instances. 
Finally, the spacing distance of a detected pair was measured by 
calculating the distance between two center lines.

2.5.1. Issue identification 
Fig. 7 presents the concept of the proposed issue identification 
method. It is divided into a Scan-vs-BIM registration algorithm and a 
Scan-vs-BIM comparison algorithm, which constructs rebar correspon -
dences and checks attributes in each correspondence. 
The Scan-vs-BIM registration algorithm is further divided into (1) 
geometry extraction, (2) structural-component-level registration, and 
(3) rebar-level correspondence. First, Autodesk Revit was used to 
construct the LOD 400 BIM model and convert it to the IFC format. The 
geometric information of each rebar in the IFC file was interpreted and 
saved in a point-cloud format. Second, the 3D model and BIM point 
clouds were aligned by coarse and fine registrations. At the coarse 
(global) stage, Fast Point Feature Histogram (FPFH) [ 51 ] was utilized as 
a feature descriptor, and RANSAC was utilized to retrieve the best 
alignment in multiple iterations. At the fine (local) stage, the best 
transformation matrix of coarse registration was inputted as an initial 
constraint in the Iterative Closest Point (ICP) algorithm [ 52 ]. Iteratively, 
the optimum transformation was found through the cycle of finding 
correspondences and finding alignment. Third, after the structural 
components were aligned, the rebar correspondences between the 3D 
model and the BIM model can be recognized by querying neighboring 
rebars and calculating the average point distance. 
The Scan-vs-BIM comparison algorithm is divided into (1) attribute 
extraction and (2) attribute-wise comparison. First, the rebar attributes 
of interest (i.e., diameter and spacing) were extracted from the LOD 400 
BIM model of IFC format. A rebar attribute contains a specified value 
and a tolerance. Second, the inspection results in the 3D model were 
compared with the attributes in the BIM model. During the comparison 
of diameter, majority voting was performed (i.e., the diameter of a outer 
tie rebar is determined by the majority of diameters of four lines on each 
side), while during the comparison of spacing, averaging was per -
formed. These are attributed to the fact that rebar in the BIM model may 
correspond to multiple lines in the 3D model. 
This study utilizes LOD 400 BIM models to fulfill issue identification, 
which can be extended to more complicated rebar DQC such as the 
location of lap splices and shape of tie rebars. However, if only a basic 
inspection is required, such as checking rebar diameters or spacings, 
using LOD 300 BIM models or pure attribute information would be 
sufficed.

rectification process of dimensional issues automatically. It is assumed

i.e., the design of the structural component remains the same. This holds 
in most cases since the design rarely changes at the rebar installation 
stage. First, the issue identification algorithm was executed on the se -
mantic 3D models at different checkpoints. All issues were registered to 
the rebar instances in the BIM model. Then, an attribute-wise

3. Experimental validation 
3.1. Experimental setup 
To validate the proposed system, an experiment on a column rebar 
cage was conducted to simulate the defects contained in the structure 
and to identify and rectify them ( Section 3.2 to Section 3.5 ). Further -
more, the proposed system was tested on a concrete slab at a construc -
tion site. This was done to showcase its applicability when BIM models 
are unavailable. ( Section 3.6 ). 
Fig. 8 (a) shows the configuration of the column. It was designed to 
be realistic and complicated regarding diameter, shape, arrangement, 
and splicing. D25 and D29 were adopted for the main rebars, while D13 
was adopted for the tie bars. The hook angle, hook extension length, 
spacing of tie bars, and size of the rebar structure all complied with 
common practice. Lap splicing and couplers were both adopted to in -
crease the complexity. Fig. 8 (b) shows the two checkpoints of the 
experiment. At checkpoint 1, the top-layer tie rebar was moved away 
from the second-layer tie rebar, which forms a defective enlarged 
spacing, while at checkpoint 2, the rebar arrangement conformed with 
the project requirements. 
3.2. Digital twin generation 
In the DT generation module, the results of data collection, the SfM- 
to-MVS pipeline, and scale calibration are presented. First, at both 
checkpoints, images were collected from top to bottom at each angle 
surrounding the column. It took 136 s and 102 s to collect 273 images 
and 205 images, respectively, with the interval shooting mode at each 
checkpoint. Second, the camera model and the camera poses were suc -
cessfully generated. Fig. 9 presents the obtained camera poses and 
sparse point cloud on the left side. The ratio of aligned images reaches 
100% in both cases. Furthermore, the depth maps, the point cloud, and 
the mesh were successfully generated. Fig. 9 presents the obtained dense 
point cloud on the right side. Third, all AprilTags contained in the 
collected images were detected in both cases. Corners and orientations 
of the AprilTags were extracted. Subsequently, the scale factor was 
calculated to calibrate the point cloud. Manual measurement on the 
point cloud is utilized as the ground truth to evaluate the proposed al -
gorithm. The average relative error of the scale factor is only 0.25%, 
which should be sufficiently accurate for construction-related activities. 
3.3. Segmentation 
In the segmentation module, the results of 2D instance segmentation

4. Discussion 
Due to the inherent complexity, rebar DQC is conventionally con -
ducted through manual procedures, such as measurement and inter -
pretation of project requirements. In this study, the manual procedures 
are replaced with automated algorithms, such as automated scale cali -
bration in 3D reconstruction and automated spacing detection. As a 
result, merely attaching fiducial markers and collecting images are 
manually conducted. The processing algorithms are fully automated, 
thereby benefiting construction management. First, video recorders 
could partially substitute for qualified inspectors. This reduces time, 
effort, and cost to accomplish rebar inspection. Second, the proposed 
pipeline can be further integrated with UAV, which would be the 
desirable inspection scheme in the long term. Third, information is 
digitalized, well-organized, and accessible through the DT- and BIM- 
based system, forming a rebar dataset and enabling later profound 
analytics. 
In the past, semantic rebar information acquisition vastly relied on 
time-consuming and labor-intensive work. This study establishes a rebar 
3D model that represents and synchronizes with the real rebar structure 
by integrating DL and CV techniques. First, a tuned SfM-to-MVS pipeline 
and image detection are successively utilized to build 3D models. Sec -
ond, in segmentation, DL fundamentally detects rebars in complex 
structures, and conventional 3D clustering recovers the missing parts 
and removes the redundant parts. Third, the diameter inspection is 
achieved by combining robust 3D line detection and robust 2D circle 
fitting. The spacing inspection is achieved by combining 3D clustering 
and segmentation. The extracted semantic information enables further 
construction management, such as inspection and documentation. 
Traditional rebar DQC depends on inspector-driven number check -
ing between measurement results and design tolerances. This paper 
proposed a comparison of semantic 3D models and BIM models (i.e., 
status DT and intent DT). The identified issues can also be effectively 
tracked across time by using BIM technology. The introduced system has 
good potential to be extended to other domains. For instance, the DT- 
and BIM-based scheme may be applied to other rebar DQCs (e.g., the 
bending radius of hooks), other rebar inspections (e.g., binding wire 
tying and material inspection of rebar chairs), and other construction 
inspections (e.g., progress monitoring and inspection of embedded 
pipes). 
5. Conclusions 
This study established an autonomous system that fuses deep 
learning (DL), computer vision (CV), digital twins (DT), and building 
information modeling (BIM) to conduct novel rebar dimensional quality 
control. The system consists of four modules. The DT generation module 
collects images of a structural component using a GoPro. It reconstructs 
a scale-calibrated 3D point-cloud model via a structure-from-motion to 
multi-view stereo pipeline and an AprilTag-based algorithm. The seg -
mentation module decomposes the 3D model via 2D instance segmen -
tation using Mask R-CNN and 3D clustering using Density-Based Spatial 
Clustering of Applications with Noise. The inspection module measures 
the rebar diameter using a 2D circular Hough transform, while rebar 
spacing is measured by integrating segmentation results and CV

An experiment on a column rebar cage was conducted to validate the 
proposed system. Collected images were all aligned during 3D recon -
struction. The error of scale calibration was 0.25%, which should be 
sufficient for construction-related tasks. The hybrid model reported an 
average precision of 50.4% on the column images of the test dataset in 
the 2D instance segmentation stage. Subsequently, it reported a preci -

rebars in the 3D instance segmentation stage. In the case of rebar 
instance, the diameter inspection reached a high accuracy of 95.5% and 
77.8% for large-size and small-size rebars, respectively. The spacing 
inspection approach achieved a low mean relative error of 0.98%. In 
issue identification, the proposed method reported a root-mean-square 
error of 12.32 mm during the structural-component-level registration 
and an accuracy of 100% during the rebar-level correspondence. Finally, 
the defective spacing was successfully identified and tracked. 
In summary, three highlights of the proposed system were revealed. 
First, the system was built as an autonomous rebar inspection pipeline 
by developing automated algorithms, such as automated scale calibra -
tion. Furthermore, the autonomous pipeline benefits construction 
management. For example, qualified inspectors can be partially replaced 
with video recorders. Second, a semantic rebar 3D model was estab -
lished by integrating DL and CV techniques. For instance, the combi -
nation of DL-based 2D instance segmentation and conventional 3D 
clustering accomplishes rebar segmentation. Consequently, accurate 
and robust information can be utilized for construction management, 
such as inspection and documentation. Third, underlying issues were 
effectively identified and tracked by the DT- and BIM-based information 
systems. This system is essentially based on Scan-vs-BIM methods, i.e., 
the comparison between status DT and intent DT. Furthermore, the 
proposed approach can be extended to other applications, such as ma -
terial inspection of rebar chairs and progress monitoring.

revealed, indicating future research direction. First, unsatisfying
