Developing WSN/BIM-Based Environmental Monitoring
Management System for Parking Garages in Smart Cities
Yu-Cheng Lin 1 and Weng-Fong Cheung, Ph.D. 2
Abstract: In recent years, there have been many serious problems with air pollution (such as hazardous gases) in environmental management
for parking garages of cities. These problems should be continuously improved and enhanced for smart cities. Building information modeling
(BIM) has been revolutionary in construction industry applications; it integrates different kinds of construction information into a 3D digital
model and can be applied in all stages of a project lifecycle. Furthermore, the wireless sensor network (WSN) is an important key technology
in Internet of Things (IoT) development. In order to improve the performance of environmental monitoring management of parking garages
in smart cities, this study proposes a planning approach for integration of WSN and BIM technologies and develops an advanced monitoring
and control system for underground parking garage environment monitoring, adopting WSN and BIM technologies for digital twin (DT)
application of Industry 4.0 (I4.0). Many WSN nodes with sensing and control capabilities were placed in a parking garage to collect carbon
monoxide (CO), temperature, and humidity conditions in real time. All the collected information was transferred by a WSN self-constituted
network, and the different location conditions were integrated and dynamically displayed on corresponding spatial components of a 3D BIM
model. The proposed system was applied in a case study of a parking garage in Taipei to verify the system and demonstrate its effectiveness in
practice. The results demonstrate that the proposed system is a visually effective monitoring system for environmental monitoring management.

Introduction
Rapid growth of traffic and vehicles makes air pollution an in-
creasing concern in cities. There are many parking garages built
in underground basements, which are closed spaces where the air
is frequently polluted by vehicles. In garage management, people
often focus attention on parking space optimization but ignore the
air quality problems. During rush hour, high quantities of vehicles
are moving in the closed space, constantly emitting exhaust gases.
As the analysis displayed, the main composition of the polluting
gas emitted by vehicles was CO, which is a colorless and odorless
gas and is not easy to measure. According to the Journal of the
American Medical Association (JAMA), 1,500 people die annually
due to accidental CO gas exposure, and an additional 10,000 seek
medical attention ( Modic 2003 ). CO poisoning may be possible
after inhaling only a 35-ppm concentration of CO gas, and if in-
haling the hazardous gas continues, unconsciousness and death
may result. Moreover, in many cases, garage air quality is main-
tained by forced air circulation or air conditioner units. However,
long-term operation of this equipment will result in high elec-
tricity costs and energy waste; hence, a smart and proactive gas and
environmental monitoring and control system is needed for main-
taining air quality and people ’s health and safety.
The evolution progress of smart cities depends on the develop-
ment of industry and technical levels. Industry 4.0 (I4.0) has focused
on enhancing industry intellectualization, which integrates existing
technologies and processes and then builds an adaptive, resource-
efficient, intelligent production process. The Consortium II fact
sheet (Consortium II 2013) defined I4.0 as “the integration of com-
plex physical machinery and devices with networked sensors and
software, used to predict, control, and plan for better business and
societal outcomes. I4.0 facilitates interconnection and computeriza-
tion into the traditional industry, which makes automatic and flex-
ible adaptation of the production chain and provides new types
of services and business models of interaction in the value chain ”
(Lu 2017 ).
During the modular structured smart factories of I4.0, a
cyber-physical system (CPS) creates a virtual copy of the physical
production and makes decentralized decisions ( Antonio 2018 ).
CPS is a mechanism that is controlled or monitored by computer-
based algorithms, tightly integrated with the internet and its users
(Monostori et al. 2016 ). Digital twin (DT) is another concept as-
sociated with I4.0; it is a dynamic virtual representation of a physi-
cal object or system across its lifecycle, using real-time data to
enable understanding, learning, and reasoning ( Bolton et al. 2018 ),
and has a similar essence to CPS. Furthermore, DT has the potential
to gather and analyze information at different scales, facilitate more
efficient construction processes, and allow for informed decision-
making, which will ultimately promote a safer environment at re-
duced costs and improved efficiency ( Kan and Anumba 2019 ).
The two key factors of I4.0 are integration and interoperability
(Chen et al 2008; Romero and V ernadat 2016). Integrating computer
and network systems, I4.0 achieves seamless cooperation across

Building information modeling (BIM) is a relatively new inno-
vation for the construction and civil engineering industry ( Tulubas
Gokuc and Arditi 2017 ; Ma et al. 2018 ; Hosseini et al. 2018 ).
BIM can not only enhance productivity in particular activities but
can build system capabilities that enable new ways of working
(Holmström et al. 2014 ). One of the characteristics of BIM tech-
nology is that it can be communicated by 3D illustration among
participants. This characteristic is why BIM is selected as the main
technology in the study. Also, wireless sensor networks (WSNs) are
one of the important key technologies for Internet of Things (IoT)
development. The sensing and control capabilities of WSN devices
enable them to be placed in locations of the garage to collect carbon
monoxide (CO), temperature, and humidity conditions for applica-
tions of DT.
In this study, a planning approach for integration of WSN and
BIM technologies is proposed and a WSN/BIM-based environmen-
tal monitoring system is developed for underground parking garage
environmental management. A smart WSN system integrated with
environmental monitoring, hazardous gas detection, and risk status
capability is set up in the garage to monitor the CO gas concen-
tration and maintain environmental safety. Data on CO concentra-
tion, temperature, and humidity (environmental conditions) of the
different locations are collected and visually represented on a dig-
ital building model in real time. The locational risk status is de-
signed to be dynamically indicated by color, from blue to red, and
the manager can master the overall safety and environmental con-
ditions of the garage via the active model. The proposed system
provides a presentation of the DT model of I4.0 and ubiquitous
computing for smart city application. Moreover, in view of integra-
tion and interoperability, the WSN provides active information
measured from the real world and enables the BIM model to be
a real-time DT system and show the real status; on the other side,
the BIM provides a digital structure so that the WSN data can
be correctly embedded and show the dimensional relation of the
occurring position for observation. The multifocus information in-
tegrated BIM model also demonstrates a real-time dimensional re-
lation of risk priority and overall environmental information. The
integrated system provides not only a solution for DT application
but a meaningful, efficient system for safety management and
decision-making in an emergency.
The proposed system can enhance the regular management and
decision-making in a critical situation. Additionally, an intelligent
function was designed that can remove the hazardous gas automati-
cally when detecting critical conditions. The main purposes of this
research include: (1) to propose a planning approach for the inte-
gration of WSN and BIM technologies for parking garages of smart
cities, (2) to develop a WSN/BIM-based environmental monitoring
system, and (3) to discuss the advantages and disadvantages of the
developed system applied in the Taiwan case study.
Literature Review
Smart city refers to the use of various information technologies
or innovative ideas to integrate urban systems and services to im-
prove the efficiency of resource utilization and optimize urban
management and services. The foundational concepts are instru-
mented, interconnected, and intelligent ( Harrison et al. 2010 ).
Instrumented refers to sources of near-real-time real-world data
from both physical and virtual sensors; interconnected means the
integration of those data into an enterprise computing platform and
the communication of such information to the various city services;
intelligent refers to the inclusion of complex analytics, modeling,
optimization, and visualization in the operational business process
to make better operational decisions.
With the onset of Industry 4.0, many advanced technologies
have brought great potential applications for smart city environ-
mental quality management. The DT is one of the main technolo-
gies associated with Industry 4.0 and is defined as “a unified system
model that can coordinate architecture, mechanical, electrical, soft-
ware, verification, and other discipline-specific models across the
system lifecycle” (Bajaj et al. 2016 ). A DT can be used to simulate
the physical asset or operation for various purposes. Many sensors
with various functions can be adopted for understanding the real
situation, exploiting a real-time synchronization of the sensed data
originating from the field level, and deciding between a set of ac-
tions with the focus to orchestrate and execute the whole produc-
tion system in an optimal way ( Negri et al. 2017). WSNs are one of
the core technologies for IoT solutions. A number of independent
sensor nodes comprise a network that is able to monitor, commu-
nicate, and even judge and react to external circumstances ( Cheung
et al. 2018). With the advantages of small dimension, low price and
power consumption, and ability in environmental detection and ac-
tive networking, WSNs are widely applied for environmental mon-
itoring, building intelligentization, smart city functions, etc. BIM
provides digitalized and parameterized building information in a
3D representative model to enable professionals to efficiently plan,
design, construct, and manage by visualization ( Cerovsek 2011 ).
The definition of BIM by the US National Institute of Building
Sciences (NIBS) is “A digital representation of physical and func-
tional characteristics of a facility” (NIBS 2019). In intelligent build-
ing and smart city application, BIM provides a powerful platform to
integrate multifocus building information, such as asset spatial re-
lations, regional conditions, and the simulation of asset movement
and the construction process. A combined system using WSNs and
BIM can establish a real-time active model that works as an appli-
cation of a DT and provides efficient information for managers to
make decisions. Moreover, the BIM model contains assets and spa-
tial information, which provides a framework for the IoT data to be
organized and analyzed, and the combined information is meaning-
ful to the building operations and management ( Haines 2016 ).
In environmental air quality research of parking garages,
Papakonstantinou et al. ( 2003) examined ventilation effectiveness
by computational and experimental investigation. They found that
an efficient ventilation system will result in a better quality of air
inside buildings, and reducing CO levels in occupational spaces is
an important issue for short- and long-term health problems. Zhao
et al. ( 2017) evaluated the seasonal patterns of particulate matter
PM10, PM2.5, and PM1.0 in a naturally ventilated underground
residential parking garage using field measurements collected at
various times over a 1-year period. The results showed the PM10
and PM2.5 concentration exceeding the limit of the Chinese stan-
dard by eightfold during the spring, fall, and winter using natural
ventilation. Kim et al. ( 2007) proposed a time-resolved measure-
ment method, evaluated air toxin levels within an urban parking
garage, and assessed the influence of vehicle activity and type
on those levels. Carbon monoxide (CO), particle-bound polycyclic
aromatic hydrocarbons (pPAH), and volatile organic compounds
(VOCs) were measured and calculated. The influence of traffic
was quantified using longitudinal models, and the pollutant coef-
ficients provide an indication of the average contribution to air pol-
lution from vehicle sources. This information demonstrates that the

Some studies have been done on the integration of WSN and
BIM technologies. Tang et al. ( 2019a) comprehensively reviewed
97 papers from 14 architecture, engineering, and construction

approach of BIM-IoT integration. Farghaly et al. ( 2018) explored
the asset owner requirements from BIM in the operation and main-
tenance stage from an asset management (AM) perspective and out-
put asset-consuming energy information management (ACE-IM)

Cao et al. ( 2018) used the structural social capital perspective
and a longitudinal data set on BIM implementation practices in the
construction industry in Shanghai (from 2007 to 2015) to explore
the structural characteristics of the project-based collaboration net-
works. Tang et al. ( 2019b) explored the direct relationship between
BIM implementation and the performance of off-site manufactur-
ing projects through their key influential factors. Awolusi et al.
(2019) provided an evaluation of the potential applications of wear-
able sensing devices and IoT for the continuous collection, analy-
sis, and monitoring of construction workers ’ safety metrics. Dave
et al. ( 2018) developed a platform that integrates the built envi-
ronment data with IoT sensors campuswide to provide informa-
tion regarding energy usage, occupancy, and user comfort. Kan and
Anumba ( 2019) presented a comprehensive, up-to-date literature
review and provided critical analysis of existing research into DT
applications. Fernández-Caramés et al. ( 2018) developed a system
to allow for identifying and tracking the pipes of a ship through
their construction cycle. Giel and Issa ( 2016) developed a frame-
work for building-owner organizations to use in the assessment
of their BIM competency and identify 66 critical factors about
the evaluation of owners ’ BIM competency using the Delphi tech-
nique. Zhou et al. ( 2019) proposed a cyber-physical-system-based
safety monitoring system to simulate and monitor the hoisting pro-

construction and successfully implemented the system in Wuhan
Metro’s road tunnel. Gao et al. ( 2019) proposed a scalable data ac-

buildings, smart communities, and smart cities that enables the uti-
lization of the data housed in separate building systems for inno-
vative IoT use.
Based on the literature surveyed, BIM-sensor technologies have
been applied to facility automation and energy saving since 2007,
propelling the progress of building intelligence . In recent decades,
owing to advances in wireless sensor technologies, the relative re-
search issues and applications have become more profound and di-
versified. Due to sensor size and price reduction, a large number of
sensors can be utilized to collect a huge amount of information,
which supports more extensive and detailed analysis of smart cities
such as IoT and Internet of V ehicles (IoV) or the human/object in-
teraction detection in virtual reality (VR) and augmented reality
(AR) applications. In Industry 4.0, WSNs provide real-time infor-
mation for DT models in the manufacturing and construction indus-
tries. The provided multifocus information enables the manager to
understand the real situation of the object or system and respond to
various changes accurately and quickly, thereby improving the op-
eration of the system while enhancing efficiency and human safety.
Although there is some previous academic and practical re-
search regarding integration of BIM and WSN technologies based
on the literature review, few studies focus on the initial planning
and considerations for WSN/BIM-based environmental monitoring
management for parking garages in smart cities. Therefore, this
study will focus on the aforementioned content for effective envi-
ronmental monitoring management.
Methodology
It is difficult to measure air quality and detect the presence of haz-
ardous gases in underground parking garages because gas concen-
trations and flow conditions respond dynamically to the quantity
and movement of vehicles. This is compounded by CO gas being
colorless and odorless and requiring a specific gas sensor to be
measured. Moreover, the overall conditions at several locations are
not easy to measure simultaneously by a single gas detector. With a
lack of information about the status of each location and a smart
control system, the manager cannot easily confirm where the high-
concentration gas is coming from and has to keep the air condition-
ing equipment running for a long time to reduce the risk posed by
the hazardous gases, leading to high electricity and maintenance
costs. Efficient management of the overall garage conditions and
the control of the air conditioning equipment will be a challenge.
The integrated DT becomes an important solution for smart
cities. The purpose of DT is to provide a digital virtual illustration
for the actual situation and enhance the dynamic change manage-
ment for smart cities. Many related technologies can assist in the
application of DT for smart cities. Specially, the assistance of BIM
technology can provide visual 3D communication for DT. With the
use of IoT technologies, much information can be acquired from
the devices located in the selected areas. Using integrated BIM and
IoT technologies, the 3D visual illustration can enhance the man-
agement of DT for smart cities (Fig. 1). The major purpose of this
study is to propose an approach for DT integrated with BIM and
IoT technologies for parking garages of smart cities. The following
is a detailed description of this approach.
In this study, BIM models of DT are used to show different con-
trol statuses and illustrate where the identified problems are located.
Therefore, it is important to remove unnecessary information from
the final as-built model for a management-based BIM model. Usu-
ally, the performance of a BIM-based information system will be
reduced if the BIM model is too detailed and complicated, so
the degree of detail of BIM model development should be low
for management. The suggested level of development (LOD) of
the BIM model is LOD 200 or LOD 300 for suitable management.
The aforementioned process at the initial stage of the operation
phase can enhance the performance of a BIM-based information
system for management purposes.
WSN is selected as the IoT solution for monitoring and record-
ing the physical conditions of the environmental monitoring man-
agement in the study. With the use of WSN technologies, essential
information can be acquired for monitoring management of devices
located in the selected areas. It is necessary to identify the require-
ments of monitoring information acquisition initially. The monitor-
ing information includes the monitoring contents and frequency.
The monitoring content will influence all the plans for the environ-
mental monitoring management. Therefore, it is essential to decide
which monitoring content needs to be detected in the beginning.
Furthermore, it is suggested to consider the setup quantity and lo-
cation of the WSN devices for monitoring management. The reason
is that the setup cost is too high if there are many WSN devices, but
the performance of monitoring management will be ineffective if
there are only a few WSN devices. Fig. 2 shows the workflow of
integrated WSN and BIM technologies for parking garages of the
smart cities in the study.
Regarding platform development for environmental monitoring
management, a web-based system is selected as the DT monitoring
system to allow all related participants to log in and refer to the
monitoring information in order to make suitable decisions. Based
on the aforementioned major requirement considerations, the DT
monitoring system is developed via integration of a WSN and

System Design and Development

establish the DT system in the study. The WSN system was con-

hardware control. Every node is designed to have a unique function
according to the required duty in the WSN system; the interaction
of various functions enables the intelligent capability of the system.
The other subsystem, BIM (DT), is a digital 3D model of the build-

WSN Topologies and Nodes Development
Topology Design
The WSN provides the DT system with information for updating
the status in real time. The WSN is configured by a host and sensor
nodes. In general, for a typical WSN, topologies can be classified as
star, tree, and mesh (Farahani 2018 ). In the star topology, each
node is connected with the coordinator (host) individually and
the data is transferred directly to the host with no node relay, there-
fore resulting in short and restrictive communication distances. The
tree topology is comprised of several star networks in a clustered
arrangement, but the communication may be disconnected if the
cluster-head node has failed. In a mesh topology network, every
node cooperates with each other; the communication can be ex-
tended to a longer distance by ad hoc node relays of nodes. In
addition, the mesh topology can recover the network automatically
by self-connecting capabilities if any internal node has failed. In
this study, mesh topology was designed as the backbone of net-
work, as it can provide a longer communication distance and data
transfer stability (Fig. 6).
Developing Functional WSN Nodes
The hardware framework of the sensor node was constructed by the
Microsoft .NET Gadgeteer embedded system and the functions
were designed by the .NET Micro Framework of Microsoft (.NET
MF). The WSN system is comprised of different functional nodes.
In this study, four types of WSN nodes were developed, including
the coordinator, sensors node , router,a n d control node . The co-
ordinator is connected with a computer and acts as a host, which
is in charge of launching the network and receives the data sent

in monitoring. To enable the node to sense gas and environmental
conditions and communicate the information, the following sensor
components and modules were designed (Fig. 7):
1. CO gas sensor: The MQ-7 CO gas-sensing component ( Hanwei
Eletronics 2019 ) is equipped to detect CO gas and transfer a
proportional direct current output.
2. Temperature- and humidity-detecting module: The DHT-
11(Mouser Electronics 2018 ) module was adopted to detect
the temperature and humidity conditions.
3. Wireless module: The Zigbee CC2530 wireless module ( Texas
Instruments 2019) was selected to construct the WSN backbone,
and the mesh topology was developed.
4. Main chip board: The Microsoft .NET Gadgeteer board with an
ARM Cortex-M4 processor provides the hardware platform for
the embedded system.
5. Power supply module: The power supply comprises 18,650
lithium ion batteries that provide the operating power source
for the nodes.
The node mainboard comprises the components by cable lines,
and during operation the environmental condition data is measured
and then transferred by the Zigbee module. The data recording fre-
quency of the nodes was programmed to routinely operate at a low
frequency (0.2 Hz) for power saving; when the emergent situation
is detected, the frequency will increase to 3 Hz automatically, as the
information is important for future investigations. Meanwhile, the
sensor node will judge the abnormal condition smartly and inform
the control node to switch on safety devices, which reduces the risk
posed by the accumulating CO gas and enhances human safety.

Build and Command BIM Model
For the purpose of the DT development, the BIM model of the
garage site must first be established. The model provides a digital
and visual representation for the application of the DT to monitor-
ing real-time location-specific conditions. The CO gas concentra-
tion, temperature, and humidity conditions collected by the sensor
nodes are indicated by dynamic color-coded model components.
The risk status of the monitoring location, varying from high to
low, is indicated by a red-to-blue color gradient according to the
returned CO gas concentration. The comfort level in terms of tem-
perature and humidity is also indicated by the green-to-red color
gradient. Hence, both the safety and environmental information
at various monitoring locations can be displayed simultaneously.
The real-time active BIM model provides a virtual DT function
to represent the actual safety and comfort level in the monitored
garage, which also greatly enhances the efficiency of management
of the quality of the environment.

System Integration
The developed DT system comprises three categories of infor-
mation, including the BIM model, WSN, and database, as well as
integrated and C# applications. The system also has an optimized
user interface: in addition to an active BIM model for the inte-
gration of location-specific conditions, the measured gas concen-
tration, temperature, and humidity values and relative X-Y curve
diagram are also displayed on a specific window in real time. When
the system is activated, the coordinator launches the network first.
The other nodes later join the network in sequence and begin to
automatically return data. The data is sent from sensor nodes in
various measuring locations in the garage, communicated through
the node relay. Each data set is then checked to confirm which
location the data is from, and then the relative component color
will be displayed corresponding to the location and measured
condition. Moreover, the control commands of BIM enable the
model to be moved, rotated, cut, zoomed in/out, or adjusted for the
best view sight/angle for monitoring by the user (Fig. 13). The mea-
sured data, including the transferring time, node address, signal
strength, gas concentration, temperature, and humidity, is then
stored on a Microsoft Access database for long-term observation
and tracking. A screenshot of the integrated DT system is shown
in Fig. 14.

Performance Test of Gas Detection
This test checks the system performance by simulating the oc-
currence of a hazardous gas. First, a notebook installed the DT
system of the parking garage model and was linked to a coordinator
for receiving the detection data from the sensor nodes. Then, the
WSN network was established by the coordinator and the joined
WSN nodes. Staff moved the sensor nodes to different monitoring
locations in the garage and provided feedback by handheld radio
when the sensor nodes were set up. In the next step, the combus-
tible gas was emitted by a lighter held close to the sensor node,
which simulated the emission of hazardous gases so as to check
whether the system could immediately react to the emergency sit-

The experimental results displayed that the proposed system
reacted to the hazardous gas in approximately 1 –2 s and a red-color
warning immediately indicated the abnormal areas. Meanwhile,
the control node trigged the ventilator and flashing alarm auto-
matically, which eliminated the hazardous gas accumulation and
prevented nearby persons from inhalation injuries. The results dem-
onstrated that the system could execute the monitoring and removal
tasks for hazardous gases, significantly reducing the risk in parking
garage management (Fig. 17).
Signal Transmission Test
A limitation in communication was introduced to test the longest
transfer distance of the WSN signal. The sensor node was carried

the signal became weak and disappeared. The test results demon-
strate that the maximum signal communication distance in a straight
line was approximately 250 m between two nodes, with the signal
strength decreasing approximately 0.3 dBm per meter. However,
if a router node was installed between the sensor nodes, the signal
would then be relayed, resulting in the transmission distance in-
creasing to more than 450 m (Fig. 18).

Evaluation and Discussion
This paper proposed a DT application for garage safety and envi-
ronmental quality management via integration of WSN and BIM
technologies. The proposed system was tested and demonstrates
the applicability in underground parking garages. The function
and performance were tested to determine the capabilities of the
system. The proposed system provides an advanced method to en-
hance the environmental quality and safety as well as decrease the
power consumption and operating costs. The proposed method can
be applied to other infrastructure management applications.
In the case study, the WSN communication distance was 250 m.
However, the signal became attenuated when the communication
route was nonlinear. The test results displayed an approximate
15%–20% loss of signal strength after a 90° turn, with similar re-
sults for both horizontal and vertical directions. Additionally, the
WSN signal can pass through a concrete wall, although the signal
strength will decrease depending on the material. In the test case,

The installation of a WSN is easy and convenient. Just switch on
the power of the node and place it in the desired measuring loca-
tion. The node will link with the WSN network and begin to return
data automatically, demonstrating flexibility and applicability.
In the duration assessment, the WSN power source was either
batteries packs or an external power supply. If the node is in-
stalled on a moving object or creature or the external power is
not available, then individual batteries packs are required. In this
study, the power supplied by six 18,650 lithium batteries packs
enabled the WSN node to operate for approximately 1 week. An
extra power source is needed for longer operation, and thus the
power supply and battery capacity are important concerns in
WSN operation.
In view of the cost assessment, the cost for each of the proposed
nodes is about $80 –$100, and a basic measurement (single point
detection) requires a sensor node and the coordinator, which costs
nearly $200 (not including the price of a computer). If the detection
point number is increased, the additional cost is $100 for each node.
When compared with the most commonly used detector (the hand-
held detector, which has a price ranging from $200 to $400 each),
if four locations are desired to be simultaneously monitored, the
total cost of the handheld detectors comes to $800 ($200 each), with
additional cost for human operation. If the proposed WSN detection
system is used instead, the cost is reduced to approximately $500
(a coordinator with four sensor nodes), resulting in nearly 40% cost
savings as compared to the handheld detectors.
In view of expandability, the proposed WSN system involves
incorporation of individual sensing components and a wireless com-
munication module. The design enables the node to be equipped
with different types of sensing components as required and yet still
operate within the original wireless backbone. The detection func-
tion can be easily modified or expanded for application to different
task requirements.

as-built BIM model is developed at the end of construction
phase, it is necessary to understand and decide which elements

parking management system.
Conclusions
Currently, there are many related technologies suitable for applica-
tion of DT for smart cities. Specially, the assistance of BIM tech-
nology can provide visual 3D communication for DT. Furthermore,
it is easy to obtain the required information from the devices located
in the selected areas using WSN technologies for IoT application in
smart cities. Although there are some previous research or practice
case studies using BIM and WSN technologies, few studies focus on
the approach for the integration of WSN and BIM technologies for
environmental monitoring management in parking garages of smart
cities. The proposed approach in the study has major differences and
contributions compared with previous researches.
This study proposed an advanced monitoring and control sys-
tem for underground garage environment management adopting
WSN and BIM technologies. The WSN acts as a nervous system
for hazards detection, working to maintain the safety of the garage
environment. The collected WSN data enables the BIM model to
actively reflect the current state in the garage and provide a visually
dynamic interface for management. The integrated system provides
an efficient platform in garage environment monitoring while main-
taining safety. This model can also be easily transplanted to other
monitoring systems for further application.
In analysis of the interoperability of the integrating system of
WSN and BIM, the WSN provides the active information measured
from the real world and enables the BIM model to be a real-time DT
system and show the real status for management; on the other
hand, the BIM provides a digital structure for the WSN data to
be correctly embedded and shows the dimensional relation of the
monitoring position for observation and judgement. Moreover, the
color gradient displays the risk degree of multiple locations, so that
the risk priority of the monitoring locations can be visually dis-
played by the model to provide an awareness of risk distribution
of the whole space, which enhances the safety management, emer-
gency response, and decision-making.
The DT may contain many assets of different sizes which are
closely distributed on the model and may obstruct each other. Addi-
tionally, many conditions and statuses have to display on the model
area simultaneously, increasing observation obstruction. How to
properly arrange the layout will be an important concern in system
design. Furthermore, the proposed system achieves remote, real-
time, and multipoint conditions monitoring. Meanwhile, the spatial
relation and environmental condition information is provided simul-
taneously, and the observation and analysis of gas concentration and
flow status can provide the information needed to determine the pri-
ority or the key position of garage air pollution alleviation mecha-
nisms. The designed smart functions enable the system to start the
ventilation and safety devices automatically when high concentra-
tions of CO gases are detected, which can significantly enhance
the safety of the garage environment and eliminate human injuries.
Additionally, the operating and maintenance costs of the garage are
also reduced. All the conditions are dynamically displayed by cor-
responding spatial components and indicate the location risk status
by changing colors. The real-time information can be used for regu-
lar management and emergency response. Finally, the intelligent
function of the WSN system was designed to remove hazards auto-
matically when detecting abnormal situations. In the future, more
related IoT technologies for underground garage environment man-
agement can be adopted and improved for advanced control and
management. Furthermore, the applications of BIM can be inte-
grated with artificial intelligence (AI) technologies for visually ef-
fective smart environment management in the public area of smart
cities.
