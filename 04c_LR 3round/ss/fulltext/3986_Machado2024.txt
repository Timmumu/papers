ABSTRACT  
Civil infrastructures inevitably undergo a degradation process due to extended periods of operation, frequent 
service requests, and weathering. This paper proposes a methodology for predicting necessary interventions and 
presents a database of inspection information for tunnels. The model developed correlates performance loss, 
measured by the tunnel serviceability index ( TSI ), to defects detected through a 2D grid composed of classified 
unit cells converted into a virtual representation. To support the framework developed, intervention time esti -
mates were compared to stochastic degradation models presented in the literature. A limit analysis was per -
formed using inverse exponential equations, utilizing data from different sources and supported by a digital 
representation of the analysis structure. This was done to estimate times with unitary inspection data, deterio -
ration mapping control, and data connection to the developed inspection model. A railway tunnel for ore 
transport in northern Brazil was selected as a case study to apply the forecast model, as it showed an accelerated 
deterioration process within a few years of service. The tunnel was divided into four sections that were analyzed 
using the methodology developed through inspection-sensitive analysis. The section with the lowest service level 
( TS 1) matched the geological-geotechnical mapping, which indicated a rock mass with high metamorphic grade 
and susceptibility to weathering agents. The digital model resulted in an inspection digital twin ( IDT ) prototype 
that maps the zones with the greatest problems regarding the degradation process. Metadata is assigned to 
control elements, and there is online connection to a visual database of the tunnel. The target intervention time 
was set at 16 years by the developed structure, which falls within all forecast intervals calculated by the limit 
analyses. The inspection DT enables digital monitoring of the structure under analysis, allowing for efficient and 
optimized management.   
1. Introduction 
Tunnel constructions are known for their long service life compared 
to other structures, but several factors, such as material quality and the 
impact of climate change can reduce their durability ( Sandrone and 
Labiouse, 2011 ; Bakhsi and Nasri, 2019). These infrastructures are 
specifically designed to facilitate interactions between the structural 
elements and the geotechnical material, with a service life of up to 100 
years, and many transportation structures remain operational for more 
than half of that period ( Hu et al., 2019; Huang et al., 2017; FTA, 1997 ). 
Over time, the loads acting on the structure can vary significantly, and 
the structural changes can result from weathering and environmental 
factors. 
Degradation processes are associated with tunnels, such as chemical 
interactions between the tunnel and the environment, aging and 
wastage of materials, besides accidental events such as mechanical 
shocks, fires, and explosions. These processes are connected to the 
concepts of robustness and resilience, which refer to the tunnel ability to 
withstand and recover from damage while avoiding collapses ( Stochino 
et al., 2019 ).

effective inspection programs, routine maintenance, and rehabilitation

As stated by McKibbins et al. (2009) , the gradual decay of tunnel 
infrastructures can lead to a premature end or reduction in their useful 
life, resulting in a disruption of traffic flow, economic losses, and the 
need for costly remediation and replacement works. The structural 
decay of tunnels can be monitored by verifying the evolution of various 
responses, such as displacements, leaks, and cracks. These responses 
serve as indicators of the structural performance degradation. 
When evaluating the structural performance of an operational tun -
nel, it is crucial to accurately determine its service condition and to 
identify any deviations from the design assumptions ( Yuan et al., 2012 ). 
Therefore, this paper aims to introduce a methodology for assessing the 
service state of tunnels and a flexible approach to predicting rehabili -
tation times. This method is applied to a tunnel located in the northern 
region of Brazil, which is used for transporting ore via rail. The ultimate 
goal is to provide insights into the condition of the tunnel and make 
informed decisions regarding maintenance and rehabilitation in -
terventions to ensure its continued safe and efficient operation. 
The digitalization and massive data generation drive the need for 
updated methodologies in underground infrastructures. Collaborative 
use of technologies is essential for the current requirements of virtuali -
zation, control, and the ’ intelligence ’ of tunnels ( Huang et al., 2021 ). 
The process of integrating data that mirrors the real-time conditions of a 
physical system into its virtual counterpart shares certain similarities 
with the concepts of a digital twin ( DT ) and a cyber-physical system 
( CPS ) ( Anumba and Roofigari-Esfahan, 2020 ). 
A digital twin is a highly realistic digital replica of a physical prod -
uct, system, or process. The underlying technology aims to achieve an 
integrated modeling of the system or product by using asset information 
to mirror the life of its corresponding twin across multiple scales and 
uncertainties ( Dan et al., 2021; Glaessgen and Stargel, 2012 ). The 
resulting digital twin facilitates operation management throughout the 
asset lifecycle, enabling real-time monitoring and control of its opera -
tion ( Fig. 2 ) (Broo et al., 2010). 
Recent advances in digitalization have created new opportunities for 
extracting data, insights, and situational awareness from the perfor -
mance of physical systems, enabling better management and mainte -
nance of critical infrastructure ( Boyes & Watson, 2022 ). Structural 
health diagnostic methods based on data are increasingly becoming the 
norm for structural diagnosis ( Housner et al., 1997 ). 
The Operation and Maintenance (O & M) phase is the stage in the life 
cycle that should ensure proper service, meet user requirements, and 
maintain reliability throughout the structure ’ s lifespan. The perfor -
mance, technological control, and diagnostic data of large civil con -
struction assets, such as tunnels, have transitioned from optional to 
mandatory due to the high costs associated with their malfunction or 
failure. 
The data essential for constructing the virtualization model in this 
study is acquired through visual inspections, which entail manual pro -
cessing and integration. Consequently, the absence of automated syn -
chronization justifies categorizing the digital twin developed in this 
research as an initial prototype. The resulting virtualization adheres to 
the fundamental principle of digitally connecting the representation to 
the physical asset, thereby fulfilling the criteria of a DT. Defining the 
concept remains a significant engineering challenge for the future, as it 
has not been widely established. 
2. Deterioration of tunnels 
Deterioration is a common occurrence in all types of constructions, 
but it poses a particularly significant challenge to infrastructures that 
must maintain a minimum level of performance over an extended ser -
vice period as defined in the project. The deterioration process is pro -
gressive in nature and is typically associated with the operating time and 
external conditions. Defects tend to propagate throughout the service 
life of the infrastructure, as illustrated in Fig. 3 .

2.1. Defects and service classification 
The methodology for generating conditional state data is a funda -
mental component for predicting the years of service of infrastructure, 
projecting monitoring campaigns, investing in proper maintenance, and 
optimizing the life cycle cost of infrastructure ( LCC ). This infrastructure 
condition is obtained by comparing data from inspection campaigns and 
monitoring with specific project conditions. 
The development of new technologies has increasingly provided 
greater control of activities in the O & M stage for underground in -
frastructures ( Huang et al., 2021 ). The use of technologies such as deep 
learning for recognition and interpretation, as well as point cloud 
scanning, generates more precise insights into the structure ’ s behavior. 
Thus, the creation of a DT based on the integration of this new data and 
historical informations and inspections avaible into models and the 
generation of a data layer within a structural timeline, as presented in 
this research, represents a significant advancement for tunnel 
virtualization. 
The development of a maintenance program ( Fig. 4 ) requires quan -
tifying and classifying the degree of degradation and problems in the 
infrastructure to understand the extent of their impact. Without clear 
pre-established criteria for distinguishing between different degradation 
categories, the classification process can become subjective. Therefore, 
it is crucial to establish clear and objective criteria for classifying 
infrastructure degradation to ensure consistency in the maintenance 
program development process. 
The control plan for the structure was designed by engineering 
consultants hired by the tunnel administrators, as featured in the case 
study of a highway ore transport tunnel in Brazil. However, it was never 
implemented or the data from these inspection processes were lost. 
Therefore, the authors aimed to propose an efficient methodology to 
adapt the results of the available and effectively conducted inspections 
within the analysis tunnel. The company ’ s database only includes visual 
defect recognition campaigns and minor maintenance activities per -
formed every 5 years. 
The Federal Highway Administration (FHWA) has developed a 
classification system for evaluating the deterioration of tunnel infra -
structure ( Table 1 ). The system assigns each component of the 
infrastructure to one of four degradation states, based on its condition. 
The FHWA ’ s specification manual for national tunnels provides guide -
lines for this classification process. The manual also includes a set of 
units of measure for each element of the infrastructure, and specifies the 
fractions of these units that apply to each degradation state. The final 
report on the infrastructure condition presents this information. 
Wang (2018) also developed a deterioration classification system, 
which, as FHWA, comprises four degradation states. However, Wang ’ s 
system classifies each type of defect (such as corrosion, cracking, 
delamination, etc.) differently, as outlined in Table 2 . In contrast to the 
FHWA ’ s system, Wang ’ s classification represents the analyzed structure 
areas, as shown in Fig. 5 . The methodology begins by dividing the area 
under study into a grid composed of smaller regions of equal size.

2.2. Prediction models 
Forecast models are widely used to estimate the time required for 
maintenance campaigns and infrastructure interventions. However, it is 
essential to recognize that the systematization and description of future 
events using calculation models only generate estimated results, and are 
not exact answers to prediction problems. The complexity of infra -
structure interactions and the environment is not fully captured by such 
models. Therefore, the relevance of results derived from the prediction 
of tunnel infrastructure aging is directly linked to the quality, quantity of 
data available, the prediction model to be used and its development. 
There are two types of degradation models: probabilistic models and 
deterministic models. Probabilistic prediction models take into account 
the randomness of structures and external factors. These models have an 
advantage in solving problems associated with uncertainties. However, 
the predicted changes in performance of tunnels using probabilistic 
models may be subject to randomness ( Huang et al., 2017 ). In contrast, 
deterministic models can be obtained using mechanics, experiments, or 
a combination of both. Models that rely on experiments can be influ -
enced by various factors, leading to result variability and inaccurate 
descriptions of tunnel performance ( Xiao et al., 2020 ). 
A tunnel exhibiting a concave performance loss behavior experiences 
a significant decline in performance within a few years, requiring a 
shorter rehabilitation time. Conversely, a convex behavior indicates a 
stabler condition of the structure, with minimal influence from weath -
ering agents and service demands. An S-shaped behavior represents an 
intermediate situation between the concave and convex behaviors. 
Empirical data based on engineering experience and reasonable as -
sumptions suggest that tunnel infrastructure exhibits typical long-term 
performance loss behaviors ( Fig. 6 ) due to deterioration over time.

in-service behaviors of tunnel infrastructure can be predicted using a 
model based on an inverse exponential function (Eq. (2) .

by the prescribed design standards. This information may be lost or 
disregarded due to various issues with the predetermined construction 
control guidelines. The case study proposes campaigns as shown in

five years for infrastructure associated with basic repair and

The absence of data hinders the outcomes of prediction models. 
Forecasting methodologies need to adapt efficiently to the type of data,

the available information. Consequently, the subsequent forecasting

3.1. Approximate average TSI calculation 
The development of a framework for converting the commonly 
employed classification of tunnel defects into service levels and quan -
tifying the current state of the structure is relevant to the process of 
prediction and decision-making management. The calculation method -
ology for an average TSI using degradation classification data from in -
spection campaigns was verified and validated through comparison with 
stochastic model predictions based on data available in the literature. 
The TSI was calibrated by employing the TSR (Tunnel Serviceability 
Rating), which represents the average assessment rating of the tunnel 
structure ’ s condition provided by various professionals ( Li et al., 2017 ). 
This calibration process inherently involves a degree of subjectivity, 
akin to the classification method introduced by Wang (2018) in the 
degradation grid assessment. Consequently, the primary goal is to 
transform inspection data into quantifiable indicators of structural 
serviceability and subsequently assess the validity of the inverse expo -
nential prediction equation through a stochastic Markov matrix 
approach.

To convert the deterioration classification values provided by Wang 
(2018) into the tunnel serviceability index ( TSI ) proposed by Li et al. 
(2017) , criteria were established. The conversion process allowed for 
calculating the TSI based on the observed deterioration, enabling future 
projections of the tunnel service index and estimating long-term 
degradation. These calculations provide insights into the degradation 
trends over time. 
The monitoring data for the tunnel structure was quantified and 
linked to the corresponding deterioration classes identified using the 
categorization process. The entire monitoring area of the tunnel floor 
slab, measuring 4.5 m x 150 m, was divided into 225 elements that 
experienced significant deterioration over the 20-year service cycles. 
Using the average TSI values associated with each degradation class 
from Table 4 , along with the limits established by Li et al. (2017) from 
Table 3 , and considering the number of elements in each class for 
different years, factors were calculated for the respective years of the 
infrastructure inspection data collection campaigns (as shown in 
Table 6 ). This approach allows quantifying the tunnel performance in 
different service periods by adjusting the curve using an inverse expo -
nential equation. 
The calculation of the tunnel service factor involved quantifying el -
ements in different states of degradation and assigning tunnel service 
factor values to the corresponding deterioration classes. This analysis 
revealed a significant decrease in the performance of the infrastructure 
over a 40-year period. Note that the maximum service limit was assumed 
for the analysis at the start of operations ( TSI
0 
= 5.0). 
Applying Equation (2) , the α and β coefficients that provide the best 
fit to the calculated TSI values were determined through regression 
analysis (refer to Table 5 ). The coefficients obtained for fitting the ser -
vice factors are 19 for α and 1.1 for β , indicating an S-shaped curve. The 
curve was divided into states intervals based on the limits proposed by Li 
et al. (2017) ( Fig. 9 .a) (0 to 5). 
The stochastic curve presented in Fig. 9 .b was developed by Wang 
(2018) using a Markov matrix process, which employed a probabilistic 
state change matrix to model the structure service behavior in time. The 
figure depicts the number of elements in each state throughout the 
lifespan of the structure, providing valuable insights into its degradation 
progression. 
When comparing the curve obtained from deterministic calculations 
with the stochastic predictions based on the deterioration matrix pre -
sented by Wang (2018) ( Fig. 9 .b), several observations can be made. The 
transition from State I to State II closely aligns with the upper limit 
forecasted by the Markov curve for State I. The maximum deterioration 
in State III coincides with the point where the TSI degradation curve 
transitions from State II to State III. Furthermore, the endpoint of 
maximum growth in the State IV deterioration curve corresponds to the 
period when the curve transitions from Stage III to Stage IV were 
obtained. 
The results demonstrate a robust correlation between the stochastic 
and deterministic calculation methods, emphasizing the reliability of 
using the TSI as a key indicator for assessing the condition of the tunnel. 
A comparative calculation was conducted to streamline the conversion 
process of the categorized elements in the deterioration state grid into 
estimated mean TSI values. 
An important observation regarding the conversion methodology 
developed, tested for compatibility with alternative prediction ap -
proaches, lies in the change of maximum limits. According to the 
methodology, a tunnel that has all its elements in State I or State IV 
would be classified with TSI values of 4.375 and 0.625, respectively, 
with intervention estimations based on an interval rather than a fixed 
TSI value ( Fig. 9 ). This approach allows for structural improvement 
within the upper limit, as shown in Fig. 1 , while the lower limit may 
continue with the degradation process beyond the methodology 
boundary. 
4. Case study and inspection digital twin model 
The presented methodology was applied using data from visual in -
spections conducted by the railway company in the northern region of 
Brazil. The railway connects the northern and northeastern regions of 
the country for ore transportation ( Fig. 10 ), encompassing various rail 
infrastructures. Among these, Tunnel 01 was selected for the application 
of the framework and the creation of the digital inspection twin. 
4.1. Analysis of a railway tunnel 
The target tunnel, renamed to Tunnel Laboratory 01 ( TL 01 ), exhibits 
significant damage to its shotcrete lining structure, despite its few years 
of operation ( Fig. 11 ). With a length of approximately 740 m on a single 
track, even minor disruptions result in the complete cessation of oper -
ations. The selection of this tunnel was based on a comparison of the 
degradation levels among different tunnels, its geographical location, 
and technical site visits to assess its condition. 
The infrastructure is constructed using the drill and blast method, 
with a lining composed of shotcrete reinforced with fibers. The signifi -
cant damages observed are linked to water infiltration, corrosion and 
oxidation of the mesh or metallic reinforcement, as well as cracking in 
the lining. Certain areas of the inspection grid exhibit two instances of 
deterioration, while one particular area, there are three instances asso -
ciated with observations and inspection reports as documented, leading 
to its classification as state IV. 
The geological mapping is depicted in Fig. 12 . The image illustrates 
the various layers comprising the local geology, with significant pro -
portions of metamorphic rocks, as well as regions displaying heightened 
vulnerability to weathering agents in proximity to the northern 
entrance. The mapping highlights structural alignments of the rock 
formations, groundwater levels, and the tunnel excavation. 
As a result, excavations in metasandstone and soil are expected to 
present higher levels of weathering. The TL 01 is thus divided into three 
sections ( TS ) of 200 m and one of 140 m for applying the methodology.

6. Conclusions 
The results from the application of the methodology for quantifying 
service levels were consistent with the initial hypotheses regarding the 
degradation levels determined from the geological-geotechnical map -
ping. The analysis also identified areas with the highest rates of dete -
rioration, particularly in the section near the north opening ( TS 1), 
which exhibited a service level index ( TSI) of 3.708. Specifically, the 
right wall exhibited the lowest TSI value of 5.542, indicating a higher 
level of deterioration in that particular area. These findings provide 
valuable insights into the areas that require immediate attention and 
targeted maintenance interventions to ensure the long-term perfor -
mance and safety of the tunnel. 
The methodology employed in this study exhibits flexibility, allow -
ing for further improvements in predictions as more data is collected 
about the structure. A visual inspection conducted after ten years of 
tunnel service can provide additional data to calculate the TSI
10 years 
(service index at ten years). The forecast, based on an inverse expo -
nential equation and the proposed quantification of classified data, is 
complemented by stochastic methods. The target intervention time, 
estimated at 16 years by limit analysis, offers the potential to optimize 
the management of the tunnel performance throughout its life cycle and 
to enhance monitoring plans. However, further data collection is still 
required, both for the tunnel under analysis and for other structures, in 
order to improve the calibration of the predictions and to improve their 
accuracy. 
The construction of the inspection digital twin enables the manage -
ment and assessment of the tunnel lifecycle using the latest tools from 
industry 4.0. It leverages the structural knowledge and standardization 
of the information extracted from the actual asset, attributing it to the 
virtual model to create a continuous cycle of updates. In future work, 
efforts will be made to design methods for automating the input of in -
formation and the creation of the model using field data, such as point 
clouds. 
Our proposal is to create limited analyses with more detailed service 
data for a given structure characteristics for future work, to particularize 
the forecast by simple data, and promote increased confidence. The 
performance level database for different structures must be imple -
mented. In addition, we also intend to analyze the next steps of the 
methodology that consist of the regression to calculate α and β in the 
inverse exponential equation to find the shape of the forecast curve.
