ABSTRACT  
The increasing aging of underground pipe networks and the lack of effective inspection technologies present 
considerable challenges for whole life-cycle management of these infrastructures. Modern laser scanning tech -
nology offers a cost-effective and safe means to obtain dense and accurate 3D topographic data of the inner 
surface of pipelines. However, laser scanning point clouds contain substantial noise and outliers, and efficiently 
extracting valuable information for structural and functional mapping remains in its infancy. This paper presents 
an innovative method for fast processing point clouds data of large-diameter pipelines, enabling the accurate 
extraction of geometric features and efficient establishment of geometric digital twin using density-based clus -
tering, fitting and region growing algorithm. Experimental tests were conducted to evaluate the accuracy, effi -
ciency, and feasibility of the proposed method. The results demonstrate that the proposed approach not only 
robustly achieves high accuracy but also maintains high computational efficiency. Additionally, the geometric 
digital twin shows promise as tools for quantitatively assessing structural deformation and blockage defects.   
1. Introduction 
Large diameter pipelines, serving as critical urban infrastructure, are 
extensively employed in urban sewage and water pipeline systems amid 
rapid urbanization. For example, concrete pipes (CP) and reinforced 
concrete pipes (RCP) with diameters larger than 650 mm are commonly 
used in sewer systems ( Sever et al., 2017; United States Environmental 
Protection Agency, 2023 ), while pre-stressed concrete cylinder pipes 
(PCCP) and ductile iron pipes with diameters larger than 750 mm are 
gaining popularity in water supply systems ( Polvi et al., 2018 ). As urban 
pipelines continue to expand gradually, automating their condition 
assessment has become increasingly difficult due to structure deterio -
ration under hidden underground and intricate service environments ( Li 
et al., 2022a; Li and Feng, 2022; Huang et al., 2023 ). 
Cross-sectional deformation, or ovality, serves as a critical perfor -
mance indicator for pipelines ( Loganathan et al., 2022; Sun et al., 2022 ), 
as required by Chinese code ( CJJ 181 – 2012, 2012 ) and American 
standard ( ASTM F-1216 – 22, 2022; DNS-OS-F101, 2012 ). For instance, 
cracks may happen when deformation reach approximately 2 – 3 % of the 
concrete pipe diameter ( Bai and Bai, 2005; García and Moore, 2016; 
Munro et al., 2009; Xu et al., 1998 ). If radial deformation exceeds this 
threshold, the pipeline may be unable to withstand vertical loads due to 
reduced stiffness from pipe wall fracture, leading to larger cracking, 
spalling, and eventually pipeline fracture failure (
´
Ekes, 2018; Haktanir 
et al., 2007 ), as illustrated in Fig. 1 ( Tang, 2023 ). In addition, presence 
of ovality in a pipeline can significantly shorten its lifespan and impair 
its overall performance ( Hawari et al., 2018 ). Modern inspection tech -
nologies for pipeline internal surface can be classified into three cate -
gories: image-based visual inspections, acoustic methods, and laser- 
based scanning techniques. Image-based visual inspection technologies 
(such as CCTV) ( Elmasry et al., 2019; Haurum and Moeslund, 2020 ), 
bolstered by deep learning advancements, are quickly becoming a 
research hotspot for pipeline condition assessment ( Tan et al., 2021 ). 
Several studies leverage CNNs, RegNetY networks, and transformer- 
optimized networks for automatic defect identification in sewer pipe -
lines ( Hassan et al., 2019; Li et al., 2019; Li et al., 2022b; Ma et al., 2021, 
2023a ). However, these methods mainly focus on classification tasks, 
not quantitative assessments. Various computer vision techniques, 
including edge detection ( Kirstein et al., 2012 ), morphological detection 
( McKim and Sinha, 1999 ), and contour segmentation, have been 
employed to quantify pipeline cracks ( Sinha, 2001; Fang et al., 2022 ), 
deformation ( Xu et al., 1998; Kannala et al., 2008 ) and settled deposits

( Hawari et al., 2018 ). However, the limitations of 2D images only render 
them inappropriate for pipeline deformation assessment due to factors 
such as limited camera view and lens distortions ( Moon et al., 2019 ), 
difficulty in aligning pipeline profile perpendicular to the pipe ’ s axis, 
and poor interior illumination ( Duran et al., 2003, 2007; Clemens et al., 
2015; Lepot et al., 2017; Stani ´c et al., 2017 ). Additionally, acoustic 
methods are mainly employed when the pipeline is above 2/3 of the 
water level, they lack of the accuracy of laser scanning ( Plihal et al., 
2016 ). For example, geometric deformation measurement of concrete 
pipelines demands high accuracy and clarity, with a 1-meter diameter 
pipeline requiring millimeter-level precision. 
The recent advancements in laser scanning provides a suitable 
approach for capturing the global geometry of pipelines ( Zhou et al., 
2022 ), with the aid of tripods ( Olsen et al., 2010 ), unmanned aerial 
vehicle (UAV) ( Chen et al., 2020 ), unmanned surface vessel (USV) ( He 
et al., 2022 ), and robots ( Wang and Yin, 2022 ). For example, Kawashima 
et al. (2014) adopted normal-based region growing and RANSAC-based 
cylinder surface fitting to automatically recognize piping systems. Qiu 
et al. (2014) utilized Gaussian sphere and RANSAC to extract pipe-runs 
from large-scale point clouds. Czerniawski et al. (2016) located and 
extracted pipe spools in cluttered point clouds, primarily using local 
data level curvature estimation, density-based spatial clustering of ap -
plications with noise (DBSCAN), and bag-of-features (BoF) matching. 
Patil et al. (2017) presented an area-based adaptive Hough transform for 
detecting cylinders. However, most existing automated algorithms, 
which utilize circles or ellipses to fit pipe cross-sections, focus on 
extracting and reconstructing pipeline systems rather than measuring 
geometric deformation. Laser scanning data generate massive point 
clouds, containing considerable noise and outliers ( Yadav et al., 2021 ). 
In general, noise results from the measurement error of laser scanner, 
obeying Gaussian distribution and not exceeding 5 mm in range, while 
outliers arise from lighting or the reflective nature of surface in the scene 
( Zaman et al., 2017 ) and are sparsely distributed spatially. Owing to 
these point cloud characteristics, numerous density-based point cloud 
denoising methods, such as kernel density estimation (KDE) ( Laefer and 
Truong-Hong, 2017; Truong-Hong and Lindenbergh, 2022a, 2022b; Yan 
and Hajjar, 2021 ), DBSCAN ( Liu et al., 2022 ), voxel grid-based sampling 
( Pang et al., 2022 ), and statistical-based outlier removal (SOR), have 
been developed in recent years ( Hou et al., 2017 ). Unfortunately, fast 
and accurately measuring the geometry of pipelines ’ complex curved 
surfaces remains challenging currently ( Liu and Kleiner, 2013 ), as 
existing methods perform better for planar building surfaces (i.e., hor -
izontal and vertical planes). Consequently, the use of laser scanning 
point clouds for accurate deformation measurement in the geometric 
digital twin of pipelines necessitates addressing the following critical 
issues: (1) How can noise and outliers be effectively filtered out of 
pipeline surface point clouds? (2) How can the feasibility of the pro -
posed algorithm, especially concerning accuracy and time consumption 
evaluation, be effectively verified? 
To address the aforementioned challenges, this paper proposes a 
novel method for the fast and accurate extraction of pipeline surfaces 
and geometric digital twin modeling using density-based fitting, clus -
tering and region growing. The proposed algorithm comprises three 
components: (1) point cloud preprocessing, (2) geometric feature 
extraction utilizing least-median-of-squares (LMedS) fitting and 
DBSCAN clustering, and (3) 3D geometric digital twin modeling through 
slice region growing. A series of laboratory experiments are designed to 
assess the accuracy and computational time of the proposed method, 
thereby verifying its feasibility for quantitatively assessing structural 
deformation as well as blockage defects in the pipelines. 
The remainder of this paper is structured as follows. Section 2 details 
the methodology and overall framework. In Section 3 , the proposed 
approach is validated, and its performance is compared with existing 
methods. Lastly, Section 4 presents the conclusions. 
2. Methodology 
The underground pipeline system has been deemed one of the most 
deteriorated infrastructures, with a “ D + ” rating evaluated by ASCE 
( 2021 Infrastructure Report Card, 2021 ), due to harsh service environ -
ments and hidden degradations such as deformation, concrete spalling, 
scaling, and blockages ( Kirkham et al., 2000 ). Laser scanning, an effi -
cient technique for accurately capturing object geometries, enables the 
acquisition of 3D spatial data mapping the actual deformation and de -
fects of underground pipelines in hidden and dark environments that are 
inaccessible to humans. Nevertheless, the point cloud data typically 
contains substantial noise and outliers, making it unsuitable for direct 
use in computing deformation and defect volumes. The overarching goal

digital twin to quantitatively assess the deformation and defects for 
large diameter pipelines. 
2.1. 3D point-to-2D region 
Since laser scanning point clouds are typically massive, it can be 
time-consuming to analyze each point in a 3D manner. In this study, 
because 2D processing is simpler and more efficient than 3D processing, 
the 3D point cloud is segmented into several 2D slice-regions for data 
processing (i.e., 3D point-to-2D region), which are perpendicular to the 
pipe axis direction. However, since a pipeline can have an arbitrary 
orientation in geo-space, it is necessary to align the pipeline axis parallel 
to the coordinate axis z. Consequently, the 3D point-to-2D region 
methodology consists of two sub-steps: pipeline axis alignment and slice 
region subdivision.

directly simplified it to circle ( Liu et al., 2022 ) or ellipse ( Kannala et al., 
2008; Xu et al., 1998 ). However, research has shown that the pipeline 
deformation under pipe-soil interaction takes on irregular shapes 
( Haktanir et al., 2007 ), as shown in Fig. 1 . Cracks may happen when the

3. Experimental verification 
This section aims to validate the feasibility, accuracy and computa -
tional efficiency of the proposed methodology in pipeline deformation 
measurement and geometric digital twin modeling. The accuracy and 
computational efficiency of the deformation or profile measurement is 
fundamental to the geometric digital twin of the pipeline. The defor -
mation experiment and accuracy estimation method are first designed, 
followed by a comparison with three widely-used point cloud processing 
algorithms to demonstrate the superiority of our method. Finally, the 
results of geometric digital twin-informed quantitative condition 
assessment are analyzed. 
3.1. Implementation details 
Laboratory experiments were conducted for a total of thirteen cases, 
as shown in Table 1 . The first twelve groups were deformation tests, 
which were carried out by spring compression to simulate the process of 
vertical loads on underground pipelines ( Fig. 11 c), followed by blockage 
test, which used irregular pieces of paper to simulate the appearance of 
gravel obstacles at the bottom of sewage pipes. The experiment was 
designed to verify the accuracy of deformation measurement rather than 
the mechanical properties of a pipeline. A plexiglass pipe segment with 
better deformation capability and ease of operation was employed in the 
experiments. To enhance the reflective ability of the plexiglass pipe 
surface and to better simulate the negative effects of rough concrete pipe 
surface, a layer of rough and opaque wallpaper was pasted on the inner 
surface of the pipe. The accuracy of the proposed method can be

the marks in the point clouds, as total station measurements are more

3.2.1. Accuracy evaluation 
To analyze the performance of the four methods across various 
pipeline deformations and point cloud sampling resolutions, the laser 
scanner was employed to measure three different deformations at each 
of the three different sampling resolutions (as shown in Table 1 ). Next, 
errors were calculated using the four methods for six groups of target 
points (including 1 – 7, 2 – 8, 3 – 9, 4 – 10, 5 – 11 and 6 – 12), resulting in 3 ×
3 × 4 × 6 = 216 error samples. Fig. 12 depicts the calculation results of 
all errors for the four methods. It is evident that the KDE method and the 
proposed method outperformed others, with their mean errors both 
below the 1 mm accuracy threshold, thus satisfying the requirements of 
deformation measurement. The SOR method demonstrated an accuracy 
of 1.1 mm, which followed the KDE and proposed methods. The Voxel 
method exhibited the worst performance, with an error of up to 1.52 cm. 
However, the maximum error of the SOR method approached 6 mm, 
significantly higher than that of the KDE and the proposed method, 
indicating that the proposed method is more robust than the SOR 
method. Moreover, the calculation results of geometric features shown 
in Fig. 13 further demonstrate the superior performance of KDE and the 
proposed method. Notably, regarding sampling resolution, the error of 
the proposed method, the KDE method and the SOR method all expe -
rienced a minor increase with lower sampling resolution. Conversely, 
the Voxel method displayed a different trend due to its poor outlier 
removal performance, as a point cloud with higher sampling resolution 
might contain more outliers. Although the KDE method demonstrated a 
more stable performance in comparison with the proposed method, this 
obtains at the expense of computational time. The computational effi -
ciency of the four methods will be discussed below, which can reflect the 
potential of the proposed method to be applied to large-scale under -
ground pipe networks. 
3.2.2. Computational time 
The aforementioned results demonstrate that both the KDE method 
and our proposed method can extract the geometric features of the 
pipeline surface point cloud with high accuracy, exhibiting a mean error 
of less than 1 mm. However, laser scanning point cloud data typically 
consists of millions of points, resulting in processing efficiency or 
computational time being one of the most crucial issues in assessing the 
practical application value of methods. Fig. 14 depicts the computa -
tional time required for extracting the geometric features of a slice for 
each of the four methods with different data volumes. From the 
perspective of processing efficiency, the performance of our method is 
comparable to that of the SOR method, with processing times of 171.73 s 
and 105.31 s at the 1.02 million points level, respectively. However, the 
KDE method becomes increasingly time-consuming as the volume of

of each data point to the probability density ( Picos et al., 2020; Silver -
man, 1986 ), and the performance of KDE methods is heavily influenced 
by the bandwidth selection ( Armesto et al., 2010 ), leading to a challenge 
for processing large datasets. Although some techniques (such as k-fold 
cross validation) were developed to automatically determine bandwidth 
( Shimazaki and Shinomoto, 2010 ), they require a significant amount of 
run time, especially within dense laser scanning point clouds ( Laefer and 
Truong-Hong, 2017 ). Compared with KDE method, the proposed 
method has the advantage of much smaller computing load. The method 
first removed outliers by clustering the data via DBSCAN which has a 
much smaller computational time than KDE. Then, in contrast to KDE 
that needs to compute the contribution of all point data (including 
outliers and noise), our method used a simple Gaussian fit to compute 
the contribution of the remaining measurement noise to the probability 
density, which is a process that has a small computational complexity 
and almost negligible computational time. The results in Fig. 14 showed 
that the proposed method achieves the same high accuracy as the KDE 
method but has a running time of only 7 % of that of the KDE method, 
indicating that the proposed method significantly outperforms the KDE 
method. 
Finally, three representative examples using the proposed method 
are listed in Table 4 to illustrate the total computational time required 
for the complete processing workflow, from the raw point cloud to the 
geometric digital twin. It is evident that the majority of the computa -
tional time is spent on the DBSCAN-based clustering and fitting step. The 
running time of Point Cloud 2 is greater than that of Point Cloud 1 due to 
the higher volume of data that needs to be analyzed at each cell in a slice 
with high sampling resolution. Moreover, Point Cloud 3, which contains 
pipe defects, results in more slices that do not meet the similarity 
threshold, leading to a longer executing time compared to Point Cloud 1 
(233.65 s for Point Cloud 3 vs. 52.71 s for Point Cloud 1). Consequently, 
it can be argued that the processing time primarily depends on not only 
the sampling resolution but also the presence of pipeline defects. In 
summary, the proposed method can process large datasets accurately 
and efficiently, with total processing times of approximately 52.71 s for 
2.98 million points in Point Cloud 1, 115.53 s for 11.94 million points in 
Point Cloud 2, and 233.65 s for 3.74 million points in Point Cloud 3. 
Note that the performance reported herein was based on the imple -
mentation of all methods in Python 3.10 and was performed on a normal 
personal computer with the following system configuration: a 12th Gen 
Intel(R) Core(TM) i7-12700F CPU @ 2.1 GHz with 64 GB RAM, a 
GeForce RTX 3080 GPU.

4. Conclusions 
The maintenance of underground water and sewer pipelines can be 
challenging due to their narrow internal environments and the presence 
of corrosive and toxic gases ( Khan et al., 2021; Wang et al., 2021 ). To 
overcome these difficulties, this study proposes a novel method for fast 
and accurately processing point cloud data acquired by laser scanning 
technology to build a geometric digital twin of underground pipelines. 
The proposed method filters out noise and outliers based on the char -
acteristics of laser scanning point cloud density distribution and finely 
describes the 3D surface of a pipeline to achieve an accurately mapping 
of pipeline conditions. A geometric digital twin-informed approach was 
presented to quantitatively assess the deformation and blockage in 
pipelines. 
The accuracy and computational efficiency of the proposed method 
were demonstrated through experimental tests. Results indicated that 
the proposed method exhibited a mean error of less than 1 mm in 
deformation measurement, which satisfies the accuracy requirements 
for large diameter pipelines when compared to the measurements ob -
tained the high-accuracy total station. Furthermore, the proposed 
method demonstrated superior accuracy and robustness when compared 
with the popular point cloud processing algorithms, namely SOR and 
Voxel methods. The computational time of the proposed method was

applications in better informed O & M during life-cycle service. However, 
the method has some limitations at current stage, such as being unable 
to inspect the underwater part of the pipeline and not being suitable for 
bends with curved axes. 
Further research directions include exploring the geometric digital 
twin and artificial intelligence (AI) for quantitative assessment of other 
defects, such as concrete spalling, corrosion, and open-joints. Addi -
tionally, an in-pipe inspection robot should be designed to carry the 
laser scanner for practical applications in engineering. Recently, ro -

( Ma et al., 2023b ) are rapidly developing. In the future, we could further 
leverage these recent studies to validate the effectiveness of our method 
in dense point clouds acquired by these advanced devices and systems. 
Overall, the proposed method provides a promising tool in the assess -
ment of underground pipe networks and can potentially reduce costs 
associated with pipeline failures and unplanned repairs, while also 
minimizing environmental impacts.
