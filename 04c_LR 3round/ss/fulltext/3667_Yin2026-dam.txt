ABSTRACT
Traditional electrical resistivity tomography (ERT) inversion for dam leakage detection faces heavy computa -
tional loads and smoothness priors that fail to localize anomalies. This study introduces the Topology-Guided 
Fourier Neural Operator framework. A Fourier Neural Operator replaces the numerical solver based on the 
governing equations, speeding up forward potential field simulations. To easily locate anomalies, a decoupled 
geometric parameterization separates the deterministic defect from the stochastic background. A Navigator 
network extracts coarse topological priors to initialize a parallel multi-hypothesis workflow. Tested across 
multiple trials, the framework achieves a 99.7% average success rate and an 89.3% high-fidelity reconstruction 
rate. It accelerates forward modeling by 345 times and overall inversion by 72 times compared to finite element 
methods. The method detects unseen low-contrast early leakages and remains stable under 20% measurement 
noise and 50% data loss. This approach provides the efficiency and reliability requisite for routine safety 
monitoring and operational digital twins.
1. Introduction
Internal leakage represents a leading factor in earth dam failures, a 
risk exacerbated by increasingly extreme hydro-climatic events ( ICOLD, 
2019 ; Moreno-Rodenas et al., 2025 ; Nasser et al., 2025 ). While methods 
such as Ground Penetrating Radar (GPR) ( Bekele et al., 2024 ; Dong 
et al., 2020 ) and Distributed Optical Fiber Temperature Sensing (DTS) 
( Bekele et al., 2024 , 2025 ) characterize internal structures effectively, 
they are operationally complex for routine monitoring. Electrical Re -
sistivity Tomography (ERT) offers a practical alternative. Electrical 
conductivity is a sensitive indicator of structural integrity ( Guo et al., 
2022 ; Xiao et al., 2024 ; Zhang et al., 2026 ), highly responsive to mois -
ture variations and leakage. The technique operates by injecting direct 
current through source electrodes and measuring the resulting potential 
differences to reconstruct the internal conductivity distribution. 
Reconstructing this distribution requires solving a highly non-linear, 
ill-posed inverse problem constrained by the Poisson equation ( Loke & 
Barker, 1996 ). Conventional methods typically enforce smoothness 
constraints to stabilize the inversion ( Constable et al., 1987 ; Liu et al., 
2021 ). However, this regularization tends to smooth out the sharp 
boundaries of early-stage leakage and masks high-contrast anomalies. 
Despite recognition of this topological mismatch ( Chan & Tai, 2004 ; 
Ishizu et al., 2025 ), accurately resolving such distinct targets within 
heterogeneous backgrounds remains difficult, especially when using 
reduced-order parameterizations.
To mitigate the computational burden of stochastic inversion for 
earth dams, recent research has focused on two main directions: algo -
rithmic acceleration and surrogate modeling. Fast direct solvers expe -
dite Jacobian matrix calculations ( Ambikasaran et al., 2013 ; Saibaba & 
Kitanidis, 2012 ); however, high-fidelity probabilistic characterization 
remains expensive due to the cumulative demand of repeated forward 
simulations. Standard surrogate modeling frameworks project 
high-dimensional parameters onto truncated linear subspaces, often 
employing the Karhunen-Lo `eve (KL) expansion ( Ghanem & Spanos, 
1991 ) combined with shallow regressors such as Gaussian Processes 
( Rasmussen & Williams, 2006 ). Nevertheless, dependence on global

projections fail to resolve sharp and high-contrast discontinuities found 
in internal leakage, inevitably blurring localized anomalies and 
compromising topological fidelity in reduced-dimensional spaces.
Deep learning provides a powerful tool for inverse modeling and 
structural health assessment. Recent studies demonstrate that data- 
driven models successfully evaluate structural conditions and predict 
physical responses, even under data scarcity or unsupervised settings 
( Amezquita-Sanchez et al., 2018 ; Entezami et al., 2026 ; Javadinasab 
Hormozabad et al., 2021 ; Rafiei & Adeli, 2018 ). While standard network 
architectures are effective for parameter estimation ( Quqa et al., 2023 ; 
Taghizadeh et al., 2025 ; Xue et al., 2025 ; Zhang et al., 2026 ), they map 
between fixed grids, making their performance heavily dependent on 
the grid density used during training. This dependency limits general -
ization capabilities ( Cha et al., 2024 ; Zhang et al., 2023 ). Raissi et al. 
(2019) proposed Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs) to incor -
porate physical constraints by embedding partial differential equation 
(PDE) residuals into the optimization objective. PINNs perform effec -
tively in various fields ( Guo et al., 2023 ; Haghighat et al., 2021 ; Ji et al., 
2024 ). Yet, because they function as solvers for specific instances rather 
than learning the general solution operator, each new inversion task 
mandates retraining from scratch. Furthermore, optimization frequently 
fails from ill-conditioned loss landscapes ( Karniadakis et al., 2021 ; 
Krishnapriyan et al., 2021 ; Wang et al., 2021 ). Operator learning 
frameworks extend neural networks to infinite-dimensional function 
spaces. Prominent examples include the Fourier Neural Operator (FNO) 
( Li et al., 2021 ) and spectral graph networks ( Yin et al., 2026 ). The FNO 
efficiently captures the global dependencies of the steady-state Poisson 
equation by parameterizing the integral kernel within the frequency 
domain. This spectral convolution ensures resolution-invariant forward 
modeling, overcoming the grid-dependence of standard convolutional 
networks ( Kaewnuratchadasorn et al., 2024 ; Kovachki et al., 2023 ). 
Applying these operators to ill-posed inverse problems remains chal -
lenging. Most existing neural-operator frameworks execute a direct, 
unconstrained inverse mapping from measurements to physical prop -
erties ( Long et al., 2025 ). This unparameterized approach inherently 
diffuses sharp topological boundaries under the severe ill-posedness of 
ERT ( Molinaro et al., 2023 ). While classical PDE optimization exten -
sively employs geometric parameterizations to maintain sharp in -
terfaces, integrating such explicit structural priors into data-driven 
neural operator frameworks remains underexplored, particularly for the 
severely ill-posed ERT inverse problem. The absence of topological 
priors leaves gradient-based optimization vulnerable to local minima, 
causing failures in identifying localized leakage anomalies.
Within this context, a novel Topology-Guided Fourier Neural Oper -
ator (TG-FNO) framework is presented for leakage anomaly detection in 
earth dams. This framework integrates a decoupled geometric parame -
terization to disentangle anomalies from stochastic soil backgrounds. A 
lightweight Guided Network extracts coarse geometric priors directly 
from electrical resistivity tomography (ERT) measurements to initialize 
the inversion. Driven by a differentiable FNO surrogate, this topology- 
guided workflow enables efficient gradient-based optimization, 
bypassing the computational bottlenecks of conventional finite element 
solvers. The overall architecture of the TG-FNO framework is shown in 
Fig. 1 .
The primary methodological contributions are: 
1. Operator-Driven Inversion Framework: The TG-FNO framework ex -
tends the application of Fourier neural operators from standard 
forward modeling to complex geometric inversion, enabling the 
resolution of anomalies in ERT.
2. Decoupled Geometric Parameterization: A strategy is developed to 
mathematically separate deterministic leakage defects from the sto -
chastic background. This resolves the inherent conflict between 
generic smoothness priors and the sharp topology of leakage 
anomalies.
3. Topology-Guided Optimization: A hierarchical initialization work -
flow constrains the inversion search space using geometric priors 
derived from the Navigator. This mitigates the local minima stag -
nation typical of gradient-based solvers without requiring expensive 
global search algorithms.
4. Fast Differentiable Surrogate: An end-to-end differentiable pipeline 
is established by replacing iterative finite element modeling with the

2. Methodology
The methodology establishes the Topology-Guided Fourier Neural 
Operator (TG-FNO) framework for electrical resistivity tomography 
(ERT) inversion. Formulated upon the steady-state ERT governing 
equations ( Section 2.1 ), the inverse problem is resolved through a 
differentiable pipeline. A Fourier Neural Operator (FNO) serves as the 
forward surrogate to accelerate potential field simulations ( Section 2.2 ). 
A decoupled geometric parameterization is established to disentangle 
anomalies from the stochastic background ( Section 2.3 ). These compo -
nents are integrated into a topology-guided inversion workflow, where a 
Navigator network initializes the gradient-based optimization using 
coarse geometric priors ( Section 2.4 ).

3. Numerical experiments
3.1. Dataset generation and experimental setup
The numerical setup involved a 2D vertical dam profile Ω measuring 
20 m × 20 m. A mesh of 128 × 128 uniform quadrilateral cells covers 
this domain. Data acquisition simulated a cross-borehole configuration 
with a 7 × 10 electrode array. The array yields horizontal and vertical 
sensor spacings of 3.0 m and 2.0 m, respectively. Anomalies smaller than 
this spacing generate weak electrical signatures. These limited potential 
variations are readily masked by background heterogeneity, inherently 
restricting the minimum detectable size. The conductivity field σ ( x ) 
combines a stochastic background with a discrete anomaly. This 
approach allows for independent control over background heterogene -
ity and anomalous features, aligning with the decoupled inversion 
framework. The Karhunen-Lo `eve (KL) expansion represented the back -
ground heterogeneity as a Log-Normal Gaussian Random Field (GRF). 
Key statistical settings defined the mean conductivity at 0.005S/m and 
the standard deviation at 0.002S/m. The correlation length was 5.0 m. 
To simulate seepage, a high-conductivity circular anomaly was 
introduced, parameterized by its centroid c = ( c
x
, c
y
) and radius r . The 
centroid was confined to the central region [5.0, 15.0] m to reduce 
boundary effects, with the radius varying between 1.0 m and 3.0 m. A 
log-normal distribution determined the anomaly conductivity. The 
mean value stood at approximately 0.3 S/m. A sigmoid transition mask 
smoothed the interface to maintain numerical stability. Latin Hypercube 
Sampling ( Helton & Davis, 2003 ) generated 1000 independent samples, 
split into 900 for training and 100 for testing. Forward modeling was 
performed using the SfePy finite element library. To ensure a unique 
solution for the Neumann problem, a single-node Dirichlet reference ( u 
= 0) was enforced at the vertex (0,0). This anchor serves strictly as a 
mathematical gauge-fixing condition. The Neumann boundary value 
problem for the forward model is singular without a defined reference 
potential. The anchor ensures the convergence of the finite element 
solver. Relocating this anchor to an alternative node introduces a global 
constant shift to the computed potential field. However, the observa -
tional inputs for the inversion framework consist exclusively of potential 
differences between electrode pairs. The constant shift perfectly cancels 
out during the calculation of these differences. Therefore, the spatial 
placement of the Dirichlet anchor exerts no influence on the final 
inversion performance. A "5-stim" protocol was employed (see Fig. 2 ). 
The acquired potentials were log-transformed to compress the dynamic 
range. Key parameters are listed in Table 1 . All numerical simulations 
were run on a standard desktop workstation powered by an Intel Core 
i7 – 12,700 K CPU 4.9 GHz. The framework was implemented in Python 
3.12.3, using the SfePy library (version 2025.1) for finite element 
analysis ( Cimrman et al., 2019 ).

3.2.2. TG-FNO inversion configuration
The inverse problem is formulated as an optimization task, wherein 
the objective is to minimize the discrepancy between the acquired 
electrode measurements and the potential fields predicted by the sur -
rogate. Gaussian noise is added directly to the log-transformed poten -
tials to simulate measurement uncertainty. This additive operation in 
the logarithmic domain equates to applying multiplicative log-normal 
noise in the linear domain. This approach physically models instru -
mentation errors that scale with signal magnitude. Measurements 
coinciding with active source and sink electrodes are excluded to avoid 
singularity artifacts. To ensure robustness against local minima, a 
topology-guided parallel search strategy is adopted. Specifically, the 
number of parallel chains N
h 
is set to 16. These chains execute simul -
taneously via GPU batch processing. A dual-scale initialization strategy 
is implemented to mitigate potential misguidance from the neural prior 
under severe noise. Among the 16 parallel chains, five are instantiated at 
the Top-5 probability predictions extracted from the GuideNet as the 
topological priors h
k
. These five chains are coupled with a standard 
exploration jitter ξ
k 
sampled from (0, 0.05
2
) within the normalized 
latent space. To compensate for guide mismatch, the remaining 11 
chains are initialized around the Top-1 prediction but are subjected to a 
fourfold larger exploration jitter sampled from (0, 0.20
2
). This config -
uration balances the exploitation of topological priors with latent space, 
preventing the optimization process from being trapped in misleading 
local minima. The dimension of the background latent vector d
bg 
is set to 
20. This truncation threshold is determined through an eigenvalue en -
ergy spectrum analysis of the covariance kernel. For the baseline cor -
relation length of 5.0 m, the first 20 modes capture 99.20% of the total 
variance. Incorporating additional dimensions increases the optimiza -
tion burden without meaningfully altering the physical representation of 
the background field. The background latent variables z
bg 
are randomly 
initialized from a normal distribution (0, 0.1
2
). The latent vectors are 
updated deterministically over a fixed 100 iterations via the Adam 
optimizer. To synchronize the convergence of background and anomaly 
features, distinct learning rates of 0.01 and 0.05 are assigned to z
bg 
and 
z
a

nisms, explicit gradient clipping, and supplementary regularization 
terms. The geometric parameterization architecture inherently enforces 
the physical bounds of the conductivity field. The final reconstruction is 
selected from the optimization trajectory yielding the lowest data re -
sidual. This data-driven inversion is highly efficient, achieving an 
average runtime of approximately 2.39 s per case on the utilized 
computational platform.

4.2. Inversion performance of TG-FNO framework
4.2.1. Baseline selection
The proposed framework is benchmarked against two baselines to 
evaluate its inversion performance and isolate architectural contribu -
tions. The first baseline is a conventional stochastic inversion combining 
the finite element method (FEM) with the Covariance Matrix Adaptation 
Evolution Strategy (CMA-ES) ( Alkayem et al., 2018 ; Marty et al., 2023 ; 
Tiprak et al., 2025 ). Preliminary evaluations detailed in Appendix A 
justify the necessity of this derivative-free global optimizer. Standard 
continuous inversion and unconstrained generative models struggle 
with the extreme sparsity of cross-borehole measurements. As illustrated 
in Fig. A1 and quantified in Table A1 , Total Variation-regularized pix -
el-wise inversion produces over-smoothed conductivity fields, reducing 
the high-fidelity localization metric Acc
0.5 
to near zero. Furthermore, 
applying the Gauss-Newton algorithm directly to the decoupled 
parameterization leads to severe local minima entrapment, while a 
Conditional GAN introduces background artifacts. Given the poor 
reconstruction metrics of these local gradient algorithms and 
data-driven approaches, the robust global search capacity of CMA-ES 
provides a suitable conventional benchmark. The second baseline is a 
Random FNO configuration. This internal baseline shares the identical 
frozen surrogate and continuous optimization scheme as the proposed 
framework, differing only by relying entirely on random latent initiali -
zation to explicitly isolate the performance contribution of the guide 
network.

4.4. Robustness and generalization analysis
4.4.1. Generalization to complex anomaly configurations
Table 2 evaluates the model generalization on complex anomaly 
configurations without parameter fine-tuning. The framework is 
initially tested on low-contrast anomalies representing early-stage 
leakage with weak electrical signatures. The model trained on a 
contrast ratio of 60 is directly applied to new test sets with reduced 
ratios of 25 and 10. The framework maintains a 100% Acc
0 
across both 
groups. For the most challenging 10-fold contrast ratio, it achieves a 
Mean IoU of 0.6804 and an Acc
0.5 
of 82%. This performance indicates 
that the FNO surrogate approximates the physical mapping between 
potential and conductivity instead of memorizing high-contrast training 
patterns. To evaluate out-of-distribution inversion capabilities, the 
framework is tested on two independent sets of 100 samples featuring 
geometric distribution shifts: noncircular elongated profiles and multi -
ple anomalies with varying intensities ( Fig. A2 ). While the training 
dataset exclusively contains single circular anomalies, the solver adapts 
to these unseen configurations. In scenarios with multiple defects, the 
pronounced potential distortion from the primary leakage masks weaker 
secondary signatures. Constrained by the single-circle parameterization, 
the model prioritizes the dominant anomaly and reconstructs an 
equivalent enclosing circle. Consequently, it locates the primary leakage 
zones with an Acc
0.5 
of 100% for noncircular anomalies and 98% for 
multiple anomalies. The Mean IoU values of 0.6892 and 0.6866 reflect 
the geometric difference between the parameterized circle and the 
actual irregular boundaries. These results confirm the robust out-of- 
distribution generalization of the topology-guided search against geo -
metric prior mismatches and masking effects.

4.5. Computational and practical challenges in field deployment
While transitioning to fully three-dimensional inversion is a logical 
step for comprehensive dam monitoring, two-dimensional longitudinal 
profiling remains the main and practical setup for routine, continuous 
safety assessment of long embankments ( Rahimi et al., 2025 ). Extending 
the proposed operator learning framework to 3D environments faces 
significant mathematical and computational challenges, chiefly the 
curse of dimensionality. The standard three-dimensional FNO architec -
ture increases training time complexity to ( N
3 
log N ), which requires 
large amounts of memory during network backpropagation on typical 
engineering hardware ( Li & Ye, 2025 ). Additionally, capturing the 
spatial variance of a 3D background field requires a vastly expanded 
latent parameter space. Integrating this expanded representation with 
3D geometric coordinates creates a highly complex, non-convex opti -
mization landscape, significantly increasing the probability of local 
minima entrapment. Maintaining the current sub-second inference ef -
ficiency and localization accuracy in 3D necessitates more advanced, 
memory-efficient operator architectures.
Beyond dimensional extensions, field deployment faces a funda -
mental simulation-to-reality gap related to boundary conditions. Neural 
operators approximate Green's functions mathematically, making the 
surrogate very sensitive to changes in boundary conditions. Achieving 
out-of-distribution generalization across different cross-sections remains 
a core challenge. However, operational dam monitoring generally in -
volves static structural profiles and predictable environmental states. 
Therefore, training a universal operator is unnecessary for this specific 
use. A practical solution is to pre-train a small set of targeted operators 
tailored to known seasonal or operational boundary conditions. Using 
these condition-specific surrogates during field inspections effectively 
overcomes the boundary mismatch issue at a minimal offline compu -
tational cost. The proposed 2D framework provides a computationally 
efficient and practical foundation for operational digital twins.
5. Conclusion
This study introduces the TG-FNO framework to address the conflict 
between smoothness regularization and the sharp topological bound -
aries of leakage anomalies in electrical resistivity tomography. By 
combining decoupled geometric parameterization with a topology- 
guided warm start, the method explicitly separates stochastic back -
ground heterogeneity from deterministic structural defects. This repre -
sentation aligns with structural health monitoring needs, which focus on 
localizing anomalies against known geological backgrounds. The FNO 
surrogate acts as a high-fidelity alternative to traditional finite element 
methods. It accurately captures complex non-linear mappings with a 
mean relative error of 3.18% and achieves a 345 × speedup in forward 
modeling. This effectively removes computational bottlenecks in sto -
chastic inversion. Guided by this surrogate and topological initializa -
tion, TG-FNO overcomes the issue of local minima stagnation commonly 
seen in baseline approaches. Repeated trials confirm its statistical sta -
bility and accuracy. The framework achieves a mean Inversion Success 
Rate ( Acc
0
) of 99.7% ± 0.6% and a high-fidelity reconstruction rate 
( Acc
0.5
) of 89.3% ± 2.1%, outperforming conventional methods. Addi -
tionally, it maintains an overall 72 × computational acceleration over 
FEM-based inversion. This computational efficiency facilitates rapid 
routine monitoring, a critical requirement for timely decision-making in 
dam safety. The framework also shows robust out-of-distribution 
generalization. Without retraining, it accurately resolves incipient 
leakage signatures at a 10-fold contrast ratio and severe adverse con -
ditions, including 0.20 Gaussian noise and 50% sensor data loss. This 
confirms that the learned operator approximates the underlying physical 
laws rather than merely memorizing statistical patterns. Current limi -
tations define three primary research directions. First, explicit parame -
terization restricts detection to predefined shapes. Future work will 
adopt implicit representations, such as level set methods, for multi- 
anomaly scenarios. Second, the underlying Fourier transform is sus -
ceptible to spectral truncation. Integrating the synchrosqueezed wavelet 
transform ( Li et al., 2017 ; Perez-Ramirez et al., 2016 ) will address this 
by improving localized feature resolution. Finally, the 
simulation-to-reality gap limits direct field deployment. Because neural 
operators approximate Green's functions, they remain highly sensitive to 
physical boundary variations. Future implementations must mitigate 
this by developing libraries of condition-specific operators to accom -
modate seasonal boundary shifts. Together, these advancements will 
build a practical foundation for operational digital twins in structural 
health monitoring.
