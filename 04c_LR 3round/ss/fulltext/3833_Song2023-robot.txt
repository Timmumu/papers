Abstract
The robot joint is an important component of the construction robot, and its fault
diagnosis can ensure the exact execution of building jobs, stable operation, and
timely prevention of probable safety mishaps. However, deep learning ‐based fault
diagnosis needs a multitude of measured fault data, which is difficult to obtain for
various reasons. To solve the problem of insufficient data, a digital twin ‐assisted
fault diagnosis system for robot joints is proposed. First, a simplified dynamics model
of the robot joint is developed to generate the virtual entity data which can be used
as the X ‐domain data for the digital twin model. Second, a CycleGAN ‐based digital
twin model is proposed to map the virtual entity (X ‐domain) data to the physical
entity (Y ‐domain) utilizing only a small amount of measured data. In the end, a
test‐rig for the robot joint is built to simulate the robot's working conditions, and the
CNN‐ResNet classifier is utilized to verify the effectiveness of the simulated data
generated by the digital twin model. The results show that the fault diagnosis
accuracy can be increased from 32.5% to 98.86% utilizing only 400 sets of
measured data.

1 | INTRODUCTION
The construction robot is an intelligent construction equipment that
can improve the working environment and increase efficiency.
Considering the building process in the construction industry is
complex, there have been a multitude of intelligent construction
robots that assist in construction, such as wall construction robots
(Han et al., 2006), painting robots (Asadi et al., 2018), and
maintenance building robots (Follini et al., 2020). Construction
robots can improve construction efficiency, ensure the health and
safety of construction workers, and solve the labor shortage in the
construction industry (Mascaro et al., 2021).

construction industry. In 1982, the SSR ‐type robot for spraying

The RV reducer is widely used as the construction robot joint
because of its high transmission efficiency, high torque, and smooth
transmission performance (Chen, 2018). However, it is easily
damaged under long ‐term work and overload operation conditions.
At present, there have been a multitude of scholars pouring attention
into transmission efficiency (Wang, 2021), optimization of design
(Yang et al., 2021) and error analysis of RV reducers (Li et al., 2018),
but little into the fault diagnosis aspect. The existing fault diagnosis of
RV reducers is mostly based on deep learning (Chen et al., 2021; Long
et al., 2021; Peng & Wang, 2019). Traditional fault characteristics are
extracted from vibration signals (Shi & Shang, 2022), sound signals
(Bai et al., 2022) or sound ‐vibration signals (Shi et al., 2022)t o
achieve accurate fault classification. Considering the complex
construction environment and working conditions of construction
robots, it does not seem feasible to collect sound signals with the
interference of a strong noise background. Therefore, deep learning
based on vibration signals has become the mainstream method for
fault diagnosis of robots. A sparse deep learning method was
proposed by (Sun et al., 2018) to reduce the risk of overfitting in
deep networks with a large number of layers and neurons. A deep
transfer learning ‐based fault diagnosis framework was proposed by
Shao et al. ( 2019a) to obtain faster model convergence and higher
fault diagnosis accuracy. A one ‐dimensional residual convolutional
autoencoder‐based feature learning was proposed by Yu and Zhou
(2020) to better extract the features of vibration signals under noisy
conditions. The above deep learning ‐based fault diagnosis methods
all require sufficient measured data to train the neural network. But
the measured data on faulty robot joints are difficult to obtain under
the actual working conditions. The reasons may be as follows:
(1) most of the construction robots are in normal working condition;
(2) obtaining failure data through experiments is a time ‐consuming
and costly process; (3) some fault data cannot even be obtained
through experiments.
To provide different kinds of fault data for the RV reducer,
some scholars have established dynamics models (Qiao et al.,
2020; Wang et al., 2019; Xie et al., 2022; Xu et al., 2019).
However, there are such varying degrees of idealization in the
dynamics modeling that the dynamics model cannot completely
describe the motion behavior of the internal components of RV
reducers. This will cause there to be a gap between the simulated
signals of the dynamics model and the measured signals of the RV
reducer. Therefore, the key to improving the accuracy of the fault
diagnosis of the RV reducer is how to reduce the gaps between the
simulated dynamic response and the measured dynamic response.
To establish a relationship between simulated dynamic response
and measured dynamic response, digital twin provides a feasible
method. The concept of the digital twin can date back to 2003,
when Professor Grieves developed the Mirror Space Model in a
product life cycle management class at the University of Michigan

2 | THEORETICAL BACKGROUND
The digital twin ‐assisted fault diagnosis system consists of three key
methods: (1) the simplified robot joint dynamics model; (2) the
CycleGAN‐based digital twin model; and (3) the deep learning ‐based
fault diagnosis. This section will introduce the fundamentals of the
methods mentioned above.
2.1 | The simplified robot joint dynamics model
The RV reducer is a closed two ‐stage planetary ‐transmission
mechanism that consists of planetary gears and cycloid ‐pinwheels.
Because of its high transmission efficiency, high torque, and smooth
transmission performance, the RV reducer is widely used in the
transmission joints of construction robots. Its transmission principles
are shown in Figure 2.

3 | DIGITAL TWIN ‐ ASSISTED FAULT
DIAGNOSIS SYSTEM FOR ROBOT JOINTS
In this section, the framework and the specific process of the digital
twin‐assisted fault diagnosis system are described. And we also
summarize the advantages of the system at the end of this section.
3.1 | Framework of digital twin ‐assisted fault
diagnosis system
The digital twin ‐assisted fault diagnosis framework proposed in this
paper consists of three main parts: virtual entity modeling, virtual ‐to‐
physical mapping, and digital twin ‐assisted fault diagnosis. First, the
virtual entity model is built based on the gear dynamics and generates
the vibration signals as the domain X. Second, a small amount of the
measured data collected by the sensor is used as the domain Y.
Furthermore, CycleGAN is used to map the domain X to the domain
Y, which means the virtual domain is mapped to the physical domain.
The above constitutes the CycleGAN ‐based digital twin model. The
simulated vibration signals generated by the digital twin model can
effectively improve the fault diagnosis accuracy. Finally, CNN ‐ResNet
is used for fault classification. As shown in Figure 6, the general
framework of the digital twin ‐assisted fault diagnosis system mainly
includes the following elements:
1. Virtual entity modeling: The virtual entity model based on gear
dynamics can generate noiseless and faultless vibration signals,
which are used as X ‐domain data.
2. Mapping of virtual to physical domain: The vibration signals
generated by the dynamics model are mapped to the physical
domain by CycleGAN. The simulated vibration signals mapped to
the physical domain will have the same distribution and fault
characteristics as the measured vibration signals.
3. Digital twin ‐assisted fault diagnosis: The data generated by the
digital twin model can be used directly as training datasets. And
the datasets can effectively assist in fault diagnosis and provide
good accuracy.

correct model of the virtual entity. Therefore, only by building the
correct model of the virtual entity can it accurately simulate the
physical entity. In this paper, the 6 ‐DOF gear system dynamics model

3.3 | Mapping of the virtual to the physical domain
As shown in Figure 8, the mapping process of CycleGAN mainly
covers the following aspects: (1) Discrete measured data and
simulated data, and each set of samples should have the same
dimensionality; (2) Configure the CycleGAN network structure and
parameters to ensure the adversarial balance of the generator and
discriminator; (3) Digital twin models for different fault types need to
be trained separately.
3.4 | Advantages of the digital twin ‐assisted fault
diagnosis system
Deep learning ‐based fault diagnosis requires a multitude of fault
data, which is difficult to obtain. To solve the problem of
insufficient training data, a digital twin ‐assisted fault system is
proposed. There are three main benefits to the digital twin ‐
assisted fault diagnosis syst em: (1) Only a small amount of
measured data is required to generate simulated data with the
same distribution and fault characteristics as the measured data.
(2) The simulated data obtained uti lizing the digital twin model can
be directly used to train the classifier and provide high classifica-
tion accuracy. (3) High er accuracy and faster convergence can be
obtained if the simulated data and the measured data are used
together as the training samples.

4.3 | Model training and data generation
The CycleGAN ‐based digital twin model is trained with a small
amount of real data to generate simulated vibration signals for fault
diagnosis. To verify the generation capability of the digital twin
model, all four types of vibration signals are generated. The X ‐domain
data of the digital twin model is provided by the noiseless and
faultless vibration signals generated by the virtual entity model. They
are divided into groups of samples, where each group contains 1000
data points. And the Y ‐domain data of the digital twin model is
provided by the measured data. To verify that the proposed digital
twin model can solve the problem of insufficient training data, we use
only a few measured data to generate simulated data for fault
diagnosis. Each sample of Y ‐domain data contains 1000 data points,
and only 100 samples of each type of Y ‐domain data are used. The
digital twin model for each fault type needs to be trained separately.
After four training sessions, the digital twin model for each fault type
is saved for subsequent generation of simulated vibration signals.

4.4 | Fault diagnosis analysis and discussion
First, we test the performance of the proposed CNN ‐ResNet
classifier trained utilizing different amounts of measured data. The
600, 1200, 2400 and 4800 sets of training samples are input to the
classifier respectively. The fault diagnosis accuracy is shown in
Figure 14.
As shown in Figure 14, the fault diagnosis accuracy can reach
almost 100% when the training samples are sufficient, such as the
numbers of 2400 and 4800. However, the accuracy of the test set is
quite low when the training samples are insufficient. When the
training samples are 1200, the accuracy is about 39%. When the
training samples are 600, the accuracy is only 32.5%. Therefore, it
cannot provide a good performance of fault diagnosis by only utilizing
the measured data in the case of insufficient training samples.
Subsequently, we will introduce simulated data to improve the
performance of fault diagnosis in the case of insufficient mea-

5 | CONCLUSION
In this paper, a digital twin ‐assisted fault diagnosis system is
proposed for solving the problem of insufficient training data. The
main contributions are as follows.
1. There are a multitude of problems in traditional manual building
construction technology, which have become the shortcomings of
the new intelligent building construction technology. The devel-
opment of construction robots with a high automation level has
become an inevitable trend in the new era. To ensure the normal
operation and accurate work of construction robots, a digital
twin‐assisted fault diagnosis system is proposed in this work for
the robot joint (RV reducer). The system can effectively diagnose
faults in robot joints with insufficient data.
2. A CycleGAN ‐based digital twin model is proposed to generate
different types of simulated data for fault diagnosis. Based on the
features of CycleGAN, the model can map the X ‐domain data to
the Y ‐domain data. The noiseless and faultless vibration signals
generated by gear dynamics are the X ‐domain data of this digital
twin model, and the measured data of various fault types are the
Y‐domain data. The digital twin model requires only a small
number of measured data as the training samples to generate the
simulated data, which has the same distribution and fault
characteristics as the measured data.
3. To verify the feasibility of the digital twin ‐assisted fault diagnosis
system, we establish a test ‐rig simulating robot joint conditions
and propose a CNN ‐ResNet classifier. First, we train the CNN ‐
ResNet model by utilizing different amounts of measured data.
The results show that the fault diagnosis accuracy and conver-
gence speed of the model decrease as the training sample
decreases. Then, only 400 samples of measured data are used to
generate the simulated vibration signals, which can be directly
used for fault diagnosis. The results show that the simulated
vibration signals can provide 98.86% accuracy, which can
demonstrate that the proposed method can solve the problem
of insufficient training data. Finally, the proposed method is
compared with other mainstream methods. The results demon-
strate that the proposed method can obtain higher fault diagnosis
accuracy with fewer measured data.
