Advancements of Digital Twin Technology for Bridges
and Tunnels: A Systematic Literature Review
Lucas Bellini Machado 1; Ruan Richelly Santos 2; Dianelys Vega Ruiz 3;
Túlio Nogueira Bittencourt 4; and Marcos Massao Futai 5
Abstract: Bridges and tunnels are essential transportation infrastructure components, yet they are highly susceptible to deterioration due to
natural aging and unexpected factors. As a result, continuous monitoring and proactive maintenance are essential to ensure their safety and
functionality. The integration of advanced technological solutions into asset management is becoming increasingly important for companies to
stay competitive. Digital twins have emerged as a promising tool to optimize asset performance and enhance decision-making. Consequently,
advancing and updating knowledge on this technology is of paramount importance. This paper presents a systematic literature review that
explores the current state of digital twin technology in civil infrastructure, with a particular focus on bridges and tunnels. Drawing on data from
89 relevant research papers, it contributes to the field by examining the frequency of digital twin applications across various life-cycle phases,
evaluating their specific purposes and uses, identifying the most commonly applied technologies for these critical infrastructures, and assessing
the level of development based on the integration of technologies. Additionally, the paper highlights key challenges and provides directions for
future research regarding the application of digital twins to bridges and tunnels. DOI: 10.1061/JITSE4.ISENG-2745. © 2025 American
Society of Civil Engineers.
Introduction
Civil infrastructure is a significant part of any nation’s critical assets
and is indispensable for economic development. Its suboptimal per-
formance or performance that is lower than expected can severely
impact the country ’s gross domestic product ( Aktan et al. 1998 ).
Bridges and tunnels are critical components of modern infrastruc-
ture, ensuring connectivity and supporting economic activities. The
safety and functionality of these structures are paramount, as their
failure can lead to catastrophic consequences, including loss of life,
economic disruption, and extensive repair costs.
Bridges and tunnels exhibit distinct characteristics and chal-
lenges that distinguish them from other infrastructure systems.
Bridges face challenges related to dynamic load-bearing, material
degradation, and environmental exposure, requiring careful mon-
itoring and maintenance. Conversely, tunnels are characterized by
their geotechnical complexity, confined space, water infiltration,
ventilation, and emergency safety considerations. Hence, ensuring
the proper functioning of these infrastructures requires implement-
ing effective preventive and predictive maintenance practices.
These practices are essential for minimizing deterioration effects,
reducing the risk of failure, minimizing costly repairs, and extend-
ing the infrastructure ’s operational lifespan.
The implementation of smart technologies can effectively address
infrastructure challenges and meet growing demands ( Berglund
et al. 2020 ). One such emerging technology, the digital twin (DT),
is gaining increasing attention as an integrative tool to improve asset
performance via advanced computational techniques ( Wang et al.
2022). A DT is a dynamic, realistic digital representation of a physi-
cal product, system, or process and is continuously updated with
real-time data ( Mahmoodian et al. 2022 ). It represents a significant
evolution in modeling by extending traditional static information
with dynamic data, including both data and algorithms that help ex-
plain and predict the behavior of the physical system. By leveraging
sophisticated physical models and asset information, a DT mirrors
the life of its real-world counterpart (Dan et al. 2022; Glaessgen and
Stargel 2012). This connection between physical and virtual envi-
ronments enables operation management throughout the asset life
cycle, facilitating real-time monitoring and control of its operations
(Gürdür Broo et al. 2022 ).
The level of development of a digital twin is closely related
to technological advancements and the integration of new technol-
ogies into the virtualization process. While recent efforts have
been made to characterize and standardize digital twins ( Boyes and
Watson 2022; Jones et al. 2020 ), clear standards and definitions for
their creation and implementation are still lacking. This lack of
standardization leads to ambiguity and makes distinguishing be-
tween a digital twin and traditional virtual models challenging.
Therefore, well-established methodologies, such as building infor-
mation modeling (BIM), bridge information modeling (BrIM), and
finite-element modeling (FEM), are essential components for the
development and evolution of digital twins, serving as key refer-
ence points and foundational tools.

The applications of DTs are diverse, including smart cities,
healthcare, automotive, aerospace, and construction industries
(Fuller et al. 2020 ). The adoption of digital twins holds significant
potential for improving infrastructure management and enhancing
operational efficiency. However, the civil engineering industry is
often slower in adopting new technologies, which can introduce
environmental, economic, and human risks. Therefore, it is fun-
damental to explore and disseminate updated knowledge on this
technology to facilitate its broader adoption in civil engineering
practices.
While previous research has reviewed trends and applications of
DTs in infrastructure ( Taherkhani et al. 2024), the primary technol-
ogies involved (Long et al. 2024 ), data integration, interoperability,
and technological adaptation (Michael et al. 2024; Larbi et al. 2025),
workflow and applications of digital twins ( Pregnolato et al. 2022 ),
and an industrial overview on digital twin definitions, practices, ben-
efits, and challenges (Broo and Schooling 2023), this study makes a
unique contribution by providing a comprehensive and up-to-date
systematic literature review with a specific focus on bridges and
tunnels, critical assets in transportation infrastructure. This paper
advances the state-of-the-art by exploring the specific life-cycle
phases in which digital twins are applied to bridges and tunnels,
investigating their intended purposes and the key technologies in-
volved and evaluating the current stage of development for digital
twins in each life-cycle phase. Furthermore, the review identifies
the main challenges and future perspectives on the adoption of
digital twins in critical infrastructures such as bridges and tunnels.
Methodology
In the context of this paper, the systematic literature review (SLR)
development was fragmented into three key blocks of chronologi-
cally ordered activities: planning, execution, and summarization
(Fig. 1), as established by Kitchenham et al. ( 2015). The planning
stage is characterized by SLR protocol elaboration, in which the
research question (RQs) and search strings related to the topic of
interest are defined. The research databases, inclusion/exclusion
criteria, and quality assessment metrics are also determined in this
phase.
In an effort to bolster the construction and employment of the
review protocol, a framework was developed using the popula-
tion, intervention, comparison, outcome, and context (PICOC)
criterion (as illustrated in Table 1). This criterion was employed
to elucidate the target population for evidence extraction, discuss
the specific intervention incorporated in the study, establish the
comparative control against which the intervention was appraised,
ascertain the resulting outcomes, and provide an understanding of
the context or practical significance (application) of these findings
(Wohlin et al. 2012 ).
To address the literature gaps in the use of digital twins for
bridges and tunnels, four RQs are established as follows:
 RQ1: During which specific life-cycle phases of civil infrastruc-
ture assets are digital twins most commonly utilized, and with
what frequency are they applied to bridges and tunnels?
 RQ2: What specific purposes underlie the implementation of
digital twins in civil infrastructure, such as bridges and tunnels?
 RQ3: What are the associated technologies and levels of devel-
opment of DT for bridges and tunnels?
 RQ4: What are the primary challenges associated with the adop-
tion of digital twins in civil infrastructure, and which future per-
spectives can be identified for advancing their utilization in the
field?

and Springer citation databases yielded consistent results. Therefore,
Scopus, Engineering Village, and Springer were selected as the ci-
tation databases for this paper. Digital twins constitute a novel and
broad field of study with a diverse scope of utilization, crossing many
fields of study. To ensure that all the relevant literature was captured,
data extraction from publications was developed via defined keyword
search strings (Table 2), categorizing and filtering articles presenting
relevant aspects for creating DT in civil infrastructure, emphasizing
the technologies, tools, and framework involved in DT implementa-
tion. The string is based on Boolean operators that connect keywords

The authors opted to utilize references that did not explicitly
address the creation of a digital twin but were linked to pertinent
technologies. This decision followed a thorough analysis of articles
related to SLR. Owing to the widespread use of the term “digital
twin” in the scientific community, numerous studies have identified
their scientific constructs as DT, even though they present a less
intricate process or address fewer aspects of the digital twin defi-
nition than other studies involving related technologies do. Given
the absence of a standardized definition for DT ( Mahmoodian et al.
2022; Scully 2020 ), the search string S2, inclusion/exclusion cri-
teria, and evaluation questions were proposed to encompass devel-
opments in correlated technologies relevant to the progression of
the virtual twinning field.
To ensure the inclusion of a significant number of articles for
analysis, the study incorporated terms that concerned virtual models,
such as BIM platforms. The papers that presented these modeling
strategies associated with technologies relevant to the DT context
were included in the review process. The articles extracted from
the chosen citation databases were filtered according to the inclusion
and exclusion criteria stipulated to admit suitable papers for the re-
view, as shown in Table 3.
The inclusion and exclusion criteria established for paper se-
lection are employed in the second main stage of the SLR. This
step defines the articles selected for full reading and relevance
evaluation. The execution and summarization phases are associ-
ated with applying the SLR protocol. The software StArt version
3.3 from the Laboratory of Research on Software Engineering of
the Universidade Federal de São Carlos supported and optimized
the execution process. The software generates an initial score on
the basis of the paper information relevance, regarding the key-
words established for the protocol search. Next, a reading priority
is established, in which the title, abstract, and keywords of the
articles are analyzed according to the inclusion and exclusion
criteria.
The first batch of selected papers was subsequently read, and
to evaluate and classify the studies in terms of their importance
for the SLR, four evaluation questions (EQs) were elaborated
(Table 4) on the basis of the research questions. For the score,
the papers that presented a solution to the question EQi (EQi,
where i ∈ 1; 2; 3; 4) received 1 point. Conversely, articles that did
not completely answer the EQi received a zero value. Each paper
was evaluated for each question, with the sum of the paper grades
being the final score. Papers with a score lower than 2 (score < 2)
were considered insufficiently relevant to answer the research
questions and were therefore excluded from the review process.
Table 5 displays the quality evaluation results of the 89 articles

The final phase of the SLR, the summarization stage, encom-
passes the data extraction process of the relevant papers to develop
a statistical analysis and data synthesis. The results extracted were
organized and interpreted to translate the knowledge of the litera-
ture and answer the research questions. The summarized frame-
work employed for the SLR is illustrated in Fig. 2. From the
filtering process, the initial batch of articles, which presented more
than 8,000 papers, was reduced to 89 relevant studies.
The SLR approach systematically searches a database, employ-
ing a forward/backward process for transparency, precision, repli-
cability, and bias reduction ( Allen et al. 2023 ). This methodology
ensures accuracy in addressing research questions through careful
planning, providing a publicly available framework for each step to
enhance transparency and reproducibility. The analysis combines
qualitative and quantitative assessments for increased reliability.

Results and Discussion
This section presents the findings from the literature review process
described in the section “Methodology.” The relevant articles ob-
tained by the SLR were subjected to a rigorous synthesis and com-
pilation of the key findings. The first step focused on exploratory
data analysis, which quantifies the papers selected and the evolu-
tion of the research field over time. The findings were subsequently
divided into the main topics that help to answer the proposed re-
search questions: the life-cycle phase, intended uses, level of de-
velopment, and challenges/future directions.
Exploratory Data Analysis
Fig. 3 illustrates the annual distribution of journal papers published
from 2015 to October 2024 in English, revealing an upward trend
in adopting digital twins and their associated technologies for civil
infrastructure. The data were obtained through a search within one
of the main journal indexers (Elsevier) using the string [ “Digital
Twin” AND ( “Civil Infrastructure ” OR “Civil Engineering ” OR
Infrastructure)]. Different search string search configurations were
tested, all indicating significant growth in interest in research in this
area. This substantial increase in research output indicates the rising
popularity of digital twins among the scientific community and
their increasing consideration as a viable solution for the challenges
faced in the field of study ( Adeagbo et al. 2024 ).
RQ1: Life-Cycle Stages
RQ1 aims to identify the life-cycle phases in which digital twins are
employed. The life-cycle stages describe a critical phase for the
infrastructure life, from its design to the decommissioning phase.
Thus, establishing digital twin adoption for each stage supports
understanding the technology most commonly used and the par-
ticularities for different life-cycle periods of the asset. The life-
cycle phases established by the SLR were divided into design,
construction, operation and maintenance (O&M), special cases,
and general cases. Articles were classified as “special cases ” when
the application of DT was focused on managing atypical conditions,
and natural hazards [e.g., seismic events ( Ghahari et al. 2022 ;
Lin et al. 2021), extreme conditions (Kaewunruen et al. 2021), fires
(Wu et al. 2022b ), and emergency evacuation measures ( Wu et al.
2022c; Han et al. 2020 )]. On the other hand, “general cases” refers
to articles that either cover multiple life-cycle phases or propose
broadly applicable DT frameworks that are not restricted to a single
phase or specific event.
The studies were initially organized according to Table 6, clas-
sifying each reference by the life-cycle stage approached and the
type of infrastructure used for the development of DT.
Fig. 4 shows the data analysis of the life-cycle stage to which
digital twin technology was applied. It illustrates the significant
prevalence of the application to the domain of O&M of infrastruc-
ture, accounting for 75.3% of the cases, followed by processes
focused on the asset construction stage at 11.2%.
Another key aspect of the analysis focuses on the frequency
of investments in each category of infrastructure asset. RQ1 fur-
ther aims to examine the specific application of the technology to
bridges and tunnels. In Fig. 5 the distribution of DT developments
in bridges, tunnels, and other broader applications in illustrated.
The term “general” refers to instances in which the technology is
employed for large-scale infrastructure, encompassing bridges and
tunnels.

ture. Tunnel monitoring often involves additional challenges, such
as a lack of natural light, limited access, and the need for specific

RQ2: Intended Uses
RQ2 aims to identify the specific purposes underlying the imple-
mentation of digital twins in civil infrastructure such as bridges and
tunnels. Unlike the categorization based on the life-cycle stage and
asset type, which is relatively straightforward, the classification that
pertains to the digital twin ’s intended use involves a certain level of
subjectivity. The intended use was classified according to Scully
(2020) to establish an application metric that correlates the classi-
fication process to the digital twinning complexity level: digitize,
visualize, simulate, emulate, extract, orchestrate, and predict. This
classification seeks to understand the complexity and maturity level
of the methodology for civil infrastructure.
A digital twin focused on simulation intends to accurately re-
present the physical system behavior under real-life conditions
within its environment. For this development stage, the FEM and
BIM/BrIM are typically employed to implement a digital model. A
digital twin for extraction focuses on establishing data streams from
the physical asset to the virtual system. Cloud-based ( Dang et al.
2022) and online ( Meixedo et al. 2022 ) strategies have emerged as

The review results confirm that classification of the intended use
of digital twins involves varying levels of complexity: digitizing,
visualizing, simulating, emulating, extracting, orchestrating, and pre-
dicting (Scully 2020). Each objective represents an evolution in DT
development, adding capabilities to the virtual model. Examples of
uses include digitizing data, creating digital replicas, accurate sim-
ulation, emulating physical systems, real-time data extraction, vir-
tual model orchestration, and future behavior prediction. Tunnels
tend to concentrate at higher complexity levels, especially in field
data extraction, whereas bridges and large infrastructure projects
generally have more simulation use cases. The most common DT
applications include simulation, extraction, and visualization, pri-
marily for infrastructure management during the O&M life-cycle
stage. Simulation is a key application in both the O&M and con-
struction stages, where replicating physical system behavior under
real-life conditions is essential.
RQ3: Associated Technologies and Level of
Development
RQ3 maps the leading technologies implemented for a civil infra-
structure DT, which are categorized into various aspects, including
modeling, SHM (Tan et al. 2022), numerical analysis, code develop-
ment (Naser 2023), inspection, machine learning (Ruiz et al. 2024),
deep learning ( Yang 2022; Ruiz et al. 2025 ; Rajkumar et al. 2023 ),
databases, virtual reality/augmented reality (VR/AR) ( Awadallah
and Sadhu 2023 ), and single-board computers.
Fig. 7 shows the frequency of application of leading technologies
in the construction of digital twins. The most common technologies
applied to civil engineering digital twins are modeling, structural
sensing, and numerical analysis, which are linked through infor-
mation exchanges facilitated by the development of programming
routines. Technologies used for field data acquisition, such as
IoT, image capture, sensors, and machine learning, require synchro-
nization with DT models to ensure effective implementation. The
low utilization of virtual reality may indicate a potential future di-
rection for its integration with civil engineering digital twins, mainly
because of its benefits for operation and maintenance activities.
The critical aspect lies in the integration of various technolo-
gies into the DT process, which is also an objective of RQ3. The
levels of DT development are closely tied to the incorporation of
new technologies into the twinning process, with different meth-
odologies and technologies being applied for various purposes.
The use of only one technology is rare in creating a virtual copy
of reality. Based on the data collected, the association of at least
three of these technologies is a limiting factor in the methodology
for constructing digital twins for analytical assets. Fig. 8 shows
the association of technologies with DTs. The application of more
than three of the standardized technologies represents more than
66% of the applications.
In addition, the connection between the life-cycle phases and
technologies is illustrated qualitatively in Fig. 9.
In the design phase, virtualization relies on high-level modeling
with detailed structural calculations fed by field data. These are
then analyzed using visual immersion technologies and algorithms
for interpretation. Modeling and numerical analysis are required
during construction, with a focus on chronological construction
steps (simulation). Additionally, there is a certain level of data ac-
quisition automation, which can be based on the Internet of Things
(IoT) technology and data archiving during construction. These
measures are essential to address unforeseen issues during asset
implementation.
The O&M management phase is associated with obtaining and
connecting data on structural behavior and performance, a crucial
stage for applying the digital twin methodology to civil infrastruc-
ture. This phase involves the most comprehensive application of
technologies and processes, creating a holistic digital representation

previously acquired data and those continuously provided by

Using digital twins to simulate behavior in scenarios involving
special situations during different life-cycle phases serves signifi-
cant purposes. First, it facilitates the emulation of extreme requests
during the design phase, aiding in understanding how the structure
will respond to such conditions. Second, it enables the extraction
and storage of information from unexpected design issues, primarily
focused on optimizing construction decisions. Finally, digital twins
can be employed to develop an alert system for readings that devi-
ate from the predicted behavior in the design phase and are ob-
served during construction, allowing prompt action. Additionally,
the historical storage of occasional events in the structure provides
valuable data for future analyses and decision-making processes. By
combining these functionalities, digital twins play a crucial role in
enhancing the overall performance and resilience of infrastructure
throughout its life cycle.
The transition of digital twin technology from conceptual frame-
works to real-world infrastructure applications requires a structured
and strategic approach. This involves clearly defined key perfor-
mance objectives and the development of a robust data ecosystem
capable of integrating heterogeneous sources. Key enablers include
scalable storage solutions, data quality protocols, data interoperabil-
ity, and multidisciplinary collaboration across engineering, data sci-
ence, and software development. Although many studies remain
conceptual, recent studies have demonstrated practical applications
in bridge and tunnel infrastructure. For instance, Meixedo et al.
(2022) developed a DT framework that integrates SHM and three-
dimensional modeling for a historical bridge, enabling real-time
visualization and predictive maintenance. Febrianto et al. ( 2022)
proposed a hybrid system combining SHM and BIM to support
bridge asset management. In the tunnel infrastructure, Y u et al.
(2023) introduced a DT-based risk evaluation system utilizing real-
time sensor inputs for emergency response, while He et al. ( 2024)
implemented a simulation-based DT to model tunnel fire scenarios.
These examples reflect growing adoption through modular and
pilot-scale implementations. However, broader applications are still
challenged by integration complexity, organizational readiness, and
costs. Nonetheless, these cases confirm that DTs and their associate
technologies are actively shaping modern infrastructure manage-
ment rather than remaining theoretical constructs.

tation strategies can make digital twins more accessible to industry
professionals. Rather than requiring a full-scale digital transforma-
tion, practical adoption can begin with isolated components tailored
to stakeholder needs or based on deployments with lower maturity
levels. Aligning DT tools with existing asset management systems,

RQ4: Main Challenges and Future Directions
DT development for civil bridges and tunnels is still incipient, and
the current practices do not provide the necessary skills for ade-
quately developing and implementing the technology. Additionally,
there are no well-defined standards, processes, or tools to support
their development Thus, projects concerning digital twins are multi-
disciplinary efforts of civil engineers and computer scientists, which
combine practices, procedures, terminology, the level of maturity of
the employed technology, and data fluency of both domains and
from different stakeholders ( Gürdür Broo et al. 2022 ).
For the specific context of bridges and tunnels, some of the main
challenges and innovations for DTs are related to organizing and
storing all the structural information necessary to compare with
real-time SHM data, correlating real-life data with computational
simulations, improving inspection and maintenance activities, auto-
mating alerts through machine learning and new technologies,
incorporating sensors and inspection information into the DT for
continuous monitoring and diagnostics, predicting future structural
behavior based on the stored data accumulated over the structure life
cycle, applying computer vision to detect and track damage evolu-
tion, combining cloud-based computational analysis with real data,
and enabling comprehensive infrastructure management by integrat-
ing inspection, monitoring, and maintenance with automatic analy-
sis into the DT ( Futai et al. 2022 ). Integrating data from real assets
to the virtual model is one of the main concerns stipulated as es-
sential future improvements for digital twin research ( Marra et al.
2021; McGuire et al. 2016 ; Chakraborty and Adhikari 2021 ; Zou
et al. 2019 ).
Managing and processing data, especially in civil infrastructure,
is a significant challenge since data in civil infrastructure projects
are often presented in a disconnected and heterogeneous manner.
Understanding the relationship among various data parameters is
also a challenge for developing DT structures. Tunnel monitoring
often involves additional challenges, such as a lack of natural light,
limited access, and the need for specific sensing technologies.
Digital twins depend on cutting-edge data processing solutions,
such as machine learning and optimization algorithms ( Sacks et al.
2020). Thus, future research may focus on overcoming the current
constraints of these techniques. At their core, AI-based solutions
rely on a few key factors, such as generalization, data availabil-
ity, historical context, training time, and computational capacity.
Generalization is crucial because it enables models to apply their
knowledge to new, unseen data, making them versatile for prac-
tical applications. Thus, unsupervised and semisupervised learn-
ing algorithms should be used to increase generalization ( Dang
et al. 2022 ).
In addition to technical knowledge regarding asset structural
and operational behavior, for implementing DTs it is essential to
assimilate expertise from new subjects, such as project management,
data management, data quality, data architecture, cyber security, and
even privacy and ethics. Integrating these different areas and provid-
ing comprehensive communication between stakeholders are vital
to bridging the gap between the real world and the virtual world, en-
abling the implementation of a full-scale digital twin. In addition to
the organizational barrier, implementing DT will require overcom-
ing technical, sociological, and commercial challenges ( Sacks et al.
2020).
Another important point for future development is related to the
DT nomenclature. Many studies often develop high complexity
level models associated with current technologies; however, they
are unaware that they are creating a twinning of the infrastructure.
The term DT and its framework are not widespread, promoting
problems with increasing the number of virtual twinning applica-
tions. Approximately 76% of review papers use the term DT to
describe the application methodology, whereas others build com-
plex models using more current technologies without acknowledg-
ing that they are creating DTs. Therefore, a standardization of the
definition of the digital twin is essential to determine what differ-
entiates a digital twin from a less complete virtual model.
Moreover, future state-of-the-art reviews may benefit from ex-
panding the scope of analysis of this study to include gray litera-
ture, such as theses, conference proceedings, and technical reports.
Additionally, incorporating practitioner-oriented sources, such as
industry reports and trade publications, may enrich the understand-
ing of practical applications and real-world challenges. Broadening
the range of databases enhances the comprehensiveness of the re-
view and reduces potential publication bias. Finally, considering
publications in languages other than English is recommended
for a more inclusive and globally representative synthesis of the
literature.
In light of these considerations, future research on DT in civil
infrastructure, particularly for bridges and tunnels, could benefit
from addressing specific research questions. These questions are
proposed to guide complementary systematic reviews and further
investigations:
 What are effective strategies for integrating real-time sensor and
inspection data into DT models for continuous and automated
monitoring?
 Which data architectures best support the management of dis-
connected and heterogeneous civil infrastructure data for DT
functionality?
 How can infrastructure administrators ensure secure, efficient
data acquisition and management to meet the demands of DT
systems?
 How can DT system design overcome monitoring constraints in
civil infrastructure (e.g., poor lighting, limited access)?
Conclusion
This systematic literature review examined the advancements in DT
technology for bridges and tunnels, aiming to address four research
questions related to life-cycle phases, intended uses, associated
technologies, and main challenges and perspectives. One notable
challenge encountered was the inconsistent use of the term “digital
twin,” which complicates the identification of virtual twinning ap-
plications within the published literature. To mitigate this problem,
this research employed alternative search strings to capture nonre-
ferenced DTs. The findings revealed that digital twins have been
adopted in all stages of the civil infrastructure life cycle, with a
predominant focus on O&M, representing 75.3% of the cases.
Bridges are the most common application of DTs, followed by tun-
nels and general applications. The varying levels of complexity in
the intended use of digital twins are illustrated, ranging from digi-
tizing and visualizing to more advanced functions such as simulat-
ing, emulating, extracting, orchestrating, and predicting. Tunnels
tend to have a higher concentration of virtual twin cases at the more
complex levels, particularly in field data extraction, while bridges
