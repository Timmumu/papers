ABSTRACT
With the rapid development of building intelligence, the efficient management and control of 
central air-conditioning systems have become a research hotspot in the HVAC (Heating, Venti -
lation, and Air Conditioning) field. The effective integration of BIM (Building Information 
Modeling) technology and digital twins into HVAC systems enables the visualization of smart 
operation and maintenance processes, as well as information sharing across complex equipment 
systems, providing technical support for modeling, simulation, fault detection, and optimized 
control of air conditioning systems. This paper proposes a BIM-based digital twin framework for 
central air-conditioning systems. Through secondary development of a laboratory air condition -
ing system ’ s BIM model, a communication architecture and data-driven logic for the central air 
conditioning digital twin system is presented. The system is designed and developed using OPC/ 
UA technology and the Unity platform. Comprehensive performance testing validates the stability 
and reliability of the digital twin platform, The test results show that, under typical operating 
scenarios, the platform consistently maintains a CPU frame time within 10 ms, memory usage 
below 2 GB, script execution time under 10 ms, and fewer than 2000 rendering batches. All 
functional test cases were successfully passed, demonstrating stable feature responsiveness and 
validating the platform ’ s excellent performance in response speed, security, and interactive ca -
pabilities. These results provide strong technical support for the intelligent operation and 
maintenance of central air-conditioning systems.
1. Introduction
The emergence of new information technologies such as the Internet of Things (IoT) [ 1 ], big data [ 2 ], and artificial intelligence (AI) 
[ 3 ] has positioned digital twins [ 4 ] as a bridge connecting physical entities with their digital counterparts, becoming a key enabler for 
digital transformation and intelligent upgrades [ 5 ]. Digital twin technology utilizes IoT to collect and synchronize real-time parameter 
data from the physical world, enabling comprehensive information monitoring, in-depth data analysis, accurate predictive simula -
tions, and efficient optimization control. This opens new pathways for intelligent development across numerous fields. In recent years, 
digital twin technology has been widely applied in energy management, building environmental control, and industrial system 
optimization.

Against the backdrop of increasing demands for intelligence and efficiency in today’s built environment, heating, ventilation, and 
air conditioning (HVAC) systems—being a critical component of building operations—have received growing attention for their 
optimized management and efficient maintenance. Building Information Modeling (BIM) provides detailed information about 
buildings and systems, offering strong support during the planning, design, and construction phases. It enables all project stakeholders 
to communicate efficiently and make precise decisions within a visualized and collaborative platform, thereby improving overall 
project execution efficiency and quality.
In response to these challenges and opportunities, this study proposes a BIM-based digital twin framework designed to support the 
intelligent operation and maintenance of central air-conditioning systems. The framework leverages real-time data integration and 
secondary development of BIM models to enable dynamic, data-driven management. Unlike previous research that treats BIM, digital 
twins, or HVAC systems in isolation, this work presents an integrated platform that combines these technologies into a unified and 
practical solution.
The paper is structured as follows: Section 2 introduces the literature review; Section 3 introduces the digital twin framework 
required for the platform; Section 4 details the modeling and secondary development of laboratory equipment; Section 5 implements 
real-time data communication for the platform; Section 6 presents the design, development, and functional modules of the digital twin 
platform; Section 7 tests the platform’s performance and presents results; Section 8 discusses the research findings; The final section 
concludes the study.
2. Literature review
In recent years, Building Information Modeling (BIM) and digital twin (DT) technologies have gained increasing attention in the 
fields of architecture, energy, and facility management. Their integration not only enhances building energy efficiency and intelligent 
operations but also shows broad application prospects in energy system optimization, indoor environmental control, and industrial 
system modeling. Studies have demonstrated that digital twins, when combined with AI and IoT, enable real-time monitoring, fault 
diagnosis, and performance optimization of HVAC systems. Meanwhile, the collaboration with BIM supports dynamic control and 
efficient management across the building lifecycle. Existing literature has proposed BIM-based HVAC optimization models, indoor 
thermal comfort prediction systems, and systematic reviews, highlighting the importance of standardization and data interoperability 
in building effective BIM–DT platforms. Building on this foundation, the present study focuses on the integration strategies and 
practical value of BIM and digital twins in addressing real-time changes in HVAC system usage.
In the field of energy system optimization, digital twins are used to coordinate and control multi-vector smart energy systems. 
O’Dwyer et al. [ 6 ] integrated energy management tools with digital twins to achieve dynamic coordination of smart energy systems, 
significantly improving sustainability. Butean et al. [ 7 ] proposed a digital twin framework for optimizing high-temperature heat 
pumps, reducing energy waste in industrial renewable energy systems. Bani-Hani et al. [ 8 ] combined sustainable refrigerants with 
digital twins to develop an energy-efficient and eco-friendly solution for machine tool cooling systems. In the building environmental 
control domain, digital twin technology enhances the intelligence of building energy management. Cui et al. [ 9 ] developed a digital 
twin model for PERA’s air conditioning system, optimizing performance through real-time monitoring and simulation. Manfren et al. 
[ 10 ] demonstrated the application of digital twins in building energy monitoring using smart thermostatic radiator valves and gas 
absorption heat pumps. Lu et al. [ 11 ] introduced a clustering compression-based calibration method for HVAC digital twins, reducing 
computational complexity in modeling. ElArwady et al. [ 12 ] integrated digital twins with machine learning to establish a 
high-precision indoor thermal comfort prediction model, supporting dynamic environmental regulation. In industrial system modeling 
and optimization, digital twins are widely adopted for complex system performance enhancement. Yang et al. [ 13 ] built a compressor 
characteristic surrogate model using digital twins and proposed optimization algorithms to improve efficiency. Cui et al. [ 14 ] 
developed a data-driven digital twin model for supercritical coal-fired boilers, enabling early detection of metal temperature anom -
alies. Tao et al. [ 15 ] proposed a five-dimensional digital twin model and systematically summarized its technical framework and ten 
major applications, providing methodological guidance for industrial intelligence.
Research on digital twin technology in building energy management, industrial system optimization, and indoor environmental 
control continues to deepen, with applications gradually expanding to HVAC systems. In HVAC systems, digital twin technology is 
widely used for fault detection, model updates, and performance optimization. Xie et al. [ 16 ] developed a digital twin-based fault 
diagnosis method to improve equipment maintenance efficiency. Chen et al. [ 17 ] utilized a broad learning system (BLS) algorithm to 
achieve dynamic updates of HVAC digital twin models. Ma et al. [ 18 ] combined sparse autoencoders (SSAE) with transfer learning to 
build a chiller fault diagnosis digital twin model, enhancing anomaly detection robustness. Digital twin technology, integrated with 
IoT and AI, drives dynamic monitoring and intelligent regulation of indoor environments. Zhang et al. [ 19 ] proposed a digital 
twin-driven ventilation system for automated humidity optimization in underground heritage sites. Qian et al. [ 20 ] designed an 
intelligent digital twin platform for real-time indoor air quality monitoring and control. Zhang et al. [ 21 ] innovatively proposed a 
digital twin-driven fresh air curtain wall generation method to dilute air pollutants, dynamically protecting historical building facades. 
Li et al. [ 22 ] optimized the performance of cascade refrigeration systems using digital twin models. Machado et al. [ 23 ] developed a 
digital twin model for solar absorption chillers, advancing renewable energy applications in cooling systems. Yoon et al. [ 24 ] inte -
grated digital twin technology with ontology through an AI agent-based framework, enabling buildings to achieve real-time opera -
tional management.
In building lifecycle management, the integration of BIM and smart building technologies provides more accurate support for real- 
time monitoring and optimized control of equipment. Alshehri et al. [ 25 ] proposed enhancing the sustainability and resilience of the 
construction industry by combining BIM with emerging technologies such as artificial intelligence and the Internet of Things (IoT).

The integration of digital twin technology in these studies mainly focuses on the following areas: (1) Visualization during the

Based on the analysis of the above studies, three major issues remain insufficiently addressed in current research: First, most

architecture that simultaneously ensures the integrity of the BIM model, real-time equipment data, and system scalability. Therefore, 
the motivation of this study is to extend the application of BIM technology from the design and construction stages to the operation and 
maintenance phase of central air-conditioning systems, thereby enhancing their intelligent management. The aim of this research is to 
develop a BIM-based digital twin platform for central air-conditioning, enabling real-time mapping of equipment operation status, data 
synchronization, and interactive control to support intelligent operation and maintenance. To achieve these goals, the study focuses on 
the following research questions: (1) How can BIM models be efficiently integrated with digital twin systems? (2) How can a system 
architecture be designed to ensure scalability, interactivity, and real-time performance?
To address the above issues, this study proposes a modeling and operation platform for central air-conditioning systems that in -
tegrates BIM and digital twin technologies. By conducting secondary development of the BIM model for a laboratory air-conditioning 
system, the study establishes a mapping logic between equipment, model, and data, and designs a data communication mechanism 
based on the OPC/UA protocol along with a visualized platform architecture. This platform fills the research gap in digital twin 
modeling for central air-conditioning systems; it builds a data-driven logic structure that supports the fusion of multi-source het -
erogeneous data and real-time communication, thereby enhancing the platform’s scalability and general applicability. Systematic

engineering deployment in building energy management.
3. Proposed framework
With the proliferation of digital twin technology, this study proposes a BIM-based digital twin framework for central air condi -
tioning systems, based on the established five-dimensional digital twin model framework [ 15 ]. The framework synergistically in -
tegrates expertise in sensor technology, communication technology, and digital signal processing to develop a highly efficient and 
accurate BIM-based digital twin platform. As shown in Fig. 1 , the system architecture comprises four layers: Physical Layer, Data Layer, 
Model Layer, and Application Layer, which interact closely through collaborative support, providing a solid technical foundation for 
smart operation, maintenance, and precise control of central air conditioning systems.
3.1. Physical Layer
As the foundational layer of the framework, the Physical Layer comprises physical equipment such as refrigeration units, air 
handling units, cooling towers, ducts, and air outlets. It generates data from physical entities and their related subsystems, utilizing 
sensors and communication protocols for data collection and processing. Physical devices operate based on predefined start-stop 
requirements, while sensors capture multi-source heterogeneous data (e.g., device attributes, operational status, process metrics, 
and fault signals). After preliminary processing, the data is transmitted to the next layer. Additionally, the physical systems must 
respond to commands or feedback issued by the digital twin platform. In this framework, a gateway acts as middleware that connects 
the Physical Layer and Data Layer through standardized protocols. The gateway converts machine-specific protocols into standard 
communication protocols to ensure seamless data flow throughout the architecture.
3.2. Data Layer
The Data Layer comprises three core components: data transmission, data processing, and database management. For data 
transmission, it employs a wide variety of communication protocols and flexible transmission methods to ensure stable and efficient 
data transfer between diverse devices and systems. In the data processing phase, specialized equipment and sensors are used to perform 
in-depth analysis of raw collected data, promptly detect anomalies, and process faulty data, thereby ensuring data accuracy and 
reliability. The database, serving as the storage core, encompasses a broad spectrum of data types, including digital twin data 
generated during equipment operation, long-term accumulated experiential knowledge, and physics-based model data. When selecting 
database management software, relational or non-relational systems—such as SQL Server, MySQL, MongoDB, and Redis—can be 
chosen based on actual needs to meet the storage and management requirements of data with varying scales and structures.

3.3. Model layer
The core of the Model Layer is the BIM model developed using Autodesk Revit software. This model accurately simulates detailed 
information of various equipment in the mechanical room through parametric equipment modeling and family library construction. 
The Revit family library provides robust flexibility, enabling rapid adjustments to device models and specifications (e.g., cooling 
towers, chillers) based on actual requirements. For equipment with no existing families available, users can develop custom families 
tailored to specific needs, ensuring that the BIM model closely aligns with the operational conditions of physical equipment. This 
process thereby establishes an accurate digital model foundation for subsequent simulation analyses and operational maintenance 
management.
3.4. Application Layer
The Application Layer integrates core functions such as 3D visual monitoring of equipment status, real-time start/stop control, and 
predictive maintenance decision optimization. By utilizing technologies including HTML, CSS, JavaScript, WebGL, Unity3D, and 
MQTT, this layer achieves real-time, comprehensive monitoring and precise control of equipment through the synergy of twin data and 
BIM models. Additionally, it provides robust data support and technical assurance for remaining useful life (RUL) prediction and data- 
driven predictive maintenance strategies, thereby facilitating a transition from reactive to proactive maintenance. This significantly 
improves operational efficiency and reliability, reduces maintenance costs, and enhances the system ’ s intelligent capabilities and 
sustainable development potential.
Compared with existing research, this framework demonstrates three innovations: First, it proposes a universal data communi -
cation structure based on the OPC/UA protocol, which addresses the issue of static data silos in traditional BIM models during the 
operation and maintenance phase, enabling real-time bidirectional interaction and mapping between the physical and model layers. 
Second, the framework can simultaneously integrate BIM data and sensor data, establishing a data fusion channel specifically designed 
for central air-conditioning systems. Third, the framework includes modules such as real-time equipment status visualization and start- 
stop control, offering a high degree of integration and practical applicability.

5. Real-time mapping of data for central air-conditioning twin model systems
5.1. Data requirements for virtual-real interaction in central air-conditioning
The central air conditioning mechanical room, as a complex and critical system, generates data with distinct multi-source, real- 
time, and heterogeneous characteristics. The underlying building data are categorized into three types: First, there are BIM-based 
building entities and equipment data, which provide stable static data for the system; Second, there are the real-time operating 
data of the internal building equipment, which change with the operation of the equipment and constitute dynamic data; Third, there 
are other equipment parameters and maintenance data, which include both static inherent parameters of the equipment and dynamic 
parameters due to maintenance, ensuring that the system comprehensively reflects the operational status of the building. As shown in 
Fig. 4 . Among them, building materials are categorical attribute data (non-numeric), used to identify the material of components; 
building height is continuous numerical data, which can be used for elevation modeling analysis; and building type is discrete 
enumerated data, used to define the functional category of equipment or space.
5.2. OPC/UA and communication function based on central air-conditioning system
In digital twin systems, efficient and accurate data exchange and deep integration are key to achieving intelligent and refined 
control. With the increasing complexity of device types, data structures, and communication scenarios, there is a pressing need for a 
unified communication protocol and technical framework that can handle multi-source heterogeneous data and support real-time 
bidirectional communication [ 37 ]. Among the many industrial protocols available, OPC/UA is widely regarded as one of the most 
representative solutions due to its high scalability, security, and cross-platform compatibility.
To illustrate the advantages of OPC/UA technology, consider its application in central air-conditioning systems. The OPC/UA 
server captures key environmental parameters in real-time and with high-precision, including temperature variations, humidity 
fluctuations, air quality changes, and detailed equipment status and performance metrics during operation. Through the OPC/UA 
protocol, these data are securely, rapidly, and accurately transmitted across the real-digital boundary to the digital twin platform, 
these data thereby serve as a robust driver for system simulation and predictive analysis. UA clients are deployed across applications, 
devices, and systems to receive and process server data in real time. This enables remote monitoring and intelligent, precise regulation 
of the central air-conditioning system, ensuring optimal operational performance at all times.

8. Discussion
Building upon the five-dimension digital twin model proposed by Tao et al. [ 15 ], this study further develops a digital twin 
framework specifically for central air-conditioning systems. This model systematically defines five core components: physical entities, 
virtual entities, connections, data, and services, thereby providing a theoretical foundation for the structural modeling of digital twin 
systems. In the modeling process of this platform, the layered design concept of the five-dimension model was integrated to construct a 
scalable digital twin framework tailored for central air-conditioning systems. This framework not only supports the digital trans -
formation of such systems but also lays a solid architectural foundation for future platform expansions, offering valuable insights for 
the intelligent upgrading of other industrial systems as well. Systematic testing, including functional and non-functional assessments, 
demonstrates the superiority of the DT-BIM platform in user interaction, response speed, and security, laying a practical foundation for 
its engineering promotion and optimization of building energy management.
Compared to existing research, this study achieves deep synergy between BIM and digital twins. By breaking through the limitation 
of traditional studies where BIM serves merely as a visualization tool [ 28 ], we realize dynamic integration of BIM models with the 
digital twin platform was realized through secondary development, enabling real-time mapping of equipment status to provide 
high-fidelity data support for precise control. The proposed OPC/UA protocol-based general-purpose data-driven logical architecture 
for central air conditioning systems supports efficient processing and synchronization of multi-source heterogeneous data, resolving 
compatibility issues in existing methods [ 6 ] caused by over-reliance on specific software frameworks, thereby enhancing system 
scalability and standardization. For the first time, platform testing incorporated both functional (e.g., interface interaction, control

Despite these interim progresses, limitations require further investigation. The current model exhibits limited adaptability to

9. Conclusion
This paper proposes a BIM-based digital twin platform for central air conditioning systems and demonstrated its feasibility through 
functional testing and non-functional testing. The conclusions are as follows: 
(1) Through theoretical and analytical studies, BIM model parameters and equipment specifications for central air conditioning 
systems were obtained. A BIM model and family library based on room equipment parameters were constructed, with through 
secondary development to extract equipment information. This provides the digital twin platform with precise visualization 
models and accurate device data.
(2) A BIM-based digital twin platform for central air conditioning systems was established, achieving real-time data mapping based 
on OPC/UA. This created an efficient bidirectional channel for real-time data transmission in central air conditioning systems. 
The proposed platform enables real-time monitoring of equipment status, provides technical support for fault detection and 
predictive maintenance.
(3) Functional and non-functional testing ensured the secure and stable operation of the platform, providing a technical framework 
for smart O&M of central air conditioning systems.
This study makes the following key contributions: First, the proposed platform addresses a significant research gap in the digital 
twin modeling of central air-conditioning systems. Second, it establishes a robust data-driven logic structure that supports the inte -
gration of multi-source heterogeneous data and facilitates real-time communication, thereby enhancing the platform’s scalability and 
versatility. Finally, the platform has been rigorously validated through comprehensive functional and performance testing, providing a 
solid foundation for its future implementation in building energy management.

Despite achieving preliminary results, this study still faces several limitations that warrant further exploration. One key issue is the 
lack of adaptability to dynamic scenarios—the current model’s ability to predict extreme conditions or unexpected faults has not yet 
been validated, and future work should incorporate reinforcement learning to enhance system adaptability. In addition, the cross-
