Multisensory and BIM-Integrated
Digital Twin to Improve Urban
Excavation Safety
Donghai Liu 1; Chenyang Sun 2; Junjie Chen, Aff.M.ASCE 3; and Lei Liu 4
Abstract: Urban excavation is an indispensable process for many construction activities such as road paving, house building, and pipe
rehabilitation. However, the everincreasing complexity of underground utilities (e.g., water mains, gas lines, and sewage pipes) in urban
environments challenges the safety of urban excavation, posing tremendous risks of potential collision and damage accidents. By obtaining
real-time excavation information and high-fidelity simulation to evaluate safety risks, digital twin (DT) has an unexplored potential to
improve urban excavation safety (UES). This research aims to investigate how a DT for urban excavation can be developed and used
to improve UES. First, a multisensory solution is proposed to equip the physical excavators with the capability to precisely estimate their
three-dimensional (3D) poses based on the kinematic model and social spider algorithm (SSA). Second, a building information model (BIM)
of buried utilities and a 3D model of the excavators are integrated to form a dynamic virtual model that mirrors the actual excavation process.
Third, based on the physical-virtual coupling DT, a real-time safety control method is proposed to proactively monitor urban excavation,
dynamically assess collision risk, and timely warn against unsafe behaviors. A system prototype was developed and applied in a case study
in Shandong, China. Results show that the system can precisely twin the pose of the excavator, increasing the estimation accuracy of
the translation by at least 4.0 cm. The system can display the dynamic spatial position of the excavator and the buried pipes in 3D and
automatically guide the excavator to operate safely in real-time, thereby avoiding potential collision accidents. DOI: 10.1061/JCCEE5.

Introduction
With rapid urbanization, various types of construction take place in
different corners of our cities, ranging from tunneling, house devel-
opment, and road renovation, to pipeline rehabilitation. Many of
these construction activities involve an excavation process, which
removes earth materials on the surface either in order to install/
repair utilities underground, or to replace with manmade structures
for foundation engineering. Excavation in urban areas has always
been a challenge for the underground surface of our cities has
already been filled with an extremely complex network system
consisting of water mains, power lines, natural gas pipes, sewage,
drainage, inter alia . Urban excavation among such a complex but
invisible network is like “a man walking on eggshells, ” which,
with any carelessness, can cause a collision with the underground
utilities, potentially leading to destructive consequences. Over the
years, there have been many major accidents in which excavators
hit and destroyed underground pipes, resulting in huge loss of life
and property ( Li et al. 2015 ; Talmaki et al. 2013 ; Talmaki and
Kamat 2012). According to the report of the Underground Pipeline
Committee of China Urban Planning Association (UPCC), from
2019 to 2021, 3,450 pipeline accidents occurred in total, of which
26.1% (902 incidents) were due to excavation damage and incorrect
operation, and caused 242 fatalities, 648 injuries, and hundreds of
millions in property damage.
The challenges of UES reside in several aspects. First, the tradi-
tional excavation operation depends on the experience of the oper-
ator, which will lead to incorrect operation because the position of
the bucket tip and the body cannot be accurately grasped. Second,
due to the invisibility and uncertainty of the underground pipe,
the excavator operator cannot accurately judge the relative posi-
tion between the bucket tip and the underground pipe in real-time
(Su et al. 2013 ; Tanoli et al. 2019 ). Third, the lack of a convenient
and friendly way to accurately and timely transmit the 3D pose of
the excavator and the spatial position of the underground pipe to the
excavator operator.
In light of its challenging nature, research efforts have been
made to improve UES. In terms of excavator 3D pose estima-
tion, the current research estimates the 3D pose of the excavator
from visual and nonvisual perspectives, respectively. Although the
method of visual estimation has the advantages of low cost and
convenient data acquisition, it is difficult to apply it to improve
UES due to sight occlusion, camera distance limitation, and other
problems (Feng et al. 2018 ; Liang et al. 2019 ; Lundeen et al. 2016 ;
Zhao et al. 2021); The method of nonvisual estimation integrates all

pipes is vividly transmitted to the excavator operator in real-time to
help him judge the relative position of the excavator and the under-
ground pipe and further realize safety warnings so as to avoid pipe
collision and damage accidents.
Digital twin (DT) technologies have the potential to improve
collaboration between humans and excavators in urban excava-
tion. A DT is a virtual replica of a physical entity (e.g., a product,
process, system) that can exert influence on the physical counter-
part by predictive analytics and simulation based on real-time col-
lected data. This bidirectional communication mechanism and
dynamic simulation capability of DT can benefit UES from vari-
ous aspects. First, the DT can collect and aggregate real-time
pose information of physical excavators with the capability to
precisely estimate its 3D pose. Second, the DT can integrate a
BIM of buried utilities, urban terrains, and a 3D model of the
excavators to form a dynamic virtual model that mirrors the ac-
tual excavation process. Third, an intuitive and interactive human-
robot interface enabled by the DT will proactively monitor urban
excavation, dynamically assess collision risk, and timely warn of
unsafe behaviors.
The objective of this research is to improve the accuracy of
excavator 3D pose estimation and establish a visual, dynamic,
and high-precision intelligent guidance system to ensure the safety
of urban excavation. Based on the presented demand, this research
proposes a digital twin-based intelligent guidance and safety warn-
ing technology for urban excavation and develops the correspond-
ing software and hardware systems. The main contributions of this
article are summarized as follows:
1. Using the Global Navigation Satellite System (GNSS) and in-
ertial measurement unit (IMU), a multisensory solution is pro-
posed to equip the physical excavators with the capability to
precisely estimate their 3D poses based on the kinematic model
and SSA.
2. Based on the Internet of Things and BIM technology, a BIM of
buried utilities, urban terrains, and a 3D model of the excavators
are integrated to form a dynamic virtual model that mirrors the
actual excavation process.
3. Based on the physical-virtual coupling DT, the system can pro-
vide the excavator operator with the 3D pose information of the
excavator and the relative distance between the excavator and
the underground pipe. It further evaluates the risk of excavator
operations in real-time and ensures the safety of urban excava-
tion through early warning.
The remainder of this article is organized as follows. “Related
Works” reviews the previous work related to this article. In “Meth-
odologies,” the proposed method is introduced and elaborated in
detail. “System Prototype Development ” develops the system.
“Case Study” verifies the proposed method and developed system.
“Discussion ” elaborates on the research results of this article.
A conclusion is drawn in “Conclusion and Prospect. ”
Related Works
Excavator Pose Estimation Method
In the current practical research, there are mainly two methods
based on nonvisual sensors and based on a vision for excavator
pose estimation.
Vision-Based Pose Estimation Method
The input information of the vision-based excavator pose estima-
tion method often comes from the camera on the construction site.
Compared with precision instruments such as laser scanners, the
camera is widely used in construction sites and has the advantages
of low cost and convenient data acquisition ( Liang et al. 2019). The
vision-based pose estimation method can directly analyze the po-
sition and orientation information from the input data, identify the
on-site construction equipment (Golparvar-Fard et al. 2013; Soltani
et al. 2016; Zou and Kim 2007), and then estimate the pose of its six
degrees of freedom ( Feng et al. 2018 ; Liang et al. 2019 ; Luo et al.
2020; Mahmood et al. 2022 ). According to whether the marker
needs to be preinstalled on the device, it can be divided into un-
marked pose estimation methods and marked pose estimation
methods.
The marker-based pose estimation method uses an optical
camera to monitor the fiducial markers preinstalled on the exca-
vator manipulator and obtains the manipulator ’s pose through
computer vision technology. Lundeen et al. ( 2016) developed a
marker-based excavator pose estimation system, and the bucket
tip depth error can be controlled within 2.5cm, but due to the
complexity of the electromechanical system, the implementation
on the construction site is very difficult. Feng et al. ( 2018) pro-
posed a camera marker network structure in which two cameras
and markers can achieve centimeter-level measurement accuracy
within a range of 15 m. Zhao et al. ( 2021) proposed a method for
estimating the pose of an excavator through a monocular camera
and markers. The depth error of this method is less than 2.2cm,
but it has a relatively large orientation error. Although the marker-
based pose estimation method has achieved relatively good re-
search results in terms of accuracy, this method has obvious
shortcomings: on the one hand, the marker is prone to damage.
On the other hand, it is difficult to keep the cameras and markers
free from occlusion in a dynamic construction environment
(Lundeen et al. 2016 ).
The unmarked pose estimation method omits the step of
installing markers in advance and can directly extract pose infor-
mation from images, which is convenient for application on
construction sites. Soltani et al. ( 2017) estimated the 2D pose
of the excavator by training on synthetic images of the excavator
components. Traditional image processing and computer vision
methods rely on the quality of the dataset, and their pose estima-
tion bias may increase due to the constantly changing background
of the construction site ( Luo et al. 2020 ). In recent years, the
combination of computer vision and convolutional neural net-
works (CNN) has achieved good results in estimating the 3D
pose of excavators, and the decisive factor for the dramatic im-
provement in performance is a large, accurately labeled, and di-
verse dataset ( Liang et al. 2019 ; Luo et al. 2020 ; Xu and Y oon
2019). However, in a practical setting, the samples that can be
used to make datasets are limited, and it takes a lot of time and
effort to prepare datasets with high-quality annotations ( Mostafa
and Hegazy 2021). Therefore, Mahmood et al. ( 2022) studied train-
ing a CNN on a synthetic dataset to estimate the 3D pose of an
excavator, and the 3D position error of the proposed method was
51.42 cm. Using the game engine Unity, Assadzadeh et al. ( 2022)

the collision between the excavator and the underground pipe,
the accuracy is difficult to meet the requirements of on-site con-

between the bucket tip and the soil during the construction pro-

are changed, so it is difficult to accurately determine the posi-
tion of the bucket tip ( Assadzadeh et al. 2022 ; Soltani et al.
2017).
Although the vision-based excavator pose estimation method
may be an efficient, low-cost, and user-friendly method, it has the
following obvious drawbacks:
1. The marker-based pose estimation method has the possibility
that the marker will be occluded or damaged ( Feng et al. 2018 ;
Lundeen et al. 2016 ; Zhao et al. 2021 ).
2. The 3D position accuracy of the unmarked pose estimation
method cannot meet the requirements of anticollision in urban
road excavation operations ( Mahmood et al. 2022 ; Soltani et al.
2017).
3. The influence of environmental conditions such as camera dis-
tance and lighting will lead to a decrease in the accuracy of pose
estimation ( Feng et al. 2018 ; Liang et al. 2019 ).
4. CNN processing speed of video frames is difficult to achieve in
real-time ( Luo et al. 2020 ; Soltani et al. 2017 ).
In summary, the vision-based excavator pose estimation method
is difficult to solve the collision detection problem of urban road
excavation operations.
Nonvision-Based Pose Estimation Method
The nonvisual-based pose estimation method obtains the pose in-
formation of its mechanical components by installing monitoring
equipment on the mechanical surface of the excavator. Previous
studies used precision devices to obtain accurate excavator pose
information. For example, V ahdatikhaki et al. ( 2015) combined
with a genetic algorithm and proposed an excavator pose estimation
method based on an ultra-wideband (UWB) real-time positioning
system, improving the excavator ’s positioning accuracy. In com-
parison, IMU sensors are widely used in the industry because of
their easy installation and low price. Sun et al. ( 2020) provided
guidance for sensor selection by comparing draw-wire sensors and
IMU sensors in excavator pose estimation. Talmaki and Kamat
(2012) proposed a hybrid virtual excavator real-time collision de-
tection and safety early warning framework. In this framework,
they control the IMU sensor data as the input for the rotation of
the excavator’s mechanical components. Although the IMU sensor
is not actually used in field tests, this method confirms the potential
of the excavator attitude estimation based on the IMU sensor. Tang
et al. (2022) proposed a method for estimating the full-body pose of
an excavator based on IMU. The method uses quaternions to
calculate the pose information of each link, and the 3D position
accuracy can reach 5 cm.
The presented research proves the advantages of IMU sensors in
excavator pose estimation. In terms of operability, IMU sensors are
easy to install, low in cost, and less affected by the environment,
such as object occlusion and lighting conditions. In terms of data
performance, IMU data are reliable and accurate. Therefore, the
excavator pose estimation method based on the IMU sensor has
broad application prospects in urban road excavation operations
and provides a potential solution to the problem of preventing
the collision between the excavator and the underground pipe.
However, the current IMU-based pose estimation is limited by
the installation and calibration operation, despite its accuracy
having reached 5 cm, so there is still the possibility of further
improvement.
Digital Twin for Urban Road Excavation
The advancement of data acquisition technology and virtual mod-
eling technology has promoted the development of DT technology
(Madubuike et al. 2022 ). The DT consists of three parts, which
are divided into physical models, virtual models, and their com-
munication platforms ( Akanmu et al. 2021 ). The emergence of DT
has brought enormous opportunities to multiple industries, with
its first applications in aerospace, manufacturing, and medical
(Croatti et al. 2020 ; Liu et al. 2021 ; Shao and Helu 2020 ). In
recent years, DT has shown great potential in the design, construc-
tion, operation, and maintenance of buildings, and it has also
achieved many applications ( Hosamo et al. 2022 ). Based on
DT, Kaewunruen and Lian ( 2019) developed a management sys-
tem for the sustainability-based lifecycle of railway turnouts,
which can reduce construction costs and improve operational ef-
ficiency. Jiang et al. ( 2022) propose a general framework based on
DT for the human –machine interaction construction activities.
This framework is used to analyze the influence of unsafe hoisting
behaviors on the stability of the tower crane. By integrating DT,
deep learning (DL), and mixed reality (MR) technologies, Wu
et al. ( 2022) develop a real-time visual alarm system that enables
construction workers to proactively judge their safety status and
avoid accidents.
Despite DT has been widely used in engineering construc-
tion, the research on urban road excavation based on DT is rarely
involved. The current related research mainly includes the devel-
opment of an excavator intelligent guidance system and the para-
metric modeling and visualization method of underground pipe
networks. Behzadan et al. ( 2015) proposed to use augmented
reality (AR) technology to superimpose the underground pipe
network on the video frame to continuously help the excavator
operator to determine the spatial position. Talmaki and Li et al.
(Li et al. 2015 ; Su et al. 2013 ; Talmaki et al. 2013 ; Talmaki and
Kamat 2012 ) studied the probabilistic modeling method of
underground pipes and developed a simulation framework for
real-time visualization of excavation operations, combined with
collision detection algorithms to improve the spatial awareness
of excavator operators. However, it does not establish a physical
model of the excavator for on-site testing and does not use real
sensor data as input. There may be certain differences in real-
time and accuracy between on-site testing and simulation frame-
works. Based on BIM-GIS technology, Sharafat et al. ( 2021) and
Tanoli et al. ( 2018, 2019) developed an integrated framework of
the underground pipe network management system, providing
real-time 3D spatial information of the underground pipe network
to assist the excavator operator during the construction process.
Although this study combines the 3D model of the excavator and
the underground pipe network to provide visual guidance and
safety warning for the excavation operation, it focuses on the
parametric modeling method of the underground pipe network
and does not discuss the full-body pose estimation of the exca-
vator in detail. Moon and Seo ( 2017) proposed a 3D modeling
technology for excavators to display the pose of the excavator in
a 3D virtual environment, but the error of the pose estimation
reached 9cm.

Limitations of Existing Studies
Despite the advancements made by the presented studies, existing
studies in the excavator pose estimation method and DT for urban
road excavation have the following limitations.
1. Based on computer vision technology, the error of the unmarked
excavator 3D pose estimation method is too large, and the
marked method has problems such as marker occlusion and
camera distance limitation. Based on the IMU sensor, although
the accuracy of the excavator pose estimation method reaches
5 cm, it is difficult to accurately calibrate the IMU sensor equip-
ment and the geometric size of the excavator, so the accuracy
may be further improved.
2. For urban excavation, the current research mostly focuses on the
parametric modeling method of the underground pipe network,
and there are few studies on the combination of the dynamic
twin model of the excavator and the 3D model of the under-
ground pipe network for safety warning. The developed exca-
vator’s intelligent guidance and safety warning system has
problems such as poor display effect and low real-time accuracy
of the bucket tip.
In summary, it is necessary to combine DT to carry out intelli-
gent guidance and safety warning for urban road excavation oper-
ations so as to realize real-time coupling of the physical model and
virtual model of the excavator and high-precision 3D pose estima-
tion and then realize real-time safety warning and remote monitor-
ing of urban excavation operations to ensure construction safety.
Methodologies
In view of the shortcomings of previous research, this research
proposes an intelligent guidance and safety warning technology
for urban excavation operations based on DT, which aims to solve
the problem of collision damage between excavators and under-
ground pipes. Fig. 1 shows the workflow of the proposed tech-
nique, which consists of three parts: (1) physical model, (2) virtual
models, and (3) real-time guidance and security warning.
Physical Model
Multisensory Hardware Setup
The hardware device of the physical model mainly includes four
IMU sensors, two GNSS receivers, a data controller, and a vehicle
navigation screen. The installation location is shown in Fig. 2(a).
IMU sensors collect the pose data and GNSS receivers collect the
position data of the excavator in real-time. The data controller is a
bridge between the physical model and the virtual model and real-
izes dynamic coupling by transmitting the data of the sensor and
the GNSS receiver to the virtual model in real-time. The vehicle
navigation screen displays the system information of intelligent
guidance and safety warning to the excavator operator in real-time
and gives warnings to dangerous areas.
Pose Data Sensing
As shown in Fig. 2(b), the pose data collected from the physical
model can be divided into two types, one is the real-time data col-
lected by the IMU sensor and the GNSS receiver, and the other is
the mechanical structure size data of the excavator. The four IMU
sensors are used to obtain the pose data of the body, the boom, the
arm, and the bucket, respectively. Based on this data, the 3D pose of
the excavator can be estimated. The two GNSS receivers obtain the
3D coordinates and heading angle of the excavator respectively,
and then the 3D pose coordinates of the excavator can be con-
verted to the World Geodetic System (WGS)-84 coordinate system

3D Pose Estimation Based on SSA
In this section, first, based on the Euler coordinate transformation
method, combined with the mechanical structure characteristics
and the collected pose data, a kinematic model of the excavator
was established to realize the 3D pose estimation of each mechani-
cal component, and then, based on SSA, the geometric parameters
in the calculation process of the bucket tip coordinates are opti-
mized to improve the accuracy.
Step1: Kinematic Model of Excavator Based on Euler
Transformation. Based on the pose data collected in the section
“Pose Data Sensing,” a kinematics model of the excavator is estab-
lished using forward kinematics to calculate the three-dimensional
coordinates of the excavator bucket tip control points in the real
world. As shown in Fig. 2(b), the excavator is simplified into four
components, namely, the body, the boom, the arm, and the bucket.
The calculation sequence is O-A-B-C-D, where the starting point O
is the coordinate of the main GNSS receiver, and the ending point D
is a bucket tip control point. The detailed calculation process is as
follows:
1. Establish the space rectangular coordinate system of each
component.
The establishment of the coordinate system of all compo-
nents conforms to the right-hand rule of the Cartesian coordi-
nate system in order to facilitate the subsequent coordinate
transformation. As shown in Fig. 2(b), the body coordinate
system takes point O as the origin, the lateral direction of the
body is the x-axis, and the longitudinal direction is the y-axis.
The boom coordinate system takes point A as the origin, the
lateral direction of the body is the x-axis, and L
AB is the z-axis.
The arm coordinate system takes point B as the origin, the lat-
eral direction of the body is the x-axis, and LBC is the z-axis.
The bucket coordinate system takes point C as the origin, the
lateral direction of the body is the x-axis, and the LCD is
the z-axis.
2. Determine the 3D coordinates of the target control points of
each component in its own coordinate system.
All components are rigid bodies, and their coordinate sys-
tems are independent of each other. Therefore, the 3D co-
ordinates of each component target control point in its own
coordinate system will not change with the movement or rota-
tion of the component, so this method can realize the 3D coor-
dinate transformation of any point on the component. As shown
in Fig. 2(b), this research selects point A as the target control
point of the body, and the 3D coordinate of point A in the body
coordinate system is ( x1; y1; z1). Point B is selected as the target
control point of the boom, and the 3D coordinate of point B in

As the simplest way to express attitude, the Euler angle can
express rigid body attitude with only three elements. It has a
wide range of applications in aerospace ( Zeng and Guo 2017 ),
animation ( Perumal 2014), and human pose estimation ( Huang
et al. 2022; Panchal and Shah 2013; Sun and Lu 2020). Depend-
ing on the order of the rotation axis, there are as many as 12
expressions for Euler angles ( Perumal 2014 ). This research
selects the z –y–x Euler angle for calculation, first rotating
the heading angle around the z-axis, then rotating the roll angle
around the y-axis, and finally rotating the pitch angle around the
x-axis, so as to realize the conversion from the WGS-84 coor-
dinate system to the component coordinate system. Because the
roll angle of the excavator cannot be 90° during the actual ex-
cavation operation, this rotation sequence can avoid the singu-
larity associated ( Zeng and Guo 2017 ).

Step2: Bucket Tip Coordinate Optimization Based on SSA . The
error of the 3D pose estimation of the excavator is mainly caused by
the following three reasons. (1) Random errors of GNSS receivers
and IMU sensors. (2) Systematic errors of GNSS receivers and
IMU sensors. (3) Some target control points may be inside the com-
ponent, which are difficult to measure directly, so there is a meas-
urement error in the mechanical structure size data. Therefore, an
intelligent optimization algorithm can be used to calibrate the geo-
metric parameters in the solution of the bucket tip coordinates to
improve the accuracy.
This research ignores the random error of hardware equipment
and considers that the 3D coordinates measured by the laser total
station are the real spatial position of the bucket tip and the point D
of the excavator bucket tip, which is the most critical in urban ex-
cavation, is selected for optimization. On the one hand, the occur-
rence of urban excavation accidents is often caused by the collision
between the bucket tip and the underground pipe. On the other
hand, point D is the end point of the 3D pose estimation, and
its error is the accumulation of the calculation error of the pre-
vious component ’s target control point. Therefore, the calculation
e r r o ro fp o i n tD is the largest, and the 3D coordinate estimation
error of the target control point of the remainder component is
smaller than it.
The bucket tip coordinate optimization model proposed in this
paper is a single-objective optimization model. The optimization
goal is that the point D coordinate obtained by the 3D pose
estimation method is as close as possible to the coordinate mea-
sured by the laser total station. The geometric parameters in the
3D pose estimation of point D are optimized. As shown in
Table 2, there are a total of 18 optimization variables, and the
size variables and angle variables are adjusted within a certain
range, respectively.

Steps of Real-Time Guidance and Security Warning
Multilevel safety warning mode is adopted for urban road excava-
tion, and the specific steps are as follows:
1. Spatial division
According to the BIM model of the underground pipe net-
work in the section “BIM of Underground Pipes, ” the working
environment is divided into three spatial areas: high-risk area,
low-risk area, and safe area. Based on engineering experience,
the high-risk area is the space area 0 to 30 cm away from the
outer wall of the underground pipes. The low-risk area is the
space area 30 to 50 cm away from the outer wall of the under-
ground pipes. The safety zone is set to be more than 50 cm away
from the outer wall of the underground pipes.
2. Spatial area determination
According to the calculation method of the relative dis-
tance between the bucket tip and the underground pipes in
the section “Real-Time Calculation of the Relative Distance be-
tween Bucket Tip and Underground Pipes, ” calculate the rela-
tive distance R between the bucket tip and the underground
pipes, and further identify the spatial area where the bucket
is located in real-time. When R is greater than 50cm, it indicates
that the excavator bucket is in a safe area. When R is greater than
30cm and less than 50cm, it indicates that the excavator bucket
has entered the low-risk area. When R is less than 30cm, it in-
dicates that the excavator bucket has entered a high-risk area.
3. Multilevel security warning
The safety warning is carried out according to the different
space areas where the excavator bucket is located. When the
excavator bucket is located in the safety area, the operator can
work with confidence. When the excavator bucket is located in
the low-risk area, the voice will remind the excavator operator
to be careful with the construction, and the BIM model of the
underground pipes will turn yellow. When the excavator bucket
is located in the high-risk area, the BIM model of the under-
ground pipes will turn red and the excavator operator should
stop the excavation operation for detection to avoid a collision
between the excavator tip and the underground pipes.
System Prototype Development
Fig. 6 is the architecture diagram of intelligent guidance and safety
early warning systems for urban excavation. The system frame-
work is divided into four parts, which are the vehicle terminal, data-
base and application servers, remote monitoring client, and field
managers.
The vehicle terminal is divided into a physical model, virtual
model, and data transmission module. The physical model consists
of an excavator, two GNSS receivers, four IMU sensors, a data con-
troller, and a Pad. The data controller has a multifunction port and
a built-in data transmission unit (DTU). The multifunction port in-
cludes a power port, an RS-232 serial port, a CAN serial port, and
a GNSS antenna port. The power port is used to connect the
excavator power supply module, and RS-232 serial port is used
to connect the Pad. The CAN port is used to connect the IMU sen-
sors, and the antenna port is used to connect the GNSS receivers.
Considering the real-time performance of subsequent calculations,
we use wired transmission protocols for data transmission between
various hardware devices of the vehicle terminal, instead of using
wireless local area networks (WLANs) or public networks. The
data controller obtains tasks from servers through DTU, including
work area, work time, and model information, integrates GNSS
receivers data and IMU sensors data, and transmits the above data
to Pad in real-time through RS-232 serial port to improve the re-
sponse speed. The vehicle terminal adopts a Client/Server architec-
ture, and the Pad can directly perform calculations and analysis
after receiving data. Unity is used as the 3D visualization frame
to establish the virtual model on the Pad, which can realize the
3D pose estimation, relative distance calculation, real-time safety
warning, and visualization guidance. The Pad visually displays
the calculation results and 3D models of the excavator and its sur-
rounding scenes to the excavator operator in real-time to guide the
excavation operation. The results are transmitted back to the data
controller in real-time. The data controller transmits the above data
to the server through DTU.
The server receives the returned data, which is further stored and
analyzed. The remote monitoring client adopts a Browser/Server
architecture and implements remote three-dimensional visualiza-
tion based on Three.js. The remote monitoring client should be pre-
pared for monitoring first, including establishing a BIM model and
setting excavation tasks. Meanwhile, it realizes remote real-time
monitoring and safety warning for excavation work, helping the
system administrator judge the operation situation of the entire
work area. After excavation, the system administrator can inquire
about the warning information and excavation trajectory and play-
back the excavation process on the remote monitoring client to
judge the cause of the warning. Personal digital assistants (PDAs)
can receive warning messages and task information sent by servers
to assist field managers in controlling.

Discussion
The digital twinning of urban excavation is a meaningful yet chal-
lenging task. It is meaningful in the sense that can achieve high-
precision 3D pose estimation of excavators, real-time calculation of
the relative distance between the bucket tip and the underground
pipes, and multiterminal visualization of the calculation results to
relevant personnel to improve UES. The challenge, nevertheless,
is the difficulty in the accurate calibration of equipment parameters,
resulting in low accuracy of 3D pose estimation, which is fatal
for UES. Another challenge is how to build a system with high
accuracy, fast response, and satisfactory 3D visualization effects.
To tackle the challenges, this study proposes a digital twinning
method of multisensory and BIM-integrated to improve UES. The
contributions are three-fold.
First, a high-precision 3D pose estimation method for the
excavator is proposed. Compared with the visual pose estimation
method, since the excavator pose data is acquired based on IMU,
there is no need to set up a camera or installation marking on the
site, and it is not limited by the influence of light, distance, and
object obscuration. Compared with the previous nonvisual attitude
estimation methods, the excavator kinematics model proposed in
this paper based on Euler transformation can obtain a coordinate
transformation matrix directly from IMU, which is easy to calcu-
late. More importantly, the SSA is innovatively applied to the
parameter calibration of 3D pose estimation. The calculation accu-
racy of related works is mostly about 5 –10 cm, and the accuracy
before optimization in the paper is about 6 cm, which is similar.
However, after optimization, the accuracy is raised from 64.59 mm
to 24.61 mm, demonstrating its effectiveness.
Second, the paper innovatively proposes a DT design frame-
work for urban excavation, which integrates IMU, BIM, and 3D
pose estimation algorithms to realize virtual mapping of urban
excavation. Previous scholars mostly carried out research on ex-
cavator 3D pose estimation and intelligent excavator guidance sys-
tems, respectively. In the aspect of intelligent excavator guidance
system research, more attention was paid to the uncertain modeling
of underground pipes. Relevant research lacks a complete set of
closure realization methods from hardware installation, data ac-
quisition, model establishment, pose estimation, 3D visual display,
and alarm feedback.
Third, a software and hardware system has been developed, and
through on-site testing, the system has achieved satisfactory appli-
cation results. It can achieve 3D visual display, multiterminal real-
time monitoring, dangerous behavior early warning, task query and
statistics, and other functions. In particular, the delay in data trans-
mission and computation in the system can reach the millisecond
level, which can be negligible. The real-time performance of the
system can reach 0.05 s, which is determined by the sampling fre-
quency of the hardware device.
Conclusion and Prospect
In order to solve the problem of excavators colliding with under-
ground pipes in urban excavation, this research proposes an intel-
ligent guidance and safety early warning technology based on DT,
develops an intelligent guidance and safety warning system for
urban excavation, and verifies the practicability and performance
of this technology through two case studies. In light of the results
of this research, it can be concluded that (1) The DT can collect
and aggregate real-time pose information of physical excavators
with the capability to precisely estimate its 3D pose based on the
kinematic model and SSA. The first case study shows that this
method can effectively and stably estimate the 3D position and pose
of the excavator without being constrained by the construction
