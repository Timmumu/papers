- **Authors:** Deng, B.; Osmadi, A.; Deng, Y. H.

## Abstract summary
Construction risk categories are complex and the most current assessment platforms tend to place focus on theoretical assessments. There is an urgent need to simulate construction risks to manage projects effectively. As such, this article proposes a digital twin construction risk assessment model (DTCRAM) based on Delphi and a case study for predicting and controlling construction risk to reduce project loss. The model has been proven to detect risks quickly and match strategies efficiently. The outcomes revealed that DTCRAM displayed better results (key risks) in predicting and controlling construction management (more than 1% probability reduction/project) while simultaneously improving management efficiency (screen out risk factors). As a result, scholars and companies can gain risk management experience by using this tool in advance.

**Key evidence (AG):** DTCRAM: five-layer DT construction-risk assessment model (physical twin / data twin / twin model / data analysis / decision feedback) built via 3-round Delphi (9 experts, 15 risks incl. worker safety A1, equipment safety A2, construction quality A3) + case study (Thames Tidal Tunnel); real-time sensor data feeds simulation/risk prediction; claimed ~1% risk-probability reduction per project (A2 5%->3%, C1 3%->2%) but figures are estimates; feedback/optimisation loop described conceptually, not a demonstrated learning mechanism.

**Strength & gap filled (AH):** Responds to the lack of practical construction risk models: DTCRAM combines a three-round Delphi study with nine industry experts, identifying 15 construction risks and 15 countermeasures classified into 12 strategy types, with a five-layer digital twin structure (physical twin, data twin, twin model, data analysis, decision feedback) for dynamic monitoring and risk prediction, verified on the Thames Tidal Tunnel case with over 1 percent probability reduction per project.

**Deficiencies & future work (AI):** Expert-driven and weakly operationalized: only nine Delphi experts limit objectivity, results are tied to one specific project, and the model remains at a development stage with unquantified technical and methodological challenges; future research targets higher applicability and accuracy, larger samples across project types, and artificial intelligence and big data integration.
