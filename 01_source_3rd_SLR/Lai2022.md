## Abstract summary
Undetected fatigue and overload damages at the key locations of the crane boom are among the biggest threats in construction, leading to structural failure. Thus, the structural health of the crane boom should be monitored in real time to ensure that it works under the designed load capacity. In this study, we developed a lightweight digital twin by the multifidelity surrogate (MFS) model to improve the real-time monitoring and prediction accuracy of the structural safety of a crane boom. Digital twin technology, which can establish real-time mapping between the physical space and the digital space, has a promising potential for online monitoring and analysis of structures, equipment, and even human bodies. **Key evidence (AG):** Lightweight digital twin of a crane boom for real-time structural safety monitoring using a multi-fidelity surrogate (MFS) model: low-fidelity surrogate (mechanistic, large LF data) + high-fidelity surrogate (statistical, small HF data) fused with live sensor inputs (inclination, strain at 18 test points) rendered in a Web app; real-time posture mirroring and stress prediction; uncertainty from physical-space sensors quantified (140-run trajectory data). Validated on a lab crane boom; MFS-RBF improves accuracy over single-fidelity and cuts cost vs numerical DT. Offline-trained surrogate, one-way monitoring/prediction — no online learning loop.

**Strength & gap filled (AH):** Fills the real-time cost versus accuracy gap between mechanistic and statistical twins: a multi-fidelity surrogate combining a low-fidelity RBF trained on large low-fidelity data with a high-fidelity RBF on few high-fidelity samples yields a lightweight crane boom twin predicting stress and deformation in 0.035 s with R2 above 0.98 at 18 test points, plus Gaussian uncertainty quantification from 140 repeated lifts, rendered in a WebGL scene.

**Deficiencies & future work (AI):** Restricted to the linear elastic stage of materials, excluding plasticity, nonlinear behavior, and multiphysics coupling, and validated only on a laboratory crane boom; prediction accuracy depends on the limited high-fidelity sample set, and sensor uncertainty is modeled but not eliminated. Future work should explore nonlinear materials, plastic deformation, and multiphysics coupling for a more realistic twin.
