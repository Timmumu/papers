## Abstract summary
Little attention has been given to the quality of construction as a potential influence on the energy and carbon performance of buildings in many developing economies. In addition to causing post-construction defects that compromise building envelopes, poor construction quality reduces the long-term operational efficiency of building materials. In this paper, we reconceptualize construction defects as indirect drivers of energy and carbon use by integrating defect severity, repair frequency, and lifecycle considerations into a Quality-Sustainability Integration Index (QSII). We employed a mixed-methods approach comprising a survey of 83 construction professionals in Nigeria and a longitudinal review of maintenance records for 14 residential, commercial, and institutional buildings in Lagos, Nigeria. **Key evidence (AG):** Quality-Sustainability Integration Index (QSII) reconceptualising construction defects as indirect drivers of energy/carbon use: survey of 83 Nigerian professionals + longitudinal maintenance records for 14 buildings in Lagos + BIM-based Lifecycle Assessment quantifying embodied carbon from defect-induced repair cycles (envelope/waterproofing defects highest, up to 3.4x carbon multiplier, 82.3 tCO2e per 1000 m2 over 5 yr, 44% lifecycle-carbon gap between high/low quality); a conceptual Digital Twin prototype visualises defect propagation pathways and intervention strategies but is explicitly "not operationally validating... presently a conceptual tool only" — no live data, no implementation, no learning loop.

**Strength & gap filled (AH):** Reconceptualizes construction defects as latent energy and carbon drivers: the Quality-Sustainability Integration Index combines severity, carbon and repair frequency calibrated from 83 professionals, 14 Lagos buildings and 847 repair entries, showing waterproofing defects carry a 3.4x carbon multiplier and 82.3 tCO2e per 1000 m2 over five years, with a 44 percent lifecycle carbon gap between high and poor quality scenarios.

**Deficiencies & future work (AI):** Operational energy penalties are conceptually inferred rather than dynamically simulated, embodied carbon uses ICE v3.0 proxy factors without Nigerian EPDs, records span only five years, and the digital twin is a conceptual prototype not validated for operational control; the authors plan Nigerian EPDs, 15-20 year monitoring, climate-zone verification and gradual twin operationalization.
