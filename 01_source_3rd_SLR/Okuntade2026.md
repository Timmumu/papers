## Abstract summary
Little attention has been given to the quality of construction as a potential influence on the energy and carbon performance of buildings in many developing economies. In addition to causing post-construction defects that compromise building envelopes, poor construction quality reduces the long-term operational efficiency of building materials. In this paper, we reconceptualize construction defects as indirect drivers of energy and carbon use by integrating defect severity, repair frequency, and lifecycle considerations into a Quality-Sustainability Integration Index (QSII). We employed a mixed-methods approach comprising a survey of 83 construction professionals in Nigeria and a longitudinal review of maintenance records for 14 residential, commercial, and institutional buildings in Lagos, Nigeria.
