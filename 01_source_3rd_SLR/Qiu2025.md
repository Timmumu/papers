## Abstract summary
This study presents a novel deep-learning framework designed to efficiently generate high-fidelity three-dimensional (3D) point clouds of rail fasteners. The proposed method overcomes limitations associated with traditional light detection and ranging (LiDAR) technology, including high cost and incomplete data acquisition. The framework utilizes transformers and Bayesian neural networks for self-supervised contrastive learning, enabling real-time 3D point cloud generation from depth and grayscale images. This approach fosters automation and efficiency in maintenance tasks by producing accurate geometric reconstructions of rail fasteners. Beyond 3D generation, the framework learns latent representations of two-dimensional (2D) data, potentially impacting downstream tasks. **Key evidence (AG):** Deep-learning "LiDAR-simulated digital twin" framework generating probabilistic 3D point clouds of rail fasteners in real time from depth + grayscale images (Siamese self-supervised contrastive learning with vision transformer + Fourier net + multilevel fusion + Bayesian NN for uncertainty; 30 image-pairs per fastener from high-speed track inspection vehicle; CD 0.3926, HD 1063.79, contrastive loss 0.3142). Framed as "a step toward digital twin" — the DT itself is future work (foundation-model depth estimation); rail-fastener tightness/wear monitoring is the safety application, but no asset twin, no live loop, no learning.

**Strength & gap filled (AH):** Presents a LiDAR-simulated digital twin approach generating probabilistic 3D point clouds of rail fasteners in real time from grayscale and depth images via a Siamese self-supervised contrastive network (vision transformer, Fourier network, multilevel fusion) and a Bayesian neural network; trained on structured-light data from a 10 km/h track inspection vehicle, it reaches a Chamfer distance of 0.3926 and Hausdorff distance of 1063.79 with noise augmentation, followed by RANSAC segmentation and cubic interpolation.

**Deficiencies & future work (AI):** Evaluation rests on only 30 samples of one fastener type, with no comparison against real LiDAR scans, no long-term field tests and no validation of downstream anomaly detection; accuracy depends on input image and depth quality, and uncertainty remains for other fastener types and environments. Future work includes MiDaS-based depth estimation, anomaly detection algorithms and additional data sources.
