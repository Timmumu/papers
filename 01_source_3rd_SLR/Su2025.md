## Abstract summary
The construction phases of bridges involve a wide range of elements and tasks, generating a vast amount of data in terms of type and volume. Traditional manual and document-based methods are no longer sufficient for effective and accurate project management. Consequently, more comprehensive and systematic management approaches are required. This study proposes a three-dimensional digital twin information display platform that links with the actual state of the structure, facilitating bridge construction monitoring and safety warning through the integration of digital twin technology with multi-source monitoring information. A dynamic multi-resolution digital twin model for bridge construction has been established with a bridge digital model and structural finite element model. **Key evidence (AG):** Digital-twin-driven bridge construction safety-monitoring platform (Zhanlin Yellow River cable-stayed bridge, incremental launching): dynamic multi-resolution DT (web-BIM model + Midas Civil FE model, 267 rod elements/19 stages); 120 embedded strain gauges + support-reaction/temperature/displacement sensors stream real-time to the twin; stress/deformation warning mechanism with 3 warning levels (e.g., stress deviation 11% → L1, 20-30% → L2, >30% → L3) triggers alerts to on-site personnel who adjust construction. One-way monitoring→analysis→human action; no automated actuation and no learning loop.

**Strength & gap filled (AH):** Fills the construction-phase gap left by operation-focused bridge digital twins: a multi-resolution digital twin for the Zhanlin Yellow River cable-stayed bridge links a web-BIM model with a Midas Civil finite element model (267 elements, 19 launching stages) and 120 strain gauges, applying standardized stress and deformation warning thresholds that triggered Level 1 and Level 2 alarms during jacking at 573 m.

**Deficiencies & future work (AI):** Theoretical simulation results were not validated by thorough field tests, the case focuses mainly on main-girder stress, and warnings still require personnel to execute predefined procedures rather than automatic control; future work includes comparing the digital twin approach with conventional construction safety control and building a large-scale cable-stayed bridge construction monitoring database.
