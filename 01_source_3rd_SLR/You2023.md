# You et al. (2023) — Earthwork digital twin for teleoperation of an automated bulldozer in edge dumping

- **Authors:** You, K.; Zhou, C.; Ding, L.; Chen, W.; Zhang, R.; Xu, J.; Wu, Z.; Huang, C.

## Abstract summary
The unstructured construction site with a complex and changeable environment brings challenges to human–machine interaction. In this study, the earthwork digital twin (DT) is proposed to realize accurate and real-time perception, which can support the teleoperation of an automated bulldozer. The overall framework includes anterior-time DT, real-time DT, and posterior-time DT, which integrates three-dimensional modeling, edge detection, and blade trajectory algorithms. Multidimensional heterogeneous data in bulldozer teleoperation can be displayed in real-time to ensure construction safety. Big data during teleoperation of the construction process can be collected, stored, and analyzed. The DT proposed in this study was successfully applied in a major construction project in China, and testing results show its universality, robustness, and advanced performance. **Key evidence (AG):** Earthwork digital twin for teleoperation of an automated bulldozer in mine edge dumping (Hulunbuir open-pit): three-stage DT — anterior-time (UAV tilt-photography 3D model, 0.6 cm avg error; improved BoundED point-cloud edge detection of retaining wall, precision 0.923/F1 0.877; blade-trajectory generation), real-time (5G teleoperation platform displaying video, radar obstacle detection, RTK/IMU attitude, force feedback, eye-tracking), posterior-time (big-data storage/analysis; +2 h/day effective work). DT provides real-time perception/visualisation for human teleoperation (control via operator, not automated by twin); no learning loop.

**Strength & gap filled (AH):** Fills the gap of accurate real-time environment perception for bulldozer teleoperation in hazardous mine dumping: an anterior-time digital twin from UAV tilt photography achieves 0.6 cm average 3D error over 35,000 square meters, deep-learning retaining-wall edge detection reaches 0.923 precision and 0.877 F1, and a real-time platform with force, sound, and naked-eye 3D feedback supports a Shantui SD90 bulldozer, raising effective working time by 25%.

**Deficiencies & future work (AI):** Validation is confined to one open-pit mine backfill condition and the authors state more practical tests are needed to verify reliability; blade paths still depend on expert operator knowledge. Future work continues optimizing the system, improving algorithm accuracy and reliability, and advancing intelligent mine construction.
