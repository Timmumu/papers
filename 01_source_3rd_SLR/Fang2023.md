## Abstract summary
Efforts to address unsafe behaviors of construction workers have predominantly focused on identifying causative factors, unraveling formation mechanisms, and devising pre-emptive control strategies. Nonetheless, these studies have inadequately examined the reasons behind workers' engagement in unsafe behavior from a behavioral psychology perspective, specifically the collision reaction between impulsivity and self-control in the decision-making process, as well as the lack of exploration of psychological processes and behavioral response mechanisms of workers under different management strategies on construction sites. This paper describes an explanatory closed-loop structured model to simulate the safety performance of workers' behavior under different intervention strategies, considering the behavioral decision process and interactions between workers and managerial environment. **Key evidence (AG):** Hybrid simulation digital twin model for construction worker unsafe-behavior intervention (Central South U): closed-loop structured model with control panel, proactive management panel, and reactive panel; simulates workers behavioral safety under different intervention strategies using dual-attitude theory and reinforcement theory; proactive management maintains behavioral safety longer than reactive management. Simulation model only - no live data link to a real site, no actuation, no learning loop.


**Strength & gap filled (AH):** Fills the behavioral-psychology gap in worker unsafe-behavior research by building a closed-loop hybrid simulation digital twin in AnyLogic that couples agent-based, system dynamics and discrete-event models with dual attitude theory and reinforcement learning. Calibrated with 84 valid questionnaires (Cronbach alpha 0.868), experiments with 84 worker agents show proactive management sustains safety after withdrawal while reactive management decays fast.

**Deficiencies & future work (AI):** No live site data link; the model is a static simulation whose parameters come from a limited questionnaire sample, and its reliability and predictive capability are acknowledged as weaker than data-driven methods. Authors propose larger samples, EEG and eye-tracking for psychophysiological data, and adding agent-to-agent interactions as future work.
