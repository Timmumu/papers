## Abstract summary
Monitoring the structural condition of miter gates in locks and dams on inland waterways is vital to prevent unforeseen failures. Considering that the application of hydrostatic loads on miter gates is a quasistatic process resulting in two primary structural states, monitoring displacements induced by these loads is an effective method for indicating common degradations and examining miter gate safety. Noncontact sensors such as dual-camera systems are preferred for ensuring continuous operation. A dual-camera system records pre- and postload three-dimensional positions of a miter gate, allowing for the calculation of load-induced structural displacements. However, movement of the dual-camera system itself during the measurement process, referred to as “ego-motion,” introduces measurement errors, as if the structure undergoes additional rigid body motion.
