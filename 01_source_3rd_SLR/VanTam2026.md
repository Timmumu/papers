## Abstract summary
Agentic artificial intelligence (AI), defined as AI systems capable of autonomous perception, reasoning, planning, and action execution, signals a decisive shift in how the built environment is operated, managed, and optimized. However, increasing autonomy alone does not guarantee safe, legitimate, or socially acceptable outcomes in long-lived, safety-critical, and occupant-centered socio-technical systems. This paper advances a clear argument structured around three interlinked dimensions: promise, peril, and governed autonomy. Firstly, it synthesizes emerging empirical evidence demonstrating the promise of agentic AI in autonomous HVAC control and building energy management, agentic fault detection and diagnosis, intelligent digital twins, indoor environmental quality, and autonomous virtual building modelling.
