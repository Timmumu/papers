## Abstract summary
Modern monitoring systems in structural health monitoring (SHM) and transportation generate continuous streams of heterogeneous, high-resolution data from distributed sensor networks. These data capture complex spatiotemporal interactions across multiple modes, including time, space, operating conditions, and environmental variables. High-order tensor representations provide a natural framework for preserving this multi-way structure, and tensor decompositions such as CANDECOMP/PARAFAC (CP), Tucker, and Tensor Train (TT) have been widely used for imputation, reconstruction, anomaly detection, and forecasting. However, most existing tensor decomposition methods are formulated in a batch setting, requiring repeated refitting as new data become available, which limits their applicability for real-time monitoring.
