## Abstract summary
Near-coast utility tunnels constructed in soft-soil environments are vulnerable to long-term settlement accumulation and structural response evolution, posing challenges for serviceability assessment and operational safety management. Existing studies generally focus on either deformation monitoring or structural response prediction, while integrated forecasting frameworks for heterogeneous monitoring data remain limited. This study proposes a dual-stream monitoring and predictive framework for operational-phase assessment of near-coast utility tunnels. Hydrostatic leveling system (HLS) measurements are employed to characterize vertical deformation and are modeled using the AutoRegressive Integrated Moving Average (ARIMA) approach after stationarity diagnosis and differencing treatment.
