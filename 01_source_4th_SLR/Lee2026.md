## Abstract summary
Background: Dominant frameworks in transgender health, particularly Minority Stress Theory, presume an individualist subject for whom “outness” is a primary pathway to well-being. This model often fails to capture the experience of transition in South Korea, where selfhood is tethered to Neo-Confucian lineage and gender is policed by rigid administrative binaries. Aim: This study investigates the lived experience of transgender women in South Korea to operationalize how hypervigilance functions within a high-surveillance, collectivist society. Methods: Using Giorgi’s descriptive phenomenological method, in-depth interviews were conducted with six transgender women (ages mid-20s to early 40s). Data were analyzed in Korean to preserve indigenous sociolinguistic meanings.
