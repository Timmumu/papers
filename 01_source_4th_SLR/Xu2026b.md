## Abstract summary
Antarctic wharf infrastructure forms a critical interface between polar maritime logistics and station-based scientific operations. Its planning, construction, operation, and decommissioning must address extreme ice conditions, permafrost degradation, short construction windows, limited equipment accessibility, and stringent environmental governance. This review proposes an integrated Environment–Structure–Equipment–Governance (ESEG) framework for conceptualising Antarctic wharves as coupled engineering systems rather than conventional port facilities. Within this framework, environmental processes define dynamic constraints, structural systems provide engineering responses, equipment and logistics determine constructability, and governance sets the boundaries of acceptable engineering activity.
