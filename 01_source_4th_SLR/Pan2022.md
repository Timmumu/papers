# Pan, Y et al. (2022) — Enriching geometric digital twins of buildings with small objects by fusing laser scanning and AI-based image recognition

- **ID:** R894 | **Source:** Scopus | **DOI:** 10.1016/j.autcon.2022.104375
- **Journal:** Automation in Construction
- **Authors:** Pan Y.; Braun A.; Brilakis I.; Borrmann A.

## Abstract summary
This paper addresses the challenge of enriching geometric digital twins of buildings, with a particular emphasis on capturing small but important entities from the electrical and the fire-safety domain, such as signs, sockets, switches, smoke alarms, etc. Unlike most previous research that focussed on structural elements and processed laser point clouds and images separately, we propose a novel method that fuses laser scanning and photogrammetry methods to capture the relevant objects, recognise them in 2D images and then map these to a 3D space. The considered object classes include electrical elements (light switch, light, speaker, socket, elevator button), safety elements (emergency switch, smoke alarm, fire extinguisher, escape sign), plumbing system elements (pipes), and other objects with useful information (door sign, board). **AH — strength & gap filled:** Fills the gap of enriching geometric building twins with electrical and fire-safety objects that prior scan-to-BIM methods ignored: a mostly automated pipeline fusing laser scanning and photogrammetry, with Mask-RCNN detections mapped into point clouds via COLMAP, ICP alignment and voxel raycasting, then RANSAC shape fitting and OCR text extraction, raising mIoU for smoke alarms from 29.1% to 48.6% and lights from 69.4% to 79.9% versus KPConv.

**AI — deficiencies & future work:** Validation is limited to TUM office interiors with a manually labelled dataset; detection is weaker for objects that vary across buildings and many classes like furniture remain missing; only simple geometric primitives are fitted, point-cloud registration stays manual, and the photogrammetric step needs enough overlapping images. Future work adds more classes, CAD-level shapes, and joint 2D and 3D deep learning.
