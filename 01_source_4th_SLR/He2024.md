# He, Y et al. (2024) — Reliable simulation analysis for high-temperature inrush water hazard based on the digital twin model of tunnel geological environment

- **ID:** R569 | **Source:** Scopus | **DOI:** 10.1016/j.tust.2024.106110
- **Journal:** Tunnelling and Underground Space Technology
- **Authors:** He Y.; Ding Y.; Zhu Q.; Wu H.; Guo Y.; Wang Q.; Zhou R.

## Abstract summary
In complex mountainous terrains, tunnel construction faces unique challenges from high-temperature water inrush hazards, a systemic risk arising from the interplay of stress, seepage, and temperature fields. Traditional simulation methods, focusing on isolated disaster scenarios, fall short in addressing the multifaceted nature of these risks due to geological ambiguity and data incompleteness. Digital twin technology presents an effective solution to these challenges; however, its core challenge lies in how to utilize digital twin technology for data-model-co-driven simulation analysis of coupled multi-physical fields in situations of incomplete data. This paper introduces a digital twin paradigm for the simulation analysis of water inrush, which significantly enhances efficiency and accuracy through the integration of advanced machine learning and finite element analysis techniques. **Key evidence (AG):** DT of the tunnel geological environment for high-temperature water-inrush simulation (SWJTU, TUST 2024): voxel structure integrates multi-field attributes (stress, seepage, temperature); Gaussian-Process geological modelling fuses multi-source geophysical prediction data (TSP, TEM, IP, GPR); GP-Differential-Evolution back-analysis calibrates environment parameters; validated on a 230-m-burial ultra-high-temperature inrush tunnel in China and corroborated by tunnel monitoring data, supporting dynamic optimisation design and safety prevention during construction. One-way data->twin->simulation/safety measures; no actuation, no learning loop.
