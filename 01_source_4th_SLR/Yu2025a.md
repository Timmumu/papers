## Abstract summary
The cognitive digital twin (CDT) concept provides a promising way to organize and integrate the full life-cycle data of urban road emergency scenarios, as well as facilitate scenario deduction and prediction. The construction of twin models constitutes the prerequisite for the practical implementation and application of CDT. However, the existing research on emergency scenario twin modeling falls short in providing a unified modeling method that comprehensively captures the multidimensional characteristics of scenario elements. This deficiency, coupled with inadequate analysis of interelement interactions and constraints posed by semantic inconsistencies and interoperability among twin models, hampers the accurate mapping of physical scenarios and their evolution dynamics. **Key evidence (AG):** Cognitive Digital Twin modelling method for urban road emergency scenarios (Southeast U, JCCEE 2025): element-level and scenario-level twin models defined in geometry/attribute/behaviour dimensions with resilience theory; SysML + ontology modelling and a SysML2Ontology transformation tool build the Emergency Scenario Cognitive Digital Twin (ESCDT); case study constructs an ESCDT for a collision accident on the North-South Elevated Road (Shanghai) and uses it for scenario deduction in emergency management. Static scenario models for deduction; no live data link, no actuation, no learning loop.
