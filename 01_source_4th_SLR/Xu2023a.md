## Abstract summary
Structural health monitoring (SHM) is a critical component of ensuring the safety of buildings, yet the effective management of sensors and their corresponding monitoring data is complex. Thus, a digital twin health monitoring information model for buildings was proposed to simultaneously locate sensors in space and monitor data. We described the requirements of this process and designed a conceptual model by combining a building information model (BIM) and a real-scene 3D model. The key steps in the fusion process include reconstructing the BIM into sub-BIMs considering building component types and geometric transformations, 3D spatial position registration with a rigid-body transformation method, and semantic information mapping. **Key evidence (AG):** Digital twin model for monitoring building structural health by combining BIM and real-scene 3D model (Nanjing Tech U): fuses BIM with real-scene 3D model via rigid-body transformation (3D spatial position registration) + semantic information mapping (JSON); reconstructs BIM into sub-BIMs; sensor data stored in database and integrated with fused model; Nanjing Museum Old Hall case study. One-way monitoring→fusion→visualization; no actuation and no learning loop.

**AH — strength & gap filled:** Addresses the lack of spatial linkage between internal BIM components and external building scenes for structural health monitoring: IFC BIM is split into sub-BIMs and fused with an oblique-photogrammetry real-scene 3D Tiles model through rigid-body SVD registration and JSON semantic mapping, integrating 60 vibration, displacement, strain, and temperature sensors of the Nanjing Museum Old Hall into a Cesium-based monitoring system.

**AI — deficiencies & future work:** Tie-point selection is manual and the separate merging programs take about 25 minutes to run; the fusion lacks level-of-detail handling which slows BIM loading, the structural safety evaluation function is reserved and not yet developed, and dynamic analysis of overall building health from local sensor data requires further investigation.
