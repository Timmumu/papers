## Abstract summary
Urban excavation is an indispensable process for many construction activities such as road paving, house building, and pipe rehabilitation. However, the everincreasing complexity of underground utilities (e.g., water mains, gas lines, and sewage pipes) in urban environments challenges the safety of urban excavation, posing tremendous risks of potential collision and damage accidents. By obtaining real-time excavation information and high-fidelity simulation to evaluate safety risks, digital twin (DT) has an unexplored potential to improve urban excavation safety (UES). This research aims to investigate how a DT for urban excavation can be developed and used to improve UES. First, a multisensory solution is proposed to equip the physical excavators with the capability to precisely estimate their three-dimensional (3D) poses based on the kinematic model and social spider algorithm (SSA). **Key evidence (AG):** Multisensory + BIM-integrated digital twin for urban excavation safety (Tianjin U, Shandong case): physical excavator equipped with multi-sensors, 3D pose estimated via kinematic model + social spider algorithm (SSA) (bucket-tip error 24.61 mm, translation accuracy +4.0 cm); BIM of buried utilities + 3D excavator model form a dynamic virtual model mirroring the excavation; physical-virtual coupled DT enables real-time safety control - dynamic collision-risk assessment with 3D visual guidance and safety warning (pipe model turns red when bucket enters risk area, operator stops) to avoid pipe-collision accidents; system prototype field-tested in two case studies, 0.05 s sampling. One-way monitoring to guidance/warning to the human operator; no automated actuation and no learning loop.

**AH — strength & gap filled:** Equips an excavator with four IMU sensors and two GNSS receivers, estimates its 3D pose via an Euler-based kinematic model refined by social spider algorithm optimization, cutting bucket tip mean error from 64.59 mm to 24.61 mm, and couples it with a BIM of buried pipes in Unity for real-time collision risk assessment with 30 cm and 50 cm warning zones, applied to a 1760 m Shandong pipe project.

**AI — deficiencies & future work:** Safety warning distances are set from experience rather than from the uncertainty of the pipe BIM, and the excavator case is validated in one project; hardware random errors are ignored. Future work will derive reasonable warning distances through field tests and extend the pose estimation method to other heavy machinery.
