## Abstract summary
High-speed railway (HSR) bridges are critical transportation infrastructure operating under stringent constraints (e.g., zero tolerance for operational disruptions, limited maintenance windows) and demanding real-time safety monitoring. Traditional train load identification methods suffer from complex modeling, poor anti-noise performance, and inadequate real-time applicability, failing to balance accuracy, feasibility, and timeliness for HSR scenarios. To address this gap, this study proposes a novel train gross weight identification method based on bridge strain data: an Integral Mean of Strain (IMS) index is introduced to suppress transient interference and simplify the load–strain relationship, with a derived theoretical IMS-gross weight correlation that avoids cumbersome parameter calibration.
