## Abstract summary
Purpose – This paper aims to develop a proactive construction safety management framework capable of predicting near-miss incidents by leveraging Digital Twin (DT) technology. The Digital Twin (DT) framework focuses on integrating environmental and human-related risk factors – particularly the trajectories and behaviors of workers – before accidents occur. Design/methodology/approach – The paper proposes a computer vision-based digital twin framework that integrates real-time data on workers' movements, postures, and interactions to construct a dynamic 3D model of the construction site. Deep learning algorithms are employed to predict human trajectories and behaviors, while collision detection techniques are used to identify potential near-miss scenarios. **Key evidence (AG):** Computer-vision-based digital twin framework for proactive near-miss prediction (Harbin IT): integrates real-time worker trajectories, postures and interactions into a dynamic 3D construction-site model; non-autoregressive transformer predicts future movement sequences (human motion model trained on Human3.6M dataset, inverse kinematics for joint angles) + collision detection identifies potential near-misses with early warnings; field-validated in three high-risk zones (forklift paths, floor openings, material lifting). One-way perception to prediction to early warning for safety managers; no actuation and no self-improvement loop (model is offline-trained).

*Full-text source: 04c-port*

**AH — strength & gap filled:** Extends near-miss management from 2D detection to in-process 3D prediction: YOLOv5, SGBM, DeepSORT and MediaPipe keypoints feed a non-autoregressive transformer that forecasts worker trajectories (ADE 0.134) and postures (ADE 0.501), reconstructed by an LBFGS inverse-kinematics solver in Unreal Engine, predicting near-misses with 93.32% precision and 96.08% recall across forklift, floor-opening, and material-lifting zones.

**AI — deficiencies & future work:** The motion prediction model is trained on the Human3.6M laboratory dataset, creating a generalization gap to real construction postures; occlusions, lighting variability, and camera positioning limit reliability, and validation covered only three relatively simple high-risk zones. Future work adds more sensor types, lightweight edge-distributed architectures, domain-adaptive learning, and broader site validation.
