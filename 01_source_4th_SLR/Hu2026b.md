## Abstract summary
The increasing frequency of typhoons and other extreme climate events has intensified the risks of natural hazard-triggered technological accidents (Natech), posing significant challenges to ocean engineering infrastructures. Traditional disaster-prevention approaches, largely dependent on empirical judgments and static analyses, are no longer sufficient for real-time risk identification or proactive mitigation in complex ocean environments. Advances in digital technologies offer new pathways for disaster reduction and have become the critical frontier for ocean engineering safety. This review synthesizes recent progress in digital disaster prevention for ocean engineering under climate extremes.
