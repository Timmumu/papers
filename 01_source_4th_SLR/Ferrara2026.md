## Abstract summary
Structural Health Monitoring (SHM) of existing bridges increasingly relies on dynamic measurements to assess structural performance and detect potential damage. However, the practical implementation of long-term vibration-based monitoring is still constrained by the volume of data required and the complexity of continuous acquisition systems. In the context of ensuring the safety and performance of existing bridge infrastructure, vibration-based monitoring offers a powerful tool for detecting changes in structural behavior. This study presents an extended investigation of dynamic monitoring applied to composite steel–concrete viaducts, focusing particularly on the signal-analysis framework and methodological enhancements.
