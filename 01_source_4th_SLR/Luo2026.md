## Abstract summary
Unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) are emerging as key enablers for low-altitude wireless networks (LAWNs), especially when terrestrial infrastructure is unavailable. In such scenarios, the environmental topology is typically unknown, making efficient and safe UAV trajectory design both essential and challenging. To address this problem, we propose a digital twin (DT)-assisted training and deployment framework (DTTDF), in which virtual environments (VEs) are constructed from scratch and progressively refined using real-time sensing data. During training, diverse VEs accelerate and stabilize policy learning, while during deployment a synchronized VE is continuously updated to support safety-aware control and reliable navigation.
