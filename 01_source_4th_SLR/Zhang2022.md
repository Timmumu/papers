## Abstract summary
The lack of integration between the digital and physical world results in a lower level of efficiency and collaboration in the construction industry. Digital twin technology, which creates a visual and digital model of a corresponding physical object for simulating, monitoring, analyzing, and other actions throughout the whole life cycle, is considered as an effective solution to address these problems. This research proposes a framework to utilize digital twins and extend the existing level of details (LoDs) of building information modeling (BIM) for construction site management. This study analyzes and improves the operation principle and mechanism of digital twins, including the digital representation based on BIM, Internet of Things (IoT), data storage, integration, and analytics, as well as interaction with the physical environment. **Key evidence (AG):** Framework extending BIM Level-of-Details (LoDs) for construction-site digital twins: defines four core DT components (physical environment, digital representation/BIM, IoT, data storage-integration-analytics, interaction/feedback) plus new LoD-Graphics and LoD-Information (100–500) for construction-site components (site space, roads, facility, equipment, material, people) and maps minimum LoDs per BIM use/stage. Validated only via 31 questionnaires + 10 international expert interviews (>77% agree DT improves construction safety); no DT implemented or tested; feedback/autonomy described as future direction, no learning loop.

**AH — strength & gap filled:** Fills the gap of a framework jointly covering digital twins and BIM level-of-details for construction site management: four interacting components (BIM digital representation, IoT, data storage-integration-analytics, physical-environment interaction) are extended with LoD-Graphics and LoD-Information levels from approximate to verified across all site stages, and 31 questionnaires plus 10 expert interviews confirm support for monitoring, quality, efficiency and safety.

**AI — deficiencies & future work:** Conceptual framework without a completed application case, so overall performance improvement cannot be quantified; validation relies on 31 questionnaires and 10 interviews, which the authors note are limited by time and resources. Future directions include autonomous feedback and control from twin to physical world, human-in-the-loop research, and governance and ethics of twin data.
