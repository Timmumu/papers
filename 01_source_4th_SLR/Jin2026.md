## Abstract summary
This research centers on structural health monitoring of bridges, a critical transportation infrastructure. Owing to the cumulative action of heavy vehicle loads, environmental variations, and material aging, bridge components are prone to cracks and other defects, severely compromising structural safety and service life. Traditional inspection methods relying on manual visual assessment or vehicle-mounted sensors suffer from low efficiency, strong subjectivity, and high costs, while conventional image processing techniques and early deep learning models (e.g., U-Net, Faster R-CNN) still perform inadequately in complex environments (e.g., varying illumination, noise, false cracks) due to poor perception of fine cracks and multi-scale features, limiting practical application.
