## Abstract summary
A short review is provided regarding modern technical tools allowing to build digital twins for modelling ancient stone masonry structures and their physical behaviour. The objective of such tools is to assess the structural safety of cultural heritage masonry structures. The present work focuses on the particular case of Sardinian nuraghi, which are ancient corbelled stone masonry structures whose typical form is a truncated cone. As a starting point we consider a careful historical analysis of the construction techniques of those nuraghi. From this analysis, we address the choice of theoretical and numerical tools apt to construct a digital twin of complex nuraghi, in addition to delineating future challenges in building digital twins capable of simulating any physical process which may be relevant to ancient buildings. © 2025 Academie des sciences. **Key evidence (AG):** Review + methodology proposal for "physical digital twins" of ancient corbelled stone masonry (Sardinian nuraghi, cultural heritage): combines historical analysis of original construction techniques (dry masonry, thrust-free pseudo-domes, block distribution) with proposed mechanical-modelling approaches (discrete-element / variational-inequality granular models, multiphysical weathering: water diffusion, salt crystallisation, thermal, chemical) to assess structural safety and predict preservation-state evolution. No DT is actually built or validated — approaches are "possible roads... under investigation for future structural studies"; no live data, no learning.

**AH — strength & gap filled:** Addresses the absence of modeling methodology for heritage corbelled dry-stone masonry: historical analysis of nuraghe construction techniques is linked to physical digital twins combining 3D surveys, historical documentation, and laboratory tests, with discrete block-based kinematics, friction, damage variables, and multiphysical weathering couplings, such as water diffusion, salt crystallisation, and thermal and chemical effects, framed through variational principles.

**AI — deficiencies & future work:** Purely a review and methodology proposal: no digital twin has been constructed, no numerical experiments or validation are reported, and no live data connection is considered. Future challenges listed by the authors include progressively adding coupled multiphysical weathering phenomena one at a time and building calibrated models of specific nuraghe structures.
