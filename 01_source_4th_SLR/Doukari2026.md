## Abstract summary
Achieving the EU’s energy and decarbonisation goals requires more efficient management of building renovation projects. Whilst digital twin (DT) technology is increasingly adopted across the built environment, most existing research and practice focus on its application during the operational phase of built assets such as energy monitoring, space and occupancy management, and predictive maintenance. However, there remains a gap in leveraging DT potential in renovation projects, supporting multi-domain performance tracking. This study presents a pilot implementation and preliminary evaluation of a Renovation Digital Twin (RDT) platform tailored to the specific challenges of renovation projects. The RDT integrates real-time data and dashboards to monitor 13 Key Performance Indicators (KPIs) across five domains: cost, schedule, quality, safety, and environment. Designed with GDPR compliance…

**Key evidence (AG):** Renovation Digital Twin (RDT) platform (Northumbria U, RINNO Project): pilot implementation on French demonstration site; real-time data + dashboards monitoring 13 KPIs across 5 domains (cost, schedule, quality, safety, environment); TAM evaluation (102 professionals, Attitude Toward Use RII 0.894); 73% emissions reduction achieved but economic viability challenges. One-way monitoring→dashboard→decision support; no actuation and no learning loop.

*Full-text source: 04c-port*

**AH — strength & gap filled:** Fills the renovation-phase gap in digital twins: a stakeholder-informed Renovation Digital Twin built with Design Science Research in the RINNO project monitors 13 KPIs across cost, schedule, quality, safety and environment on the Project Sarrazins demo site in Lille, and a Technology Acceptance Model survey of 102 professionals gave attitude toward use RII 0.894 while the site achieved a 73 percent emissions reduction.

**AI — deficiencies & future work:** Proof-of-concept scope: a single renovation project, functional verification only, no quantified cost, schedule or safety improvements, a short evaluation period, a TAM sample biased to early-career professionals, manual data entry, and no AI or predictive analytics beyond historical KPI visualization; future work calls for multi-site validation, longitudinal studies, IoT automation, deep-learning prediction and cognitive twin capabilities.
