## Abstract summary
Recent urban practice has highlighted the need for safe and efficient pedestrian movement in urban settings. Urban planners and policymakers are tasked with ensuring that individuals can navigate cities while avoiding crowding and minimizing safety risks. Among the various proposed solutions to achieve these planning objectives, enhancing public space adaptability to changing circumstances, such as fluctuations in pedestrian demand and crowding, has garnered significant interest. However, constrained municipal budgets limit the feasibility of large-scale, capital-intensive upgrades to pedestrian infrastructure. As a result, there is an urgent need for a short-term, temporary, and cost-effective strategy to redesign pedestrian public space.
