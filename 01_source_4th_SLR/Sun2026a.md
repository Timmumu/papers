## Abstract summary
Traditional inspection and diagnosis methods for civil infrastructure operation and maintenance (CI O&M) rely heavily on human efforts. Such efforts are always affected by subjective judgment and human errors due to engineering knowledge and prior experiences of field engineers. On the other hand, recent development of AI-driven tools could achieve effective information acquisition but lacks interpretability and engineering credibility. How to integrate human knowledge with AI capacity for safe and effective CI O&M is thus necessary in this new era. This paper presents a human-in-the-loop digital twin (HITL-DT) framework that enables safety risk sensing, prediction and control for smart CI O&M. **Key evidence (AG):** Human-in-the-loop digital twin (HITL-DT) framework for smart civil infrastructure O&M (Beijing U Tech): fuses human cognition (perception, team situation awareness), AI, and engineering knowledge for risk sensing/diagnosis (spatiotemporal changes) and risk prediction/control; formalizes structural mechanics knowledge, embeds engineering code rules, expandable case knowledge base; multi-source spatiotemporal sensing (human inspection + machine vision). Human-in-the-loop bidirectional with human oversight → Tier 3; no learning loop (knowledge is rule-based, not learning).

*Full-text source: 04c-port*

**AH — strength & gap filled:** Bridges the divide between data-driven automation with limited interpretability and human-centered assessment with limited scalability: a human-AI-knowledge HITL-DT framework formalizes engineering codes and human cognition (individual perception, team situation awareness) alongside AI reasoning into a closed-loop device-model-decision architecture for risk sensing, prediction and control in civil infrastructure O&M.

**AI — deficiencies & future work:** Validation is qualitative and conceptual only, demonstrating operational feasibility and logical consistency without numerical performance tests or comparisons with other systems; formalizing context-dependent expert knowledge into computable rules remains unresolved, multi-source data systems are costly, and future work targets autonomous learning, cross-infrastructure knowledge transfer, and full-lifecycle risk management.
