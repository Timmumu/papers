## Abstract summary
Geological uncertainty ahead of the tunnel face presents significant challenges to the safety and efficiency of tunnel construction. To address this issue, this paper explores the feasibility of using a digital twin approach to enhance geological modeling and rock classification. The proposed method integrates multi-source geological data to construct a multi-dimensional geological virtual model, and applies a fuzzy cloud probability model to quantify uncertainty, enabling intelligent classification of surrounding rock. Results from tests on five rock tunnel projects using the iS3 platform show that the overall rock classification accuracy of the method is 92.95 %. These findings clearly demonstrate that the digital twin approach significantly improves geological transparency, risk management, and decision-making efficiency in complex rock tunnel environments. **AH — strength & gap filled:** Closes the data-model-decision loop for geological uncertainty ahead of tunnel faces: TEM, GPR and borehole data become point-cloud models of water content, lithology and integrity via KNN interpolation, and a fuzzy cloud probability model quantifies uncertainty to classify surrounding rock per meter within a 30 m range, achieving 92.95 percent accuracy across 539 exposed-rock records of five tunnels on the Tongji iS3 platform.

**AI — deficiencies & future work:** Decision factors cover only water content, lithology and integrity; GPR captures a single cross-section so longitudinal integrity variation is missing, databases are unevenly sized, and per-cycle computation of 10-60 s grows to 2-5 minutes for concurrent tunnels; the authors propose GPU and KD-tree acceleration plus extension to soft-soil and underwater tunnels.
