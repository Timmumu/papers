## Abstract summary
Bridges are fundamental components of transportation infrastructure, facilitating the efficient movement of people and goods. However, the conservation of heritage bridges introduces additional challenges, encompassing environmental, social, cultural, and economic dimensions of sustainability. This study investigates risk mitigation strategies for a heritage-listed, 120-year-old reinforced concrete bridge in Australia—one of the nation’s earliest examples of reinforced concrete construction, which remains operational today. The structure faces multiple risks, including passage of overweight vehicles, environmental degradation, progressive crack development due to traffic loading, and potential foundation scouring from an adjacent stream. **AH — strength & gap filled:** Fills the gap between heritage-bridge conservation and quantitative risk assessment: on a 120-year-old Australian reinforced concrete bridge, ambient vibration testing with ten wireless accelerometers plus laser displacement sensing feeds a Spacegass finite-element digital twin validated against measured natural frequencies (20.15 Hz versus 19.51 Hz, 3.2 percent difference), then used for what-if simulations of overweight vehicles and foundation scour.

**AI — deficiencies & future work:** Monitoring is campaign-based rather than live: the twin is an offline validated finite-element model with no continuous data link or actuation, higher-order modes are missed at the 100 Hz sampling rate, and the six-phase Incident Response Framework is proposed but not yet deployed; higher-rate testing is noted for the future.
