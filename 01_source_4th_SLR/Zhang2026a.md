## Abstract summary
Unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) and electric vertical takeoff and landing (eVTOL) platforms are increasingly considered for urban air mobility (UAM), last-mile logistics, and emergency-response operations, yet large-scale low-altitude deployment remains limited. Digital twin (DT) technology supports real-time monitoring, prediction, and control, while existing studies provide limited explanation for why some low-altitude DT application domains advance toward deployment more readily than other domains do. This survey interprets application maturity through a barrier-centric framework that combines a four-layer architecture, feedback-loop timing distinctions, and an application taxonomy organized around three recurring constraints: the Fidelity-Latency Paradox, Information-Theoretic Limits, and the artificial intelligence (AI) Verification Challenge.
