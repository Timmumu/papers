## Abstract summary
Construction sites routinely face multi-trade concurrency, spatiotemporal coupling, and high safety risk; relying solely on manual inspection and heuristic scheduling often leads to lagging detection and inconsistent execution. In response, recent practice has introduced digital twins (DT) to fuse video, sensors, and BIM and thus improve site visibility; however, most implementations remain at monitoring/visualization, lacking a mechanism to convert cognition into executable, verifiable decisions. Meanwhile, Transformer foundation models show strong capabilities in multimodal perception and representation learning, yet they are rarely closed-looped with engineering constraints and on-site execution. **AH — strength & gap filled:** Closes the perception-to-execution gap on a real high-rise self-climbing platform project: Component-ID plus timestamp aligns video, sensor, BIM and text streams; a multimodal Transformer recognizes operation states and predicts short-horizon risks; constraint-encoded policies generate feasible task sequences delivered via AR with acknowledgments, raising warning lead time from 1.8 to 4.6 seconds and cutting violations by 52 percent.

**AI — deficiencies & future work:** High-resolution perception and large-model inference raise computational and maintenance costs, edge computing depends on stable power and network, AR execution relies on worker adaptation, and validation covers a single SCP project with confidential raw data. Future research targets model compression, lightweight deployment and adaptive zoning for scalability.
