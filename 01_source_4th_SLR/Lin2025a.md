## Abstract summary
Worker-robot collaboration (WRC) has emerged as a transformative approach to augmenting the productivity of the construction industry. However, the development of a safety monitoring method or system for stopping robot operations in emergency is imperative, especially for seamless WRC on site. This paper presents a digital twin-enabled safety monitoring system for seamless WRC on site, characterized by its comprehensive perception of dynamic entities and dynamic calculation of protective separation distances during seamless WRC. The effectiveness of the proposed system is substantiated through a series of experiments, the result demonstrates its proficiency in mitigating collisions during robot operation in both static and dynamic WRC scenarios. **Key evidence (AG):** DT-enabled safety monitoring system for seamless worker-robot collaboration (Tsinghua U): depth camera + LiDAR perceive workers/robots/materials → geometric-solid virtual representations in a digital twin; computes separation distance (SD) and dynamic protective separation distance (PSD, per ISO 15066/13855); when SD exceeds PSD the system "accesses the physical actuators of robots to stop it" — explicit virtual→physical control (emergency stop). Experiments: 9.8 FPS, 0.177 s reaction latency, 0.09 m perception error, static+dynamic scenarios. No ML used and no learning loop (predictive motion methods are future work).

*Full-text source: 04c-port*

**AH — strength & gap filled:** Closes the gap between industrial speed-and-separation monitoring and seamless construction worker-robot collaboration: a digital twin system perceiving workers via Azure Kinect 32-point skeletons with 14 capsule representations, robots via URDF plus encoders, and materials via ArUco and QR tags with IFC data, computing rapid separation distance and dynamic protective separation distance to halt the robot, achieving 9.8 FPS, 0.177 s reaction and 0.09 m perception error with zero collisions in static and dynamic conflict tests.

**AI — deficiencies & future work:** Validation covers one mobile manipulator prototype in controlled scenarios with protective equipment and human supervision, and virtual-state updates still depend on robot actuators for stopping. Future work targets more robot types including humanoids, faster perception methods with less latency, and predictive motion models to improve protective separation distance calculation.
