## Abstract summary
The rapid expansion of civil infrastructure in China underscores the critical need for advanced solutions to ensure the structural health of aging bridges. This study introduces a novel data and knowledge-driven digital twin modeling (DK-DTM) framework designed to enhance the safe and efficient operation and maintenance (O&M) of bridges. Such a system should be capable of (1) monitoring structural dynamics in real time, (2) capturing spatiotemporal details and changes (e.g., defects and deformations), (3) analyzing structure deterioration patterns, (4) predicting structure failure risks, and (5) generating optimal maintenance and repair actions for ensuring structural safety. Previous studies have developed advanced sensing techniques and robust artificial intelligence algorithms for capturing and analyzing bridge health conditions. **Key evidence (AG):** Data and knowledge-driven bridge digital twin modeling (DK-DTM) framework for smart O&M (Beijing U Tech): multi-source data collection + spatiotemporal modeling + expert knowledge reasoning; real-time data from spatial/temporal models; expert knowledge model for automated structural health assessment; supports monitoring, prediction, and decision-making for efficient maintenance. One-way monitoring→assessment→decision support; no actuation and no learning loop (expert knowledge is rule-based, not learning).

*Full-text source: 04c-port*

**AH — strength & gap filled:** Fills the gap between data-driven bridge SHM and expert knowledge: the DK-DTM framework combines UAV imagery processed by YOLOv8, LiDAR point clouds processed by PointNet, and LSTM-forecast sensor series with a fuzzy-logic expert knowledge base and rule engine, applied to the 612 m Hedong Bridge where it found a 12 mm mid-span displacement and early-stage fatigue risks.

**AI — deficiencies & future work:** Validation is limited to the Hedong Bridge case with no reported quantitative accuracy for the detection and prediction models; real-time performance depends on multi-source data integration speed, machine-learning robustness depends on training data quality, and uncertain expert rules complicate knowledge base construction; future work includes advanced ML algorithms and automatic knowledge-base selection per bridge type.
