## Abstract ^sec-abstract
Mixed reality is advancing exponentially in some innovative industries, including manufacturing and aerospace, but advanced applications of these technologies in architecture, engineering and construction (AEC) businesses remain nascent. While in demand, the use of these technologies for AEC digital pedagogy and improving professional competence has received little attention. The paper presents a set of five novel digital technologies utilising virtual and augmented reality and digital twin that add value to the literature by showing their usefulness in the delivery of construction courses. The project designed, developed and implemented a construction Piling AR (PAR) smartphone module and a Virtual Tunnel Boring Machine (VTBM) multiplayer module with avatars, demonstrating digital pedagogy practices and the capacity of virtual technologies for teaching from a distance, including during COVID-19. ^p1

## Introduction & Background ^sec-intro
AEC education has historically relied on site visits, physical mock-ups, and synchronous studio teaching. The COVID-19 pandemic exposed the fragility of this model and accelerated interest in remote, immersive pedagogy. The author positions DT and mixed-reality (VR/AR) as the technical substrate for an "AEC digital pedagogy" that can deliver site-comparable learning experiences without the site. ^p1

The paper notes that DT in AEC remains immature compared with manufacturing/aerospace, and that the educational use case is itself a stress-test of DT fidelity, multi-user interaction, and integration with web-delivery infrastructure. ^p2

## Method ^sec-method
1. Design five digital modules covering construction equipment, structural systems and process flows, prioritising scenarios that students cannot easily access in person. ^p1
2. Develop the Piling AR (PAR) smartphone module to present footing system elements and required equipment in augmented reality. ^p2
3. Develop the Virtual Tunnel Boring Machine (VTBM) module as a multiplayer, avatar-enabled environment for collaborative learning. ^p3
4. Pilot the modules in a construction-management course and gather qualitative feedback on usability and learning effect. ^p4

## Key Findings ^sec-results
- Web-delivered AR/DT modules can convey complex construction equipment behaviour without physical site access. ^f1
- Multiplayer, avatar-based DT environments support collaborative learning analogous to site walk-throughs. ^f2
- The technical stack (smartphone AR, web-based gaming, DT) is feasible with current off-the-shelf tooling, but content authoring is the dominant cost. ^f3
- Validation is necessarily small-scale and context-specific: the modules were tested in a single course at a single institution. ^f4

## Discussion & Implications ^sec-discussion
The work demonstrates that DT and immersive technologies can be repurposed for pedagogy, but also exposes a recurring problem in AECO DT literature: studies are validated in narrow, controlled settings (one course, one institution), making generalisation to broader construction operations uncertain. ^p1

The paper supports the broader argument that AECO DT applications, even when technically successful, often lack the validation breadth needed for confident scaling. ^p2

## Conclusion ^sec-conclusion
DT and web-based virtual gaming offer a viable path for online construction education and demonstrate feasibility, while exposing limits in validation scope that mirror those of operational AECO DT studies. ^p1
