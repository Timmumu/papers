## Abstract ^sec-abstract

The paper examines patterns, gaps and trends in DT research in the AECO-FM industry (architecture, engineering, construction, operation, facility management) and proposes future directions. A bibliometric search over Scopus retrieved 197 papers; 151 were retained for scientometric analysis and mapping of the field's evolution. Studies cluster around model-based information management, building information management, and building–smart-city interaction. The dominant subjects are virtual–physical building integration, building lifecycle management, and information-integrated production, while information-based predictive management and virtual-based information utilisation are identified as still-required directions for holistic DT adoption. The sample size is acknowledged as a limitation. ^p1

## Introduction & Background ^sec-intro

The author scopes DT research to the AECO-FM industry, distinguishing it from generic construction-DT reviews by explicitly including operation and facility management. ^p1

The work positions cognitive technology and Construction 4.0 as the contextual frame in which AECO-FM DT must be evaluated, anticipating cognitive capability as a discriminating dimension. ^p2

## Method ^sec-method

1. Bibliometric search of Scopus retrieving 197 papers. ^p1
2. Screening to 151 retained papers for scientometric analysis. ^p2
3. Co-occurrence and keyword mapping to reveal research subjects and their evolution. ^p3
4. Trend and gap analysis to identify under-addressed subjects. ^p4

## Key Findings ^sec-results

- 197 papers retrieved, 151 analysed via scientometrics. ^f1
- Three dominant subjects: virtual–physical building integration, building lifecycle management, information-integrated production. ^f2
- Two under-addressed subjects: information-based predictive management and virtual-based information utilisation. ^f3
- Future studies should target full integration of DT with its physical counterpart for lifecycle performance. ^f4

## Discussion & Implications ^sec-discussion

The under-addressed subjects (predictive management, virtual-based information utilisation) explicitly name the cognitive-DT gap that the SLR mobilises as its central contribution. ^p1

By naming AECO-FM rather than only construction, the paper authorises the SLR's scope choice of treating operation and facility management as first-class lifecycle stages. ^p2

## Conclusion ^sec-conclusion

AECO-FM DT research has consolidated around information integration but lacks predictive and full virtual-utilisation capabilities — precisely the cognitive layer a full-lifecycle DT must add. ^p1
