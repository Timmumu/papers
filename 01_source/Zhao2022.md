## Abstract ^sec-abstract
Comparative illustrative case study of traditional FM vs. DT-driven FM across four geospatially representative buildings during the O&M phase. Findings show DT technologies enable efficient and responsive planning and control of FM activities via real-time asset status, but adoption is hampered by data-integration and data-standard misalignment. A bottom-up conceptual framework is proposed to facilitate wider DT implementation in building O&M. ^p1

## Method ^sec-method
Multi-case illustrative case study (n = 4 geographically diverse buildings) comparing traditional FM with DT-driven FM, followed by bottom-up framework synthesis. ^p1

## Key Findings ^sec-results

- DT-driven FM measurably improves responsiveness and planning quality versus traditional FM across all four cases. ^f1

- Data-integration and data-standard misalignment is the dominant barrier to wider DT-FM adoption — not sensor or modeling cost. ^f2

- A bottom-up framework (asset → process → decision) is more deployable than top-down reference architectures for FM stakeholders. ^f3


## Discussion & Implications ^sec-discussion
Empirical complement to bibliometric reviews; provides concrete cross-site evidence that data-standard misalignment — not technology readiness — is the principal blocker to DT-FM adoption. Frequently cited alongside Hakimi2024 and Zhang2023 to triangulate the same conclusion. ^p1
