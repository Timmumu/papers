## Abstract ^sec-abstract
Railway turnouts ("switches and crossings") are complex systems by design and construction. They route trains between tracks and demand high construction and maintenance quality to avoid rapid degradation and component failures that could cause derailments. The paper proposes a Digital Twin–aided, sustainability-based lifecycle management approach for railway turnout systems, integrating design, manufacturing, construction, and maintenance information into a shared digital representation so that economic, environmental, and safety performance can be assessed and optimized across the full asset lifecycle. ^p1

## Introduction & Background ^sec-intro
Railway turnouts are safety-critical infrastructure whose failure modes propagate quickly into operational and safety consequences. Conventional management treats design, construction, and maintenance as siloed phases with limited information flow, which prevents holistic optimization for sustainability. ^p1

The authors frame the Digital Twin as the integrating layer: by maintaining a continuously updated digital counterpart of each turnout system, decisions taken in one lifecycle phase (e.g., design choice, manufacturing tolerance) can be evaluated against downstream sustainability outcomes (energy, materials, maintenance interventions, end-of-life). ^p2

## Method ^sec-method
The work develops a DT-aided lifecycle management framework specifically for railway turnouts, organized around lifecycle stages (design, manufacture, construction, operation/maintenance) and around sustainability indicators that span economic, environmental, and safety dimensions. ^p1

The framework is demonstrated through case-based reasoning on turnout systems, illustrating how information captured in early stages is reused for operational decision-making and how operational data is fed back to inform sustainable design and maintenance choices. ^p2

## Key Findings ^sec-results
- A DT-aided lifecycle management framework is feasible for railway turnouts and can be structured around sustainability KPIs. ^f1
- Integrating design, manufacturing, construction, and maintenance information into a shared DT enables cross-phase optimization that siloed practice cannot achieve. ^f2
- The approach exposes how decisions at early lifecycle stages propagate into long-term sustainability outcomes for safety-critical infrastructure. ^f3
- Single-asset / single-site demonstration: scalability and external validity to other turnouts and railway networks remain to be established. ^f4

## Discussion & Implications ^sec-discussion
The contribution illustrates a lifecycle-wide DT in an infrastructure (rather than building) context and explicitly links DT capability to sustainability performance — a framing that AECO-building DT work often lacks. ^p1

It also exemplifies the SLR's validation-fragility concern: the framework is demonstrated on turnout systems under controlled assumptions, and generalization to cross-network, cross-firm operations is not established. ^p2

## Conclusion ^sec-conclusion
A Digital Twin–aided, sustainability-based lifecycle management framework can integrate the fragmented design–manufacture–construction–maintenance chain of railway turnouts and turn lifecycle data into actionable sustainability decisions. ^p1
