## Abstract ^sec-abstract

Safety management of hoisting is central to prefabricated-building construction, but current practice lacks an effective cyber-physical fusion method and treats safety-risk factors in isolation rather than as a coupled system. The paper presents a DT-based hoisting safety-risk management framework and a "digital twin hoisting safety risk coupling model" that integrates IoT, BIM, and a risk-analysis method combining the **Apriori algorithm** with **complex networks** to mine association rules and coupling relationships among risk factors and to visualise time-varying data. A demonstration on a large prefabricated-building project shows information fusion between the hoisting site and the virtual model, visual management, identification of key control factors among coupled risks, and improved efficiency of information integration and sharing. ^p1

## Introduction & Background ^sec-intro

Prefabricated construction shifts a large share of safety risk to the hoisting stage, where heavy components, dense work zones, and tight schedules interact. Existing safety analyses examine risk factors one at a time and miss the coupling that drives actual incidents. ^p1

Conventional site monitoring also fails to fuse physical sensing with the BIM model in real time, so the visual and the analytical layers stay disconnected. ^p2

## Method ^sec-method

1. **DT framework for hoisting safety** — IoT sensors at the site feed real-time data; BIM provides the virtual representation; the DT layer synchronises both and supports virtual–real interaction. ^p1
2. **Risk-coupling analysis** — the **Apriori algorithm** mines association rules between hoisting safety-risk factors; a **complex-network** representation captures coupling relationships and identifies key control factors. ^p2
3. **Visualisation of time-varying data** — the framework visualises evolving risk states on the BIM-DT to support real-time decision-making. ^p3
4. **Case demonstration** — application to a large-scale prefabricated-building project, with site information fused into the virtual model. ^p4

## Key Findings ^sec-results

- Real-time perception and virtual–real interaction of multi-source hoisting information are realised in the DT framework. ^f1
- Apriori + complex-network analysis exposes **coupling relationships** among hoisting safety-risk factors that single-factor analyses miss. ^f2
- **Key control factors** governing the coupled risk network are identified, supporting prioritised intervention. ^f3
- The demonstration project shows improved efficiency of information integration and sharing and effective safety decision-making. ^f4

## Discussion & Implications ^sec-discussion

The work moves construction safety from static checklists to *coupled, evolving* networks visualised on a DT — a small but explicit step toward reasoning-on-the-twin rather than reasoning-beside-the-twin. ^p1

By integrating sensor streams, BIM geometry, and rule mining inside one framework, it operationalises what the SLR labels Level-3-to-4 DT maturity for a specific high-risk construction operation. ^p2

## Conclusion ^sec-conclusion

A DT-based hoisting safety-risk coupling framework integrating IoT, BIM, Apriori and complex networks closes the gap between site monitoring, model visualisation and risk reasoning for prefabricated-building hoisting, and identifies the key control factors driving coupled risks. ^p1
