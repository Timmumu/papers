## Abstract ^sec-abstract

With the increasing urgency to achieve ambitious energy reduction targets in both existing and newly constructed buildings, Digital Twins have emerged as a transformative solution for optimizing building operations. By enabling data-driven decision-making, these virtual replicas help minimize energy waste while enhancing occupant comfort and overall building performance. This study explores the potential of ontology-based Digital Twins by demonstrating their scenario implementation and testing capabilities. A case study is conducted on a hospital building, which includes 12 conditioned thermal zones and an integrated HVAC system. The scenario development is performed by employing three strategy complexity levels: Flexible strategies, Rule-based strategies, and Preheating strategies. The scenarios are evaluated using occupant-centered KPIs for indoor comfort while determining energy consumption and cost of operation. The main aim is to limit thermal discomfort while maintaining a energy consumption. Different alternatives for lowering thermal discomfort are evaluated, providing building operators with the possibility of informed decision-making. By increasing CO 2 setpoints in each thermal zone while increasing the heating setpoints in all spaces, the thermal discomfort was lowered by 82% and related monetary energy costs by 22.7%. By increasing the supplied air temperature, the thermal discomfort decreased by 98%; however, this strategy resulted in a 129.7% increase in monetary energy costs. Preheating strategies proved relevant, lowering thermal discomfort by 15%, and ensuring air quality while marginally decreasing consumption and cost. The proposed work demonstrates how ontology-based digital twins can be used for informed decision-making by implementing and testing various operation scenarios as a service. • Showcases how ontology-based digital twins can be implemented and used for building performance optimization, in an automated manner. Providing practical insights into optimizing thermal comfort, energy efficiency, and operational costs for building operators. • Utilizes occupancy-based Key Performance Indicators enabling more accurate assessments of comfort, energy consumption, and costs for better decision-making. • Highlighted potential for large-scale implementation of automatically generated digital twins for intelligent building control, paving the way for model predictive control and advanced scenario testing in future smart buildings.

## Introduction & Background ^sec-intro

Digital Twins have emerged as a transformative solution for optimising building operations, enabling data-driven decision-making that minimises energy waste while enhancing occupant comfort. The study explores **ontology-based Digital Twins** through scenario implementation and testing on a hospital building with 12 conditioned thermal zones and integrated HVAC.

## Method ^sec-method

Three strategy complexity levels (Flexible, Rule-based, Preheating) are evaluated using occupant-centred KPIs for indoor comfort, energy consumption, and operation cost. CO₂ setpoint adjustments, heating setpoint variations, and supply-air-temperature strategies are tested.

## Key Findings ^sec-results

Increasing CO₂ and heating setpoints lowered **thermal discomfort by 82% and energy costs by 22.7%**. Increasing supply air temperature reduced discomfort by 98% but increased energy costs by 129.7%. Preheating strategies lowered discomfort by 15% while marginally decreasing consumption and cost. Ontology-based DTs enable automated scenario testing as a service.
