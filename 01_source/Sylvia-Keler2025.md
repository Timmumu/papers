## Abstract ^sec-abstract

The fib is dedicated to advancing the scientific study and application of concrete construction on an international scale, aiming to improve its technical, economic, aesthetic, and environmental performance. Within its 10 commissions, Commission 3 (COM3) focuses on creating reliable procedures to ensure the safety of existing structures and determining necessary interventions to extend their operational lifespan. To support the ongoing management of these structures, Task Group (TG) 3.3 concentrates on testing and structural health monitoring. Its goals include (i) developing service life extension concepts using NDT and SHM, (ii) promoting specific testing and monitoring techniques, including those with autonomous robotic systems, (iii) assessing the reliability of NDT/SHM methods, and (iv) supporting in decision-making to transition from periodic to predictive/preventive maintenance. Extending the service life of our infrastructure is crucial for achieving sustainability goals in the construction sector. Beyond sustainability, structural safety and durability are essential for the effective through-life management of existing concrete structures. The cornerstone of any condition assessment and performance prediction is data, obtained from Non-Destructive Testing (NDT) and Structural Health Monitoring (SHM), in addition to highly developed decision-making tools. NDT and SHM techniques span a wide spectrum, from well-established methods to cutting-edge technologies such as Autonomous Robotic Systems (ARS), Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAV), image-based monitoring, and self-monitoring materials. These advanced technologies are becoming increasingly significant, generating substantial volumes of data. To manage and analyze this data efficiently, advanced tools like machine learning techniques, Building Information Modeling (BIM), Digital Shadows (DS), and Digital Twins (DT) are indispensable. These tools facilitate rapid decision-making by processing large datasets and integrating them into comprehensive models of the structural behavior. Effective decision-making in the maintenance and management of concrete structures relies on defining and estimating Key Performance Indicators (KPIs). These indicators help schedule repair and replacement measures, while considering constraints such as owner requirements, accessibility, load changes, sustainability considerations, and performance predictions. The reliability of condition assessments and performance predictions is contingent on the capabilities of the applied NDT/SHM methods. Various indicators, each with different levels of maturity, such as Value of Information (VoI), Probability of Detection (POD), Probability of Identification (POI), and Probability of Localization (POL), are used to describe the effectiveness of these methods.
