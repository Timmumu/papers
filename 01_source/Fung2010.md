## Abstract
^sec-abstract

> Construction-safety performance in Hong Kong has long been hindered by inadequate risk assessment at the trade and activity level. This paper develops a Risk Assessment Model (RAM) that quantifies the safety risk of individual trades by combining (i) accident likelihood derived from historical injury frequency and (ii) severity weighted by injury type. Data were collected through a structured questionnaire of construction practitioners and triangulated against site-injury records. The resulting RAM ranks trades by composite risk score and identifies the highest-risk activities (formwork, scaffolding, working-at-height). The authors argue that quantitative trade-level risk scores enable proactive safety planning and resource allocation, rather than reactive post-incident analysis.

## Core Claims
^sec-claims

- ① Construction safety risk must be quantified at the **trade/activity** level, not aggregated at project level, to drive useful planning decisions.
- ② Combining historical **likelihood** with weighted **severity** yields a composite risk score that ranks trades reliably.
- ③ Working-at-height, scaffolding, and formwork dominate risk in Hong Kong building works.
- ④ The model surfaces **epistemic uncertainty** — risk scores depend on the practitioner sample and historical injury data quality.

## Method
- Structured questionnaire (Hong Kong practitioners).
- Statistical analysis (frequency × severity) per trade.
- Triangulation against archival injury records.

## Limitations Noted
- Hong Kong sample limits generalization.
- Treats risk scores as point estimates; no probabilistic propagation.
- 2010 vintage — predates modern data-driven and DT methods.
