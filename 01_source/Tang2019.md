## Abstract ^sec-abstract
The integration of Building Information Modeling (BIM) with real-time data from Internet of Things (IoT) devices presents a powerful paradigm for applications to improve construction and operational efficiencies. Connecting real-time data streams from the rapidly expanding set of IoT sensor networks to high-fidelity BIM models provides numerous applications. However, BIM and IoT integration research is still in nascent stages, and there is a need to understand the current situation of BIM–IoT device integration. This paper conducts a comprehensive review with the intent to identify common emerging application areas and design patterns for tackling BIM-IoT integration, along with current limitations and predicted future research directions. ^p1

## Introduction & Background ^sec-intro
BIM has matured as a design and pre-construction tool, but it is fundamentally a *static* repository: it does not automatically reflect operational state or update in response to real-world events. IoT sensor networks generate exactly the kind of continuous, fine-grained data that BIM lacks, motivating efforts to bridge the two. ^p1

The authors observe that despite multiple proof-of-concept projects, BIM–IoT integration research remains conceptually and theoretically driven, with limited robust deployment evidence. ^p2 ^sec-1-bimstatic

## Method ^sec-method
1. Define inclusion criteria for BIM-IoT integration studies and search 14 AEC-related journals and adjacent industry databases. ^p1
2. Screen and retain 97 papers spanning roughly a decade of publication. ^p2
3. Classify each paper by application domain, integration mechanism, and reported maturity level. ^p3
4. Synthesise five recurring integration methods, with descriptions, examples and discussion of trade-offs. ^p4

## Key Findings ^sec-results
- Four dominant application domains: Construction Operation & Monitoring; Health & Safety Management; Construction Logistics & Management; Facility Management. ^f1
- Five recurring integration methods are identified, ranging from manual data import to API-mediated middleware to web-service architectures. ^f2
- The field is dominated by conceptual and prototype work; full-scale, long-term deployments are rare. ^f3
- BIM's static nature is the structural barrier driving most integration effort; without IoT, BIM cannot represent operational reality. ^f4

## Discussion & Implications ^sec-discussion
The review crystallises the AECO sector's central data problem: BIM holds intent and geometry but cannot, by itself, deliver the continuous physical-to-digital synchronisation that defines a DT. IoT is the missing data layer, but most integration approaches lack the bidirectional update mechanisms required for true DT operation. ^p1

The dominance of "conceptual / prototype" studies signals that field is technically feasible but not yet operationally mature — a pattern our SLR also observes for AECO DT more broadly. ^p2

## Conclusion ^sec-conclusion
BIM-IoT integration is the technical pathway from static BIM toward live, data-linked building models, but in 2019 the literature remained in a nascent, prototype-heavy phase with five recurring integration architectures and four dominant application domains. ^p1
