## Abstract ^sec-abstract

Railway infrastructure degradation due to repeated trainloads and environmental reasons necessitates continuous maintenance to ensure safety and reliability. With data-driven insights, predictive maintenance has emerged as a superior alternative to traditional approaches, enabling early detection of defects and minimizing disruptions. Among the diagnostic methods, vibration-based damage detection has proven effective in identifying railway track defects. This review explores advancements in these techniques for identifying track geometry and component defects, covering traditional methods and approaches leveraging machine learning (ML), deep learning (DL), and digital twin (DT) technologies. An analysis of selected literature reveals a paramount focus on track superstructure, specifically track geometry irregularity and rail defect, while the substructure defect, including ballast and subgrade, remains relatively underexplored. Moreover, limited research has addressed the critical interdependencies between track geometry irregularities and component defects despite their significant influence on the overall track performance and degradation mechanisms. The findings emphasize the need for integrating modern technologies with vibration-based diagnostics to enhance the monitoring of substructure conditions and extensively explore these correlations. This review highlights the importance of addressing these gaps to achieve a more holistic and practical approach to railway track structural health monitoring (SHM).
