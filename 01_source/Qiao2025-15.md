## Abstract ^sec-abstract

LiDAR point clouds and BIM are two distinct modalities from different sources and reference frames in robot perception and construction. The paper proposes a cross-modality global registration method to align them into a shared frame so they can "speak the same language". At the front end, triangle descriptors are extracted by identifying walls and intersected corners, enabling corner-triplet matching with complexity independent of BIM size. At the back end, the Hough transform maps matched triplets to the transformation space and a hierarchical voting mechanism hypothesises multiple pose candidates; the final transformation is verified by an occupancy-aware scoring method. Multi-session experiments in a large-scale university building with two LiDAR sensors validate the approach; datasets and code are released. ^p1

## Introduction & Background ^sec-intro

The authors frame BIM-vs-LiDAR alignment as a fundamental obstacle to bringing as-designed and as-built/as-is information into a single reference frame for robot navigation and construction monitoring. ^p1

They argue that classical point-cloud registration fails on the BIM–LiDAR pair because the modalities differ in density, completeness and semantic content, motivating a structure-aware, semantic-feature approach. ^p2

The practitioner note emphasises suitability for scenarios with well-defined structural layouts (walls, corners), with larger LiDAR submaps providing richer structural cues. ^p3

## Method ^sec-method

1. **Front end** — detect walls and intersected corners in both LiDAR submaps and BIM; build triangle descriptors from corner triplets so matching complexity is independent of BIM size. ^p1
2. **Back end** — apply the Hough transform to map matched corner triplets into transformation (pose) space. ^p2
3. **Hierarchical voting** — hypothesise multiple pose candidates from the Hough accumulator. ^p3
4. **Verification** — score pose candidates with an occupancy-aware metric to select the final transformation. ^p4

## Key Findings ^sec-results

- A cross-modality global LiDAR-to-BIM registration pipeline is realised end-to-end. ^f1
- Triangle-descriptor matching achieves complexity independent of BIM size, addressing scalability. ^f2
- Hierarchical Hough voting produces multiple candidate poses that an occupancy-aware score then disambiguates. ^f3
- Real-world multi-session experiments in a large-scale university building with two LiDAR sensors validate the method; datasets and code are released. ^f4

## Discussion & Implications ^sec-discussion

The work provides the perception/alignment substrate a cognitive AECO DT needs: without robust BIM ↔ LiDAR registration, runtime sensor streams cannot be referenced to the design model. ^p1

By releasing data and code, the paper lowers the barrier for downstream cognitive-DT components (semantic update, change detection, navigation) to be benchmarked on a common base. ^p2

## Conclusion ^sec-conclusion

Wall–corner triangle descriptors plus pose Hough voting with occupancy-aware verification deliver scalable, validated global LiDAR-to-BIM registration, supplying a key perception primitive for AECO DT pipelines. ^p1
