## Abstract ^sec-abstract
Digital Twin (DT) refers to the virtual copy or model of any physical entity (physical twin), both of which are interconnected via exchange of data in real time. Conceptually, a DT mimics the state of its physical twin in real time and vice versa. Applications of DT include real-time monitoring, designing/planning, optimisation, maintenance, remote access, and more. Implementation is expected to grow exponentially over the coming decades. The advent of Industry 4.0 has brought complex industrial systems that are more autonomous, smart, and highly interconnected, generating considerable data — and DT is positioned as a key technology for turning these data streams into operational value across the full product lifecycle. ^p1

## Introduction & Background ^sec-intro
The authors trace DT from Grieves' original PLM-era conceptualisation through NASA/USAF aerospace applications, into Industry-4.0 manufacturing, and onward toward smart cities and infrastructure. The review presents this evolution as a continuous lineage rather than separate inventions, motivating their "Origin to Future" framing. ^p1

They emphasise that the present generation of DTs is enabled by the convergence of IoT, big data, cloud computing, AI/ML and cyber-physical systems — a stack that did not exist when the concept was first proposed. ^p2

## Method ^sec-method
1. Trace the historical development of DT from Grieves (2003) through aerospace adoption (Glaessgen & Stargel, 2012) to Industry-4.0 manufacturing. ^p1
2. Survey DT definitions across domains and synthesise common features (virtual replica, real-time data link, lifecycle scope). ^p2
3. Catalogue current application areas (manufacturing, healthcare, smart cities, AEC) and the enabling technology stack (IoT, AI, CPS, edge/cloud). ^p3
4. Identify open challenges and future research directions, particularly around standardisation, security and lifecycle integration. ^p4

## Key Findings ^sec-results
- The DT concept has a documented, continuous evolution from PLM (Grieves) through aerospace (NASA/USAF) to Industry-4.0 CPS. ^f1
- The "modern" DT is inseparable from the underlying IoT + AI + CPS stack that emerged in the 2010s. ^f2
- Adoption is uneven across domains: manufacturing and aerospace lead; AEC and healthcare lag. ^f3
- Open challenges include standardisation, security/privacy, and full-lifecycle integration beyond single use-cases. ^f4

## Discussion & Implications ^sec-discussion
By presenting DT as the culmination of a 20-year evolution, the review supports the argument that AECO's recent "discovery" of DT is part of a longer pattern of paradigm transfer from manufacturing into infrastructure. The enabling-technology stack is now mature enough that the bottleneck is conceptual and organisational, not computational. ^p1

The paper's future-research catalogue (standards, security, lifecycle integration) overlaps strongly with the gaps our SLR identifies in AECO DT practice. ^p2

## Conclusion ^sec-conclusion
DT has evolved from a PLM concept into a lifecycle-spanning, Industry-4.0-enabled paradigm, with implementation growing rapidly but unevenly across sectors. ^p1
