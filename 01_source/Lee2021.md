## Abstract ^sec-abstract

Construction projects depend on efficient inter-stakeholder collaboration, but stakeholder fragmentation hinders accountable information sharing. The authors develop and test an integrated **Digital Twin + Blockchain** framework for traceable data communication: the DT updates BIM in near real-time using IoT sensors, while the blockchain authenticates and adds confidence to all data transactions written to the twin. The framework is tested on a case project in which virtual positioning data from a prefabricated brick is transmitted to the DT in near real-time and recorded on the blockchain with time stamps, demonstrating that all data transactions become traceable. The primary contribution is a framework that operationalises accountable, project-wide information sharing across stakeholders. ^p1

## Introduction & Background ^sec-intro

Fragmentation of construction stakeholders breaks the audit trail of who-said-what-when about a building, undermining quality and dispute resolution. ^p1

DT alone supplies real-time visibility but not non-repudiation; blockchain supplies non-repudiation but not domain models. Combining them targets *accountable* DT updates rather than merely *live* ones. ^p2

## Method ^sec-method

1. Framework design — DT layer (BIM + IoT) for near-real-time state; blockchain layer for authenticated, time-stamped logging of all data transactions to the twin. ^p1
2. Integration design — every sensor-driven update to the BIM-DT is hashed and committed to the chain, so subsequent reads can verify origin and integrity. ^p2
3. Case demonstration — virtual positioning data from a prefabricated brick is captured by IoT, fed into the DT in near real-time, and recorded on the blockchain with time stamps. ^p3
4. Evaluation by tracing each data transaction end-to-end to confirm traceability. ^p4

## Key Findings ^sec-results

- The integrated DT–blockchain framework makes **all data transactions traceable** across the construction project. ^f1
- Near-real-time IoT-driven BIM updates can be combined with blockchain logging without breaking the DT update cadence (demonstrated on a prefab brick case). ^f2
- Time-stamped, authenticated logging supplies the *accountability* layer that conventional DTs lack. ^f3
- The framework operationalises information sharing across fragmented stakeholders by making provenance verifiable rather than asserted. ^f4

## Discussion & Implications ^sec-discussion

Pairing DT with blockchain reframes the DT from a *truth source* (assumed) to a *provenance source* (verifiable), which is the property that multi-party construction governance actually requires. ^p1

The construction-stage prefab demonstration is small but isolates the right primitive (one sensor → one update → one chain entry), which is the building block for life-cycle audit trails. ^p2

## Conclusion ^sec-conclusion

An integrated DT–blockchain framework, validated on a prefab-brick case, enables accountable and traceable information sharing across construction stakeholders. ^p1
