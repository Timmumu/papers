## Abstract ^sec-abstract

The widespread adoption of BIM together with the emergence of IoT has produced new decision-making capabilities across the building life cycle, motivating the concept of a Digital Twin of the built environment that synchronises a virtual platform with the physical asset for construction, FM, and environment monitoring. The authors argue that research on DTs for the built environment is still nascent and conduct a systematic review of 100 papers (including 23 reviews) to chart the BIM→DT evolution. They develop a five-level ladder taxonomy along the building life cycle, classify applications by domain (construction process, energy performance, indoor environment monitoring, etc.), summarise enabling technologies, and propose a baseline concept of an advanced DT for building management. ^p1

## Introduction & Background ^sec-intro

BIM provides the static information backbone for AEC-FM, but on its own does not deliver real-time synchronisation with the operating asset. The convergence of online sensing and IoT created the conditions for a DT of the built environment, in which the virtual and physical evolve together throughout the life cycle. ^p1

Despite growing interest, the review identifies fragmentation: terminology, scope, and technical depth vary widely across publications, and there is no convergent context for ongoing research. A structured map of where current studies sit on the path from BIM to a "true" DT is therefore needed. ^p2

## Method ^sec-method

1. Systematic search and selection yielded 100 related papers, of which 23 were reviews, covering BIM, BIM+simulation, BIM+IoT, BIM+AI, and DT applications in the built environment. ^p1
2. The authors construct a **five-level ladder taxonomy** organised by the building life cycle: Level 1 BIM; Level 2 BIM-supported simulations; Level 3 BIM integrated with IoT; Level 4 BIM integrated with AI for predictions; Level 5 ideal Digital Twin (bi-directional, autonomous control). ^p2
3. At each level, papers are further classified by research domain (construction process, energy, indoor environment, etc.) and the enabling technologies (sensing, communication, modelling, AI) are summarised. ^p3

## Key Findings ^sec-results

- A five-level ladder from BIM to ideal DT is established and used to position the 100 reviewed studies. ^f1
- **No reviewed study reaches Level 5**; most cluster at Levels 2–3 (simulation or one-way IoT integration). ^f2
- Bi-directional virtual–physical interaction and automated feedback/control of the built environment remain the principal unmet capability for an "ideal" DT. ^f3
- The authors propose a baseline conceptual architecture for an advanced DT for building management as a reference for future work. ^f4

## Discussion & Implications ^sec-discussion

The taxonomy clarifies what "DT" actually means in AEC-FM contexts and exposes that most published "DTs" are in fact BIM+IoT dashboards or BIM-supported simulations without closed-loop control. This grounding is important for any subsequent SLR that wants to evaluate cognitive maturity rather than visual sophistication. ^p1

The gap to Level 5 is presented less as a technology bottleneck and more as a systems-integration and autonomy bottleneck: closed-loop feedback, semantic interoperability across life-cycle phases, and decision authority delegated to the twin. ^p2

## Conclusion ^sec-conclusion

BIM→DT in the built environment is an unfinished evolution; the proposed five-level ladder and advanced DT concept give the field a shared yardstick against which future implementations and reviews can be compared. ^p1

## Relevance to Cognitive Full-Lifecycle DT SLR ^sec-relevance

- **Lifecycle stage(s):** design, construction, operation (full life cycle by design)
- **Cognitive capability:** perception (IoT), reasoning/prediction (AI), autonomy (Level 5 target)
