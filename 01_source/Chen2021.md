## Abstract ^sec-abstract

Existing maturity models do not understand DT implementation comprehensively or quantitatively. This paper proposes a DT maturity model for asset management grounded in the UK CDBB **Gemini Principles**. The model is organised as **3 main dimensions → 9 sub-dimensions → 27 rubrics**, validated via a 40-expert questionnaire and two case studies (Shanghai and Cambridge). The model is shown to qualitatively evaluate and compare DT maturity at the project level and to initiate development roadmaps. ^p1

## Introduction & Background ^sec-intro

Asset-lifecycle management is being reshaped by computerisation; AI is forecast to add 10 % to UK economy by 2030, and improved data sharing can reduce consumer bills and environmental impact (per CDBB). DT, as a dynamic representation that mimics real-world behaviour, is positioned as the integrative technology — but security, data quality, and interoperability must be designed-in. ^p1

The DT has matured in aerospace/manufacturing but is only now gaining traction in AEC and O&M. The 5-layer canonical DT (data acquisition → transmission → digital modelling → integration → application) is broader than BIM. ^p2

## Method ^sec-method

Three-stage research design: ^p1
1. **Literature review** of prior maturity models (BIM Quick Scan, VDC Scorecard, etc.) — finds none cover security/openness adequately. ^p2
2. **Model construction**: 3 dimensions derived from Gemini Principles — *Purpose* (public good, value creation, insight), *Trust* (security, openness, quality), *Function* (federation, curation, evolution). Each sub-dimension articulated as a rubric scaled 1-5. ^p3
3. **Validation** via 40-expert questionnaire (assess rubric clarity and relevance) and two case studies — a Shanghai project and a Cambridge project — applying the rubric instrument. ^p4

## Key Findings ^sec-results

- The 27-rubric instrument is internally coherent and applicable in both case studies. ^f1
- Shanghai and Cambridge projects differ markedly across dimensions, demonstrating the model's discriminating power. ^f2
- Trust dimension (security/openness/quality) is consistently the weakest in current AECO DT implementations. ^f3
- The model successfully translates qualitative Gemini Principles into a quantitative rubric scaffold. ^f4

## Discussion & Implications ^sec-discussion

The authors argue the model fills a gap in prior maturity scorecards, which assess BIM or VDC adoption but cannot assess DT-specific concerns like federation, curation, evolution, or data-trust governance. The model is presented as a practical roadmap instrument — projects identify low-scoring dimensions and target investment accordingly. ^p1

Limitation: only two case studies, so external validity remains to be demonstrated; rubric scoring is still expert-judgement-based. ^p2

## Conclusion ^sec-conclusion

A Gemini-Principles-grounded, 3-dim × 9-sub × 27-rubric DT maturity model enables quantitative, comparable, project-level DT-readiness assessment for asset management, validated against expert consensus and two international cases. ^p1
