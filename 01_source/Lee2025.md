## Abstract ^sec-abstract

Agentic AI — LLM-based agents that perceive, reason, plan and act — holds significant potential for domain-specific applications in built environments, but most existing work focuses on the architectural components or technical mechanisms of the agents and not on how they are actually used in buildings. This review surveys agentic-AI applications in the built environment and proposes a classification framework with three axes: (i) **five representative application domains**, (ii) functional roles of agents along the **DIKW (Data–Information–Knowledge–Wisdom) hierarchy** from data interpretation to decision support, and (iii) **four core learning approaches** adopted by AI agents. The review uses this framework to identify gaps and future directions for agentic built environments. ^p1

## Introduction & Background ^sec-intro

LLM-based agents have moved building research beyond passive analytics: agents can plan inspections, draft reports, control HVAC, and mediate occupant requests. But the literature is dominated by architecture/mechanism descriptions rather than application taxonomies. ^p1

The review responds by classifying not the agents themselves but the *roles* they play in the built environment and the *learning regimes* they use to acquire competence. ^p2

## Method ^sec-method

1. Literature scan of agentic-AI / LLM-agent applications in built environments. ^p1
2. Construction of a **three-axis classification framework**: applications × functional roles (DIKW) × learning approaches. ^p2
3. Identification of **five representative application domains** in the built environment for agentic AI. ^p3
4. Mapping of agent roles along the **DIKW hierarchy**, from data interpretation up to decision support / "wisdom". ^p4
5. Extraction of **four core learning approaches** adopted by AI agents in this domain. ^p5

## Key Findings ^sec-results

- Five representative application areas for agentic AI in built environments are identified and described. ^f1
- Agent functional roles align with the DIKW hierarchy and progress from data interpretation toward decision support. ^f2
- Four core learning approaches are dominant across the reviewed agentic-AI systems. ^f3
- Most reviewed work concentrates at the lower DIKW levels (data/information); higher-order knowledge and wisdom roles remain under-explored. ^f4

## Discussion & Implications ^sec-discussion

By mapping agents to DIKW, the review re-grounds "agentic" claims in epistemic terms: an agent that only summarises sensor streams is operating at Information, while one that selects interventions is operating at Knowledge/Wisdom. This is directly compatible with cognitive-DT maturity ladders used elsewhere in this SLR. ^p1

The fact that current systems cluster low on DIKW mirrors Deng et al.'s finding that no DT has reached Level 5: agentic-AI in buildings is still mostly perceptive/interpretive, not decisional/autonomous. ^p2

## Conclusion ^sec-conclusion

Agentic AI in the built environment can be productively classified by application domain, DIKW role and learning approach, and the resulting map exposes a clear gap at the upper end of the DIKW hierarchy. ^p1
