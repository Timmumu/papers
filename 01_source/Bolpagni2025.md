## Abstract
^sec-abstract

> Pending verification against fulltext. The paper presents qualitative industry insights into the practical challenges of deploying Digital Twins for built-asset management across the building lifecycle, with emphasis on (i) ownership and data-stewardship of DT models after handover, (ii) interoperability between BIM authoring tools and operational platforms, (iii) skills and organisational maturity of asset owners, and (iv) governance frameworks needed for trustworthy DT decisions. The authors triangulate practitioner interviews/workshops with documentary evidence and position the DT not as a one-off deliverable but as a **lifecycle service** continuously maintained alongside the physical asset.

## Core Claims
^sec-claims

- ① The dominant industry pain point is **DT continuity at the design-to-operations handover** — most BIM-derived DTs decay because no operator owns them.
- ② Standards (ISO 19650, ISO 23247) are necessary but not sufficient; governance, contracts, and skills lag.
- ③ Asset owners need a **role-and-process model** alongside any technical DT framework — supports LR_cc.md's Sec 6.4 governance argument.
- ④ Industry insights converge on the lifecycle-continuity gap that the LR_cc.md framework's persistent priors mechanism addresses.
