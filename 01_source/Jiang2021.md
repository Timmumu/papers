## Abstract ^sec-abstract

The Digital Twin concept has recently emerged in civil engineering, but three problems persist: DT is easily confused with BIM and Cyber-Physical Systems (CPS); the constituents of DT applications in civil engineering are not well defined; and the value DT brings to the sector is ambiguous. The authors review 468 articles related to DT, BIM and CPS, propose a DT definition and constituent set for civil engineering, and compare DT against BIM and CPS. They then analyse in detail 134 civil-engineering-specific DT papers (out of the 468), extract DT research clusters via keyword co-occurrence and constituent mapping, and outline state-of-the-art and future directions, with asset management, O&M and defect detection emerging as prominent clusters. ^p1

## Introduction & Background ^sec-intro

In civil engineering, DT, BIM and CPS are used almost interchangeably in the literature, which blocks cumulative progress: different studies claim "DT" for very different artefacts. ^p1

There is also no consensus on what a DT for a civil-engineering asset must contain (sensing, modelling, synchronisation, services, intelligence), which makes it hard to evaluate maturity or compare proposals. ^p2

## Method ^sec-method

1. Broad literature search yielding **468 articles** spanning DT, BIM and CPS. ^p1
2. Synthesis of a working **DT definition and constituent set** for civil engineering, then a side-by-side comparison with BIM and CPS to clarify boundaries. ^p2
3. Detailed in-depth review of **134 civil-engineering DT papers** (a subset of the 468). ^p3
4. **Keyword co-occurrence analysis** combined with mapping to the proposed DT constituents to extract research clusters and trends. ^p4

## Key Findings ^sec-results

- A DT definition and constituent set tailored to civil engineering is proposed, distinguishing DT from BIM (mostly static information) and CPS (control-centric, often without rich models). ^f1
- Of 468 broader candidates, **134 papers** are civil-engineering-specific DT studies that warrant detailed review. ^f2
- Co-occurrence analysis surfaces dominant research clusters in asset management, O&M, and defect detection. ^f3
- DT in civil engineering remains uneven across life-cycle stages, with operation/maintenance and infrastructure monitoring better represented than design and decommissioning. ^f4

## Discussion & Implications ^sec-discussion

By giving DT a constituent-level definition, the paper turns "DT" from a slogan into a checklist against which civil-engineering implementations can be evaluated — a prerequisite for any maturity or cognitive-capability scoring used by a downstream SLR. ^p1

The comparison with BIM and CPS clarifies that DT is the integration object: BIM contributes the model, CPS contributes the sensing/control, and DT adds synchronised behaviour and services on top. ^p2

## Conclusion ^sec-conclusion

The review establishes a civil-engineering-specific DT definition, distinguishes DT from BIM and CPS, maps research clusters from 134 detailed studies, and proposes directions for future DT development in the sector. ^p1
