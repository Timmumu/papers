## Abstract
^sec-abstract

> Frequency-response functions (FRFs) are the central diagnostic signal for structural-dynamic and rotating-machinery health, yet measurement noise, modelling error, and parametric variability inject substantial uncertainty into both peak frequencies and amplitudes. This paper reviews UQ techniques specifically tailored to FRF computation and identification, organized into (i) probabilistic methods (Monte Carlo, polynomial chaos, perturbation), (ii) non-probabilistic methods (interval analysis, fuzzy logic, evidence theory), and (iii) hybrid frameworks. Application areas include rotor dynamics, vibroacoustics, and structural health monitoring. The authors compare methods on convergence, computational cost, and ability to represent epistemic uncertainty, and identify the lack of standard benchmarks and the need for online UQ in digital-twin contexts as open challenges.

## Core Claims
^sec-claims

- ① FRF-based monitoring inherits both **aleatoric** (sensor noise) and **epistemic** (model-form) uncertainty; neglecting either biases diagnosis.
- ② Non-probabilistic methods (intervals, fuzzy, evidence theory) are gaining ground where probability distributions are unjustifiable.
- ③ Online / real-time UQ for digital-twin deployment is an explicitly identified **open research problem** — directly resonating with [LR_cc.md Sec 4.3](LR_cc.md).
- ④ Hybrid probabilistic + non-probabilistic frameworks are emerging as practical compromises.

## Method
