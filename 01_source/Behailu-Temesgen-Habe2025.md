## Abstract ^sec-abstract

> Digital twins (DTs) are increasingly deployed in the built environment to enable real-time monitoring, predictive analytics, and informed decision-making within complex urban systems. When integrated with artificial intelligence (AI), DTs can optimize performance and lifecycle outcomes; however, the opacity of AI algorithms often limits transparency, trust, and regulatory compliance. This targeted systematic review investigates how DTs, Explainable AI (XAI), and circular construction principles intersect to advance sustainable and accountable infrastructure. Following a PRISMA-guided protocol, twenty-five peer-reviewed studies published between 2017 and 2025 (including in-press articles) were selected from an initial pool of 1750 records. The synthesis spans four thematic dimensions: (1) DT applications for real-time decision-support in retrofitting and circular construction; (2) XAI techniques - such as SHAP, LIME, and Scene2Model - for interpretable and trustworthy analytics; (3) lifecycle and material-reuse strategies aligned with circular-economy goals; and (4) implementation challenges related to interoperability, policy fragmentation, and scalability. The review formulates a structured research agenda emphasizing open data standards, human-in-the-loop design, and governance mechanisms to institutionalize explainability within digital-twin ecosystems. By articulating how transparency can be embedded into data-driven construction processes, this study delineates a pathway toward verifiable, adaptive, and circular urban infrastructure.
