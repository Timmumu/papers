## Abstract ^sec-abstract

The construction industry faces low productivity, weak R&D and slow technology adoption. DT, mature in manufacturing and automotive sectors, can integrate the physical and digital worlds and respond to these challenges. The paper analyses the current state of DT in construction via a systematic literature review combined with science mapping. After database search and screening, 22 academic publications on construction DT are identified and classified. The study analyses status, evolution, concepts, technologies and applications, and outlines future research directions. ^p1

## Introduction & Background ^sec-intro

The authors anchor the review in construction's structural problems — productivity lag, fragmented information, and slow digitalisation — that DT is hypothesised to mitigate. ^p1

They argue that construction-specific DT literature is still small and fragmented, which justifies both a systematic review and science-mapping treatment to expose intellectual structure. ^p2

## Method ^sec-method

1. Systematic literature review methodology with database search and explicit inclusion criteria. ^p1
2. Science-mapping analysis (keyword/network analysis) on retained items. ^p2
3. Retention of 22 academic publications on construction DT applications. ^p3
4. Classification of items by concept, technology and application area. ^p4

## Key Findings ^sec-results

- Only 22 construction-DT publications met the criteria, showing an early-stage research field. ^f1
- DT concepts, technologies and applications in construction are identified and mapped. ^f2
- Evolution of the topic shows accelerating but still nascent activity. ^f3
- Future research directions are proposed to guide the construction-DT agenda. ^f4

## Discussion & Implications ^sec-discussion

The small corpus is itself evidence of an immature field where any contribution — including a cognitive full-lifecycle DT framework — has high marginal value. ^p1

Science mapping exposes which subtopics are over- vs under-represented, giving the SLR a defensible basis for gap claims about cognitive capability and lifecycle coverage. ^p2

## Conclusion ^sec-conclusion

Construction DT research is in an early but rapidly accelerating phase; consolidating concepts, technologies and applications is a precondition for advancing toward cognitive, lifecycle-spanning DTs. ^p1

## Relevance to Cognitive Full-Lifecycle DT SLR ^sec-relevance

- **Lifecycle stage(s):** design, construction, operation
- **Cognitive capability:** perception, reasoning (early-stage classification only)
