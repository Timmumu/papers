## Abstract ^sec-abstract

The paper reviews recent DT developments in manufacturing systems and processes to analyse the connotation, application scenarios and research issues of DT-driven smart manufacturing in the Industry 4.0 context. It summarises DT definitions and state-of-the-art outcomes, organises existing enabling technologies under a proposed DT reference model, reviews representative applications aligned with that model, and concludes by identifying outstanding research issues for DT in smart manufacturing. ^p1

## Introduction & Background ^sec-intro

DT is positioned as a breakthrough that mirrors the physical world to simulate, predict and optimise manufacturing systems, and, combined with intelligent algorithms, enables data-driven operation monitoring and decision support. ^p1

The authors note the absence of a systematic methodology for building manufacturing DTs and motivate a reference model that links Industry 4.0, cyber-physical systems, big data and standards. ^p2

## Method ^sec-method

1. Narrative-review collection of recent DT literature in manufacturing. ^p1
2. Extraction of consensus on DT connotation and definition. ^p2
3. Synthesis of a DT reference model used as the spine for mapping enabling technologies and representative applications. ^p3
4. Gap analysis to derive open research issues. ^p4

## Key Findings ^sec-results

- A DT reference model is proposed and used to organise enabling technologies for smart manufacturing. ^f1
- Representative applications are mapped onto the reference model, exposing which layers are mature and which remain prototypical. ^f2
- Industry 4.0, CPS, big data and standards are identified as the four pillars contextualising DT in manufacturing. ^f3
- Outstanding research issues span standardisation, integration, and runtime intelligence. ^f4

## Discussion & Implications ^sec-discussion

The reference model is offered as a systematisation device: it converts a fragmented technology landscape into a layered structure where any new DT implementation can be positioned and benchmarked. ^p1

The unresolved standardisation and integration issues directly anticipate the AECO interoperability problem and motivate domain-specific reference models such as the one developed in LR_cc.md. ^p2

## Conclusion ^sec-conclusion

DT-driven smart manufacturing is characterised by a structured reference model and a roadmap of outstanding research issues that any sector — including AECO — must inherit when designing its own DT stack. ^p1
