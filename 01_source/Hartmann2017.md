## Abstract ^sec-abstract

This editorial frames the special issue of *Building Research & Information* on how the use of digital building information is transformative — often in unanticipated ways — for the built environment. Digitisation reshapes the relationships users have with buildings and cities, redefines professional roles, and, through BIM, machine learning and smart infrastructure, drives integration across practices and scales that were previously treated separately, with implications for the wider network of public- and private-sector organisations. ^p1

## Introduction & Background ^sec-intro

The editors position digitisation of building information not as a tool upgrade but as a socio-technical shift: the *flow* of digital information across organisations, scales and life-cycle phases is the actual transformation, while BIM is one (visible) instrument among several. ^p1

The special issue is curated to surface unanticipated consequences — new dependencies, new accountabilities, new competencies — rather than to celebrate adoption metrics. ^p2

## Method ^sec-method

Editorial/curatorial: introduces the contributions to the special issue and synthesises their cross-cutting messages. No empirical method beyond the editorial framing. {not stated in detail} ^p1

## Key Findings ^sec-results

- Digitisation of building information reshapes the relationships between users and the built environment, not only the production process. ^f1
- Roles and responsibilities of designers, operators and maintainers are redefined by digital information flow. ^f2
- Greater integration emerges across previously siloed practices and scales (project ↔ portfolio ↔ city). ^f3
- Implications extend beyond firms to the wider network of public- and private-sector organisations involved in delivering and operating the built environment. ^f4

## Discussion & Implications ^sec-discussion

The editorial frames "BIM" as a partial proxy for a larger phenomenon — the digitisation of building information — and warns against equating technical adoption with transformation. The unit of analysis it advocates is the *information flow*, which anticipates DT discourse where the operative object is the synchronised data stream rather than the model file. ^p1

## Conclusion ^sec-conclusion

Digitising building information is changing the built environment in ways the AEC professions are only beginning to recognise; researchers should attend to the cross-organisational and cross-scale consequences, not only the modelling tools themselves. ^p1
