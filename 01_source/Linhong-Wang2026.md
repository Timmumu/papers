## Abstract ^sec-abstract

; Purpose ; Unstructured and variable construction sites pose significant challenges to earthwork scheduling. However, current parameter-tuning strategies fall short in supporting dynamic optimization under such complex conditions, and real-time construction data remains underutilized. To address these gaps, this study aims to propose a method that enables the automatic and real-time allocation and scheduling of heavy construction equipment by fully leveraging real-time construction data and dynamic equipment status information. ; ; ; Design/methodology/approach ; This research proposes a digital twin (DT)-enabled real-time rolling-horizon optimization (RHO) method that integrates an online Improved Aquila Optimizer with an adaptive parameter adjustment strategy. ## Introduction & Background ^sec-intro

Unstructured, variable construction sites make earthwork scheduling difficult; current parameter-tuning strategies fall short under dynamic conditions and real-time construction data is underused. The study aims to enable automatic, real-time allocation and scheduling of heavy equipment using real-time data and dynamic equipment status.

## Method ^sec-method

A digital-twin-enabled real-time rolling-horizon optimisation (RHO) method integrates an online Improved Aquila Optimizer with an adaptive parameter-adjustment strategy, using real-time resource status and progress data from a high-fidelity DT to optimise equipment scheduling without manual intervention.

## Key Findings ^sec-results

Implemented on a large hydropower construction project in Southwest China, the method achieves high-quality dynamic allocation and scheduling, significantly reducing construction delays by 38.4%.

## Discussion & Implications ^sec-discussion

A strong *DT-enabled real-time optimisation* exemplar for construction operations — closing the loop from high-fidelity DT state to autonomous scheduling decisions. Directly relevant to the cognitive/operational-DT and construction-equipment-management themes.
