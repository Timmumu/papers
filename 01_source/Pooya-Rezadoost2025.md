## Abstract ^sec-abstract

This study introduces a novel data-driven algorithm, referred to as RBL, for the efficient and accurate development of digital twins of tubular joints in offshore jacket-type structures. The RBL algorithm integrates Random Forest, Bayesian Optimization, and LASSO Regression, and incorporates deteriorating stop operators to effectively capture nonlinear hysteretic behavior under cyclic loading. Validated against finite element (FE) simulations, the algorithm consistently achieved normalized root mean square error (NRMSE) values below 2 % across a wide range of specimens and loading scenarios. It successfully replicated key structural response features, including pinching effects, asymmetric hysteresis, and both stiffness and strength degradation. The model demonstrated strong robustness to input noise and exhibited reliable generalization, accurately predicting responses under previously unseen loading conditions. In terms of computational efficiency, the RBL algorithm performed up to 20 times faster than traditional FE simulations while maintaining high accuracy. Furthermore, it outperformed state-of-the-art data-driven models in both prediction quality and runtime performance. These results highlight the RBL framework's potential for real-time structural health monitoring and predictive maintenance in offshore engineering applications. • A novel RBL algorithm is proposed for accurate digital twin modeling of offshore tubular joints. • Achieves high accuracy (NRMSE <2 %) and robust performance under noise and unseen loading conditions. • RBL algorithm runs up to 20× faster than FE simulations while preserving high accuracy.
