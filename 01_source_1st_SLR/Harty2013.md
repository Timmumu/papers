## Abstract ^sec-abstract

Numerous BIM tools are well established and beneficial in office settings, but adoption persists as a problem for on-site use during construction. The paper presents an empirical case study of an innovative "Site BIM" system on a major hospital project, in which the main contractor developed BIM-enabled tools that let site workers use mobile tablet PCs to access design information and capture quality and progress data on-site. The accounts show that Site BIM was judged successful and actively supported its users, but was delivered through an exploratory, emergent prototyping process; IT skills entered the project via personal relationships rather than formal corporate processes, with implementation driven by project employees rather than the central IT function. ^p1

## Introduction & Background ^sec-intro

Construction has obvious affinities with IT — large information volumes, distributed participants, production focus — yet on-site IT adoption has historically lagged predictions. The paper frames the gap not as a technology problem but as one of organisational implementation in a project-based, contractor-led setting. ^p1

The hospital project is used as a critical case of "Site BIM": BIM information made available *at the workface* via mobile tablets, with two-way capture of quality/progress data feeding back into the model. ^p2

## Method ^sec-method

1. Qualitative single-case study of a large UK hospital construction project, using interviews and observation of the main contractor's project team and IT roles. ^p1
2. Tracing the trajectory of the Site BIM toolset from initial prototypes to deployed mobile tablet workflows on site. ^p2
3. Analysing how technical IT skills, relationships, and authority were configured around the toolset (project team vs. corporate IT). ^p3

## Key Findings ^sec-results

- "Site BIM" was perceived as successful by site users and actively supported the work, not just observed it. ^f1
- The system emerged via **informal, exploratory prototyping** rather than a controlled corporate IT programme. ^f2
- Technical IT skills entered the project through personal relationships and informal arrangements. ^f3
- Implementation authority sat with project employees, not the central IT function — a contractor-led, project-bounded innovation pattern. ^f4

## Discussion & Implications ^sec-discussion

The case illustrates that even when BIM tooling is technically mature, its on-site value is realised through emergent, locally driven implementations rather than corporate roll-outs — an early signal of why later DT-on-site initiatives face similar socio-technical bottlenecks. ^p1

The reliance on personal relationships and project-led IT authority foreshadows governance questions for DT: who owns the model, who edits it, and who is accountable for the data captured at the workface. ^p2

## Conclusion ^sec-conclusion

Site BIM on a large hospital project succeeded because it was prototyped and owned at the project, not the firm, level; future BIM/DT deployments at the construction stage should expect and design for emergent, relationship-driven implementation. ^p1
