## Abstract ^sec-abstract

In light of the digital transformation of power systems and the increasing integration of renewable energy, grid control architecture has gradually evolved into deeply coupled cyber-physical power systems (CPPS) with chain-like and coordinated security risks. A Digital Twin-Based Framework for Attack Chain Modelling and Defence Strategy Verification in Power Grids is proposed in this paper. A bidirectional mapping model of the physical and cyber layers is built in this way to detect abnormal behaviour in time and space, and then adjust. A data-driven digital twin model of LSTM networks is used to address typical denial-of-service (DoS) attack scenarios and realize attack detection, alert, and self-healing decision verification. According to the results of the simulation, the proposed method can effectively reconstruct the process of the attack chain and improve the robustness and adaptive recovery capabilities of the control system in multi-node parallel systems significantly.

## Introduction & Background ^sec-intro

As power systems digitalise and integrate renewables, grid control architectures evolve into tightly coupled cyber-physical power systems (CPPS) exposed to chain-like, coordinated security risks. The paper targets the gap between conventional, siloed security monitoring and the need for real-time, model-based attack-chain detection and defence verification.

## Method ^sec-method

A Digital Twin–based framework builds a bidirectional mapping between physical and cyber layers to detect anomalous behaviour in space and time. A data-driven DT using LSTM networks models typical denial-of-service (DoS) scenarios to deliver attack detection, alerting, and self-healing decision verification, evaluated through simulation of multi-node parallel systems.

## Key Findings ^sec-results

The simulated framework reconstructs the attack-chain process and significantly improves the robustness and adaptive recovery of the control system in multi-node parallel settings, supporting verification of defence strategies before deployment.

## Discussion & Implications ^sec-discussion

The work illustrates the DT-as-security-sandbox pattern: a synchronised virtual replica for threat hunting, detection, and self-healing verification. While the application domain is power-grid CPS rather than building/infrastructure AECO, the bidirectional cyber-physical mapping and LSTM-based predictive detection are methodologically relevant to cyber-resilient DTs for built-asset operations.
