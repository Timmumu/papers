## Abstract ^sec-abstract

Accurately sensing the loading status is crucial for the structural health monitoring of in-service tunnel linings. This paper proposes a method for constructing a mechanically grounded Digital Twin (DT) of tunnel linings, comprising two components: (i) Bayesian learning of the loading conditions on the linings from easily observable data, and (ii) driving a mechanical model to replicate real-world behaviour based on these learned conditions. Developed within a statistical framework, the method systematically quantifies associated uncertainties, providing insights for constructing an informative DT model. A numerical case demonstrates that the DT can accurately reproduce comprehensive structural responses throughout in-service linings and enables virtual trial-and-error simulations for predicting future performance; it is further validated on two experimentally reported cases from the literature.

## Introduction & Background ^sec-intro

Structural health monitoring (SHM) of in-service tunnel linings depends on knowing the loading status, which is rarely directly observable. The authors argue that many data-driven DT efforts lack a *mechanically grounded* link between easily observable signals and the underlying structural state, limiting their use for reliability assessment and prediction.

## Method ^sec-method

A two-component DT is proposed: (i) Bayesian learning that infers lining loading conditions from easily observable data, and (ii) a mechanical model driven by those learned conditions to replicate real-world behaviour. The statistical framework systematically quantifies uncertainty, supporting an *informative* DT. The approach is exercised on a numerical case and validated against two experimentally reported cases from the literature.

## Key Findings ^sec-results

The DT accurately reproduces comprehensive structural responses throughout in-service linings and supports virtual trial-and-error simulation for predicting future performance. Uncertainty quantification both guides DT refinement and identifies effective strategies for uncertainty reduction; validation confirms effectiveness while exposing limitations that frame future work.

## Discussion & Implications ^sec-discussion

The paper exemplifies a probabilistic, physics-grounded DT paradigm for civil-infrastructure SHM, integrating Bayesian inversion with a mechanical model and explicit uncertainty quantification. This aligns with the *predictive / cognitive* DT capabilities central to this review — load inference from sparse observations plus what-if simulation — and offers a transferable template beyond tunnels to other load-bearing assets.
