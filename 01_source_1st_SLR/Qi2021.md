## Abstract ^sec-abstract

DT is portrayed as revolutionising industry: fed by sensor updates and historical data, sophisticated models can mirror almost every facet of a product, process or service, and in the future everything physical may be replicated digitally via DT. Despite its attention, DT is far from realising its potential because it is a complex, long-drawn system and many researchers are unclear which technologies and tools to use. The 5-dimension DT model offers reference guidance for understanding and implementing DT. From that perspective, the paper investigates and summarises frequently used enabling technologies and tools for DT to give practitioners technology and tool guidance. ^p1

## Introduction & Background ^sec-intro

DT is positioned as a sensor-driven, model-rich mirroring of physical reality whose realisation has been hampered by tool/technology confusion among researchers and engineers. ^p1

The five-dimension DT model (physical entity, virtual entity, services, DT data, connections) is adopted as the structuring frame for the technology and tool survey. ^p2

## Method ^sec-method

1. Adopt the 5-dimension DT model as the analytical frame. ^p1
2. For each dimension, systematically identify frequently used enabling technologies. ^p2
3. For each dimension, catalogue the frequently used enabling tools/software supporting those technologies. ^p3
4. Synthesise into reference guidance for DT implementers. ^p4

## Key Findings ^sec-results

- The five-dimension DT model is presented as the unifying reference for organising DT technology. ^f1
- Enabling technologies are mapped per dimension (physical, virtual, services, data, connections). ^f2
- Enabling tools/software are catalogued per dimension. ^f3
- The combined map is offered as practitioner guidance for selecting DT technology stacks. ^f4

## Discussion & Implications ^sec-discussion

By tying technology choices to a structural model, the paper turns an open-ended technology landscape into a checklist-style design space that AECO-DT designers can reuse with sector-specific substitutions. ^p1

The five-dimension model becomes a natural backbone for the cognitive full-lifecycle DT framework, with cognitive capability added as a service-dimension augmentation. ^p2

## Conclusion ^sec-conclusion

DT technology and tooling can be made tractable when organised by the five-dimension model, providing the implementer guidance that the field had been lacking. ^p1

## Relevance to Cognitive Full-Lifecycle DT SLR ^sec-relevance

- **Lifecycle stage(s):** design, manufacturing, operation, service (cross-lifecycle)
- **Cognitive capability:** perception (sensor/data), prediction (virtual entity), reasoning (services)
