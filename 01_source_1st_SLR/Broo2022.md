## Abstract ^sec-abstract
The paper presents an operational design and implementation of a smart infrastructure Digital Twin spanning structural sensing, data ingestion, model synchronization and decision support on a real infrastructure asset. The authors articulate an end-to-end pipeline — from instrumentation to dashboard — and document the engineering choices needed for a deployable DT rather than a purely conceptual reference model. ^p1

## Method ^sec-method
Single in-situ case study (smart infrastructure asset) coupled with a layered DT reference architecture (physical layer → data layer → model layer → service layer). Sensor hardware, ingestion middleware and visualization layers are described together with the validation strategy used to verify model fidelity against measured data. ^p1

## Key Findings ^sec-results

- A deployable infrastructure DT requires explicit separation of data, model and service layers; conflating them at the dashboard level prevents downstream automation. ^f1

- Real-time synchronization between the physical asset and the digital model is the dominant engineering cost, dwarfing the cost of the BIM/3D component. ^f2

- The case study exposes the gap between widely-cited DT reference frameworks and the much narrower scope of what current commercial stacks can actually deliver in operations. ^f3


## Discussion & Implications ^sec-discussion
Provides one of the few end-to-end engineering blueprints for an operational infrastructure DT in AECO and is therefore frequently cited as evidence of feasibility in monitoring scenarios. However, the case demonstrates only Level 1–2 (digital shadow / one-way feedback) capability and does not close the cognitive loop. ^p1
