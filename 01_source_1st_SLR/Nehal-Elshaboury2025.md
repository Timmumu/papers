## Abstract ^sec-abstract

> Rapid urbanization has resulted in enormous quantities of demolition waste in recent years. Designing effective waste management strategies requires assessment of generated waste during the end-of-life stage of buildings; however, information on the quantity and composition of building stock is lacking. This paper presents a robust bottom-up approach using 5D geomodeling and digital twin concepts to predict building stocks and analyze their spatial distribution by leveraging the capabilities of building information modeling (BIM) and geographic information system (GIS). The material intensity and composition are estimated based on on-site investigations, laser scanning to capture detailed building geometry, and a BIM model to represent building components digitally. Additionally, the spatial distribution of building stock is modeled based on a GIS model. The proposed approach is demonstrated and verified in a case study of 23 typical residential buildings in an Egyptian district. The proposed BIM-GIS framework is a potential game-changer in the architecture, engineering, and construction industry. It thereby promotes effective waste management strategies across various districts by assessing their building stock.
