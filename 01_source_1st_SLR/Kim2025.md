## Abstract
^sec-abstract

> Construction safety assessment traditionally depends on rule-based image classifiers that recognize fixed PPE or hazard categories but fail when scene context (weather, task, crew configuration) matters. This work investigates how large vision-language models (LVLMs) can be optimized — through prompt engineering, lightweight adapter fine-tuning, and retrieval-augmented prompting — to produce **context-aware** safety judgements that include the rationale ("worker is at edge without harness; concrete pour active; high wind"). The authors benchmark several open and proprietary LVLMs against human-annotated construction scenes covering fall, struck-by, and electrical hazards. Optimized LVLMs achieve substantial gains in both accuracy and rationale plausibility versus baseline CNN classifiers, but exhibit calibration issues (overconfident wrong answers) and remain sensitive to prompt phrasing.

## Core Claims
^sec-claims

- ① **Context** (task phase, weather, crew, equipment) materially changes the safety verdict for the same visual scene — fixed-label classifiers cannot capture this.
- ② LVLMs, when prompted or adapted appropriately, can integrate visual evidence with textual safety rules and produce **explanations**, not just labels.
- ③ The dominant failure mode is **overconfidence**: LVLMs report high certainty on incorrect judgements, directly motivating the UQ research gap raised in [LR_cc.md Sec 4.3](LR_cc.md).
- ④ Domain-adapted prompts + small construction-specific adapter weights beat pure prompting and pure fine-tuning.

## Method
- Comparative benchmark on annotated construction safety scenes.
- Three optimization regimes: prompt-only, adapter fine-tuning, retrieval-augmented prompting.
- Evaluation: accuracy, rationale plausibility, calibration (ECE).

## Relevance to Cognitive DT (LR_cc.md Sec 3.4 + 4.3)
- Anchors the Table 2 **Perception** cluster claim that foundation models bring context-awareness to construction safety.
- Provides empirical evidence for LR_cc.md's argument that AECO DT systems lack calibrated uncertainty (Sec 4.3) — even SOTA LVLMs are miscalibrated.

## Limitations Noted
- Benchmark is curated; site-level deployment latency/cost not evaluated.
- Calibration remains poor — explicit conformal or Bayesian wrapping not yet attempted in this work.
- Prompt sensitivity threatens reproducibility.
