## Abstract ^sec-abstract

Construction has incurred the most fatalities of any US private-sector industry in recent years, with designers often lacking design-for-safety knowledge so that hazards are built into project designs. The authors develop a software-based Prevention through Design (PTD) tool that compiles construction safety best practices into computable rules and performs automatic compliance checking on BIM models, with emphasis on fall hazards — the most frequent fatality cause. The tool delivers PTD suggestions to designers and constructors so that safety can be optimised collaboratively, enabling preliminary safety planning from the start of the project and reducing site hazards during design and preconstruction. ^p1

## Introduction & Background ^sec-intro

The authors open with the alarming statistic that construction leads US private-sector fatalities, framing the problem as systemic rather than incidental. ^p1

They identify the root cause as designer knowledge gaps that lead to safety hazards being embedded in designs, motivating tool support for design-for-safety. ^p2

The Prevention through Design (PTD) paradigm is introduced as the conceptual frame: shift safety upstream into design via computable rules. ^p3

## Method ^sec-method

1. Compile construction safety best practices into computable rules. ^p1
2. Develop a BIM-based PTD software tool that performs automatic compliance checking against those rules. ^p2
3. Specialise rule coverage to fall hazards as the dominant fatality category. ^p3
4. Deliver PTD suggestions to designers and constructors as actionable design recommendations. ^p4

## Key Findings ^sec-results

- A PTD tool capable of automatic safety-rule compliance checking on BIM models was implemented. ^f1
- Fall-related rules are the primary focus, addressing the most frequent construction-fatality cause. ^f2
- The tool surfaces PTD suggestions to designers and constructors. ^f3
- Preliminary safety planning can be initiated from the design/preconstruction phase via tool output. ^f4

## Discussion & Implications ^sec-discussion

The work demonstrates that BIM is not only a geometric container but a substrate for executable safety reasoning — a precursor to the rule/AI reasoning layer of a cognitive DT. ^p1

By moving safety upstream into design, it establishes a lifecycle pattern (design → construction) that a cognitive full-lifecycle DT generalises to operation and decommissioning. ^p2

## Conclusion ^sec-conclusion

BIM-based PTD rule checking can systematically reduce fall hazards by embedding safety reasoning into the design phase, validating the case for executable safety logic on building models. ^p1
