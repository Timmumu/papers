## Abstract ^sec-abstract

Engineering software and digitalised equipment generate massive but hysteretic and isolated data across the industrial product lifecycle, yielding low utilisation. Conventional simulation, based on theoretical and static models, has been confined to early-stage verification and validation and ignores runtime use. With new-generation information and digitalisation technologies, the digital twin (DT) concept has emerged to deeply exploit lifecycle data. The authors search several databases, retain 240 academic publications under explicit criteria, and provide a comprehensive review of DT concepts, technologies and industrial applications. ^p1

## Introduction & Background ^sec-intro

The paper frames DT as a response to the data-hysteresis and data-island problem produced by heterogeneous engineering tools across product lifecycle phases. ^p1

It positions DT against conventional simulation, arguing that DT extends model use from planning into the runtime/operation phase, which simulation alone cannot deliver. ^p2

It situates the review against a rapidly growing but fragmented literature where definitions, paradigms, frameworks, applications and enabling technologies are still under dispute in both academia and industry. ^p3

## Method ^sec-method

1. Database search across multiple academic indexes. ^p1
2. Application of explicit inclusion/exclusion criteria to identify 240 publications on DT. ^p2
3. Classification of retained literature by concept, enabling technology, and industrial application domain, followed by comparative analysis. ^p3

## Key Findings ^sec-results

- 240 DT publications were retained and classified into concept, technology and application strands. ^f1
- DT is consistently characterised as a means to bridge physical and virtual spaces and to enable runtime data exploitation, going beyond static simulation. ^f2
- Definitions, paradigms and frameworks remain non-unified across the surveyed corpus, signalling an unsettled conceptual base. ^f3
- Industrial applications are spread across the product lifecycle but show maturity gaps between phases. ^f4

## Discussion & Implications ^sec-discussion

The authors argue that DT realisation depends on integrating dispersed lifecycle data with runtime-capable models, and that resolving conceptual heterogeneity is a precondition for cross-domain interoperability. ^p1

For AECO, the implication is that any sector-specific DT framework must inherit this lifecycle integration logic rather than only port BIM-style static models. ^p2

## Conclusion ^sec-conclusion

DT is consolidating as a runtime, data-rich extension of simulation, but conceptual fragmentation and uneven application maturity remain the dominant gaps in the surveyed 240-paper corpus. ^p1
