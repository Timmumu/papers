## Abstract ^sec-abstract
As digital technologies become widely used in designing buildings and infrastructure, questions arise about their impacts on construction safety. The review explores the relationships between construction safety and digital design practices to foster and direct further research. It surveys state-of-the-art work on databases, virtual reality, geographic information systems, 4D CAD, building information modeling, and sensing technologies, finding many digital tools that address safety issues in the *construction phase* but few that support *design for construction safety*. It also considers literature on safety-critical, digital, and design practices that raises concerns about "mindlessness" in technology use, with implications for the research agenda on construction safety and digital design. The synthesis points to new kinds of interventions — tools and processes that use digital models to promote *mindfulness* through multi-party collaboration on safety. ^p1

## Introduction & Background ^sec-intro
Digital design technologies (BIM, 4D CAD, VR, GIS, sensors, databases) had, by 2011, become integral to building and infrastructure design, but their effects on safety outcomes were under-theorized: tooling proliferated faster than evidence about safety impact. ^p1

The authors therefore set out to map the field — what digital tools touch safety, in which phases, with what assumptions — and to highlight an asymmetry: most tools serve construction-phase safety management, while design-phase support for *Designing for Construction Safety* (DfCS) remains underdeveloped. ^p2

## Method ^sec-method
The paper is a structured literature review organized around (i) safety through design, (ii) databases and information systems for safety, (iii) virtual reality and visualization, (iv) GIS, 4D CAD, and BIM, and (v) sensing technologies, followed by (vi) a critical reading of safety-critical and design-practice literatures that raises the "mindlessness" concern. ^p1

The synthesis is interpretive rather than meta-analytic: it identifies gaps, asymmetries, and research directions, and proposes that future tools should support multi-party collaboration that cultivates *mindfulness* (in the safety-science sense) rather than substituting computation for attention. ^p2

## Key Findings ^sec-results
- Digital tools for safety in the *construction phase* are well developed, but tools supporting *design for construction safety* are scarce. ^f1
- The literature on safety-critical and digital design practices warns of "mindlessness" — over-reliance on tools eroding situational attention — as a systemic risk of digital design. ^f2
- The most promising future direction is the use of digital models to promote *mindfulness* through multi-party collaboration on safety, not pure automation. ^f3
- The review sets the agenda for what later becomes the Design-for-Safety / Prevention-through-Design research stream in AECO. ^f4

## Discussion & Implications ^sec-discussion
The "mindlessness vs. mindfulness" framing is a distinctive contribution: it argues that the cognitive value of a DT or BIM-based safety tool depends not only on its algorithms but on the human-AI collaboration it enables. This anticipates current discussions of human-in-the-loop DT operation. ^p1

The review's identification of a DfCS gap directly motivated the subsequent wave of automated rule-checking work (Zhang2013, Hossain2018, Qi2014) and ontology-based DfS systems (Zhang2015). ^p2

## Conclusion ^sec-conclusion
Digital design has reshaped construction safety practice but has so far concentrated on construction-phase tools; the next research frontier lies in design-phase interventions that use digital models to foster mindful, multi-party collaboration on safety. ^p1
