## Introduction & Background ^sec-intro

The study investigates the transition to a **circular economy (CE) in construction** in 2025, exploring how digital technologies including **digital product passports (DPPs), digital building logbooks (DBLs), and digital twins** enhance circularity aligned with EN 15643-3:2012, reducing environmental impacts amid evolving EU regulations.

## Method ^sec-method

A systematic literature review (PRISMA/RoSES) thematically analysed 63 articles from Scopus and Web of Science, identifying trends and patterns to assess the role of 12 specific digital technologies.

## Key Findings ^sec-results

**BIM integrated with DPPs and DBLs**, alongside IoT, Blockchain, Big Data, and **Digital Twins**, plays a pivotal role in optimising resource use and lifecycle management for circular construction. The framework connects traceability with sustainability, offering actionable tools for material management and policy.

## Discussion & Implications ^sec-discussion

**Strongly relevant** — a CE + digital-tech framework with DT positioned alongside DPP/DBL for construction circularity, aligned with EU standards. A strong citation for the DT-circularity and regulatory-compliance strands.
